# dexcap_2024 — DexCap: Scalable and Portable Mocap Data Collection System for Dexterous Manipulation (Wang, Shi, Wang, Zhang, Fei-Fei, Liu; Stanford, RSS 2024)

sources: papers/md/dexcap_2024.md [sha ad87015f] ; code/md/dexcap_2024.md [commit 4b0bed09]

## One-line contribution
DexCap is a $4k, 3.96-lb backpack mocap rig (EMF gloves for fingers + SLAM-tracked wrist cameras + a chest RGB-D LiDAR) that records 6-DoF wrist and finger motion at 60Hz without any robot hardware, and DexIL retargets this data via fingertip IK + point-cloud diffusion policy to train bimanual LEAP-hand robot policies directly from human mocap (72% average success on 3 basic tasks from 30 minutes of data), extended by an optional human-in-the-loop residual/teleoperation correction mode that raises performance on failure-prone tasks like scissor-cutting and tea-pouring.

## Setting
- hand(s): robot side is a bimanual pair of LEAP Hands (4-finger, 16 joints, ~50% larger than a human hand)
  - arm: two Franka Emika arms
  - bimanual. Human side wears Rokoko EMF motion-capture gloves (fingertip magnetic sensors relative to a dorsal signal-receiver hub) plus an Intel RealSense T265 SLAM camera per glove for 6-DoF wrist pose, and a chest-mounted Intel RealSense L515 RGB-D LiDAR camera for 3D scene observation (mounted on a wearable vest during human collection, moved to a stand between the robot arms for evaluation, swappable via a quick-release buckle in <5s).
- simulator / physics: none — entirely real-world data collection and real-robot evaluation; both robot arms and LEAP hands run at 20Hz control (end-effector position control for arms, joint position control for hands).
- observation: point-cloud-based — RGB-D frames converted to point clouds using camera parameters, transformed into a consistent world frame (defined by the initial pose of the main SLAM tracking camera) to remove torso-movement artifacts from in-the-wild data collection
  - observation o_t downsampled to K points, each augmented with RGB color -> R^{K×6}
  - forward kinematics is used to render the robot's own link geometry into the point cloud (bridging the visual gap between human and robot hand appearance) rather than masking hands out.
- action space: robot proprioceptive state s_t = (p_t, J_t) — 6-DoF wrist pose p_t=[R_t|T_t] and LEAP-hand finger joint positions J_t (16-dim, little finger excluded from IK matching since LEAP has only 4 fingers)
  - position-control action label a_t = s_{t+1} (next future state)
  - policy outputs an action-chunk trajectory (a_t,...,a_{t+d}). Human-in-the-loop residual correction: a_t' = (p_{t+1} + α·Δp_t^H, J_{t+1} + β·ΔJ_t^H), with Δp^H, ΔJ^H the human hand's delta motion relative to its pose at rollout start, α/β scaling factors (β kept < 0.1 empirically for usable finger-correction feel)
  - a foot-pedal switch toggles to full teleoperation mode (robot fingertips directly track human fingertips via IK, ignoring the policy).
- objects / data: recorded at 60Hz, downsampled to 20Hz to match robot control (correction data collected directly at 20Hz). 30 minutes of DexCap data per task for the first three tasks yields 251 (Sponge Picking), 179 (Ball Collecting), 102 (Plate Wiping) demos
  - 1 hour of in-the-wild data (mixture from >10 scenes) yields 104 demos for Packaging
  - 1 hour each for Scissor Cutting (96 demos) and Tea Preparing (55 demos). 6 tasks total: Sponge Picking, Ball Collecting, Plate Wiping (single/dual-hand basics), Packaging (bimanual, 6 trained + 9 unseen objects), Scissor Cutting (precision tool use), Tea Preparing (long-horizon, uncap bottle -> pick up tweezers -> extract tea -> pour).

## Method
- paradigm: IL (behavior cloning via a generative/diffusion policy), trained purely on human mocap-derived data with an optional human-in-the-loop (IWR-style) fine-tuning stage — no RL, no simulation.
- algorithm: DexIL, a 3-step pipeline. (1) Data retargeting: fingertip-based IK maps human hand mocap into the 16-dim LEAP-hand joint space, matching fingertip 3D positions (motivated by evidence from HO3D/GRAB/ARCTIC that fingertips are the most frequently contacted hand region), with the mocap 6-DoF wrist pose used to initialize/anchor the IK
  - little-finger data discarded for the 4-fingered LEAP hand. (2) Point-cloud-based diffusion policy: a Diffusion Policy (Chi et al.) architecture is used as the action decoder over point-cloud + proprioceptive-state input, chosen because MLP-based BC-RNN underperforms it on high-dimensional bimanual (2×7-DoF arm + 2×16-DoF hand) action regression
  - a Perceiver encoder is used for point-cloud features (DP-perc), outperforming a PointNet encoder by up to 20 points of success rate on multi-object bimanual tasks (Plate Wiping). (3) Human-in-the-loop correction (Sec. IV-C): residual mode (small delta corrections layered on top of policy actions, scaled by α, β) or full teleoperation mode (foot-pedal-toggled), with corrected trajectories stored in a new dataset D' and sampled with equal probability alongside the original dataset D to fine-tune the policy (explicitly analogous to IWR).
- Reward/loss: standard diffusion-policy training objective (denoising loss over the action trajectory; not restated as a novel formula in the parsed text) — this is a pure IL/BC paper, no reward function.
- key trick(s): converting torso-moving in-the-wild RGB-D into a world-frame-stabilized point cloud is what specifically enables learning from unconstrained, moving-camera human data (image-based baselines collapse to near-zero on in-the-wild data because they cannot handle the moving viewpoint, Sec. V-B "Q4")
  - using point clouds (rather than masking hands with a green/white circle in images) preserves fine finger detail needed for in-hand manipulation, where the masked-image baseline (DP-img-mask) fails specifically on Plate Wiping, which needs fine-grained fingertip grasping of a plate edge.

## Evaluation
- metrics: task success rate over 20 trials per task per model variant (randomized initial placements)
  - for multi-step tasks, an additional "Subtask" metric (e.g., successfully placing an object in the box for Packaging, or picking up scissors for Scissor Cutting)
  - for the multi-object Packaging task, each of 6 trained + 9 unseen objects tested with 5 trials each (30 + 45 = 75 total trials).
- headline numbers: Table I (DexCap-data-only, 3 basic tasks, 20 trials/task) — image-input baselines fail completely: BC-RNN-img 0.00/0.00/0.00, DP-img 0.00/0.00/0.00 (visual appearance gap between human and robot hands)
  - masking helps but caps out: BC-RNN-img-mask 0.15 avg, DP-img-mask 0.42 avg
  - point-cloud methods clear 60%+: BC-RNN-point 0.33, BC-RNN-prec 0.38, DP-point-raw 0.60, DP-point 0.63, and the full method DP-perc (Ours) reaches Sponge Picking 0.85, Ball Collecting 0.60, Plate Wiping 0.70, Overall 0.72 (the paper's headline "up to 72% average task success rate" claim). Table II (Packaging, in-the-wild data vs. +30 human corrections, Subtask/All/Unseen): BC-RNN-img-mask 0.00/0.00/0.00 -> 0.23/0.07/0.00
  - BC-RNN-point 0.33/0.23/0.16 -> 0.40/0.27/0.22
  - DP-img-mask 0.17/0.00/0.00 -> 0.47/0.33/0.00
  - Ours 0.70/0.47/0.40 -> 0.83/0.57/0.42 (human-in-the-loop correction gives every method a substantial boost, largest relative gain for the image-mask baseline because correction data is collected on the same fixed camera used at eval, unlike in-the-wild data's moving camera). Table III (Scissor Cutting, Subtask/All): DexCap-only — BC-RNN-point 0.00/0.00, Ours 0.00/0.00 (both fail without correction)
  - +30 human corrections — BC-RNN-point 0.10/0.00, Ours 0.45/0.20. Table IV (Tea Preparing, Subtask=uncapping / All): DexCap-only Ours 0.30/0.00
  - +30 human corrections 0.65/0.25 (a +35-point subtask improvement and +25-point full-task success from just 30 correction trials).
- data-collection throughput: DexCap collection speed on Ball Collecting is described as "close to natural human motion and... three times faster than traditional teleoperation" (Fig. 8, no exact demos/hour number given in the parsed text).
- baselines beaten: BC-RNN (image, image+mask, point-cloud, Perceiver variants) and Diffusion Policy (image, image+mask variants) — ablating action-decoder architecture (BC-RNN vs. DP, ~25-point average gap favoring DP), observation modality (image vs. masked-image vs. point cloud), and point-cloud encoder (PointNet vs. Perceiver).
- real robot? Yes, exclusively — bimanual Franka+LEAP-Hand setup, 20 trials/task (75 for Packaging's object-generalization split), zero on-robot teleoperation data used to train the base policies (Q2's explicit claim: "DEXCAP data can directly train dexterous robot policies" with no on-robot data).

## Limitations stated by the authors
(1) Battery/power limits continuous collection to at most ~40 minutes per session (40,000mAh power bank; future work targets better power efficiency). (2) Fingertip-IK retargeting cannot fully close the human-robot hand size/thickness gap (some robot fingers are thicker than human fingers), making certain tasks (the paper's example: playing piano) difficult; future robotic hand designs are suggested as a fix rather than an algorithmic one. (3) DexCap records only 3D observations and hand motion, with no force/tactile sensing — the authors identify this directly as the cause of a specific observed failure (right hand cannot stabilize a box during lid-closing because it has no force feedback) and propose future use of conformal tactile textiles (Sec. VI).

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "the learned policies achieve up to 72% average task success rate in single-hand pick-and-place... and bimanual coordination... tasks. This result highlights the effectiveness of DEXCAP data on training dexterous robot policies without on-robot data" (Sec. V-B, Q2).
- "the policies with full image inputs fail completely (BC-RNN-img, DP-img)... diffusion policy works better than MLP-based BC-RNN policies (25% higher in averaged task success rate)" (Sec. V-B, Q3).
- "image-input baselines (BC-RNN-img-mask, DP-img-mask) have close to zero performance when learning with in-the-wild data. This observation verifies our hypothesis that the viewpoint changes caused by human body movements during in-the-wild data collection bring challenges to learning image-based policies" (Sec. V-B, Q4).
- "the absence of force information in DEXCAP data causes the right hand to struggle with stabilizing the box during box closure attempts by the left hand" (Sec. V-B, Q4).
- "With just 30 human correction trials during policy rollout, the fine-tuned policy... achieves a 33% improvement in the full task success rate for trained objects" (Sec. V-B, Q5).

## Notes for the survey (which sections this feeds; contradictions with other notes)
Feeds "portable mocap data-collection hardware" alongside dexwild_2025 (palm-camera glove rig, IK-based retargeting, larger scale: 9,290 demos) and dexumi_2025 (exoskeleton, joint-encoder-precise, no visual retargeting needed since it directly reads robot-space joint angles). DexCap is the earliest (2024) and smallest-scale of the three (dozens-to-low-hundreds of demos per task, 30min-1hr collection sessions) but is the one that most directly demonstrates the value of point-cloud observation (vs. RGB) for bridging the human-robot visual gap without an exoskeleton or palm camera — its ablation (Table I: image baselines at 0%, point-cloud-based at 60-72%) is a clean, quotable number for the survey's "observation representation" comparison. The human-in-the-loop residual/teleoperation correction mechanism (drawing on IWR) is architecturally distinct from every other paper in this batch and worth cross-referencing against dexteleop0_2026's shared-autonomy correction (both let a human intervene mid-rollout, but DexCap's correction retrains the BC policy offline via dataset mixing, while DexTeleop-0's correction is a real-time force-based QP with no learning update). No interpenetration/contact-force measurement; the paper's own diagnosis of its box-closing failure (Q4) explicitly attributes it to the lack of force sensing, which is a useful citation for the survey's argument that vision/kinematics-only data collection has a structural ceiling on contact-rich tasks.
