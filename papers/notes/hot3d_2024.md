# hot3d_2024 — HOT3D: Hand and Object Tracking in 3D from Egocentric Multi-View Videos (Banerjee et al., CVPR 2025)

sources: papers/md/hot3d_2024.md [cbe0c55d] ; code/md/hot3d_2024.md [146b34af]

## One-line contribution
833 minutes / 3.7M+ multi-view egocentric images (1.5M+ multi-view frames) from 19 subjects wearing Project Aria glasses and Quest 3 headsets, interacting with 33 high-fidelity PBR-textured 3D-scanned objects across 4 scenarios, with motion-capture-grade ground-truth poses and shapes of **both hands** (UmeTrack + MANO) and all in-scene objects, benchmarked on 3D hand tracking, 6DoF object pose estimation, in-hand-object 2D segmentation, and 3D lifting of in-hand objects (Abstract, Sec. 3-4).

## Setting
- capture setup: two headsets recorded egocentric multi-view video simultaneously across the dataset (not the same take): **Project Aria** glasses (Meta) — 1 rolling-shutter RGB camera, 1408×1408 px, 30 fps, 110° FOV fisheye; 2 global-shutter monochrome cameras, 640×480 px, 30 fps, 150° FOV; 2 monochrome eye-tracking cameras, 320×240 px, 10 fps; 2 IMUs (800/1000 Hz), barometer (50 fps), magnetometer (10 fps); GNSS/WiFi/audio disabled for privacy; total device weight 75 g (App. A.1). Aria's on-device Machine Perception Services (MPS) supply 6DoF SLAM/VIO localization (mm-accurate, 1 kHz between frames), eye gaze (as two rays + focus depth, personalized via a calibration procedure), and causally-built static-scene point clouds (App. A.2). **Quest 3** headset (developer version) — 4 global-shutter monochrome fisheye cameras, 1280×1024 px, 18 PPD, 30 fps; only the 2 front-facing cameras are included in HOT3D (App. B); gyroscope/accelerometer not recorded; camera-to-headset calibrated via a ChArUco board tracked by the same mocap rig.
- ground-truth pose source: a dedicated **motion-capture lab** with "a few dozens of infrared exocentric OptiTrack cameras and light diffuser panels" (Fig. 5) — 3 mm adhesive optical markers on both hands (19 markers/hand) and on each object (~10 markers/object), semi-automatically registered to custom 3D-scanned hand/object models; hand poses obtained by fitting the UmeTrack hand model to tracked markers, object poses by aligning tracked markers to their registered model-frame locations; multi-source sync via SMPTE timecode (App. C, Sec. 3).
- hand model / shape: **both** UmeTrack and MANO formats are provided ("UmeTrack is more accurate while MANO is more standard," Sec. 3); each of the 19 participants' hands was individually 3D-scanned with a custom hand scanner, so both formats carry subject-specific shape, not a generic template.
- **two hands jointly annotated with multiple objects**: yes, explicitly — "high-quality pose annotations of the headset and multiple objects in each scene, as well as pose and shape annotations of **both hands**" (Sec. 1, contribution list); object and wrist poses are 3D rigid transforms from model space to scene space. Up to 6 objects per recording (Sec. 3).
- objects: 33 3D-scanned mesh models (household + office objects) with PBR materials (metallic/roughness/normal maps) for photorealistic rendering, built via an in-house scanning pipeline (Sec. 3, Fig. 3). Object travel: "subjects moved the 33 objects over 13km" total (Fig. 4 caption); the white mug is called out as the most-moved object, keyboard and waffles mostly stationary.
- subjects: 19, recruited "with different hand appearances and shapes" (Sec. 3).
- scenarios: 4 — a simple inspection scenario (pick up/observe/put down) plus kitchen, office, and living-room scenarios with scenario-specific furniture in the same lab, with randomized lighting/furniture placement/decor for diversity; each recording features interaction with up to 6 objects (Sec. 3).

## Sequences, frames, hours, splits
"833 minutes of recordings" total (Sec. 3 header); 1.5M+ multi-view frames, 3.7M+ images, 30 fps. 425 recordings total (198 Aria + 226 Quest 3), each ~2 minutes long. Ground truth: 1.16M of the 1.5M frames are "fully annotated... and passed our visual inspection"; all 1.5M frames are released (the remainder useful for unsupervised training) with a validity mask for the 1.16M. Train/test split: training = 13 subjects (1M multi-view frames), test = remaining 6 subjects (0.5M multi-view frames); ground-truth annotations are public only for the training split, test-split ground truth is held by evaluation servers (Sec. 3, "Training and test splits"). **HOT3D-Clips**: 3,832 curated 150-frame (5 s) clips passing additional QA (all hand/object annotations present, ≥1 hand and ≥1 object visible, no overexposed frames) — 2,804 from training / 1,028 from test; 1,983 from Aria / 1,849 from Quest 3 (Sec. 3). **Object onboarding sequences**: static (object upright and upside-down on a desk, GT pose for every frame — suited to NeRF-style reconstruction) and dynamic (object manipulated by hand, GT pose only for the first frame, to define canonical object space) (Sec. 3). App. A.3 gives a slightly different Aria-only processing count: "we have processed 199 Aria recordings with a total length of 391 minutes" — one more Aria recording than the "198 captured with Aria" stated in Sec. 3, a minor internal count discrepancy (198 vs 199), flagged rather than resolved.

## Contact / in-hand definitions (verbatim; note this paper does not define a penetration-depth metric)
HOT3D's own tasks do not define a penetration-depth or intersection-volume metric (unlike grab_2020/oakink_2022's GRAB-style contact heuristics). Its closest analogue is an **in-hand object criterion** used to build ground truth for the 2D-segmentation task (Sec. 4.3): "Objects are considered to be in-hand if the minimum distance between object and hand mesh vertices in their ground-truth poses is below a threshold of 1cm and the object is moving with a velocity larger than 1cm/s." This is a contact/motion proxy for "being held," not a physical-plausibility or interpenetration measure.

## Method (dataset + 4 benchmark tasks, all evaluating existing/extended methods, not a new learned policy)
- paradigm: dataset + benchmarking of (mostly) prior methods, with one proposed multi-view extension (FoundPose) and two proposed baselines (StereoMatch, MonoDepth) for 3D lifting.
- **4.1 3D hand pose tracking**: method UmeTrack, trained on (1) UmeTrack dataset (Quest 2, 1397 real + 1397 synthetic 15 s/30 fps sequences, 53 participants, single-hand + hand-hand only, no hand-object), (2) HOT3D-Quest3 training split, (3) both combined; single- vs two-view input (one view randomly masked during training to allow fair single/two-view comparison). Metric: Mean Keypoint Position Error (MKPE, mm) (Sec. 4.1). Results (Table 2, MKPE on UmeTrack / HOT3D): trained on UmeTrack only, 1-view 13.6/24.2, 2-view 9.7/25.6; trained on HOT3D-Quest3 only, 1-view 23.7/18.0, 2-view 30.3/13.1; trained on both, 1-view 13.4/15.4, 2-view **9.5/10.9** (best, "a significant 41% improvement" over single-view-both-trained). Explanation: "hand-object interactions are present only in HOT3D while hand-hand interactions only in the UmeTrack dataset," causing cross-dataset domain gaps that combined training closes.
- **4.2 6DoF object pose estimation**: FoundPose (CAD-model onboarding, DINOv2 patch features, bag-of-words template retrieval, PnP-RANSAC) vs. the paper's own multi-view extension (multi-view template retrieval + generalized PnP). Metric: recall at symmetry-aware translational+rotational error thresholds (5cm/5°, 10cm/10°, 20cm/20°). Results (Table 3, recall %): HOT3D-Aria 1-view 25.2/41.7/54.5 → 3-view **33.8/52.9/66.2**; HOT3D-Quest3 1-view 28.9/46.6/58.9 → 2-view **36.9/55.9/66.4**. "8-12% higher recall rates (13-34% relative improvement)" from multi-view.
- **4.3 2D segmentation of in-hand objects**: EgoHOS (off-the-shelf) vs. two Mask R-CNN variants (MRCNN on RGB, MRCNN-DA on Depth-Anything-V2-predicted depth), trained on a proprietary 400K-image / 52K-mask Aria dataset. Metric: mIoU. Results (Table 4, mIoU "Either" hand column): EgoHOS on its own dataset 62.2(L)/44.4(R)/52.8(Both, no "Either" given); on HOT3D-Aria: EgoHOS 42.6, MRCNN 47.1, MRCNN-DA **55.2**; on HOT3D-Quest3: EgoHOS 33.1, MRCNN 37.8, MRCNN-DA **54.7**. "MRCNN-DA is the top performing method, outperforming EgoHOS on Aria and Quest 3 frames by 30% and 65% respectively."
- **4.4 3D lifting of in-hand objects**: 3 baselines — HandProxy (ground-truth 3D palm-center as object location, palm = midpoint of wrist joint and first middle-finger joint; if both hands visible, pick the one whose 2D palm projection is nearer the object mask centroid), MonoDepth (Depth-Anything-V2 monocular depth + SLAM point-cloud scale-shift alignment, Aria-only since Quest 3 lacks SLAM), StereoMatch (DINOv2 ViT-S patch matching across a stereo crop pair, up to 500 correspondences by cyclic-distance filtering, triangulated and robust-meaned). Metric: recall at distance thresholds from GT bounding-box center. Results (Table 5, recall % at 5/10/20/30 cm, GT masks): HandProxy (Aria) 0.5/13.5/90.6/98.4; MonoDepth (Aria) 14.3/30.2/53.6/69.9; StereoMatch (Aria, 3 views) **64.4/86.2/95.5/96.9**; StereoMatch (Quest3, 2 views) 76.4/96.8/99.1/99.2. With MRCNN-DA-predicted masks instead of GT: MonoDepth (Aria) 11.1/23.3/43.7/58.2; StereoMatch (Aria) 42.6/56.4/63.6/66.0; StereoMatch (Quest3) 59.1/75.3/80.4/81.3. "hand tracking alone is sufficient for localizing objects within a 30cm uncertainty radius... but a dedicated method... provides additional value for finer-grained localization within 10cm."
- baselines beaten: in each of the 4 tasks, the multi-view/combined-training variant beats its single-view/single-dataset counterpart (see numbers above); this is the paper's central empirical claim, not a comparison against unrelated external SOTA.
- real robot?: none — pure egocentric human-capture dataset for AR/VR and vision research; the introduction motivates robot skill transfer only as a hypothetical downstream use ("The skills could be similarly transferred from humans to robots," Sec. 1) and it is not benchmarked in this paper.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Not framed as a dedicated "Limitations" section in the parsed excerpt; the Conclusion (Sec. 5) is framed entirely positively (multi-view "significantly outperform their single-view counterparts across several popular tasks"). Data-quality caveats are given inline rather than as authored limitations: "Annotations in some frames may be missing or be of a lower quality" (Sec. 3), and only 1.16M of 1.5M frames pass full ground-truth + visual-inspection QA.

## Notes for the survey
- Feeds the datasets section as the corpus's clearest example of a genuinely **two-hands + multiple-objects jointly annotated** egocentric dataset, with motion-capture-grade (not RGB-D-optimization) ground truth for both hand pose/shape and object pose — a stronger claim than H2O, HOI4D, or OakInk in this batch. Use it as the reference point when the survey needs a "true two-hand, multi-object, high-accuracy" egocentric benchmark, and pair with its explicit MANO+UmeTrack dual release for methods needing either representation.
- No penetration/intersection-volume metric is defined here — if the survey's metrics table includes HOT3D, its "contact" cell should point to the in-hand distance+velocity threshold above, not a physical-plausibility number; contrast directly with oakink_2022's four physical-plausibility metrics.
- Flag the Table 1 "Both"-vs-"Single" mislabeling of HOI4D's hand count when citing HOT3D's own comparison table — cross-checked against both HOT3D's own prose (Sec. 2) and HOI4D's own Sec. 7 admission, both of which say HOI4D is single-hand.
- Corpus papers citing HOT3D (grep -l "HOT3D" over papers/md, ≤15 cap): being_h0_2025, egodex_2025, gr_dexter_2025, gigahands_2024, unidex_2026.
- Minor internal count discrepancy noted for completeness: Sec. 3 says "198 captured with Aria," App. A.3 says "we have processed 199 Aria recordings" — do not silently pick one if the exact Aria recording count matters.
