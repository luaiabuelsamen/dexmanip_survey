## One-line contribution
Turns 141 TACO two-hand tool-use demonstrations into Isaac Gym Dec-POMDP tasks, trains left/right LEAP-hand teachers with IPPO under a generic two-stage reward (align to the demo's reference pose, then track the object trajectory), and distils them by DAgger into point-cloud students that reach r_2 = 74.59 % on trained and 51.07 % on unseen tasks (Table 1, Sec. 5.4).

## Setting
- hand(s): two 16-DoF LEAP Hands (Shaw et al. 2023a), left and right (App. B.4) ; arm: two 6-DoF RealMan arms spaced 0.68 m apart in front of a 0.7 m table (App. B.4; assets `rm65_leap_left_m1.urdf` / `rm65_leap_right_m1.urdf` in `rl_policy/cfgs/task/BiLeapHandGraspV6.yaml`) ; single/bimanual: bimanual, fixed roles ("we assume the robot to be right-handed by default, i.e., the left hand handles the target object and the right hand handles the tool", Sec. 4.2).
- coordination: two separate agents. Task formulated as a Dec-POMDP with N = 2 agents (Sec. 3.1); teachers are trained with Independent PPO (IPPO), "two agents possess their own observations and execute their own actions" (Sec. 5.2); each side's policy π_θ^side(a_t^side | o_t^side, a_{t-1}^side) sees only its own proprioception plus the state of *its* object (left: target object, right: tool) and its own last action (Sec. 4.3, Algorithm 1). No cross-hand or relative-hand observation is stated. The centralised variant BiDexHD-PPO ("a single policy takes as input both observations and is trained to output all actions", Sec. 5.2) was tried and lost. Code: `rl_policy/algo/marl/happo.py`, `mappo.py` and separate left/right buffers `add_transitions(..., left_actions, right_actions, left_rewards, right_rewards, ...)` in `rl_policy/algo/common/storage.py`; config `expertModel: "IPPO"` in `rl_policy/cfgs/config.yaml`.
- simulator / physics: Isaac Gym, PhysX; `dt: 0.01667 # 1/60`, `substeps: 2`, `contact_offset: 0.002`, `max_depenetration_velocity: 1000.0`, `contact_collection: 0 # CC_NEVER` (`rl_policy/cfgs/task/BiLeapHandGraspV0.yaml`); `controlFrequencyInv: 1 # 60 Hz`; 15 000 envs for IPPO, 5 000 for DAgger (Table 5, 6); 16384 default in yaml.
- observation (teacher): "arm-hand joint angles and velocities, wrist poses, and fingertip positions, and object information includes object positions, orientations, linear and angular velocities, and a unique object identifier" (Sec. 4.2); code string `dofps+dofvel+ftps+lastact+objstate+palmpose+relps+objlabel` (`expertObservationType`, `rl_policy/cfgs/task/BiLeapHandGraspM3Dagger.yaml`). `relps` is not defined in the parsed sources. Student: same proprioception + object point cloud (P = 512 points, Table 6) + K = 5 future object positions (`futureps`), one-hot label removed (Sec. 4.4, 5.2).
- action space: "left and right policies both output 22 joint angles normalized to [-1, 1], and the robots are controlled via position control" (Sec. 3.1); `armController: "qpos"`, `useRelativeControl: False`, `dofSpeedScale: 20.0` (V0 yaml). Episode length 1000 (Table 5).
- objects / data: TACO dataset (Liu et al. 2024b), 141 demonstrations in 6 verb categories {Dust, Empty, Pour in some, Put out, Skim off, Smear}, 16 semantic sub-tasks; split 94 Train / 9 Test Comb / 38 Test New (Table 4). Hands in MANO; robot fingers matched to m = 4 of the 21 MANO joints (Sec. 4.2).

## Method
- paradigm: RL (multi-task IPPO teachers) → IL (DAgger distillation to point-cloud student); no real-world learning.
- task construction: reference timestep = "first sudden change of the distance between an object and a tool" (App. B.1); retarget human hand to LEAP with Qin et al. 2023b optimiser + IK for the arm; "By replaying all object-hand trajectories in the simulator, we can easily identify and remove invalid tasks" (Sec. 4.2). z-offset added so all objects start at the same table height (App. B.1).
- reward (paper, Sec. 4.3; Eq. 3-8 recovered by OCR from papers/md/bidexhd_2024.ocr.md — OCR text is noisier than the layout parse, symbols may be imperfect, quoted close to verbatim with OCR's own flattened norm/indicator notation):
  - Stage 1 alignment = r_appro + r_lift + r_bonus, "linear weighted sum of the three components", Eq. 6: "r^side_align = w1 r^side_appro + w2 r^side_lift + w3 r^side_bonus".
  - r_appro "penalizes the distance between the wrist, fingertips, and the grasp center"; grasp center x̂_gc = mean of the top L = 50 of 1024 sampled surface points nearest to the human wrist/fingertips at the reference timestep. Eq. 3 (OCR): "r^side_appro = −‖x^side,w_t − x̂^obj_gc‖2 − (wr/m) ‖x^side,ft_t − x̂^obj_gc‖2 where x̂^obj_gc = (1/L) Σ_NN(P,L, x̂^side,w_0 + Σm x̂^side,ft_0 / (m+1))." (the OCR's grouping of the x̂_gc average term is garbled; the wr/m coefficient scaling the fingertip term is legible).
  - r_lift, "as long as the lifting conditions are satisfied", is "a non-negative linear position reward and a negative quaternion distance reward" toward the reference pose. Eq. 4 (OCR): "r^side_lift = r^side_pos + wq r^side_quat if ‖x^side,w_t − x̂^obj_gc‖2 ≤ λw ∩ ‖x^side,ft_t − x̂^obj_gc‖2 ≤ λft, else 0; where r^side_pos = max(1 − ‖x^obj_t − x̂^obj_0‖2 / ‖x^obj_0 − x̂^obj_0‖2, 0), r^side_quat = −Dquat(q^obj_t, q̂^obj_0)." — confirms λw and λft (Table 5) are the two lifting-condition distance thresholds (wrist-to-grasp-center and fingertip-to-grasp-center), not generic reward weights, resolving the earlier "sources do not say so" gap below.
  - r_bonus "becomes positive only when the distance between an object's current position and its reference position becomes lower than ε_succ"; stage 1 succeeds when both bonuses are positive for u consecutive steps. Eq. 5 (OCR): "r^side_bonus = 1/(1+‖x^obj_t − x̂^obj_0‖2) if ‖x^obj_t − x̂^obj_0‖2 ≤ εsucc, else 0."
  - Stage 2 r_track: "a more fine-grained exponential reward ... encourages the dexterous hands to precisely track the desired positions at each timestep", with f simulation steps per dataset step (f = `frequency: 3.0` in V6 yaml). Eq. 7 (OCR): "r^side_track = exp(−wt ‖x^obj_ti − x̂^obj_i‖2) if stage 1 succeeds, else 0."
  - Eq. 8 (OCR), the unified objective: "r^side_total = r^side_align + w4 r^side_track."
  - Weights, Table 5: w_r = 2.0, w_t = 15.0, w_1 = 0.5, w_2 = 1.0, w_3 = 1.0, w_4 = 1.0, λ_w = 0.12, λ_ft = 0.48. With Eq. 3/6/7/8 now recovered, w1-w4 are confirmed as the Eq. 6/8 linear weights on r_appro/r_lift/r_bonus/r_track respectively, w_r is the fingertip-term coefficient inside r_appro (Eq. 3), and w_t is the exponential-decay coefficient inside r_track (Eq. 7); λ_w/λ_ft are the Eq. 4 lifting-condition thresholds (see above) — matching the earlier inference from the code's 0.12 / 0.12*num_fingers grasp thresholds.
- reward (code, `rl_policy/tasks/bi_leap_hand_grasp_v6.py`, `compute_bvdex_stage12_rewards`, docstring "stage 1: reach a static ref object pose (linear reward) / stage 2: stage 1 finished, follow a dynamic ref object pose (exponential reward)"):
  - `left_palm_object_dist = torch.norm(object_grasp_pos - left_palm_pose[:, :3])`, clipped at 0.5; `left_fingertips_object_dist` = sum over 4 fingertips of distance to `object_grasp_pos`, clipped at 3.0 (`# Important!`); mirrored for right palm / tool.
  - `is_grasp_left = ((left_fingertips_object_dist <= 0.12 * num_fingers) + (left_palm_object_dist <= 0.12)).float()` — the "lifting condition" is a distance test, not a contact test.
  - `left_lift_object_pos_rew1 = (1 - ref_object_pos_dist / ref_init_object_pos_dist).clip(min=0)`; `left_lift_object_rot_rew1 = quat_rew(ref_object_pose[:, 3:7], object_pose[:, 3:7]) # [-1,1]`.
  - Stage-1 success (m2dagger variant, same file family): `left_successes = torch.logical_and(ref_object_pos_dist <= success_tolerance, left_fingertips_object_dist + left_palm_object_dist < 0.12 * (num_fingers + 1))`.
  - yaml scalars: `distRewardScale: 0.3`, `actionPenaltyScale: 0.001`, `successTolerance: 0.05` (V6) / `0.1` (M3Dagger); ablation switch `rewfunc: 'multippo'  # ab_stage1, ab_funcgrasp, ab_bonus` and `compute_ab_stage1_rewards` which uses object centre instead of `object_grasp_pos` (= the "w/o gc" ablation).
  - Mismatch / gap: the body is cut off in the parsed markdown before r_track, r_bonus and the weighted sum, so the 0.5/3.0 clips, the 0.12 grasp threshold and `actionPenaltyScale` appear only in code, while w_r, w_t, λ_w, λ_ft appear only in the paper. The paper's λ_w = 0.12 and λ_ft = 0.48 = 4 × 0.12 match the code's `0.12` and `0.12 * num_fingers` thresholds; Eq. 4 (recovered by OCR, quoted above) confirms these are the grasp-condition/lifting-condition thresholds, not reward weights.
- interpenetration / contact: not addressed. No contact-force, penetration, or collision term in the paper; `contact_collection: 0 # CC_NEVER` in every task yaml; grasp detection is purely by fingertip/palm distance to the grasp center.
- key trick(s): (i) auto-constructed tasks from a bimanual dataset instead of a hand-built benchmark; (ii) functional grasp center from human fingertips (Fig. 3: geometric center fails on thin/flat/handled objects); (iii) trajectory-conditioned student with K future object positions; DAgger mixes expert actions with p = 0.05 (App. B.5). Teacher: 5-layer MLP [1024,1024,512,512], ELU; student: simplified PointNet, 128-d (App. B.6). ~2 GPU-days per sub-task teacher, ~1 day per category student on a 40 GB A100 (App. B.7).

## Evaluation
- metrics (Sec. 5.1): r_1 = "success rate of stage 1"; r_2 = "average tracking rate of stage two ... the proportion of steps where two objects both effectively follows their desired poses", averaged over n episodes. "r_2 serves as the primary metric for indicating task completion". Default ε_succ = ε_track = 0.1 (metres implied by successTolerance in yaml; unit not stated in text). The exact r_1/r_2 formulas, recovered by OCR from papers/md/bidexhd_2024.ocr.md (OCR text is noisier than the layout parse, symbols may be imperfect; indicator/sum notation is flattened): "r1 = (1/n) Σ^n_{e=1} I^e_1 averages over the number of episodes that satisfy conditions I1 at stage one. I1 : ∃ 0<t<T−u, Σ^{t+u}_t I(‖x^object_t − x̂^object_0‖2 ≤ εsucc ∩ ‖x^tool_t − x̂^tool_0‖2 ≤ εsucc) = u" and "r2 = (1/nl) Σ^n Σ^{l-1}_{i=0} I(‖x^object_ti − x^object_i‖2 ≤ εtrack ∩ ‖x^tool_ti − x^tool_i‖2 ≤ εtrack)".
- headline numbers (Table 1, ε = 0.1, %): BiDexHD-IPPO+DAgger r_1/r_2 = 99.38/74.59 Train, 92.85/48.43 Test Comb, 94.79/53.71 Test New; the abstract's 51.07 % is the average of Test Comb and Test New r_2 (Sec. 5.4). Teacher BiDexHD-IPPO: 98.71/78.18, 98.37/59.94, 75.48/21.34. BiDexHD-PPO (centralised): 90.55/53.88, 78.74/36.99, 81.42/26.24. Ablations: w/o stage-1 25.00/17.52 Train; w/o gc 90.53/66.39; w/o bonus 97.67/66.65. BC: 0.00 everywhere. Per-category student (Table 13): Dust 86.91 Train r_2, Empty 73.20, Put out 71.89, Pour in some 72.31, Skim off 71.59, Smear 76.69. Threshold sensitivity (Table 3): at ε = 0.05 Train r_2 falls to 52.58 and Test Comb r_2 to 13.02. K ablation (Table 2): K = 0 gives 72.09 Train r_2 vs 74.59 at K = 5.
- baselines beaten: BiDexHD-PPO (+DAgger), BC via Dexpilot retargeting + IK replayed in Isaac Gym (Sec. 5.4). No external method is compared.
- real robot? none. Sim only; Sec. 3.2 says the student "is promising to be deployed in real world" given multi-view RGB-D point clouds.

## Limitations stated by the authors
- "Exploring strategies for achieving more precise spatial and temporal tracking is a valuable direction" — stricter ε hurts unseen tasks most (Sec. 5.4, Sec. 6).
- Tasks limited to tool-use; deformables and handover not covered (Sec. 6). Only LEAP hands used ("In future work we will introduce more kinds of dexterous hands", App. B.3).
- One-hot object labels make state-based teachers fail on new objects (Test New r_2 21.34 %, Sec. 5.2).

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "we find that it is not feasible to directly acquire dynamic skills from static poses through imitation. Instead, we adopt reinforcement learning to develop skills like grasping, twisting and pushing." (Sec. 4.3)
- "BiDexHD-IPPO is more efficient at acquiring robust skills within limited updates by independently learning left and right policies across a wide range of tasks with smaller observation and action spaces." (Sec. 5.2)
- "In fact, the geometric center of an object does not often fall within areas suitable for manipulation." (Sec. 5.3)
- "BC performs poorly due to the loss of true dynamics in the simulation, often getting confused by unfamiliar observations and stuck in stationary states." (Sec. 5.4)
- "stricter metrics have a more pronounced impact on the performance of unseen tasks compared to trained ones, underscoring the challenges of continuous spatial-temporal trajectory tracking in bimanual manipulation tasks." (Sec. 5.4)

## Notes for the survey (which sections this feeds; contradictions with other notes)
- Feeds: bimanual coordination taxonomy (decentralised IPPO with fixed left=object/right=tool roles beats a centralised policy here, opposite to single-policy designs in other bimanual RL notes); reward design from demonstrations (two-stage align-then-track, object-pose tracking only, no hand-pose tracking after stage 1); benchmarks (141 TACO-derived tasks; r_2 tracking-rate metric).
- Interpenetration is never measured or penalised, and contact info is disabled in PhysX; "grasp" is a 12 cm distance test. Any contact claims from this paper are replay-free but unaudited.
- The 74.59 %/51.07 % headline is a per-step tracking proportion at a 0.1 tolerance, not an episode success rate; at 0.05 the same policy scores 52.58 %/~15 % (Table 3). Quote with the threshold.
- No real-robot result; contrast with twisting_lids_2024 and hato_visuotactile_2024 which deploy.
