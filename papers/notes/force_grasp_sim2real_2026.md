# force_grasp_sim2real_2026 — Closing the Reality Gap: Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Deployment for Dexterous Force-Based Grasping and Manipulation (Zhao et al. / ByteDance Seed, arXiv 2026, arXiv:2601.02778)

sources: papers/md/force_grasp_sim2real_2026.md [534fc74f] ; no code

## One-line contribution
An asymmetric actor-critic PPO recipe for a 12-DoF direct-drive hand (xHand) that fuses a fast geometric tactile simulation with a current-to-torque calibration and randomized actuator dynamics, transferring zero-shot (no real-world fine-tuning) to command-tracked grasp force and single-axis in-hand rotation (Abstract; Sec 1).

## Setting
- hand: xHand, "fully actuated robotic hand ... with 12 degrees of freedom" (Sec 3.6.1), N=5 fingers, direct-drive (Sec 3.2.2: "12-DoF direct-drive dexterous hand (xHand)"); vendor not stated beyond the name/citation [9]. arm: not stated — Sec 5 limitations call the setup "a structured setting with a fixed hand" and propose future mobile-manipulator integration, implying no arm in this work. single/bimanual: single hand.
- simulator / physics: IsaacLab [32] (Sec 3.2.2). Physics engine name, sim timestep, control rate, number of parallel envs, GPU, and training wall-clock time: not stated anywhere in the retrieved text.
- observation: full-state, itemized in Table 1. Grasping (Table 1a, same set for actor and critic — i.e. not asymmetric for this task): hand joint angles 12D, hand joint torque 12D, object position 3D, object linear velocity 3D, contact force 5D, contact center 15D, fingertip positions 15D, force command 1D. Rotation (Table 1b, actor vs critic differ — this is where the "asymmetric actor-critic" of the Abstract applies): actor gets hand joint angles 12D, relative target orientation 6D, last actions 12D, contact center 15D, contact force 5D, fingertip positions 15D; critic additionally gets object orientation 6D, fingertip velocities 30D, fingertip rotations 30D, hand joint velocities 12D, object linear velocity 3D, object angular velocity 3D (privileged, actor "×").
- action space: target joint positions, 12D, sent to a "motor-level PD controller" (Sec 3.2); rotation task also compares a Proprioception-Only vs an IMU-Augmented (object orientation, 6D rotation representation) variant (Sec 3.5.2).
- objects / data: no dataset or object-count is enumerated anywhere. Objects appear only by name in figures: Fig. 6 (real+sim grasping) — angular ball, cube, tennis ball, milk bottle, apple; Fig. 8 (force-command sweep at 10/50/100% power) — protein bar, toy, spring, pliers, chips, doll; Fig. 9 (explicitly "unseen during training") — gamepad, bowl. The exact size of the training object set and the exact number held out for the generalization claim are **not stated** — only these two named unseen objects are shown as evidence of generalization (Sec 4.3).

## Method
- paradigm: RL only (PPO, asymmetric actor-critic per Abstract). Grasp pose is not produced by an analytic planner or a generative model at all: it emerges end-to-end from the policy via an "inverted catching" training setup — the hand is fixed palm-up and objects are "procedurally dropped from above into its workspace," with the reward driving contact/force behavior rather than any pre-computed grasp target (Sec 3.4.1). The authors motivate this explicitly as a fix for reward hacking and sample inefficiency in the "conventional" tabletop-approach-then-grasp paradigm (Sec 3.4.1).
- algorithm: PPO, asymmetric actor-critic; no repo/implementation named (Abstract).
- domain randomization (every randomized quantity named in the text, with no numeric range given for any of them — recorded as such rather than guessed):
  - object mass, size, shape, friction (Sec 3.4.1).
  - friction, damping, restitution coefficients (Sec 3.6.2).
  - PD gains, "randomized" (Sec 3.6.4).
  - backlash threshold ε, "randomized during training" (Sec 3.6.4).
  - torque-speed saturation (stall torque τ0, max no-load velocity) and an efficiency factor η, "randomized factor" (Sec 3.6.4).
  - "All model parameters are resampled for each actuator at the beginning of every training episode" (Sec 3.6.4).
- reward / objective, grasping (Sec 3.5.1 — equation bodies previously reported as lost to PDF conversion; all ten equations (Eqs 1-10) have since been recovered by OCR (recovered by OCR from papers/md/force_grasp_sim2real_2026.ocr.md; OCR text is noisier than the layout parse, symbols may be imperfect)):
  - R_torque: "Rtorque = wtorque · Σ_{i∈fingers} Rtorque,i · Icontact,i" (Eq. 1); "Rtorque,thumb = I(τmin ≤ τthumb ≤ τmax)" (Eq. 2, thumb term, binary pass/fail); "Rtorque,i = exp(−(τi − τtarget)^2 / 2σ^2) · I(τmin ≤ τthumb ≤ τmax)" (Eq. 3, other four fingers, Gaussian centered on target with tunable width σ) — target τ_target = τ_max·F_cmd, active only "when the finger is in contact with the object" via indicator I_contact,i.
  - R_Force: "Rforce = wforce · Σ_{i∈fingers} Rforce,i · Icontact,i" (Eq. 4); "Rforce,thumb = I(Fmin ≤ Fthumb ≤ Fmax)" (Eq. 5); "Rforce,i = exp(−(Fi − Ftarget)^2 / 2σ^2) · I(Fmin ≤ Fi ≤ Fmax)" (Eq. 6) — target F_target = F_cmd·F_max, same structure as the torque reward.
  - R_diff: "Rdiff = wdiff · Var({qj | j ∈ inner fingers})" (Eq. 7) — four-finger consistency penalty, weight w_diff, defined as the variance Var(q_j) of the first-joint positions of the four homologous ("inner") fingers.
  - R_outer (OCR renders this "Routter"): "Routter = woutter · ||qoutter − coutter||^2" (Eq. 8) — outer-finger deviation penalty, weight w_outer, penalizing deviation of index/middle/ring/little joint positions q_outer from a center c_outer.
  - a terminal-state penalty of unspecified magnitude (functional form not given even in OCR — "a terminal state penalty is introduced" with no equation quoted); action-rate penalty: "Raction = waction · ||at − at−1||_2^2" (Eq. 9); joint-velocity L2 penalty: "Rvel = wvel · ||q̇||_2^2" (Eq. 10).
  - Functional forms of Eqs 1-10 are now recovered (above), but the caveat on numeric weight values still holds: **none of the weight values (w_torque, w_diff, w_outer, w_action, w_vel, σ, τ_max, F_max) are given as numbers anywhere in the extracted text, including the OCR pass** — the equation bodies themselves are recovered, but the specific numeric constants that would be substituted into them were never printed in the paper as running text or a table (only symbolic). Stated plainly rather than guessed: with no code/md file for this key either, the reward block cannot be numerically verified at all.
- reward / objective, rotation (Table 2, partially recovered):
  - "Close to goal" term = 1/(|d_rot|+ε) × 1/(1+e^(α(d_goal−δ_dist))), weight w_goal.
  - "Action penalty" = ||a_t − a_{t-1}||², weight w_action.
  - a reset condition gives a "Reach goal bonus" of value C_Bonus when d_rot < δ_rot^threshold and d_pos < δ_pos^threshold.
  - again, no numeric values are given for w_goal, w_action, α, δ_dist, δ_rot^threshold, δ_pos^threshold, or C_Bonus. The text explains the design intent (jointly penalize rotation error and position drift so the policy cannot exploit a "hold still" local minimum, plus a large completion bonus) but the constants are absent.
- key tricks: (i) inverted-catching training setup in place of tabletop approach+grasp (Sec 3.4.1); (ii) 6D continuous rotation representation instead of quaternions for the IMU-augmented policy, "to avoid ... the double-cover property" (Sec 3.5.2); (iii) "shifting goal" curriculum for rotation — each 90° success advances the target another 90° along the same axis (Sec 3.4.2); (iv) fast tactile simulation via parallel-forward-kinematics nearest-surface-point queries plus a linear-spring approximation f_ij ≈ k·d_ij of a Mooney-Rivlin contact model (justified because the xHand fingertip rubber is "relatively rigid" and operates near-linearly, Sec 3.6.2); (v) current-to-torque calibration mapping real motor current to the simulated torque scale via matched normalization to [0,1] (Sec 3.6.3), removing the need for physical torque sensors.

## Evaluation
- grasping success criterion: **not a pass/fail criterion at all** — no lift height, hold duration, or force threshold is defined for "success." The task is evaluated as continuous force-command tracking: Table 3 reports the contact-force range and joint-torque-percent range achieved across force commands, for reward-ablation configs R_Force+R_torque (44.50–93.92 force, 1.09–1.52 torque%), R_Force only (37.29–70.02, 0.69–0.75), R_torque only (38.45–47.12, 0.85–1.02) (Table 3, Sec 4.1); Fig. 5 shows contact force and torque rising approximately linearly with the force command.
- rotation success criterion: a success = reaching a 90° incremental target rotation "within a specified tolerance" (Sec 3.4.2) — the numeric tolerance is not stated (Table 2's symbolic δ_rot^threshold/δ_pos^threshold carry no value). Metrics per real trial (Sec 4.2, Table 4): number of consecutive successful rotations, average success duration, time-to-fall. Full observation config (contact center + contact force + force-weighted + 6D orientation): 25.1 avg / 22.0 median consecutive successes, 3.36 s avg duration/success, 84.3 s avg time-to-fall (Table 4). Quaternion-instead-of-6D ablation: 2.9 avg successes, 15.2 s time-to-fall. No-tactile-sensing baseline (all contact terms off): 1.1 avg successes, 3.10 s time-to-fall — the worst configuration (Table 4).
- real robot trials: rotation task — "Each policy was evaluated over ten trials" (Sec 4.2), across 7 ablated observation configurations in Table 4, so 70 real rollouts total for that ablation; this is the only quantified real-robot trial count in the paper. Grasping task — real deployment is shown only as qualitative demonstrations (Fig. 6, 5 named objects; Fig. 8, 5 named objects × 3 force levels; Fig. 9, 2 named unseen objects); **no trial count or numeric success rate is given for real-world grasping**.
- grasp/contact quality check: both geometric and physical, but asymmetrically quantified. Geometric: Fig. 4 compares simulated vs. real contact-force and contact-center traces during rotation and states they are "highly consistent," and that "the simulated contact region envelops the real-world data" (Sec 3.6.2) — a qualitative/visual match, not a numeric error metric. Physical: real xHand trials as above (quantified for rotation via Table 4, unquantified for grasping).
- baselines beaten: none external; all comparisons are internal ablations — Table 3 (reward-term ablation for grasping) and Table 4 (observation-component ablation for rotation, 7 configs).
- reproducibility: no code, checkpoints, or assets are released for this key (`python tools/show.py` provenance shows no code entry; no code/md file exists) — nothing in this note can be cross-checked against an implementation, which is why the reward weights above are reported as symbolic/unrecoverable rather than as numbers.

## Limitations stated by the authors
- Tactile simulation is "a geometric approximation," not a differentiable soft-body simulator, and misses shear forces and multi-directional slip (Sec 5).
- Fixed-base hand only; integrating with a mobile manipulator is left to future work (Sec 5).
- Real-world state estimation depends on external vision (SAM-segmented object mask for a Z-coordinate; X/Y held fixed relative to the hand's initial pose, Sec 3.5.1) and onboard IMU, not learned end-to-end from raw sensory input (Sec 5).

## Quotable claims
- "To our knowledge, this is the first demonstration of controllable grasping on a multi-finger dexterous hand trained entirely in simulation and transferred zero-shot on real hardware." (Abstract)
- "the Z-coordinate is computed from the distance between the palm center and the centroid of the segmented object mask ... The X and Y coordinates are held fixed relative to the hand's initial pose, simplifying the state estimation under the assumption of limited lateral motion during grasping." (Sec 3.5.1) — the real deployment's object-position observation is far cruder than the 3D position term used in simulation (Table 1a).
- "we use a linear spring approximation of the contact force in the simulator: f_ij ≈ k · d_ij" (Sec 3.6.2), justified by a Mooney-Rivlin fit (Fig. 3b) showing the xHand's rubber is "relatively rigid" and near-linear at typical manipulation forces.

## Notes for the survey
- Contrasts with the binary/point-cloud tactile approaches cited in Related Work (Touch Dexterity, Robot Synesthesia, Sec 1): this paper's tactile channel is dense force + weighted contact-center (600 simulated taxels via parallel FK, Sec 3.6.1), reduced to a compact (F_i, μ_i) per-finger feature for the policy (Sec 3.6.2).
- Feeds the survey's actuator-modeling / sim-to-real-gap comparison section (current-to-torque calibration, Sec 3.6.3, as an alternative to physical torque sensors) and the "reward-shaped vs. contact-driven initialization" comparison (inverted catching vs. tabletop-approach RL, Sec 3.4.1).
- Flag for the survey's method-comparison table: the reward block (C) is **functionally complete but numerically unverifiable** — the ten grasping-reward equations (Eqs 1-10) have been recovered by OCR from papers/md/force_grasp_sim2real_2026.ocr.md, but every reward equation's numeric weight values were never printed anywhere in the source text (not dropped by PDF conversion, genuinely absent), and there is no code/md to recover them from an implementation; only the symbolic structure of the reward terms is usable for cross-paper comparison, not their relative magnitudes.
- No quantified object-generalization number exists for grasping (only 2 named unseen objects, Fig. 9); do not cite a "generalizes to N objects" claim for this paper in the survey — none is given.
