## One-line contribution
Replaces Diffusion Policy's image encoder with a lightweight (~64-dim) MLP encoder over cropped, farthest-point-sampled, single-view, colorless point clouds, keeping the same diffusion-based action-sequence decoder, for a 24.2% relative success-rate improvement over image-based Diffusion Policy across 72 simulated tasks (Table I) and 85.0% vs. 35.0%/20.0% average success on 4 real Allegro-hand/gripper tasks with only 40 demonstrations each (Table VIII).

## Setting
- hand(s): Shadow Hand (Adroit, Bi-DexHands, DexDeform, DexMV domains, 22-52 action-dim), Allegro Hand (DexArt, HORA domains, 16-22 action-dim; also the real-robot Roll-Up/Dumpling/Drill tasks), and a parallel gripper (MetaWorld, real Pour task) (Table III). arm: Franka Arm for the real-robot Allegro and gripper rigs (Fig. 8a). single/bimanual: Bi-DexHands is bimanual (2 hands); all others single-hand/single-gripper.
- simulator / physics: task-domain-specific — "MuJoCo [65], Sapien [73], IsaacGym [34], and PlasticineLab [26]" (Sec. IV-A): Adroit/MuJoCo, Bi-DexHands/IsaacGym, DexArt/Sapien, DexDeform/PlasticineLab, DexMV/Sapien, HORA/IsaacGym, MetaWorld/MuJoCo (Table III). #envs and GPU/wall-clock training time: not stated.
- observation: single-view point clouds only, no multi-camera rig ("DP3 focuses on only utilizing a single-view camera... different from previous works [10,18] that set up multiple cameras", Sec. III-B). Pipeline: depth image (84x84, single camera) -> point cloud via extrinsics/intrinsics -> crop to a bounding box to remove table/ground points -> farthest point sampling (FPS) to 512 or 1024 points -> no color channels ("We do not use color channels for better appearance generalization", Sec. III-B). Real robot: one RealSense L515 camera (Sec. V-A).
- action space: task-native action dimension (ActD column, Table III), up to 52-dim (Bi-DexHands/DexDeform); not further specified as position/velocity/torque in the parsed text beyond "action sequences" generated by the diffusion decoder.
- objects / data: 72 simulated tasks across 7 domains (Table III): Adroit (3 tasks, 10 demos each), Bi-DexHands (6, 10), DexArt (4, 100), DexDeform (6, 10), DexMV (2, 10), HORA (1, 100), MetaWorld (50 across Easy/Medium/Hard/Very-Hard, 10 demos each). Demo source: "Human-teleoperated data is used in DexDeform; Script policies are used in MetaWorld; Trajectories for other domains are collected with agents trained by RL... VRL3 [67] is used for Adroit; PPO [53] is used in all other domains" (Sec. IV-A) — i.e. most simulation "demonstrations" are RL-agent rollouts, not human data. Real robot: 4 tasks (Roll-Up, Dumpling, Drill: Allegro+plasticine/drill, Deform/Rigid; Pour: gripper+bowl, Rigid), 40 human-teleoperated demonstrations each (Table III) — "The Franka arm and the gripper are teleoperated by the keyboard. The Allegro hand is teleoperated with human hands by vision-based retargeting [48, 17]" (Sec. V-A).

## Method
- paradigm: IL (behavior cloning, conditional diffusion) ; algorithm: same convolutional-network-based Diffusion Policy [10] backbone, re-conditioned on a compact 3D point-cloud feature instead of image features.
- Loss (quoted, Sec. III-C): "starting from a Gaussian noise a^K, the denoising network ϵθ performs K iterations to gradually denoise a random noise a^K into the noise-free action a^0" — the reverse-process equation (Eq. 1) and the training-objective equation (Eq. 2), previously unrecovered images, are now recovered by OCR: "a^{k−1} = α_k(a^k − γ_k*ϵθ(a^k, k, v, q)) + σ_k*N(0, I)" (Eq. 1), "where N(0, I) is Gaussian noise, αk, γk, and σk are functions of k and depend on the noise scheduler"; "To train the denoising network ϵθ, we randomly sample a data point a^0 from the dataset and do a diffusion process [23] on the data point to get the noise at k iteration ϵ^k. The training objective is to predict the noise added to the original data, L = MSE(ϵ^k, ϵθ(ᾱk*a^0 + β̄k*ϵ^k, k, v, q))" (Eq. 2), "where ᾱk and β̄k are noise schedule that performs one step noise adding [23]." (recovered by OCR from papers/md/dp3_2024.ocr.md; OCR text is noisier than the layout parse, symbols may be imperfect) — but the implementation departs from this at inference: "We use DDIM [62] as the noise scheduler and use sample prediction instead of epsilon prediction for better high-dimensional action generation, with 100 timesteps at training and 10 timesteps at inference" (Sec. III-C, "Implementation details"). This is confirmed exactly by the shipped config: `noise_scheduler: _target_: diffusers.schedulers.scheduling_ddim.DDIMScheduler, num_train_timesteps: 100, beta_start: 0.0001, beta_end: 0.02, beta_schedule: squaredcos_cap_v2, clip_sample: True, set_alpha_to_one: True, steps_offset: 0, prediction_type: sample; num_inference_steps: 10` (code/md, diffusion_policy_3d/config/dp3.yaml) — `prediction_type: sample` means the network is trained to directly predict the denoised action a^0, not the noise ϵ, contradicting the paper prose's "predict the noise added" framing for the shipped default config; Fig. 7 ablates both ("sample" vs. "epsilon" prediction) and finds "With sample prediction, DP3 generally converges faster, while epsilon prediction is also competitive." Code: `DP3.compute_loss(self, batch)` exists (diffusion_policy_3d/policy/dp3.py) but its body is not present in code/md — only the signature.
- action representation: action sequence a (chunk), conditioned on compact 3D feature v and robot pose/state q — "conditions on 3D visual features v and robot poses q, then denoises a random Gaussian noise into actions a" (Sec. III-C).
- prediction horizon and chunk size: horizon=16, n_obs_steps=2, n_action_steps=8 (code/md, dp3.yaml — a commented-out alternative horizon=4/n_action_steps=3 is also present in the config but not the active value). Not separately stated as a paper-text number distinct from the code config.
- observation encoder ("DP3 Encoder", Sec. III-B, quoted): "a three-layer MLP, a max-pooling function as an order-equivariant operation to pool point cloud features, and a projection head to project the features into a compact vector. LayerNorm layers are interleaved to stabilize training... The final 3D feature, denoted as v, is only 64 dimension." Code confirms: `PointNetEncoderXYZ(in_channels=3, out_channels=64, use_layernorm=True, final_norm=layernorm)` (pointcloud_encoder_cfg, dp3.yaml); a color variant `PointNetEncoderXYZRGB` exists but is unused by default (`use_pc_color: false`). Ablation (Table V, referenced but table itself not captured in the parsed excerpt) found this simple encoder "could even outperform pre-trained point encoders such as PointNeXt."
- inference-time procedure: DDIM sampling, 10 inference steps (vs. 100 training steps) starting from Gaussian noise, denoising to the action chunk; batch size 128 for DP3 and all baselines; "train 1000 epochs for MetaWorld tasks due to its simplicity and 3000 epochs for other simulated and real-world tasks" (Sec. III-C).
- key trick(s): single-view (not multi-camera) point clouds; explicit cropping + FPS downsampling to 512-1024 points to remove redundant table/ground geometry; dropping color channels entirely for appearance-invariance; reusing the unmodified Diffusion Policy decision backbone so the comparison isolates the 3D-perception contribution (Sec. IV-A "Baselines").

## Evaluation
- metrics (exact definitions): success rate. "We run 3 seeds for each experiment with seed number 0,1,2. For each seed, we evaluate 20 episodes every 200 training epochs and then compute the average of the highest 5 success rates. We report the mean and std of success rates across 3 seeds." (Sec. IV-A). Real robot: "Each task is evaluated with 10 trials" (Table VIII caption). Safety-violation rate (Table XIV): count of human-terminated unsafe rollouts during the main real-world runs, "primarily qualitative" per the authors.
- headline numbers (with the table/figure they come from): Table I, 72-task sim average — DP3 74.4±29.9 vs. Diffusion Policy 59.8±35.9 ("24.2% relative improvement"); per-domain e.g. Bi-DexHands DP3 70.2 vs. DP 61.3, HORA DP3 71.0 vs. DP 49.0. Table II (10-task extended baseline comparison) — DP3 average 74.4 vs. Diffusion Policy 44.0, BCRNN 9.8, BCRNN+3D 14.5, IBC 5.0, IBC+3D 10.4. Table VIII (real robot, 10 trials/task) — DP3 85.0±11.2 average (Roll-Up 90, Dumpling 70, Drill 80, Pour 100) vs. Diffusion Policy 35.0±25.0 and Diffusion Policy (Depth) 20.0±12.2. Table XIV — safety violation rate: DP3 0.0% average vs. Diffusion Policy 32.5% and Diffusion Policy (Depth) 25.0%.
- baselines beaten: image-based Diffusion Policy [10] (primary baseline), Diffusion Policy (Depth), IBC, BCRNN, and 3D variants of IBC/BCRNN using the DP3 Encoder ("IBC+3D", "BCRNN+3D") for fairness.
- real robot? Yes — Allegro Hand + Franka arm (Roll-Up, Dumpling, Drill) and a parallel gripper + Franka arm (Pour), 10 trials/task main results (Table VIII), plus 1-trial-per-condition generalization studies (spatial: Table IX 5 positions; appearance: Table X 5 colors; instance: Table XI 5 objects; view: Table XII 3 views; cluttered scenes: Table XIII, 10 trials in-domain + 1 trial per out-of-domain color/object).
- MetaWorld difficulty tiers used as-is from prior work: "Tasks in MetaWorld [77] are categorized into various difficulty levels based on [55]" (Sec. IV-A) — Easy(28)/Medium(11)/Hard(6)/Very Hard(5).

## Limitations stated by the authors
"Though we have developed an efficient architecture, the optimal 3D representation for control is still yet discovered. Besides, this work does not delve into tasks with extremely long horizons, which remains for future exploration." (Sec. VI, "Limitations"). Additionally: "our assessment of safety is primarily qualitative. We intend to explore a more theoretical understanding of this observation in our future work." (Sec. V-D).

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "DP3 focuses on only utilizing a single-view camera for policy learning for all the tasks, which is different from previous works [10, 18] that set up multiple cameras around robots." (Sec. III-B)
- "The training objective is to predict the noise added to the original data" (Sec. III-C)
- "We use DDIM [62] as the noise scheduler and use sample prediction instead of epsilon prediction for better high-dimensional action generation, with 100 timesteps at training and 10 timesteps at inference." (Sec. III-C)
- "Across 72 simulated tasks, DP3 outperforms its 2D counterpart by a relative margin of 24.2%." (Sec. VI)
- "we find that DP3 rarely violates the safety, showing that DP3 is a practical and hardware-friendly method for real robot learning. An intuitive explanation is that since the robots operate in 3D space, directly observing 3D information helps avoid collision." (Sec. V-D)
- "In Drill, the drill in the real world is large and heavy, which is quite difficult for the gripper to grasp." (Sec. V-A)

## Notes for the survey
- Feeds: the IL-architecture/loss table alongside diffusion_policy_2023 and umi_2024 — same diffusion decoder and receding-horizon action chunking (horizon=16 / n_action_steps=8, matching diffusion_policy_2023's own Tab. 7 defaults), the sole architectural change is the observation encoder (point cloud MLP vs. ResNet-18+spatial-softmax). Flag for the survey's method-comparison table: DP3's shipped config trains with `prediction_type: sample` (predict a^0 directly) by default, not `epsilon` — a detail easy to miss since the paper's own prose only states the noise-prediction framing before qualifying it in the same paragraph.
- Most of DP3's 72-task "demonstrations" are not human data (RL-agent rollouts for all domains except DexDeform and the scripted MetaWorld) — worth flagging against papers in this survey batch that use only human teleoperated demonstrations (dime_2022, holo_dex_2022, doglove_2025): DP3's data-efficiency claims (10 demos/task for most domains) are efficiency in imitating an RL expert's rollouts, not in imitating human dexterity.
- Contact/penetration handling: not addressed anywhere in the parsed text — no interpenetration metric, reward term, or discussion for the dexterous-hand domains (Adroit/Bi-DexHands/DexArt/HORA all involve grasp contact but DP3's paper only reports task success rate).
- The real-world Allegro-hand demonstrations were collected via "vision-based retargeting [48, 17]" (Sec. V-A) — cites AnyTeleop/DexPilot-lineage retargeting rather than a glove or exoskeleton, a useful cross-link to this survey's teleoperation notes (anyteleop_2023, dexpilot_2020) even though DP3 itself contributes no new retargeting method.
