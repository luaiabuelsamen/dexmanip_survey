## One-line contribution
GeoRT (Geometric Retargeting) replaces the standard linear task-vector-matching retargeting cost (used by dexpilot_2020/anyteleop_2023-style methods) with an unsupervised-trained per-finger neural network optimized against five geometric criteria — motion-direction preservation, C-space-coverage (via Chamfer loss), flatness (uniform response), pinch correspondence, and collision-freeness — achieving 1kHz retargeting inference (vs. 60-100Hz for online-optimization baselines), 90% C-space coverage vs. 38% for the linear baseline (Table II), and 87.5% one-time real-world grasp success vs. 55%/42.5% for offline/online linear-matching baselines (Table III) (Abstract; Sec. II-III).

## Setting
- operator interface: for real-world evaluation, a Manus glove captures human hand/fingertip keypoints (fed to the retargeting model for hand control) and an HTC Vive tracker system captures human wrist pose (used to control the robot arm's end-effector pose) (Sec. III-B, "Real-world Experimental Setup"). GeoRT's own method is agnostic to the tracking source — the paper frames it purely as the retargeting function f mapping human fingertip keypoints to robot joint positions, decoupled from whatever hand-tracking hardware supplies xH.
- robot hand(s) controlled: Allegro Hand (real-world evaluation, mounted on a Franka Panda arm, and simulation Table II/Table III numbers) and LEAP Hand (simulation qualitative retargeting only, Fig. 9) — GeoRT is explicitly a per-hand-embodiment trained neural model, not a hand-agnostic single network: "we implement our kinematic retargeting model as a set of independent retargeting models... for the Allegro Hand which has four fingers, we define f(xH1,xH2,xH3,xH4) = [f1(xH1), f2(xH2), f3(xH3), f4(xH4)], and each fi is an independent retargeting model for the corresponding finger" (Sec. II-G), each fi parameterized as an MLP with Tanh output activation over joint positions rescaled to [-1,1].
- retargeting formulation (Sec. II, quoted per-criterion — five criteria, not one cost): the paper's stated baseline formulation against which it contrasts (Eq. 1, the "linear matching form... most recent teleoperation works [8] typically take", quoted as prose since the equation is unrecovered): "viH and viR = f(viH) are the task vectors of (source) human and (retargeted) robot hands, αi are some scaling hyperparameter, and N≈10 is the number of hand keypoints. The second regularization term is usually used to ensure smoothness." GeoRT's own five criteria: **(I) Motion Preservation** — "given any position xHi for i-th finger and any small moving direction d, we require d parallel to FKi∘fi(xHi+d) − FKi∘fi(xHi), where FKi is the forward kinematics for fingertip i, and fi is the retargeting component for i-th finger... implemented as a pretrained neural forward kinematics function," yielding loss Ldir. **(II) C-space Coverage** — "FKi∘fi should be a surjection from KCHi to KCRi, and we should minimize the volume of KCRi \ (FKi∘fi(KCHi))"; approximated (since exact volume is non-differentiable) by "Chamfer loss [9] in 3D vision research as a proxy," sampling PHi~KCHi and PRi~KCRi each minibatch, giving Lcover. **(III) High Flatness** — requiring f''(x)≈0 so equal steps in human space map to equal steps in robot space ("the ideal retargeting should have high flatness... a low f''(x)... equivalently"), generalized to "the second-order directional derivative at any point along any direction should be close to 0," evaluated via finite differences, giving Lflat. **(IV) Pinch Correspondence** — "for a human gesture xH, if xHi − xHj is below a threshold d (such as 1cm), i.e., finger i and j are pinching, then we require FKi∘fi(xHi) close to FKj∘fj(xHj)," giving Lpinch (requires "human users to provide some pinch grasp examples... within 5 minutes of random play"). **(V) Collision-Free Retargeting** — "we first pretrain a collision classifier C to decide the probability of a joint configuration q leading to hand self-collision. The training dataset is generated through simulation, and the label is obtained by querying a collision checker to get the binary self-collision label... C is fixed as we train f," giving Lcol — explicit, quantified self-collision handling via a learned classifier trained on simulated self-collision labels (a different mechanism than Bunny-VisionPro's sphere-approximated geometric collision cost, but the same intent). Joint limits are handled by rescaling "the joint position range to [-1,1]" with a Tanh output activation (a hard architectural constraint, not a soft penalty) — confirmed in code by `parse_config_joint_limit` in geort/utils/config_utils.py. Total training objective: "L = Ldir + λ1 Lcover + λ2 Lflat + λ3 Lpinch + λ4 Lcol" with "only 4 hyperparameters compared to previous works that have numerous scale hyperparameters and heuristic task vectors" — empirical weight ranges given (quoted): "λ1 ∈ [10,100], λ2 = 1, λ3 ∈ [10^3, 10^6], λ4 = [10^-4, 10^-2]." Unlike the fingertip-vector optimizers in dexpilot_2020/anyteleop_2023/bunny_visionpro_2024/doglove_2025, GeoRT's retargeting function is trained OFFLINE (once, in 3-5 minutes) and then evaluated with a single fast forward pass at inference/teleoperation time — no online per-frame optimization at all.
- end-to-end / retargeting latency: Table I (self-reported comparison, "speed is claimed by the referred paper or its follow up work") — DexPilot 60-100Hz, AnyTeleop 60-100Hz, "RTelekinesis" [8, Robotic Telekinesis] 1000Hz (no online optimization), GeoRT (Ours) 1000Hz. Abstract, quoted: "GeoRT converts human finger keypoints to robot hand keypoints at 1KHz." Training time: "very fast in practice, only taking 3-5 minutes on a single NVIDIA 3060 GPU" (Sec. II-G) — separately, human calibration data collection ("stretch their fingers and move back and forth, as well as perform various pinch grasps") "typically takes less than 5 minutes and is a one-time calibration."
- cost of the rig: not stated as a dollar figure — only the tracking hardware (Manus glove, HTC Vive tracker) and compute (single NVIDIA RTX 3060 GPU for training) are named.
- data rate / demonstrations per hour: not reported — this paper evaluates teleoperation-assisted grasping trials, not a demonstration-collection throughput; a qualitative example gives "the user can use our system to clean up a pile of objects (12) on the table in around 100 seconds" (Fig. 8) but this is not converted to a rate and covers only one qualitative run.
- task(s) demonstrations were used for: no downstream imitation-learning policy is trained in this paper — GeoRT is evaluated purely as a teleoperation/grasping interface. The paper explicitly defers application results elsewhere: "For its application, we refer readers to the DexterityGen paper" (Sec. III). Real-world evaluation task: single-object grasping with an Allegro hand + Franka arm, measuring one-trial grasp success and completion time (Sec. III-C); a qualitative multi-object clearing task (12 objects, ~100s, Fig. 8) is also shown.

## Evaluation
- exact success criterion: "we record the one-trial success rate and the average time a user takes to grasp an object successfully" (Sec. III-C) — a grasp attempt succeeds if achieved on the first try; no distance/angle threshold given (task is grasping, not in-hand manipulation — "Since performing dexterous in-hand manipulation is hard as suggested by previous works, in this paper we mainly consider the grasping performance").
- number of eval episodes/seeds: not stated as an explicit trial count for Table III's real-world grasping comparison (percentages given, e.g. 55%, 42.5%, 87.5%, implying some fixed but unstated number of grasp attempts per method); simulation metrics (Table II) are computed by uniformly sampling "sufficiently many points" over the fingertip C-space, not a discrete trial count.
- real robot? Yes for Table III (Allegro Hand + Franka Panda arm, Manus glove + Vive tracker); Table II (Motion Preservation, C-space coverage) is a simulation-only metric computed directly on the retargeting function and forward kinematics, not a physical trial.
- headline numbers: Table II (simulation, Allegro Hand) — Motion Preservation: baseline offline-Eq.1 0.73 vs. GeoRT 0.94 (bounded [-1,1]); C-space coverage: baseline offline-Eq.1 38% vs. GeoRT 90% (bounded [0%,100%]) ("the online version of Eqn (1) is not time-independent and its result relies on the previous frame, so we use its offline version as an approximation" — the online baseline has no comparable number in this table). Table III (real-world grasping) — Onetime-Success: offline-Eq.1 (DexPilot-style) 55%, online-Eq.1 (AnyTeleop/RTelekinesis-style) 42.5%, GeoRT 87.5%; Completion Time: offline 9.0s, online 19.3s, GeoRT 3.2s.
- baselines beaten: the linear task-vector-matching retargeting formulation (Eq. 1, attributed to DexPilot [2]/AnyTeleop [3]-style methods), both an "offline" and "online" variant of it, and implicitly "RTelekinesis" [8]/Robotic Telekinesis (compared only on the Table I speed/hyperparameter-count axis, not re-run for Table II/III's quality metrics).

## Limitations stated by the authors
No dedicated Limitations section in the parsed text; the paper notes one caveat about its own collision-avoidance term rather than a broader limitation: "Interestingly, we find that even without this term [Lcol] our loss can lead to few collisions for certain robot hands. Nevertheless, we introduce this for completeness" (Sec. II-F) — i.e. the authors flag that Lcol's necessity is hand-dependent and not always load-bearing, without stating this as a weakness of the overall method.

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "GeoRT converts human finger keypoints to robot hand keypoints at 1KHz, achieving state-of-the-art speed and accuracy with significantly fewer hyperparameters." (Abstract)
- "This optimization objective only has 4 hyperparameters compared to previous works that have numerous scale hyperparameters and heuristic task vectors." (Sec. II-G)
- "The training procedure is very fast in practice, only taking 3-5 minutes on a single NVIDIA 3060 GPU." (Sec. II-G)
- "we first pretrain a collision classifier C to decide the probability of a joint configuration q leading to hand self-collision." (Sec. II-F)
- "due to insufficient coverage, the baseline method fails to utilize the lateral movement of fingers effectively and cannot do effective pinch grasp or finger (thumb) reaching." (Sec. III-A, "Qualitative Results")
- "It also outperforms existing methods in the teleoperation-based grasping task in real world experiments." (Sec. I)

## Notes for the survey
- Feeds: the retargeting-formulation comparison table as the sole *learned/neural* (offline-trained, feedforward-inference) retargeter in this survey batch, contrasted against every other key's per-frame online-optimization approach (dexpilot_2020's SLSQP, anyteleop_2023/ace_teleop_2024/bunny_visionpro_2024's SQP, doglove_2025's off-the-shelf Mink IK) — GeoRT trades a several-minute one-time training cost for a 10-15x inference-speed gain (1kHz vs. 60-100Hz) and removes online optimization from the control loop entirely. This is the most direct match to the bib entry's framing as "the retargeting layer under DexterityGen" and should be flagged as architecturally distinct (learned vs. optimized) when the survey compiles its retargeting-formulation table, not just numerically better.
- Table II/III's baseline numbers are self-reported re-implementations of "Equation 1"-style linear matching (an offline and online variant), NOT re-runs of dexpilot_2020's or anyteleop_2023's actual released code — the survey should attribute these comparison numbers to GeoRT's own re-implementation, not treat them as independently verified numbers for those other papers.
- Contact/penetration handling: the most explicit LEARNED collision-avoidance mechanism in the survey batch — a classifier pretrained on simulated self-collision labels, used only as a training-time gradient signal (frozen at retargeting-inference time) — worth contrasting directly with bunny_visionpro_2024's explicit geometric (sphere-distance) self-collision cost and toporetarget_2026's signed-distance penetration constraint (from the parent project's earlier notes) as three structurally different approaches to the same problem: learned classifier vs. analytic sphere-distance vs. SDF constraint.
- No dollar cost-of-rig and no demos/hour figure in this paper (mark "not stated"); this is also the only key in the batch with NO downstream imitation-learning evaluation of its own — its policy-learning role is entirely deferred to the separately published DexterityGen system, which is out of scope for this note's source files.
