## One-line contribution
A survey positioning NVIDIA Isaac Sim (Omniverse + GPU PhysX + RTX rendering + USD assets) against other simulators via a qualitative feature-comparison table, then reviewing how the community has used it for synthetic data generation, industrial/healthcare/embodied-AI/foundation-model applications, and benchmarks, closing with future-direction and usability challenges (Abstract, Secs. 2-5).

## Limitations stated by the authors
- Usability/cost: "Isaac Sim incurs substantially higher computational and memory overhead, which increases setup complexity and development costs, particularly for users with limited resources or experience" compared to Webots/CoppeliaSim (Sec. 5, "Challenge: Usability and Learning Curve").
- "its reliance on high-end hardware and complex configuration pipelines may pose barriers for smaller research teams" (Sec. 5).
- "high-fidelity simulation platforms such as Isaac Sim often require nontrivial configuration and specialized expertise ... highlighting a trade-off between realism and ease of use" (Sec. 5).
- Nambiar et al.'s Isaac-Cortex sim-to-real pipeline "lacks feedback from the physical environment, as perception relies on predefined spreadsheet data rather than real sensors, which limits realism and adaptability" (Sec. 2.4) — a limitation of a cited use case, not of Isaac Sim itself, but reported as such.
- The Conclusions note the field-level trade-off directly: "the high computational cost and steep learning curve remain practical obstacles to its broader adoption" (Sec. 6).
- "Existing surveys often treat it as one simulator among many, without a systematic analysis of its architectural characteristics, usage patterns, and limitations" is the stated gap this paper itself tries to fill (Abstract) — implicitly conceding no prior (or, arguably, this) paper had done a deep technical characterization.

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "Unlike previous simulators, NVIDIA Isaac Sim leverages GPU acceleration to enable large-scale parallel training and physics-accurate modeling." (Abstract)
- "Among the compared platforms, Isaac Sim stands out as the most comprehensive solution. It natively supports ray tracing and parallel rendering, enabling high-fidelity visual simulation through an RTX-based pipeline." (Sec. 2.1)
- "In contrast, traditional robotics simulators such as Gazebo, MuJoCo, and PyBullet mainly focus on rigid-body dynamics and control-oriented simulation. While lightweight and efficient, they lack native support for advanced rendering, synthetic data pipelines, and complex physical dynamics, limiting their use to algorithmic evaluation rather than high-fidelity perception or industrial digital twin scenarios." (Sec. 2.1)
- "These assets include up to 50 robot models ..., more than 20 sensor types ..., and human characters." (Sec. 2.2)
- "It [GR00T] defines two industrial manipulation tasks in Isaac Sim: nut pouring and exhaust pipe sorting, each requiring multi-step bimanual humanoid actions." (Sec. 4.4)
- "Despite its strong performance in physics fidelity and large-scale simulation, the usability and learning curve of Isaac Sim remain practical challenges for broader adoption." (Sec. 5)
