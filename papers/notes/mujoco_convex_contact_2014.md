# mujoco_convex_contact_2014 — Convex and analytically-invertible dynamics with contacts and constraints: Theory and implementation in MuJoCo (Todorov, ICRA 2014)

sources: papers/md/mujoco_convex_contact_2014.md [sha256 498d65ca] ; code/md/mujoco_convex_contact_2014.md [commit 2fec9223]

## One-line contribution
Formalizes MuJoCo's "complementarity-free" soft-contact formulation as a convex, regularized, and — in a restricted case — analytically invertible dynamics pipeline covering joint friction, joint/tendon limits, and frictional contacts with sliding, torsional and rolling friction (Abstract).

## Physics
- **Contact model**: soft, convex, "complementarity-free." Contact impulses act in a per-contact frame aligned with the contact normal; friction dimensionality `d(i) ∈ {2,3,5}` per contact (frictionless normal-only, sliding-friction 2D, or sliding+torsional+rolling 5D), giving each contact `1+d(i)` impulse components: normal, tangential friction (2), torsional friction (1), rolling friction (2) (Sec. II-D). "In the case of frictionless contacts" only the normal distance constraint applies. Geometry/mesh representation and number of contact points per geom pair are not stated in this paper (that is mujoco_2012's collision-detection scope, not re-derived here).
- **Solver**: forward dynamics impulse `f` is found by minimizing regularized, offset next-step kinetic energy subject to the elliptical-friction-cone and non-negativity inequalities `φ(f) ≥ 0` (Eq. 10-11) — "This is a convex optimization problem and has a unique global minimum. We solve it using our (yet unpublished) generalization of the projected Gauss-Seidel method (GPGS) that can handle cone and pyramid constraints" (Sec. II-E). Iteration counts tested: **5 and 50** GPGS iterations (Fig. 3 / Sec. VI-B): "We performed the test twice, with 5 and 50 iterations of the GPGS solver in the forward dynamics." The paper explicitly rejects the complementarity/LCP route for this formulation: "In the presence of frictional contacts, this approach yields an NP-hard problem in the forward dynamics. Furthermore its inverse cannot be defined. Thus we will not pursue it further in this paper" (Sec. II-D). Equality/holonomic constraints use a separate "soft Gauss principle" derivation (Theorem 1, Sec. II-C) rather than the contact GPGS solver.
- **Differentiable / invertible contacts**: the paper's central claim is analytical invertibility, not gradient differentiability in the ML sense. "The entire simulation pipeline can be inverted analytically, an order-of-magnitude faster than the corresponding forward dynamics" (Abstract). Theorem 3 (Sec. III-C) gives the general forward/inverse equivalence via KKT conditions; Sec. III-D restricts to a circular-cone, Cartesian-metric special case solvable in closed form ("Inverse contact dynamics: Analytical special case"). The inverse dynamics do not require computing or factorizing the inertia matrix `M`, or the dense `A` matrix — "the A matrix is no longer needed in the inverse" (Sec. VII) — only the diagonal regularizer `R`, which is why inversion is O(m) rather than O(m^2 maxiter).
- **Timestep / substeps**: "the forward dynamics of a 27-dof humanoid with 10 contacts are evaluated in 0.1 msec. Since the simulation is stable at 10 msec timesteps, it can run 100 times faster than real-time" (Abstract). Sec. VI-B repeats: "the simulation timestep is 10 milliseconds." No substep/decimation concept described.
- **Friction model**: elliptical Coulomb friction cone, with the normal impulse `f_i ≥ 0` and the friction/torsional/rolling components constrained to lie within an elliptical cone (Eq. 9-9'); in the special case of 2D sliding friction with equal coefficients `µ1(i)=µ2(i)=µ(i)` this "reduces to the more familiar definition of a friction cone." Joint dry friction is modeled as a separate impulse type: "η(i) is the (load-independent) joint torque that is lost to friction before the joint starts accelerating" (Sec. II-D). The cone is kept exact (not linearized to a pyramid) as in mujoco_2012.
- **Penetration handling / exposure**: penetration is one coordinate of a unified "violation/penetration distance" vector `x` spanning constraints, limits, and contact normals (Sec. V, para. 2: "For constraints, limits and contact normals x_i is the violation/penetration distance"). Its dynamics are driven by a virtual critically-damped PD-like stabilizer with regularization scale `ε` and time constant `κ` (Eq. 22-28); in the analytic steady-state example (free-floating object resting under gravity g), the resulting **penetration depth is derived in closed form and shown to be independent of the object's mass** (Sec. V, Eq. following (28); exact expression not preserved by PDF-to-text conversion, but the dependency claim is explicit). Limiting behavior: "in the limit ε→∞ we have a spring-damper driven by a. In the limit ε→0 the spring-damper becomes autonomous and removes constraint violations regardless of a. The latter limit corresponds to hard constraints and contacts. In practice we use small ε" (Sec. V). This `x`-space formulation (violation distance as an explicit state) is the mechanism by which penetration is a first-class, inspectable quantity in the solver's own math, though the paper does not state that a runtime API exposes it to users — that is an implementation detail outside this paper's scope.
- **GPU or CPU**: CPU only, single-threaded for the reported timing, "with AVX instructions (with custom BLAS-like routines)" (Sec. VI-B) on an Intel i7-3930K, Windows 7.
- **Throughput**: Fig. 3 (Sec. VI-B), single core, Intel i7-3930K, humanoid dragged interactively with a 3D mouse to vary contact-Jacobian size: forward dynamics run at 5 and 50 GPGS iterations; CPU time per step (microsec) plotted vs. contact-Jacobian size 0-100; inverse dynamics timed the same way. Prose values: **inverse dynamics of the 27-dof humanoid under 100 impulses: 30 microseconds; with no impulses: 10 microseconds**; simulation timestep 10 ms, so "the inverse dynamics are being computed between 300 and 1000 times faster than real time depending on the number of impulses." "The forward dynamics are slower — by an order of magnitude — but still much faster than real time." Increasing GPGS iterations from 5 to 50 "is expensive with large number of impulses," but inverse-dynamics cost is unaffected by GPGS iteration count "because computing the full A matrix is avoided." Exact numeric forward-dynamics microsecond values are only in the unparsed Fig. 3 plot, not in text.
- **Complexity** (Sec. IV, n = dim(v), m = dim(f)): forward dynamics: factorizing M is O(n^3); computing A = J M^-1 J^T is O(m^2 n + n^2 m); GPGS solve is O(m^2 · maxiter). Inverse dynamics: RNE is O(n); the new analytical impulse solver is O(m); worst case dominated by computing the contact Jacobian J, O(mn). "Sparsity makes the actual performance better" in both cases; collision detection cost is excluded from this analysis and stated elsewhere to be "a small fraction of the computational cost."
- **Robot hands shipped as assets**: none — no hand, arm, or manipulator asset appears in this paper or in the parsed code file tree.

VII).

## Limitations stated by the authors
- "For more complex simulations the forward and inverse do not agree so closely" than the single-joint correctness test, "making the inverse dynamics approach more appealing for optimal control and estimation applications" (Sec. VI-A) — i.e., the GPGS iterative solver may not reach the optimum the analytical inverse assumes.
- The general inverse case (Theorem 3 / Sec. III-C) "cannot be solved analytically because both the cone and the distance metric are elliptical, making the problem equivalent to finding the roots of a polynomial of order 2(d+1)"; the paper only solves a restricted "analytical special case" (Sec. III-D).
- Small ε, κ (closer to hard constraints) "make the forward dynamics harder to simulate (requiring smaller timesteps or larger number of solver iterations)" and make the inverse output "more sensitive to the input" (Sec. VII).
- "A systematic analysis of the effects of this approximation for complex robots remains to be done" regarding the friction-cone relaxation (Sec. I-A).
- The GPGS solver itself is described as "yet unpublished" at the time of writing (Sec. II-E).
- MPC-mode application is future work / estimated, not demonstrated: "we estimate that in model-predictive control (MPC) mode our existing optimizer will be an order of magnitude slower than real time" (Sec. VII).

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "The forward dynamics of a 27-dof humanoid with 10 contacts are evaluated in 0.1 msec. Since the simulation is stable at 10 msec timesteps, it can run 100 times faster than real-time on a single core of a desktop processor." (Abstract)
- "We soften all constraints, in a way that avoids instabilities and unrealistic penetrations associated with earlier spring-damper methods and yet is sufficient to allow inversion." (Abstract)
- "In the presence of frictional contacts, this approach [complementarity-based] yields an NP-hard problem in the forward dynamics. Furthermore its inverse cannot be defined." (Sec. II-D)
- "On a single core of a desktop processor, we can evaluate the inverse dynamics of a 27-dof humanoid subject to 100 impulses in 30 microseconds. In the absence of impulses the time goes down to 10 microseconds." (Sec. VI-B)
- "For constraints, limits and contact normals x_i is the violation/penetration distance." (Sec. V)
- "Our softness parameters ε, κ are generally related to the constraint-force mixing (CFM) and error reduction parameter (ERP) introduced in the Open Dynamics Engine (ODE), although the specifics are different ... the problem solved by ODE does not become convex even after regularization." (Sec. VII)

## Notes for the survey (which sections this feeds; contradictions with other notes)
- Physics-engine background section: this is the paper that names and formalizes MuJoCo's soft-convex "complementarity-free" contact model that mujoco_2012's "convex solver" sketches; cite mujoco_2012 for the three-solver taxonomy (implicit-complementarity / convex / diagonal) and this note for the finalized convex+invertible formulation with explicit penetration-as-state-variable treatment and GPGS iteration counts (5 vs. 50).
- Penetration-depth handling is the load-bearing fact for this survey's contact-fidelity comparisons: penetration is modeled as a continuous, mass-independent, critically-damped decay variable `x_i` (Sec. V) rather than a hard non-penetration constraint — this is the mathematical root of "MuJoCo permits controlled penetration" claims used elsewhere in the survey (e.g. against rigid/LCP engines in physics_engine_comparison_2015). Note that this paper does not itself state whether penetration depth is queryable via a public API — only that it is an explicit internal solver variable.
- No GPU, no batching, no hand assets — same caveat as mujoco_2012: throughput numbers here (single-core CPU microseconds) are not comparable to later GPU-parallel FPS figures without noting the different measurement protocol and era.
- Solver-iteration numbers (5 and 50 GPGS iterations, Fig. 3) are the only concrete "iteration count" data point found for classic MuJoCo's own contact solver across the ten notes in this batch — useful for the survey's solver/iteration-count table.
