# comfree_sim_2026 — ComFree-Sim: A GPU-Parallelized Analytical Contact Physics Engine for Scalable Contact-Rich Robotics Simulation and Control (Borse, Xie, Huang, Jin; ASU/UIUC, arXiv 2026)

sources: papers/md/comfree_sim_2026.md [c6fda8b3] ; code/md/comfree_sim_2026.md [ba8b9969]

## One-line contribution
A GPU-parallel, complementarity-free contact engine that computes contact impulses in closed form via a prediction–correction update in the dual cone of Coulomb friction (extended to a unified 6D model with tangential/torsional/rolling friction), implemented in Warp behind a MuJoCo-compatible interface as a drop-in for MuJoCo Warp (MJWarp), giving near-linear contact-count scaling and 2-3× higher throughput in dense-contact scenes (Abstract).

## Setting
- hand(s): LEAP Hand (real hardware, multi-fingered), used for real-time MPC in-hand reorientation of Cube/Duck/SpamTin/Cylinder objects (Sec. V); also an Allegro hand in-hand cube-grasping scene used purely for the parallel-throughput benchmark (Sec. IV-E).
- simulator / physics: ComFree-Sim, built on the complementarity-free contact model of prior work [8], extended with (1) a unified 6D contact model (tangential + torsional + rolling friction), (2) a dual-cone impedance heuristic, (3) GPU parallel implementation in NVIDIA Warp exposed through a MuJoCo-compatible API (Sec. III intro). Benchmarked against MJWarp using the *same* collision detection and time integration, isolating only the contact-resolution backend (Sec. IV intro).
- observation / action space: MPC formulation, state x_t / control u_t, incremental joint-position control clipped to [−0.1, 0.1] (Sec. V).
- objects / data: real-world LEAP-hand objects — Cube, Duck, SpamTin, Cylinder (convex and non-convex) — pose-tracked via Intel RealSense D435i + FoundationPose at ~25 Hz (Sec. V-A).

## Physics block
- contact model: complementarity-free, analytical (closed-form) contact resolution in the *dual cone* of the (primal) Coulomb friction cone, via a prediction–correction / impedance-style update (Sec. III-A). Dynamics: M(q)dv = (τ − c)dt + J(q)ᵀλdt, with λ interpreted as the step-averaged contact wrench (impulse = λ·dt) to sidestep the Painlevé paradox of continuous-time hard Coulomb friction. Extended to a **unified 6D model**: normal force λ_n, tangential friction impulse λ_t ∈ R², torsional friction moment m_tor ∈ R, rolling friction moment m_roll ∈ R² — each with its own friction coefficient (µ_tor, µ_roll have units of length, interpreted as an effective contact-patch radius) (Sec. III-A). Each dual-cone constraint is linearized into n_s polyhedral facets per contact, giving a per-facet, per-contact analytical impulse λ = (K·(s·dt + φ̃) + D·s)₊ (Eq. 8), where (·)₊ = max(·,0) captures sticking/sliding/separation via different facet-activation patterns "while satisfying the primal Coulomb friction constraints (3) by construction" (Sec. III-A).
- solver and iterations: none — this is the paper's central point: no per-step complementarity solve or iterative optimization; the contact impulse is computed once, analytically, per contact-facet, decoupled across contacts and separable across cone facets (Sec. III-C). A 4-kernel GPU pipeline replaces solver iterations: (I) smooth-velocity prediction, (II) per-contact/per-face dual-cone solve, (III) generalized-impulse accumulation, (IV) velocity correction (Algorithm 1).
- differentiability: not claimed or discussed in this paper (no autodiff/adjoint mechanism is described; the analytical closed form would plausibly support it, but the text makes no such claim).
- timestep: dt = 0.002 s in the GPU benchmarks (penetration, torsional/rolling friction, stability, runtime-scaling, throughput; Fig. 3 caption, Sec. IV-A/D/E) — ComFree-Sim "remains stable at a moderately large time step (e.g., dt=0.02 s), but typically benefits from smaller dt than MJWarp" (Sec. IV-C). Real-hardware MPC uses dt = 0.004 s with horizon H = 48 (Sec. V).
- friction model: unified 6D Coulomb-type model — tangential (sliding), torsional (about the contact normal), and rolling (in-tangent-plane) friction, each with a dedicated coefficient and linearized dual-cone facets (Sec. III-A); dual-cone impedance is parameterized by two global scalars (k_user, d_user) plus a MuJoCo-compatible, gap-dependent scaling factor r(|φ|) using the identical (r_min, r_max, w, m, p) = [0.9, 0.95, 0.001, 0.5, 2.0] default hyperparameter API as MuJoCo's own solver (Sec. III-B).
- how penetration is resolved / is depth exposed: resolved via the impedance stiffness/damping (k_user, d_user) acting on the signed-gap violation term φ̃ inside the analytical impulse law; NOT eliminated to machine precision (unlike Dojo) — it is exposed, measured, and tunable. Table (Fig. 3b), collision-rich drop test (five 5×5 arrays of convex primitives, ~5 cm average size, dt=0.002s, 1000 steps, mean±std over all detected contacts): **MuJoCo-Warp** 1.7 ± 4.9 mm; **ComFree-Sim** (k_user, d_user) = (0.1, 0.001) → 3.9 ± 6.9 mm; (0.1, 0.005) → 3.8 ± 5.7 mm; (0.3, 0.001) → 1.6 ± 3.3 mm; (0.3, 0.005) → 1.4 ± 2.5 mm; (0.5, 0.001) → 1.0 ± 2.1 mm; (0.5, 0.005) → 0.9 ± 1.5 mm — "penetration increases with k_user and decreases with d_user; with appropriate tuning, ComFree-Sim achieves comparable or lower penetration than MJWarp" (Sec. IV-A).
- GPU or CPU: GPU (NVIDIA, via Warp). All benchmarks run on "an AMD 32-core CPU with an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU" (Sec. IV intro) or "AMD 32-core CPU with NVIDIA 4090 GPUs" (Sec. V-A hardware).
- throughput with table and hardware: **Runtime scaling vs. contact count** (Sec. IV-D, Fig. 7; 512 parallel envs, three 2×2 stacked-primitive arrays/env, dt=0.002s, k_user=0.1, d_user=0.001, 750 steps): ComFree-Sim shows "around 3× faster simulation speed" than MJWarp with "approximately linear" step-time scaling vs. contact count, while MJWarp shows "nonlinear (often superlinear) step-time growth and higher variance" — no absolute step-time numbers given, only the plotted relative comparison. **Parallel-environment throughput** (Sec. IV-E, Fig. 8; Allegro-hand cube-grasping, single-env steps/sec, 256/512/1024/2048/4096 envs, 1500 steps, random joint perturbation every 50 steps): "ComFree-Sim achieves nearly 2× the throughput of MJWarp." **Real-time MPC on hardware** (Table I): MPPI compute time per step across 8 object/task pairs — ComFree-Sim range 13.9-28.2 ms vs. MJWarp 34.0-68.8 ms, "~2.4× average speedup (median ~2.2×)"; closed-loop control frequency 35-72 Hz depending on contact count (Sec. V-A/B).

## Evaluation
- metrics (exact definitions, Sec. V-B): success rate — trial succeeds if position error < 0.02 m AND quaternion error < 0.04, where quaternion error = 1 − (q_targetᵀq_obj)²; position error = ‖p_obj − p_target‖; MPPI time = wall-clock compute per MPC step; task completion time = elapsed real time to reach success. 20 independent trials per object/target; position/quaternion errors computed over successful trials only.
- headline numbers (Table I, 8 object×axis pairs, ComFree vs. MJWarp): success rate — Cube(Yaw) 80.00% vs. 65.00%; Cube(Roll/Pitch) 55.00% vs. 30.00%; Duck(Yaw) 50.00% vs. 25.00%; Duck(Roll/Pitch) 20.00% vs. 10.00%; SpamTin(Yaw) 65.00% vs. 15.00%; SpamTin(Roll/Pitch) 40.00% vs. 30.00%; Cylinder(Yaw) 50.00% vs. 10.00%; Cylinder(Roll/Pitch) 50.00% vs. 10.00% — "+27 percentage points on average across eight benchmarks" (Sec. V-B). MPPI time uniformly lower for ComFree-Sim on every pair (e.g. Cube-Yaw 28.23±2.01 ms vs. 54.60±5.52 ms). Position/quaternion error is mixed — MJWarp lower on some tasks (Duck-Yaw, Cylinder-Yaw), ComFree-Sim matching or better on others (Cube-Roll/Pitch, Cylinder-Roll/Pitch).
- baselines beaten: MJWarp, as a drop-in same-API comparison, on penetration (with tuning), runtime scaling, parallel throughput, and closed-loop success rate; comparable raw manipulation accuracy overall ("ComFree-Sim achieves manipulation accuracy comparable to MJWarp," Sec. V-B).
- real robot? Yes — physical LEAP Hand, 20 trials per object/target, 4 objects × 2 axis-groups = 8 configurations (Table I, Sec. V-A).

## Limitations stated by the authors
- No explicit "Limitations" section is present in the parsed text; the paper frames trade-offs rather than admitted weaknesses, e.g. that ComFree-Sim "typically benefits from smaller dt than MJWarp" for stability (Sec. IV-C), and that raw pose accuracy is not uniformly better than MJWarp, only closed-loop success rate and compute time (Sec. V-B).

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "Physics simulation for contact-rich robotics is often bottlenecked by contact resolution ... requiring per-step iterative solves whose cost grows superlinearly with contact density." (Abstract)
- "This structure maps directly to GPU parallelism, reducing computation complexity from superlinear to linear with respect to contact count." (Sec. I)
- "With appropriate tuning, ComFree-Sim achieves comparable or lower penetration than MJWarp." (Sec. IV-A)
- "ComFree-Sim reduces per-step MPPI compute time for all object–task pairs, with ∼2.4× average speedup (median ∼2.2×), which consistently boosts closed-loop success rates (+27 percentage points on average across eight benchmarks)." (Sec. V-B)

## Notes for the survey
- Table I and Fig. 3b are the two load-bearing numeric tables for this note: penetration-vs-impedance (mm, tunable, comparable to MJWarp's 1.7±4.9 mm at good settings) and real-hardware success-rate/MPPI-time. Both are self-reported by the ComFree-Sim authors against their own MJWarp baseline re-run on the same hardware — a genuinely controlled, same-API comparison, but still a single lab's benchmark, not an independent third-party measurement.
- Directly relevant to `mujoco_warp_2025` (the baseline it drop-in replaces) and to the contact-model taxonomy in `contact_models_comparison_2023`: ComFree-Sim is neither LCP, CCP, RaiSim, nor NCP in that paper's sense — it is complementarity-free by construction (no complementarity constraint is ever posed or solved), which is the main architectural point to carry into the survey's contact-model comparison table.
- Code md provenance: `code/md/comfree_sim_2026.md` (18,000 chars) is README + file tree; no Warp kernel source for Algorithm 1 was captured, so solver/kernel detail above is from the paper text only.
