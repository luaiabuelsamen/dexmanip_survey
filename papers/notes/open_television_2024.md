# open_television_2024 — Open-TeleVision: Teleoperation with Immersive Active Visual Feedback (Cheng, Li et al., CoRL 2024)

sources: papers/md/open_television_2024.md [935d6394 / arxiv 2407.01512] ; code/md/open_television_2024.md [e6e25afd]

## One-line contribution
A VR teleoperation system (Apple VisionPro/Quest) that streams active stereoscopic video from a robot-head-mounted, actively-steered stereo camera back to the operator's eyes while mirroring the operator's arm/hand pose onto the robot, letting one remote operator teleoperate a bimanual humanoid with either multi-fingered hands or grippers to collect ACT imitation-learning data (Abstract, §2).

## Setting
- hand(s): Unitree H1 with 6-DoF Inspire Robots hands (multi-fingered: "Each hand has five fingers and 12 DoFs, among which 6 are actuated DoFs: two actuated DoFs are on the thumb and one on each of the remaining fingers," with underactuated PIP/IP joints via linkage, App. C); Fourier GR-1 with a 1-DoF jaw gripper (parallel-jaw, not multi-fingered). Arm: 7-DoF arms on both robots. Bimanual: yes on both platforms.
- simulator / physics: none for the main results — all reported numbers are real-robot; code repo does include an optional Isaac Gym teleoperation demo (`teleop/teleop_hand.py`, `Sim` class) for visualizing two simulated hands, but this is a teleoperation demo, not a training/eval environment.
- observation: stereo video at 480x640 per eye, streamed at 60 Hz round-trip (§2); for the ACT imitation policy, DinoV2 ViT tokens (16x22 per image) from two stereo images plus a proprioception token projected from current joint positions (§3).
- action space: absolute joint positions. H1: 28-dim (7 per arm + 6 per hand + 2 for the active neck). GR-1: 19-dim (7 per arm + 1 per gripper + 3 for the active neck) (§3). Chunk size 60 at 60 Hz control frequency (§3.2 discussion).
- objects / data: four tasks — Can Sorting (10 cans/episode, 5 Coke + 5 Sprite, randomized), Can Insertion (6 cans/episode, 5.6 cm can into ~7.6 cm slot), Folding (one towel, two folds/episode), Unloading (4 tubes from 4 random slots, extract + in-hand pass + place). Evaluation: 5 real-world episodes per task (Table 1 caption: "We record 5 real-world episodes for each task").

## Method
- paradigm: IL (behavior cloning) via ACT [Zhao et al.], modified with a DinoV2 ViT backbone (replacing ResNet18) and two stereo images instead of ACT's original four fixed+wrist cameras (§3).
- reward or loss: not an RL paper — no reward function. Training hyperparameters (paper, §3.1): AdamW, lr 5e-5, batch size 45, 25k iterations, single RTX 4090. Code repo's training command (`README`, code/md) additionally shows `--kl_weight 10 --chunk_size 60 --hidden_dim 512 --dim_feedforward 3200 --num_epochs 50000 --lr 5e-5`, i.e. ACT's standard CVAE-style loss with KL weight 10 — this KL weight is not mentioned in the paper text itself (mismatch: paper reports 25k iterations/lr 5e-5/batch 45 in prose; code's example command shows 50000 epochs and an explicit kl_weight=10 not stated in the paper).
- key trick(s): active stereo camera on a 2-3 DoF actuated gimbal/neck mirroring the operator's head motion ("first-person active sensing"), reducing irrelevant visual tokens vs. a static wide-angle camera and giving 2x faster training/inference (Table 2: 0.41s vs 0.32s per training batch, 83 Hz vs 42 Hz deployment); hand retargeting via "dex-retargeting" vector-optimization library solved with SLSQP, using 7 keypoint vectors for dexterous hands (5 wrist-to-fingertip vectors + 2 thumb-to-index/middle vectors) vs. 1 vector (thumb-to-index) for grippers (§2); differentiated IK treatment of end-effector position (relative to head) vs. orientation (absolute, from VR init) to keep the arm stable as the head moves (§2); nullspace joint-angle offset near manipulability limits to avoid IK failures without degrading tracking (§2).

## Contact / penetration handling
Not addressed as a simulated interpenetration metric — there is no simulator in the reported results. The paper does discuss visual occlusion from hand/gripper-object contact as a perception confound (Appendix B: GR-1's gripper occludes the camera's view of the can's color, degrading placement accuracy; adding color labels to the cans raised GR-1 placing success from 60% to 100% for the DinoV2 model), but this is a visibility artifact analysis, not a contact-force or penetration measurement. No two-hand or hand-object interpenetration term appears anywhere in the paper or code.

## Coordination and embodiment (survey fields)
- two-hand coordination: one policy over both arms/hands — a single ACT model outputs the full joint-position action vector for both arms (and both hands or both grippers) plus the neck jointly from shared stereo image + proprioception tokens (§3); at the teleoperation layer, retargeting/IK is run independently per hand/arm from the operator's tracked left/right hand and wrist poses (config file `teleop/inspire_hand.yml` in code defines separate `left:`/`right:` retargeting blocks, each with its own URDF, target link names, and a `scaling_factor: 1.1`), so there is no leader/follower assignment and no explicit relative-frame two-hand observation — each hand is retargeted in the robot's own head-relative frame independently, then the trained policy fuses both into one action.
- torso: fixed-base humanoid upper body — the paper explicitly restricts to "their active sensing neck, two 7DoF arms and end-effectors, while other DoFs are not used" on Unitree H1 and Fourier GR-1 (§2); it is not driving a mobile base or legs; authors list a mobile extension as future work (§5).
- hand model/DoF each side: Inspire Robots hand, 6 actuated DoF (12 DoF total with underactuation), multi-fingered, identical on both sides, used with H1; Fourier GR-1's end-effector is a 1-DoF parallel-jaw gripper, not multi-fingered, on both sides — the two robots are separate embodiments in this paper, not two different hands on the same robot.

## Evaluation
- metrics (exact definitions): success rate per sub-task (pick/place/insert/fold/extract/pass/place), computed over "5 real-world episodes for each task" (Table 1); on GR-1, "each Can Sorting roll-out contains 6 pickings and 6 placings, accumulating 30 trials for each sub-task" (Table 1 caption). Generalization eval: success-rate heatmap over a 4x4 cm grid of can placements, 5 trials per grid cell (§3.2, Fig. 6). User study: completion time (s) and success rate per participant (4 participants, all 4 tasks each) comparing Stereo vs. Mono viewing (Table 3).
- headline numbers: Table 1 — H1 Can Sorting: DinoV2(ours) Pick 92%/Place 88% vs ResNet18 74%/58% vs w/o-Stereo 46%/52%; GR-1 Can Sorting: ours Pick 87%/Place 60%; Can Insertion (H1): ours Pick 90%/Insert 87%; Folding (H1): ours 100% at every stage; Unloading (H1): ours 100% at every stage vs ResNet18 (85% extract, 100% pass, 95% place) and w/o-Stereo (70% extract, 95% pass, 100% place). Appendix Table 5 (GR-1 with labeled cans, same trial counts as Table 1): ours Pick improves 87%->97%, Place 60%->100%. User study Table 3: Stereo mean completion time 66/58/34/67 s across the 4 tasks with 100% success on all four, vs Mono 91/85/50/97 s with success 93%/71%/100%/50%.
- baselines beaten: "w. ResNet18" (original ACT ImageNet-pretrained ResNet18 backbone) and "w/o Stereo Input" (single left-camera image only) as ablations; "Mono" viewing condition vs "Stereo" in the user study; qualitative comparison table (Table 4) against OPEN TEACH, HATO, AnyTeleop, Telekinesis, Transteleop, ALOHA, AirExo, GELLO, Mobile ALOHA, DexCap on actuation type, hand support, bimanual support, perception type, remote capability, and depth sensing.
- real robot? Yes, all numbers above are real-robot (Unitree H1 and Fourier GR-1); trial counts given above (5 episodes/task for Table 1, 30 sub-task trials for GR-1 Can Sorting, 4 participants for the user study).

## Limitations stated by the authors
"there is still a lack of other forms of feedback, such as haptic feedback, which is typically the dominant feedback with first-person visual occlusion and in tactile-intensive tasks. A system that enables the relabeling of expert data could be very helpful for increasing success rate, which is also missing from our system now. The future work can be extended to mobile version which utilizes all the DoFs of the robot" (§5).

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "Our system is 2x faster for training with the same batch size and can accommodate 4x data in one batch on a 4090 GPU. During inference, our system is also 2x faster... reach 60Hz deployment control frequency" (§3.2).
- "It will be very challenging for a parallel-jaw gripper to stably grasp a Pringles Chips tube" (§4, Bimanual Teleoperation) — motivating multi-finger hands over grippers.
- "Prior to Open-TeleVision, no system offered both remote control and depth sensing simultaneously... By utilizing stereo streaming, our system is the first to provide both functionalities within a single setup" (Appendix A).

## Notes for the survey (which sections this feeds; contradictions with other notes)
Feeds: the teleoperation-rig comparison table — Table 4 in the paper itself is a ready-made cross-system comparison (actuation type, hand support, bimanual, perception, remote, depth) that the survey can reuse directly, and this note's headline numbers are a direct counterpart to bidex_teleop_2024's Table 1/2 (glove+teacher-arm) and humanplus_2024's Table 3 (single-RGB shadowing) for a teleoperation-quality comparison, though the three papers use different task sets and cannot be compared task-for-task. Also feeds the gripper-vs-multi-finger-hand comparison: this is the one paper in this batch that runs the *same* task (Can Sorting) on both a multi-fingered hand (H1/Inspire) and a parallel-jaw gripper (GR-1) side by side, and finds the gripper's placement accuracy is bottlenecked by self-occlusion of the object color (Appendix B), not manipulation itself — a useful counterexample to any blanket claim that grippers underperform hands. Per-hand retargeting config in code/md confirms independent left/right optimization with no relative-frame coupling; cite this when the survey compares "one policy over both hands" architectures against explicit relative-frame designs like asymdex_2024.
