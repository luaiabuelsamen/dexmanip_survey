## One-line contribution
POISE: sim-to-real PPO for in-hand reaching to arbitrary palm-relative 6D object poses (not just reorientation), via diverse stable-grasp initialization, a BPS geometry-conditioned policy, an adaptive curriculum that expands rotation/translation goal magnitude, and a grasp-maintenance reward based on friction-cone wrench-margin coverage relative to the episode's own starting grasp (Abstract; Sec. IV).

## Setting
- hand(s): 22-DoF Sharpa Wave hand [41] (Sec. V-A); fixed wrist (no arm motion — "a fixed-wrist hand moving an already-grasped object ... using only finger motions", Sec. III); single hand.
- simulator / physics: Isaac Lab [40] (Sec. V-A). Physics at 120 Hz; policy issues incremental joint-position commands at 20 Hz. 64,000 parallel envs for the curriculum-ablation training run (Sec. V-B.2); env count for the main/default training run not stated separately. GPU / wall-clock not stated.
- observation: actor gets "three-frame histories of the current and commanded finger-joint positions, estimated palm-frame object pose and velocity, the gravity direction expressed in the palm frame, a confidence score for the visual pose estimate, the target pose, the current-to-target translation and rotation, and an object-geometry descriptor z_O" (Sec. III). Critic additionally gets "noise-free simulator states, fingertip states, applied joint torques, and contact forces" (asymmetric PPO, Sec. V-A). Object-geometry descriptor z_O: 64-dimensional basis point set (BPS) [29,39] — fixed query points in the object's canonical frame, each entry = normalized distance from that query point to the nearest object surface (Sec. IV-B).
- action space: "the normalized action incrementally updates the commanded finger joints" — a position-target delta on finger joints only (Sec. III); no explicit scale/range given in the parse.
- objects / data: no motion-capture dataset — objects are geometric primitives from "three training shape families and sizes," nine shape-size combinations jointly trained (Sec. V-C.1). Real-world test set: Cube, Hexagonal Prism, Square Bifrustum (all three training families) plus a held Hammer (215×50×33.6 mm, out-of-family aspect ratio, Sec. V-C.1) with an extended translation curriculum up to 100 mm.

## Method
- paradigm: RL, sim-to-real ; algorithm: asymmetric PPO [8] in Isaac Lab (Sec. V-A). Actor and critic both [512,256,128] ELU MLPs.
- goal formulation: goal-conditioned POMDP over SE(3) errors (Sec. III). Position error e_p and rotation error e_R = geodesic angle via Log(·) (Frobenius norm). "A target is reached when both errors remain within their tolerances for a prescribed number of control steps. A new target is then sampled without resetting the hand-object state during training" (Sec. III) — successive multi-goal rollouts, not single-goal episodes.

So r^pose weights the rotation term 1/6 and the position term 1/4, and (α, σ) steps (0.15, 30 mm) → (0.40, 20 mm) → (1.00, 15 mm) as e_R falls below 45° and 25° — "When the orientation error is large, the position term is broad and downweighted... As the object becomes rotationally aligned, the reward increasingly emphasizes accurate positioning."
  - "Goal completion": s_t ∈ {0,1} indicates object retained and within both tolerances; verified only after N=20 consecutive steps hold; c_t ∈ {0,1} marks the first step of verification. Eq. 7, recovered: `rgoal t = 0.5st + 45ct. (7)` — a 0.5 per-step retention term and a one-time bonus of 45. "The per-step term encourages the policy to remain inside the target region, while the one-time bonus rewards verified completion."
  - "Grasp maintenance" (r_t^grasp): each active contact approximated by four friction-cone wrench rays, moments normalized by object size; per force/torque axis m_{t,k} ∈ [0,1] = the smaller of the max ray projections in the positive/negative direction on that axis; the six axis margins are aggregated by "generalized mean emphasizing the weakest of the six bidirectional projection margins" → grasp-wrench quality Q. Eq. 8, recovered: `Qt = 1 6 6 k=1 (mt,k + ε)−8 −1/8 −ε. (8)` — a power mean of order −8 over the six margins, with ε offsetting the inversion. Reward is relative to the episode's own reset quality: Q* = mean Q over the first four control steps, clipped to [0.08, 0.35], and Eq. 9 is `rgrasp t = 1 −clip (Q⋆−Qt)+ Q⋆ , 0, 1 2 . (9)` — the trailing `2` being the squaring of the clipped relative-quality drop; "the reward remains maximal while the initial wrench coverage is retained and decreases smoothly as the grasp becomes less secure" — i.e. it is scored against the starting grasp, not an absolute threshold.
  - "Drop and actuation regularization": d_t ∈ {0,1} drop indicator; "the complete reward penalizes drops, joint torque, and instantaneous mechanical power"; the weights, recovered from Eq. 10, are −80 d_t for the drop, −0.10‖τ_t‖²₂ for joint torque and −0.15 (τ_tᵀ q̇_t)² for instantaneous mechanical power.

The reading gap this bullet used to record is closed; the only numbers still absent are ones the paper never printed.
- key trick(s): (a) diverse stable-grasp reset cache built by optimization-based grasp synthesis [37,38] — sample object pose, optimize contacts/joint config, perturb, simulate 2 s under gravity+position control, keep only candidates that retain the object (Sec. IV-A), 6,842 stable reset states in the ablation cache; (b) adaptive curriculum expands rotation 5°→180° and translation 10→30 mm; magnitudes at each stage sampled at frontier/exposed-range/current-limit in a 0.6/0.3/0.1 ratio; limit increases by 10° or 10 mm once frontier success ≥ 0.4; each object has independent curriculum progress (Sec. IV-C).

## Contact / penetration handling
Not addressed as interpenetration. Contact is handled only through the grasp-maintenance reward's friction-cone wrench-margin proxy (Sec. IV-D) and a drop penalty; no penetration metric, contact-solver setting, or interpenetration term is stated anywhere in the parse.

## Evaluation
- metrics: "first-target success" (reach + hold tolerance for a fixed dwell); "strict" success = 10 mm / 10° tolerance, "near" success = relaxed 20 mm / 20° tolerance (Sec. V-B.2, Table II caption); goals-per-episode = count of strict-criterion targets completed before reset/drop; grasp-wrench quality Q, minimum force margin, minimum torque margin, episode success rate (Sec. V-B.3, Table III); real-world "sequence success" = all 3 planned targets reached without reset (Sec. V-C.4).
- headline numbers (sim):
  - diverse vs. narrow grasp initialization, held-out grasps, 30-mm/180° first targets: first-target success 40.1% → 51.5%, mean goals/episode 0.69 → 1.00 (Sec. V-B.1).
  - post-drop recovery, 720 trials each policy, object resting ungrasped on palm: first-target success 33.8% → 72.9%; among successes, mean recovery time 11.63 s → 9.45 s (Sec. V-B.1).
  - curriculum ablation, Hexagonal Prism, 64,000 envs (Table II): full-range strict success 6.2% (no curriculum) → 59.5% (curriculum); near 18.8% → 77.0%; goals/ep. 0.08 → 1.55. Maximum-change targets: strict 3.4% → 55.3%; near 13.9% → 72.8%; goals/ep. 0.04 → 1.08.
  - grasp-maintenance reward ablation, 3 seeds each, averaged post-convergence (Table III): Q 0.032±0.002 → 0.045±0.005 (paper says "~41%" gain); min force margin 0.077±0.003 → 0.107±0.010 ("~39%"); min torque margin 0.036±0.002 → 0.047±0.004 ("~31%"); episode success rate 53.7±2.5% → 59.1±2.5% (+5.4 pp).
- headline numbers (real, grasp-reward ablation, Sharpa Wave hand, FoundationPose visual feedback, Sec. V-C.4, Table IV): both policies run 10 trials of the same 3-target sequence from the same starting grasp. Complete sequences (all 3 reached) 2/10 → 8/10, i.e. "20% to 80%" as quoted in the abstract; planned targets completed 8/30 (26.7%) → 27/30 (90.0%); mean targets reached 0.8 → 2.7; reach time 6.8 s → 4.9 s; position error 4.8 → 6.4 mm (worse with the reward); orientation error 4.7° → 5.9° (worse with the reward). So "sequence success" is a per-attempt binary (all 3 targets of a fixed 3-target sequence reached, no reset) over 10 trials, not per-pose — and the accuracy trade-off (slightly worse steady-state error) that comes with more reliable multi-target completion is stated in the table, not glossed over.
- other real-world results (video/qualitative, no trial counts given): continuous random goal reaching on 4 objects, "at least five successive goals without resetting" per rollout (Sec. V-C.1) — no n reported; user-specified sequences: 4 Hexagonal Prism targets (up to 35 mm/100°) and 7 Hammer targets (up to 93 mm/180°), mean reach times 3.0 s and 2.7 s — single representative sequences, not repeated-trial statistics (Sec. V-C.1); 3 wrist orientations, one representative rollout each completing 7/5/6 successive goals, mean reach times 5.1/5.5/7.1 s, per-target minimum error 5.3-7.4 mm / 3.2-4.4° (Sec. V-C.2); disturbance recovery — one qualitative double-perturbation sequence (Sec. V-C.3, Fig. 7), no trial count.
- baselines beaten: internal ablations only (narrow-init policy, no-curriculum policy, no-grasp-reward policy); no external baseline method is re-run or quoted.
- real robot? Sharpa Wave hand (22 DoF) + RealSense D435 + FoundationPose. Only Sec. V-C.4 (grasp-reward ablation) has an explicit trial count (10 trials/policy, 3-target sequences, 30 planned targets/policy). The geometry-generalization, wrist-orientation, and disturbance-recovery results are single representative rollouts per condition, not trial-count statistics — closer to video evidence than a controlled trial count.

## Limitations stated by the authors (Sec. VI)
- "POISE specifies the object target pose but not a desired hand or contact configuration. The policy may therefore use effective but unnatural hand postures."
- "real-world performance still lags that in simulation because of imperfect contact modeling and errors in 6D pose tracking." Future work: online point-cloud observation distillation, tactile feedback, learning residual dynamics from real interaction.

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "Unlike in-hand reorientation or translation-only tasks [1, 2], this formulation jointly constrains the object's relative 3D translation and 3D rotation." (Sec. I)
- "Prior full-pose in-hand reaching methods remain confined to object-specific simulation [6, 7]." (Sec. I) — [6] = Plappert et al. multigoal HER environments, [7] = Charlesworth & Montana offline trajectory optimization.
- "the reward preserves quality relative to the episode's stable reset rather than using one absolute threshold." (Sec. IV-D)
- "the grasp-maintenance reward increases quality by approximately 41%, the minimum force margin by 39%, and the minimum torque margin by 31% on average. All three grasp metrics improve in every run." (Sec. V-B.3)
- "the reward raises sequence success from 2/10 to 8/10 and target success from 26.7% to 90.0%, while maintaining comparable pose accuracy." (Sec. V-C.4)
- "Without the reward, progressive contact loss often led to object drops." (Sec. V-C.4)

## Notes for the survey
- What is genuinely new vs. 2022-2023 baselines: those (Andrychowicz et al. 2020 [1]; OpenAI-style continuous rotation/translation work) target reorientation, continuous rotation, or constrained translation about one axis class at a time; POISE's task couples full 3D translation and 3D rotation to an arbitrary palm-relative SE(3) target, sampled anew within a single uninterrupted rollout (successive reaching without reset), and explicitly scores whether the terminal grasp remains usable for further manipulation — none of which the reorientation-only baselines optimize or report. The closest prior full-6D-reaching work (Plappert et al. [6], multigoal HER; Charlesworth & Montana [7], offline trajectory optimization) is described as sim-only and low-success at the outset (Sec. II).
- Real-robot evidence is a mix: one condition (grasp-reward ablation) has an actual n=10-trial table with a success-rate contrast; every other real-world claim (geometry generalization, wrist orientations, disturbance recovery, user-specified sequences) is a single representative rollout — survey should not average these into one "real success rate" figure.
