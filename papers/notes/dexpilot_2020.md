## One-line contribution
DexPilot is a markerless, four-RGB-D-camera, glove-free teleoperation system that tracks a bare human hand with a model-based tracker (DART) bootstrapped by two learned neural priors, then retargets it to a KUKA-iiwa7 + Allegro hand system with a fingertip-vector optimization cost that prioritizes distance/direction between fingertips over joint-angle matching, enabling 5-consecutive-trial teleoperation of 15 tasks spanning precision/power grasps, in-hand finger gaiting, and multi-stage manipulation (Abstract; Secs. III-VIII).

## Setting
- operator interface: vision-based, glove-free (bare hand) in the deployed system — four calibrated, time-synchronized Intel RealSense D415 RGB-D cameras arranged around a black-clothed table ("teleoperation work volume is 80cm x 55cm x 38cm", Sec. III). A colored fabric glove (Fig. 5, GloveNet) is used only offline in a two-phase data-collection bootstrap to generate training annotations for the neural hand-pose/segmentation priors — "the second phase... frees the user from having to wear the glove" (Sec. VI) — so the deployed teleoperation itself does not require any worn hardware, only cameras.
- robot hand controlled: Wonik Robotics Allegro hand (16-DoF, 4x4-joint fingers), "retrofitted with four Syntouch Biotac tactile sensors at the fingertips and 3M TB641 grip tape" on phalanges/palm, mounted on a KUKA LBR iiwa7 R800 arm; "the robotic system has 92 tactile signals, 23 joint position signals, and 23 joint torque actions" (Sec. III).
- retargeting formulation (Sec. VII-A, "Kinematic Retargeting"): correspondence is over fingertip task-space vectors, not joint angles — "the joint axes and locations between the two hands are strikingly different, and therefore, no metrics directly comparing joint angles between the two hands are used... both distance and direction among fingertips were considered." Two vector sets define the correspondence (Table I, quoted): "S1: Vectors that originate from a primary finger (index, middle, ring) and point to the thumb." "S2: Vectors between two primary fingers when both primary fingers have associated vectors ∈ S1, e.g., both primary fingers are being projected with the thumb." The cost terms (quoted from the surrounding text, the equation image itself unrecovered): a task-space matching term over vectors ri (with qh/qa the human-model and Allegro joint angles) scaled by "β = 1.6"; "η1 = 1x10^-4 m closes the distance between a primary finger and the thumb"; "η2 = 3x10^-2 m forces a minimum separation distance between two primary fingers when both primary fingers are being projected with the thumb" ("These projections ensure that contact between primary fingers and the thumb are close without inducing primary finger collisions in a precision grasp"); "γ = 2.5x10^-3 is a weight on regularizing the Allegro angles to zero (equivalent to fully opened the hand)... helped greatly with reducing redundancy in solution and ensured that the hand never entered strange minima" (e.g. fingers embedding into the palm). Solved online with SLSQP (NLopt), warm-started from the previous solution each frame; forward kinematics via the Orocos KDL library; a first-order low-pass filter smooths the retargeted joint angles. Joint-limit / anti-redundancy handling: "the distal joints for the primary (index, middle, and ring) fingers of the Allegro hand were constrained to equal their medial joints" (a fixed coupling, not a general joint-limit constraint) plus the γ-regularization toward the open-hand pose; collision handling for the arm is delegated to the downstream Riemannian Motion Policies ("avoiding arm-palm collisions with the table or operator using collision planes", Sec. VII-B) — no explicit hand-self-collision or hand-object collision term is described in the retargeting cost itself, only the η1/η2 fingertip-separation projections, which the paper frames as a proxy against "primary finger collisions in a precision grasp."
- end-to-end latency: "Altogether, the system produces a latency of about one second." (Sec. IV) — not decomposed into per-stage sub-latencies in the parsed text.
- cost of the rig: not stated as a dollar figure; the paper only characterizes the system qualitatively as "a viable, low-cost solution for teleoperating a high DoA robotic system" (Sec. X, "Limitations").
- data rate / demonstrations per hour: not reported as a throughput number. What is reported (Table in App. D, "Completion Times Over 5 Consecutive Trials") are per-task mean completion times across 2 pilots x 5 consecutive trials x 15 tasks, ranging from ~16s (Pick and Place: Brick) to ~358s (Container) — these are teleoperation-session times, not a demos/hour throughput figure, and no aggregate demos-per-hour statistic is computed from them in the paper.
- task(s) demonstrations were used for: no downstream policy training in this paper — DexPilot is presented purely as a teleoperation system, evaluated by having two human pilots directly operate the robot (not collect data for later imitation learning) across 15 physical tasks (Table II): pick-and-place (foam brick/Pringles can/Spam box), 3-size block stacking, pouring beads, opening a peanut jar, 180-degree in-hand brick gaiting, opening a plastic container + cardboard box, concentric cup insertion, tea-drawer open/extract/close, card sliding, wallet money extraction, box flip, and NIST-task-board peg-in-hole insertion (App./Sec. X, low success rate, discussed under limitations). The paper explicitly frames the recorded 92 tactile + 23 joint-position + 23 torque signals as future imitation-learning data ("this rich source of data will be critical for the application of learning methods," Sec. IX) but does not itself train any policy from it.

## Method (perception pipeline, feeding the retargeting)
- DART [27] (Sec. V) tracks a 20-revolute-joint articulated human hand mesh model against the fused point cloud via nonlinear optimization, needing per-frame initialization/pose priors to avoid catastrophic local-minima failures.
- Two-phase neural bootstrap (Sec. VI): Phase 1 — "GloveNet," a ResNet-50 with spatial-softmax, regresses 2D palm-blob keypoints from RGB images of a hand wearing a colored glove, converted to 3D via 4-camera depth fusion, used only to generate hand-pose priors/segmentation for DART and thereby label raw point clouds (glove not needed later). Phase 2 — a PointNet++-based two-stage network predicts 23 hand keypoints (4 joint keypoints x 5 fingers + 3 palm keypoints) directly from the bare-hand point cloud (8192-point subsampled input), trained with "standard Euclidean loss between predicted and ground-truth keypoints together with the voting loss inspired by [11]" plus an auxiliary segmentation loss in stage 1; stage 2 refines using resampled hand-region points, no segmentation loss needed. Both stages trained on ~100K point clouds (7-8 hours of glove-tracked DART sessions), "training takes 15 hours in total on a single NVIDIA TitanXp GPU." A separate two-layer FC network ("JointNet": 69->128->256->20, BatchNorm+ReLU on the hidden layers, Table VI) maps the 23x3 predicted keypoints to a 20-D human joint-angle vector used as a DART prior. Reported accuracy: "average keypoint error on a validation set of seven thousand images... was 9.7 mm... and joint error was 1.33 degrees per joint" (Sec. VI).
- Motion generation (Sec. VII-B): Riemannian Motion Policies (RMPs) control the Cartesian pose of the Allegro palm from the tracked human hand pose while avoiding arm/table/operator collisions via collision planes; RMP-generated arm-joint trajectories go to a torque-level impedance controller at 200 Hz, retargeted Allegro joint angles are sent at 30 Hz.

## Evaluation
- exact success criterion: binary per-trial success/failure, judged by whether the task's physical goal state was reached within the work volume — "If the object falls out of the workspace volume the trial is considered a failure" (Table II caption); no time limit stated beyond the completion-time being the measured outcome.
- number of eval episodes/seeds: 2 pilots x 5 consecutive trials (after a 3-5 trial warm-up) x 15 tasks (Sec. VIII, Table II, App. D); success rates and completion times per task/pilot reported in Figs. 14-15 and App. D's table.
- real robot trial counts: all evaluation is on the real KUKA+Allegro rig — no simulation anywhere in the paper. NIST-task-board peg-in-hole insertions were also attempted "but results were mostly unsatisfactory... success rates are typically within 10%" (Sec. X, Limitations) — the one quantified real failure mode in the paper.
- baselines: none re-run for comparison; the paper contrasts its fingertip-vector cost qualitatively against a cited BioIK joint-position/direction matching approach [19] ("produced retargeting results that are not useful for precision grasps... where gaps between fingertips need to be small or zero") and against a neural-network retargeting alternative the authors themselves tried and rejected: "we also tried retargeting with neural networks but found the results to be unsatisfactory — it did not provide the accuracy commensurate with the online optimisation with sequential least squares" (App. E).

## Limitations stated by the authors (Sec. X, quoted)
- "The projection schemes in kinematic retargeting enabled successful manipulation of small objects, but can interfere with finger gaiting tasks and timely releasing grasps on small objects... this feature can be turned off when desired, but ultimately, this interference should be non-existent."
- "The lack of tactile feedback makes precision tasks difficult to complete."
- "the system latency could be reduced and the responsiveness of the RMP motion generator could be tuned for faster reactions."
- "high-precision tasks like slip-fit peg-in-hole insertions pose a challenge to the current system... success rates are typically within 10% under specific conditions."

## Notes for the survey
- Feeds: the retargeting-formulation comparison table's foundational row — its fingertip-vector task-space cost (S1/S2 sets, η-projection terms, γ-regularization) is the lineage that anyteleop_2023's dex-retargeting library and geometric_retargeting_2025 explicitly build on or contrast against; quote this note's cost-term description (not a verbatim equation — unrecoverable from this parse) when comparing "vector correspondence" retargeting methods to joint-angle-matching or neural (GeoRT-style) alternatives.
- No collision/interpenetration handling in the retargeting cost itself: the η1/η2 fingertip-separation terms are motivated by avoiding "primary finger collisions in a precision grasp" but are heuristic minimum-distance projections, not a general SDF/collision constraint (contrast with toporetarget_2026's explicit signed-distance penetration constraint, or geometric_retargeting_2025 if it has one) — flag this distinction in the survey's Contact/penetration-handling column.
- No cost-of-rig dollar figure and no demos-per-hour throughput number exist in this paper — the survey's cost/throughput table should mark this key "not stated" rather than inferring one from the completion-time table, which measures single-session teleoperation speed, not data-collection throughput for training a downstream policy (this paper trains no policy at all).
