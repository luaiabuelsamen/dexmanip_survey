## One-line contribution
A hybrid-teleoperation data pipeline (arm exoskeleton + Apple Vision Pro finger tracking) feeding a discriminator-reweighted diffusion-transformer policy that outputs full 36-DoF dual-arm dual-hand actions end to end, claimed as "the first open-source VLA system that natively targets dual-arm, dual-hand high-DoF manipulation" (Abstract).

## Setting
- hand(s): XHAND dexterous hand — "a pair of XHAND dexterous hands, each offering 12 fully actuated joints. All finger joints are independently driven, and the thumb and index additionally support lateral ab/adduction" (§III-A). Vendor not named beyond the product name XHAND; DoF: 12 per hand, 24 for the pair.
- arm: "two 6-DoF AIRBOT arms" (§III-A), vendor not further specified. Bimanual: yes — dual-arm, dual-hand, 36 DoF total (2×6 arm + 2×12 hand).
- simulator / physics: MuJoCo digital twin of the real platform, used to generate the synthetic corpus and mirrored in real-time teleoperation (§III-A, §III-B). Timestep, contact solver and env count: not stated.
- observation: "four RGB views and full 36-DoF joint states are logged at 20 Hz" (§III-A); the policy is conditioned on current multi-view RGB **o**_t, language instruction ℓ, and joint state s_t (§III-C).
- action space: joint-angle targets over the full 36-D dual-arm dual-hand vector, generated as an action chunk of length L=32 via iterative denoising, "control frequency, action chunk length L = 32 ... identical across methods" (§IV-A, "Protocol"). Control rate for inference itself is not given a Hz value (only the 20 Hz logging rate for data collection, §III-A, is stated).
- objects / data: synthetic set — "297 objects across 30 categories" (Fig. 4a caption), 200 tasks, 3-5 seed demos/task expanded via the DexMimicGen [30] recipe to 500 trajectories/task, "about 6.5M frames, 361h video" (§III-B). Real set — "347 objects, 17 categories" (Fig. 4b caption), 200 tasks, 50 demos/task, 10K episodes total. FLAGGED DISCREPANCY: the real set's duration/frame count is stated twice, inconsistently — Introduction: "10K real teleoperated episodes (177.5 hours, 3.2M frames)" (§I) vs. Dataset Construction: "The dataset amounts to 40.5 hours and 2.92M frames" (§III-B). Frame counts are close; hour counts are not (177.5h vs. 40.5h) — treat both as unverified, no code/md to cross-check.
- number of parallel envs (for synthetic data generation in MuJoCo): not stated.
- GPU used / wall-clock training time: "We pretrain the policy model for 100K gradient steps and train the discriminator model for 10K steps, using distributed data parallelism across 8 × NVIDIA A100 GPUs with a total batch size of 64. Both models are optimized using AdamW" (§IV-A). Wall-clock time is not stated. Fine-tuning of the baselines separately uses "4 × NVIDIA L20 GPUs with LoRA" and inference runs "on a single RTX 4090" (§IV-A) — not stated whether Dexora's own inference uses the same RTX 4090.

## Learning block
- paradigm: imitation learning via a diffusion-transformer policy, with an offline learned discriminator that reweights training clips by estimated demonstration quality ("data-quality-aware" training, §III-C-D). Not RL, not MPC.
- algorithm / backbone: "We employ a decoder-only Transformer as the diffusion model for the policy ... Natural language and multi-view image inputs are encoded into conditional tokens via the T5 [41] and SigLip [42] encoders, respectively, and alternately injected into the transformer blocks. The model predicts the action noise θ̂ ... We use the standard DDPM for sampling during training and employ DPMSolver++ for acceleration during action generation" (§III-C, "Diffusion Transformer"). There is no single monolithic VLM backbone (no PaliGemma/Llama-style stack); language and vision are separate pretrained encoders (T5, SigLIP) feeding a custom decoder-only diffusion transformer. Policy size: "28 layers, a hidden size of 1024, and 16 attention heads" (§IV-A); total parameter count for the policy is not stated (only the discriminator's is: "12 layers, a hidden size of 512, and 8 attention heads, for 30M parameters," §IV-A).
- teacher-student / distillation: none described — a single policy is pretrained on sim, then post-trained on real with discriminator-derived weights (§III-D); no privileged-information channel is mentioned.
- discriminator input: "a tokenized input per clip: ξt = {st, ot, ℓ, at:t+L−1, log-π̂t}, where ot are multi-view RGB observations, ℓ is the language instruction, at:t+L−1 is an action chunk of length L, and log-π̂t is a log-π chunk score (policy-compatibility proxy) computed from the pretrained diffusion policy over that clip" (§III-C).
- domain randomization: for the synthetic corpus, "we randomize initial states and retarget the seed actions to new scenes" (§III-B, DexMimicGen recipe); no explicit list of randomized quantities/ranges (texture, lighting, mass, friction, etc.) is given — not stated.

## Reward / objective block
- No RL reward; imitation loss reweighted by a learned discriminator. Data admission uses two explicit thresholds: "S_pre = {τ : τ ∈ Low-20%(A_ep) ∧ τ ∈ Low-20%(J_ep)}, which retains about 18% of episodes" and "S_high = {τ : τ ∈ S_pre ∧ Success(τ)=1 ∧ CollisionFree(τ)=1}, yielding roughly 15% high-quality demonstrations" (§III-C, "Data Quality Criteria").
- Discriminator objective: "we optimize a positive–unlabeled objective ... where η = 0.5. We apply clip scores to d ∈ [0.1, 0.9] for stability ... Following the DWBC mapping from [43], we convert calibrated scores to weights w_i" (§III-D).
- Policy loss: "the policy is trained with weighted diffusion-transformer loss that down-weights low-quality clips" (Abstract); "we post-train πθ on the real dataset ... using the precomputed weights. For diffusion training, [loss] with a short weight warm-up" (§III-D).
- CAVEAT: the actual loss/objective equations (the RMS acceleration/jerk formulas, the positive-unlabeled loss, and the weighted diffusion loss) are typeset as images/math in the source PDF and were dropped entirely by the markdown conversion (pymupdf4llm) — only the surrounding prose survived in papers/md/dexora_2026.md, so the equations themselves cannot be quoted verbatim here.
- key trick: episode-level (not chunk-level) quality scoring, because "stationary chunks can trivially exhibit low acceleration/jerk yet be uninformative" (§III-C).

## Contact / penetration handling
Not addressed as a training objective — this is imitation learning, no penetration/contact reward or penalty term appears anywhere in the text. Collision-freeness is used only as a binary data-admission gate: a clip enters the high-quality positive set only if "CollisionFree(τ) = 1" alongside task success during open-loop replay (§III-C). No MuJoCo contact-solver settings (margin, softness, friction cone) are given for the digital twin — not stated.

## Evaluation block
- success criterion: task-specific human/experimenter-judged success (no metric threshold in metres/radians/seconds is defined in the text); reported as "the success rate over 20 rollouts per task" (§IV-A, "Protocol"), matching Table I's caption "RESULTS ARE SUCCESS RATES (%) OVER 20 TRIALS" and Fig. 8's "success rate (%) over 20 rollouts."
- basic tasks (Table I, 12 tasks / 3 families — Pick-and-Place, Assemble/Disassemble, Articulated Object): Dexora avg. 89.6% vs. GR00T N1 82.1%, π0 50.4%, DP 34.2%; Dexora reaches "≥90% on 7/12 tasks." "_Dexora_ is evaluated zero-shot" here (§IV-B).
- dexterous tasks (Table II, 6 tasks — Use pen, Fetch book, Cut leek, Place plates, Rough dough, Twist cap): Dexora avg. 66.7% vs. GR00T N1 51.7%, π0 26.7%, DP 6.7% (also given in the Abstract as "66.7% vs. 51.7%"). Text states "All models are trained/fine-tuned on 100 demonstrations" for this suite (§IV-B) — as written this does not clearly exclude Dexora from fine-tuning here, unlike the explicit "zero-shot" statement for the basic suite; flagged as ambiguous rather than resolved.
- ablations: Table III (discriminator on/off) — Corn→plate S.R. 85→95%, Acc. 0.034→0.020, Jerk 0.043→0.032; Lift basket S.R. 55→80%, Acc. 0.041→0.023, Jerk 0.052→0.036. Fig. 10 (Sim Only / Sim+50%Real / Sim+All Real) — Use pen 0→35→65%, Cut leek 10→60→85%.
- baselines: Diffusion Policy (DP) [44], π0 [3] (flow-matching action head, adapted with "a 2-layer MLP projector" to the 36-D command), GR00T N1 [7] ("an open VLA (VLM + DiT) designed for humanoid control," noted in §IV-B as using "a 6-DoF hand"). All three are re-run by the authors on Dexora's own hardware via action-space adaptation (padding / MLP projection), not quoted from their original papers. Fine-tuning: "4 × NVIDIA L20 GPUs with LoRA"; inference "on a single RTX 4090"; each trained on "100 demonstrations ... for 50K steps" (§IV-A).
- real robot: yes, throughout Tables I-III and Figs. 8-10; the "100K simulated trajectories" corpus is training data only, not part of any reported success numbers.
- cross-embodiment generalization (separate from the headline benchmark, §IV-C, numbers given only as figure illustrations, not tabulated): EC-1 single-arm gripper — "Franka Emika Panda (6-DoF + 1-DoF gripper)"; EC-2 dual-arm grippers — "Cobot Magic ALOHA (2 × (6-DoF arm + 1-DoF gripper))"; EC-3 single-arm single-hand — "Unitree G1 7-DoF arm + Inspire Hand 6-DoF." Each fine-tuned on 100 demonstrations, other settings held identical.
- dexterous-hand-evaluated: yes. "As shown in Fig. 3 (a), _Dexora_ integrates two 6-DoF AIRBOT arms with a pair of XHAND dexterous hands, each offering 12 fully actuated joints."

## Limitations stated by the authors
No dedicated "Limitations" section. The worst headline dexterous result (Twist cap, 25% success, Table II) is attributed to hardware: "the absence of tactile feedback and relatively low-friction rigid fingertip pads leads to slip" (§IV-B). The Conclusion frames two open gaps as "promising directions": "(i) contact-aware control via tactile sensing to close the loop on tasks like cap twisting; (ii) long-horizon reasoning and hierarchical VLA planning that combines memory, subgoal decomposition, and language-guided tool use" (§V).

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "Dexora, the first open-source VLA system that natively targets dual-arm, dual-hand high-DoF manipulation" (Abstract).
- "Dexora outperforms competitive VLA baselines on both basic and dexterous benchmarks (e.g., average dexterous success 66.7% vs. 51.7%)" (Abstract).
- "compressing a 12-DoF hand policy to a 1-DoF gripper is simpler than lifting a gripper policy to dexterous hands" (§IV-C).
- "GR00T N1 is the strongest baseline but uses a 6-DoF hand; it struggles on in-hand skills such as Use pen and fails on Twist cap, which require thumb-index synergies and lateral finger swing" (§IV-B).
