# digit_2020 — DIGIT: A Novel Design for a Low-Cost Compact High-Resolution Tactile Sensor with Application to In-Hand Manipulation (Lambeta, Chou, Tian, Yang et al.; IEEE RA-L 2020)

sources: papers/md/digit_2020.md [dbcae130] ; code/md/digit_2020.md [de292229, github.com/facebookresearch/digit-design]

## One-line contribution
DIGIT is a compact, low-cost vision-based tactile fingertip sensor (a redesigned GelSight-style sensor) built for multi-finger hands, whose durability and cost improvements are demonstrated by training a model-predictive controller from raw tactile video to manipulate a glass marble between two Allegro-hand fingers.

## Sensor record
- sensing principle: vision-based / optical tactile sensing — a soft, deformable elastomer gel deforms under contact, and an internal camera images the deformation of the gel's surface, from which forces/contact geometry are inferred (Sec. III). DIGIT is explicitly a redesign in the GelSight lineage (Sec. II, "DIGIT improves over existing GelSight sensors"), retaining the modular elastomer approach but changing the gel manufacturing process for durability: "we construct the elastomer in three stages. A silicone-based white pigment is added to the mold... The base layer silicone is then applied to the finger-like shape mold... glued onto an acrylic window" (Sec. III-C). The elastomer is modular/swappable: reflective (surface/texture), reflective-with-markers (optical flow), or transparent-with-markers (FingerVision/TacTip-style) elastomers can be press-fit into the same housing (Sec. III-A, Fig. 4).
- output signal and resolution: Table I (quoted, DIGIT column): Size 20x27x18 mm, Weight 20 g, Sensing field 19x16 mm, Image Resolution 640x480, Image FPS 60, Cost components $15 (manufacturing 1000 pieces). Camera: "an Omnivision OVM7692, a 60 fps color CMOS hosting a microlens array with focal length 1.15 mm and depth of field 30 cm" (Sec. III-B); illumination from "three RGB LEDs, which can provide a maximum of 4 lumens over the elastomer surface" (Sec. III-B); custom electronics fit "within an area of 7 cm², only slightly larger than a human fingertip," connected via a "SuperSpeed USB 3.0 hub" to allow multiple sensors on one USB port (Sec. III-B). Output is thus a 640x480 RGB video stream at 60 fps per sensor, from which downstream models (Sec. IV) extract compact keypoint/force information — DIGIT does not output a native force/N reading itself.
- size / form factor: "20 mm width x 27 mm height x 18 mm depth, and weighs approximately 20 g" (Sec. III-A); designed to "fit on an array of end effectors or multi-fingered robot arms" — demonstrated mounted on an Allegro hand's thumb and middle finger (Fig. 1, Sec. IV). Table I compares this against the (larger, bulkier) GelSight fingertip sensor (35x60x35 mm) and GelSlim (50x205x20 mm), both "NA" for weight in the parsed table.
- what simulator model exists: none is proposed or used in this paper — this paper's own control approach (Sec. IV) is purely real-hardware (self-supervised data collection on the physical DIGIT/Allegro setup, Sec. IV-B), with no mention of a DIGIT simulator here. (A dedicated simulator, TACTO, is developed in a later, separate paper — tacto_2020 in this corpus — which the corpus-usage grep below confirms explicitly targets DIGIT; that simulator's details belong in tacto_2020's own note, not here, since this paper does not describe or use it.)

## Setting
- hand(s): Allegro multi-finger hand, mounted on a Sawyer robotic arm; two DIGIT sensors mounted on the thumb and middle finger for the demonstration task (Sec. IV-A, Fig. 1).
- simulator / physics: none — all results are on real hardware (self-supervised data collection + real-robot MPC evaluation).
- observation: raw DIGIT camera images (per finger) plus proprioceptive joint angular positions of 8 finger servos (denoted j); compact learned "keypoint" state s = [k_l, k_r, j], 14-dimensional total, derived from the two DIGIT images via a trained autoencoder (Sec. IV-C).
- action space: 8-D angular displacement commands to the four joint servos on each of the two fingers (Sec. IV-B).
- objects / data: glass marbles, picked up from a spring-loaded platform; 4800 self-supervised trials collected (950 held out for validation), each trial ~10 s of randomized finger motion after marble pickup, with an automatic reset mechanism dropping the marble back onto the platform for the next trial (Sec. IV-B).

## Method
- paradigm: self-supervised representation learning (keypoint autoencoder) + learned forward-dynamics model + model-predictive control (MPC via cross-entropy method, CEM), following and extending "tactile-MPC" (Tian et al., cited as [17]) to two simultaneous tactile sensors and an 8-DoF action space (Sec. IV intro, IV-C, IV-D).
- reward or loss: autoencoder trained "self-supervisedly with L2 image reconstruction error, together with auxiliary losses that encourage sparse, non-redundant keypoints" (Sec. IV-C). MPC cost: "the sum of Euclidean distance between the current position and the target position in (x, y, i) coordinates" summed over the planned action sequence (Sec. IV-D).
- key trick(s): "Struct-NN" keypoint-based state representation (a tiny ResNet-18-backboned autoencoder outputting K=8 keypoints [x,y,intensity], of which only 1 keypoint per finger proved active/informative) compresses each 64x64 DIGIT image to a compact code, making CEM-based MPC computationally tractable (1.4 s/MPC step vs. 69 s for a CDNA-based image-space baseline, Table III, Sec. IV-C/D).

## Evaluation
- metrics (exact definitions): video-prediction quality — per-pixel RMSE on [0,1]-normalized images, evaluated on the BAIR robot-pushing benchmark and on DIGIT marble videos (Sec. V-A, Table III). Elastomer robustness — "Degradation... measured as percentage of increase in transmittance" after 5/10/15 linear-abrasion passes with a calibrated 1.7 N weight and H-18 Calibrade abrasive plunger tip (Sec. III-D, Table II). Marble-manipulation control — Euclidean distance (median and 68th percentile) in keypoint (pixel) space to a randomly sampled goal at least 16 px away, and percentage of marbles dropped, over 50 repeated trials, plotted vs. number of actions taken (Sec. IV-D/V-B, Fig. 8).
- headline numbers: Table II abrasion — DIGIT gel degrades 0%/0.3%/0.3% at 5/10/15 passes vs. 276%/482%/805% for the Yuan et al. gel and 475%/662%/918% for the GelSight Inc. gel. Table III — Struct-NN: 4.3 ms forward-backward pass, 1.6 ms forward pass, 1.4 s per MPC step, 1.2 M parameters, RMSE 0.06023 (BAIR)/0.00657 (Marble); CDNA: 6.8 ms/2.3 ms/69 s/4 M params/RMSE 0.01082 (BAIR)/0.00028 (Marble) — i.e. CDNA has lower RMSE but Struct-NN is ~50x faster per MPC step, making only Struct-NN practical for closed-loop control. Marble control: learned-MPC distance-to-goal decreases over the trajectory while a hand-tuned proportional (P) controller's distance increases on average (Fig. 8, top); "about 25% of trials result in the marble dropping before it can be fully manipulated to the goal" (Sec. V-B, Fig. 8 bottom).
- baselines beaten: CDNA (image-space video-predictive control, from Tian et al.'s tactile-MPC) on speed, not on raw RMSE; a hand-tuned linear proportional controller in keypoint space, which "diverge[s] on average" while the learned-dynamics MPC converges toward the goal (Sec. V-B, Fig. 8).
- real robot? yes — all manipulation results are on the physical Allegro hand + Sawyer arm setup; no sim-only results reported for the manipulation task.

## Limitations stated by the authors
- "about 25% of trials result in the marble dropping before it can be fully manipulated to the goal... we believe that this is partly due to planning inaccuracies and actuation noise in the hand joints" (Sec. V-B).
- Struct-NN has higher image-reconstruction RMSE than CDNA on both BAIR pushing and DIGIT marble videos, traded off for a ~50x speedup needed to make CEM-based MPC practical (Sec. V-A, Table III).
- "Future work should aim at further miniaturizing the form factor of the sensor, and designing sensors with curved, omni-directional sensing fields" (Sec. VI, Conclusion).
