# isaacgym_2021 — Isaac Gym: High Performance GPU-Based Physics Simulation For Robot Learning (Makoviychuk et al., NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks 2021; arXiv 2108.10470)

sources: papers/md/isaacgym_2021.md [sha256 a2aa9954] ; code/md/isaacgym_2021.md [commit aeed2986] (code = `isaac-sim/IsaacGymEnvs`)

## One-line contribution
A fully GPU-resident RL-simulation pipeline (PhysX on GPU, tensor-based state/control API, PyTorch interop via CUDA with zero CPU round-trips) that trains locomotion and dexterous in-hand manipulation policies (Shadow Hand, Allegro Hand, TriFinger) orders of magnitude faster than CPU-cluster baselines, e.g. reproducing OpenAI's Shadow Hand cube-reorientation result in under 1 hour on a single A100 vs. ~30 hours on a 6144-CPU-core + 8-V100 cluster (Sec. 6.4.1).

## Setting
- hand(s): **Shadow Dexterous Hand** (cube-reorientation, "Shadow Hand Standard" and "Shadow Hand OpenAI" variants; Sec. 6.4.1) and **Allegro Hand** ("we reuse system from the Shadow Hand to the Allegro hand with minimal changes ... it has 4 fingers instead of 5 and fewer degrees of freedom as a result," Sec. 6.4, 6.4.3). Table 4 gives action dims: Shadow Hand Standard/OpenAI = 20 action dims; Allegro Hand = 16 action dims. Table 13 gives Shadow Hand DoF breakdown: 24 finger-joint positions/velocities/forces (implying 24 actuated/measured DoFs), 5 fingertips each with force/torque sensing. **TriFinger** [Wuthrich et al.] — "the manipulator has 3 fingers each with three degrees of freedom" (Sec. 6.4.2), i.e. 9 DoF total, action dim 9 (Table 4), doing 6-DoF cube reposing (translation+rotation) rather than in-hand reorientation only.
- arm: none for the hand tasks (hands are mounted/fixed bases); Franka Panda arm used separately for Franka Cube Stacking (Sec. 6.3, Operational Space Control, 7 action dims). single/bimanual: single-hand for all three dexterous tasks.
- simulator / physics: **NVIDIA PhysX**, GPU-resident. "Robots are simulated using PhysX reduced coordinate articulations. Any individual rigid bodies may be simulated using either maximal coordinate rigid bodies or single-link reduced coordinate articulations" (Sec. 3). A **Tensor API** exposes all physics state (actor root state, DOF state, rigid body state, DOF/rigid-body forces, net contact forces, Jacobian and generalized mass matrices — Table 1) and control (DOF actuation forces, DOF position/velocity targets, rigid body forces/torques — Table 2) as PyTorch-wrapped GPU buffers with "CUDA interoperability... without ever going through the host" (Sec. 2.3.2). Tendons (used for the Shadow Hand) are simulated via PhysX Fixed Tendon mechanics (Sec. 3, Appendix A.1: fixed tendons = spring+damping force proportional to deviation from rest length, propagated through a tendon-joint tree; spatial tendons = line-of-sight distance constraints between attachments).
- observation: Shadow Hand Standard (Table 13, total **211** observations): finger-joint position/velocity/force (24 each), cube pose (translation 3, quaternion 4, linear vel 3, angular vel 3), cube-rotation-relative-to-goal quaternion (4), goal pose (translation 3, quaternion 4), 5x fingertip (linear vel 3, angular vel 3, force 3, torque 3), previous action (20). Shadow Hand OpenAI (asymmetric; Table 14, policy obs total **42**): 5x finger-joint position (3 — table formatting suggests this may mean 15, or 3 per finger x5=15; flagged below), cube pose translation (3), cube-rotation-relative-to-goal quaternion (4), previous action (20); critic receives the same observations as Shadow Hand Standard (Table 13, 211-dim) plus the policy's own. TriFinger actor observations (Table 15, total **41**): finger-joint position/velocity (9+9), cube pose (translation 3, quaternion 4), goal pose (translation 3, quaternion 4), previous action (9). TriFinger critic observations (Table 16, total **113**): actor observations (41) + cube linear/angular velocity (3+3) + 3x fingertip (position 3, quaternion 4, linear velocity 3, torque 3) + finger joint torque (9).
  - AMBIGUITY FLAG: Table 14's "5x Finger joints position: 3" row is ambiguous in the flattened markdown — it may mean 3 DoF x 5 fingers = 15, not a literal "3." Verify against the original PDF table before quoting the exact Shadow Hand OpenAI policy observation dimension breakdown.
- action space: Table 4 (exact, verbatim): Ant — Joint Torques, 8 dims; Humanoid — Joint Torques, 21 dims; Ingenuity — Rigid Body Forces, 6 dims; ANYmal — Joint Position Targets, 12 dims; Franka Cube Stacking — Operational Space Control, 7 dims; **Shadow Hand Standard — Joint Position Targets, 20 dims**; **Shadow Hand OpenAI — Joint Position Targets, 20 dims**; **Allegro Hand — Joint Position Targets, 16 dims**; **TriFinger — Joint Torques, 9 dims**.
- objects / data: a single rigid cube per hand-manipulation task (Shadow Hand, Allegro Hand: cube reorientation to a target quaternion; TriFinger: cube repositioning to a target 6-DoF pose).

## Physics
- **Contact model**: rigid-body contacts via PhysX; contact geometry can be primitive shapes or meshes loaded from URDF/MJCF (Sec. 2.2); "Net contact forces" (per-rigid-body, shape (NB,3)) are exposed via the Tensor API (Table 1) as a Get-only tensor. No explicit statement of convex-vs-mesh contact generation or per-pair contact-point count is given in this paper (contrast with orbit_2023/isaac_sim_2026's SDF-collision descriptions of later PhysX versions).
- **Solver**: **Temporal Gauss-Seidel (TGS)** [cited as ref. 18], not classic PGS/LCP: "We use the Temporal Gauss Seidel (TGS) solver to compute the future states of objects in our physics simulation. The TGS solver uses the observation that sub-stepping a simulation with a single gauss-seidel solver iteration yields significantly faster convergence than running larger steps with more solver iterations. It folds this process efficiently into the iteration process, calculating the velocity at the end of each iteration and accumulating these velocities (scaled by dt/N, where N is the number of iterations) into a per-body accumulated delta buffer. This delta buffer is projected onto the constraint Jacobians and added to the bias terms in the constraints... it achieves the same effect on convergence as having sub-stepped the simulation without the computational expense" (Sec. 3). Table 3 exposes two separate iteration-count knobs to the user: **Position iterations** ("Biased (velocity + positional error correcting) solver iterations") and **Velocity iterations** ("Unbiased (velocity error only correcting) solver iterations") — no default numeric values for these are given in the main text (they are user-tunable parameters, not fixed constants). "With positional joint constraints, an additional rotational term is calculated for joint anchors to improve handling of non-linear motion... This term is not necessary (and in fact undesirable) to add to contacts" (Sec. 3).
- **Differentiable contacts**: not claimed for PhysX's rigid-body/contact solver. The only explicit differentiability claim in the paper is for a *controller*, not the contact solver: "Our OSC implementation is fully differentiable in Isaac Gym" (Sec. 6.3, Franka Cube Stacking) — this refers to the Operational Space Control law, not to gradients through contact events.
- **Default timestep / substeps**: Table 4 gives exact per-environment simulation and control timesteps (verbatim): Ant — sim dt 1/120 s, control dt 1/60 s; Humanoid — sim dt 1/120 s, control dt 1/60 s; Ingenuity — sim dt 1/200 s, control dt 1/100 s; ANYmal — sim dt 1/200 s, control dt 1/50 s; Franka Cube Stacking — sim dt 1/60 s, control dt 1/60 s; **Shadow Hand Standard — sim dt 1/120 s, control dt 1/60 s**; **Shadow Hand OpenAI — sim dt 1/120 s, control dt 1/20 s**; **Allegro Hand — sim dt 1/120 s, control dt 1/20 s**; **TriFinger — sim dt 1/200 s, control dt 1/50 s**. (Control-dt / sim-dt ratio gives implicit substep counts, e.g. Shadow Hand OpenAI = 6 physics substeps per control step, Ant/Humanoid/Standard-Shadow-Hand = 2 substeps, ANYmal = 4 substeps, TriFinger = 4 substeps.)
- **Friction model**: Table 3 (parameters exposed to the user) lists "**Static/dynamic friction**: Static and dynamic friction coefficients," "**Friction offset threshold**: Distance at which friction anchors are discarded (static friction depends on friction anchor caching)," "**Solver offset slop**: An epsilon value used to correct for round-off errors in contact gen. Corrects small skew effects with rolling spheres or capsules," and "**Friction correlation distance**: Distance at which contacts are merged into a single friction constraint." No default numeric coefficient values are given in the main text; friction is one of the randomized physics properties for Shadow Hand domain randomization ("masses, inertia tensors, friction, restitution, joint limits, stiffness and damping," Sec. 6.4.1).
- **Penetration handling / exposure**: **the word "penetration" does not appear anywhere in this paper.** Table 3 exposes "**Rest offset**: Distance at which shapes are held separated. Default is 0 but can be increased to hold objects at [a] gap. Useful for thin objects" and "**Max bias coefficient**: Limits the magnitude of position error bias" — these are the closest analogues to penetration-resolution controls (rest offset creates an artificial separation gap; the bias coefficient bounds how aggressively positional error, which includes penetration, is corrected per TGS iteration), but neither is framed by the paper as a "penetration" metric, and no penetration-depth quantity is exposed via the Tensor API (Table 1's "Net contact forces" reports force, not depth).
- **GPU or CPU**: **GPU-resident end-to-end** — "Isaac Gym eliminates those [CPU-round-trip] inefficiencies by keeping all of the computations on the GPU. Stepping physics, computing observations and rewards, and applying actions are performed on the GPU without ever copying large quantities of data between devices" (Sec. 2.1.2). Two PhysX features cited as enabling this: GPU simulation without per-step CPU fetch, and "a new direct GPU API... to access the current state, submit state changes, and apply control inputs in GPU buffers" (Sec. 2.1.2). Also supports pure-CPU multi-threaded simulation as a fallback strategy (Sec. 2.1.1), and the tensor API "can be used to interact with the running simulation on either CPU or GPU" by toggling a device flag (Sec. 2.2, 2.3.1). Main experiments: single NVIDIA A100 GPU + single 3.7 GHz Intel i7-8700K CPU (Sec. 4, "Key Experimental Details"); ANYmal rough-terrain sim-to-real trained on an RTX A6000 (Sec. 6.1.4).
- **Throughput** (exact figures, A100 GPU unless noted):
  - Ant: "the number of parallel environment steps per second... can go as high as **700K**" (Sec. 5.1, Fig. 5b); "the average simulation performance achieved during training is **540K** environment steps per second" at 4096 agents (Sec. 6.1.1). Best training config: 8192 envs, horizon 16 — reward 7000 reached in ~100 s (down from ~1000 s at 256 envs); "Ant reaches performant locomotion at 3000 reward in just 20 seconds on a single GPU."
  - Humanoid: "reduced from 700K for Ant to **200K** for Humanoid" (Sec. 5.2); best configs 4096 envs/horizon 32, or 8192/16384 envs with horizon 64/32 (Figs. 6-7); reward 7000 reached in ~10^3 s at 4096 envs (down from ~10^4 s at 256 envs); "performant locomotion starts happening at around a reward of 5000 at a training time of just 4 minutes" (4096 agents, Sec. 6.1.2 confirms "less than 4 minutes," "4x faster than our previous results [prior GPU-physics work]").
  - **Shadow Hand**: "The maximum effective frame-rate of **150K** number of parallel environment steps per second was achieved with 16384 agents" (Sec. 5.3, Fig. 8b). Training time reduced from ~5x10^4 s (~14 hours, 256 envs) to ~3x10^3 s (~1 hour, 16384 envs) to reach reward 3000/10 consecutive successes (Sec. 5.3): "the environment reaches performant dexterity of 10 consecutive successes at reward of 3000 in just 5 minutes." Best configs: 8192 envs (horizon 16) or 16384 envs (horizon 8).
  - Ingenuity: reward 5000 in "just under 30 seconds," 4096 agents (Sec. 6.1.3). ANYmal: target-following converges "in under 2 minutes," 4096 agents (Sec. 6.1.4); rough-terrain ANYmal sim-to-real "train the full task on NVIDIA RTX A6000 and transfer to the real robot in under 20 minutes" (Sec. 6.1.4).
  - Humanoid AMP character animation: "approximately 39 million samples, requiring approximately **6 minutes** with 4096 environments," vs. "about **1 day** (30 hours) on 16 CPU cores... in PyBullet" — "**300x or 2.48 orders of magnitude improvement**" (Sec. 6.2).
  - Franka Cube Stacking: 16384 agents, convergence "in under 25 minutes" (Sec. 6.3).
  - **Shadow Hand OpenAI reproduction (headline cross-system comparison, Sec. 6.4.1)**: "we achieved more than 20 consecutive successes in less than 1 hour [feed-forward, single A100]. In contrast, for the same performance it takes **30 hours** on the OpenAI setup consisting of CPU based simulation and training setup running MuJoCo simulator on a cluster of **384 16-core CPUs with 6144 CPU cores in total** and using **8 NVIDIA V100 GPUs** for training." With LSTM: "37 consecutive successes in just under 6 hours while OpenAI et al. achieve same performance in ~17 hours"; comparing best-of-5-seeds: "37 consecutive successes with LSTM experiments can be achieved in just **2.5 hours**." Shadow Hand Standard (no randomization, symmetric actor-critic, feed-forward): "achieves 20 consecutive successes in under 35 minutes."
  - TriFinger: trained to a reward plateau over ~4194 million steps (Fig. 16 axis); real-world sim-to-real "mean success rate in the real world is **55%**" (Sec. 6.4.2), citing [29] for detailed analysis.
- **Robot hands shipped as assets**: **Shadow Dexterous Hand** and **Allegro Hand** (cube reorientation), plus the **TriFinger** robot (not a hand but a 3-fingered 9-DoF manipulator, 6-DoF cube reposing) — these are IsaacGymEnvs' three "Robotic Hands" environments (Sec. 4, Fig. 13).

## Evaluation
- metrics: reward curves (Figs. 5-9, 12, 14) and, for the three hand/finger environments, **consecutive successes per episode** (Fig. 15) and, for TriFinger, **success rate (%)** (Fig. 16b). Success tolerance for Shadow Hand orientation-matching: "OpenAI used a success tolerance of 0.4 rad; we use both 0.4 rad and a tighter tolerance of 0.1 rad. We focus on results with 0.4 rad in this section" (Sec. 6.4.1, footnote to OpenAI et al. Sec. C.1 "Goals").
- headline numbers: see Throughput above. Key dexterous-hand result: Shadow Hand OpenAI-FF reaches >20 consecutive successes in <1 hour (single A100) vs. 30 hours (OpenAI's 6144-CPU-core + 8-V100 cluster) — a claimed **~30x** wall-clock speedup for matched performance; LSTM variant reaches 37 consecutive successes in 2.5-6 hours vs. OpenAI's ~17 hours. TriFinger real-world mean success rate: **55%**.
- baselines beaten: OpenAI et al.'s Shadow Hand dexterity work (wall-clock training time, same success-tolerance/consecutive-success metric, Sec. 6.4.1); Peng et al.'s AMP/PyBullet implementation (300x training-time speedup, Sec. 6.2); the paper's own prior GPU-physics work (4x faster Humanoid training, Sec. 6.1.2).
- real robot? Yes — **TriFinger only**, among the three hand/finger environments: "we also transfer results from simulation to the real world and note that our mean success rate in the real world is 55%... These results are obtained on the real TriFinger robot hosted by [28, 30]... the robot is situated in a different location and therefore the sim-to-real transfer was done remotely" (Sec. 6.4.2, Fig. 17). Shadow Hand and Allegro Hand experiments in this paper are simulation-only: "unlike [OpenAI], we do not attempt any sim-to-real transfer for the Shadow Hand experiment" (Sec. 6.4.1). ANYmal rough-terrain locomotion is also validated on a real robot (Sec. 6.1.4), unrelated to hands.

## Limitations stated by the authors
- Diminishing/negative returns from over-parallelizing at fixed horizon length: for Ant, "we do not observe gains when increasing the number of environments from 8192 to 16384 due to reduced horizon length" (Sec. 5.1); for Humanoid, "Going beyond 4096 environments for this set up resulted in no further gains and in fact led to both increase in training time and sub-optimal gaits. We attribute this to the complexity of the environment that makes it challenging to learn walking at such small horizon lengths" (Sec. 5.2).
- Allegro Hand underperforms Shadow Hand on consecutive successes despite fewer DoF, attributed to a fixed wrist and longer fingers: "despite having fewer degrees of freedom this hand does not achieve as high consecutive successes as Shadow hand. This is because the wrist is fixed and fingers are slightly longer. We observed in Shadow hand experiment that having a movable wrist allows for better manipulation when reorienting the cube" (Sec. 6.4.3).
- Explicit reproduction caveats vs. OpenAI's setup (Sec. 6.4.1, "a few important differences"): continuous vs. discrete control space; 5-seed average vs. OpenAI's 1 seed; no action delay or motor backlash randomization; a different LSTM/MLP layer ordering ("we found our setting performs better with Isaac Gym"); "we use a somewhat different reward function to OpenAI."
- GPU direct-access tensor API constraint (implicitly, from Sec. 2.2): "The setup code runs on the CPU to allow flexibility in per-instance setup, but once the simulation starts" state access moves to the tensor API — i.e., per-instance structural customization is a CPU-only, pre-simulation-start operation.
- The physics-engine "Physics of tendons" appendix (A.1) is presented as a description of the mechanism used, not as a validated-accurate model; no accuracy claim is made for the tendon simulation beyond "we tested the dynamics of tendons using the Shadow Hand simulation environment" (Sec. 3) — no quantitative validation against real tendon behavior is reported.

## Notes for the survey (which sections this feeds; contradictions with other notes)
- Physics-engine section: this is the single richest source in the batch for concrete PhysX-GPU solver detail — TGS (not PGS/LCP/Newton), the position-iterations/velocity-iterations knob pair (Table 3), the rest-offset/friction-anchor/solver-offset-slop parameters, and exact per-task sim-dt/control-dt/substep-count table (Table 4). Use this note as the primary citation for "what does PhysX's contact solver actually do" questions in the survey, ahead of isaac_sim_2026 (which states nothing about the solver) and orbit_2023 (which only names SDF collision, not the solver algorithm).
- **No "penetration" anywhere in the text** — a notable silence for a paper whose whole premise is realistic contact-rich manipulation. The survey's contact-fidelity comparison should flag Isaac Gym/PhysX-TGS as not reporting or exposing a penetration-depth metric, in contrast to MuJoCo's explicit soft-penetration-as-state-variable framing (mujoco_convex_contact_2014).
- Dexterous-hand throughput and success numbers here (Shadow Hand 150K env-steps/sec at 16384 envs; OpenAI-reproduction wall-clock comparison; TriFinger 55% real-world) are the benchmark reference point this entire survey batch's other GPU-simulator papers (orbit_2023, mujoco_playground_2025, maniskill2_2023/3) implicitly or explicitly compare themselves against — cross-reference directly when building the survey's dexterous-hand throughput/success table.
- Reward function for Shadow Hand/Allegro (quoted verbatim from code in A.2.3) is the clearest, most complete reward-term citation with exact weights in this whole batch: `reward = goal_dist*(-10.0) + rot_rew*1.0 + action_penalty*(-2e-4)`, `rot_rew = 1/(|rot_dist| + 0.1)`, plus `+250` success bonus when `|rot_dist| <= success_tolerance` and a fall penalty (0.0 in the quoted config) when `goal_dist >= 0.24` — directly reusable for the survey's reward-term comparison table, and useful as a cross-check against mujoco_playground_2025's LEAP-hand `reward.tolerance`-based reorientation reward, which is structurally different (soft-threshold tolerance function vs. this paper's inverse-distance-plus-bonus form).
- TriFinger is the only hand/finger environment in this paper with a real-robot number (55% success) — flag this prominently since it is the strongest "count what you can rebuild" data point in the note for a dexterous/multi-fingered system, whereas Shadow Hand and Allegro Hand results here are simulation-only (explicitly disclaimed, Sec. 6.4.1).
