# humanplus_2024 — HumanPlus: Humanoid Shadowing and Imitation from Humans (Fu et al., CoRL 2024)

sources: papers/md/humanplus_2024.md [94c5fe16 / arxiv 2406.10454] ; code/md/humanplus_2024.md [ff714890]

## One-line contribution
A full-stack humanoid system: an RL-trained, pose-conditioned "Humanoid Shadowing Transformer" (HST) lets one human operator with a single RGB camera whole-body-teleoperate ("shadow") a 33-DoF humanoid to collect data, then a decoder-only "Humanoid Imitation Transformer" (HIT) does vision-based BC from that data (§1 Abstract, §5, §6).

## Setting
- hand(s): two Inspire-Robots RH56DFX hands, 6 DoF each (1 DoF for index/middle/ring/little, 2 DoF for thumb), multi-fingered; connected via a 1-DoF Dynamixel wrist each (§3, §4 "Retargeting"). Arm: 4-DoF per arm; legs 5-DoF each; waist 1-DoF; total 33 DoF ("two 6-DoF hands, two 1-DoF wrists, and a 19-DoF body (two 4-DoF arms, two 5-DoF legs, and a 1-DoF waist)", §3). Bimanual: yes, on a full humanoid (Unitree H1 base).
- simulator / physics: Isaac Gym-based `legged_gym`/`rsl_rl` stack (code: `HST/legged_gym`, PPO via `rsl_rl`); paper does not state env count or timestep explicitly in the parsed text; low-level policy runs at 50 Hz with context length 8, torques sent to a 1000 Hz PD controller (§5).
- observation: HST — proprioception (root roll/pitch/base angular velocities, joint positions, joint velocities, last action) + retargeted humanoid target pose (target forward/lateral velocity, target roll/pitch, target yaw velocity, target joint angles) (§5); code's `compute_observations` in `h1.py` confirms: `base_orn_rp, base_ang_vel, commands[:3], (dof_pos-default)*scale, dof_vel*scale, actions, delayed_obs_target_jt` concatenated, plus optional height measurements and noise. HIT — binocular egocentric RGB (two head-mounted Razer Kiyo Pro webcams) + proprioception (§6).
- action space: HST outputs 19-dim joint position setpoints for the body, converted to torques by the 1000 Hz PD controller; target hand joint angles are passed directly to the PD controller (§5). HIT predicts a chunk of 50 target poses (body+hand) at 25 Hz onboard, sent asynchronously to HST (§6).
- objects / data: AMASS motion dataset (40 hours, >11,000 sequences, SMPL-X parameterized) for training HST in sim (§4). Real-world imitation data collected via shadowing: up to 40 demonstrations per task across 6 tasks (§7, Table 5: Fold Clothes 40, Rearrange Objects 30, Type "AI" 30, Two-Robot Greeting 30, Warehouse 25, Wear a Shoe and Walk 40).

## Method
- paradigm: RL (low-level shadowing policy) + IL/BC (skill policy from shadowed demos); hybrid full-stack system.
- reward or loss:
  - HST reward (Table 1, quoted): target xy velocities `exp(-|[vx,vy]-[vx^tg,vy^tg]|)`; target yaw velocity `exp(-|vyaw-vyaw^tg|)`; target joint positions `-|q-q^tg|^2_2`; target roll & pitch `-|[r,p]-[r^tg,p^tg]|^2_2`; energy `-|τ q̇|^2_2`; feet contact `c==c^tg`; feet slipping `-|v_feet·1[F_feet>1]|`; alive `1`. Trained via PPO maximizing `E[Σ_{t=0}^{T-1} γ^t r_t]` (§5).
  - Code (`h1.py`) implements a superset of these plus standard legged-gym terms not mentioned in the paper text: `_reward_lin_vel_z`, `_reward_ang_vel_xy`, `_reward_orientation`, `_reward_base_height`, `_reward_torques`, `_reward_dof_vel`, `_reward_dof_acc`, `_reward_action_rate`, `_reward_collision` (`torch.sum(1.*(torch.norm(contact_forces[penalized_contact_indices])>0.1))`), `_reward_termination`, `_reward_dof_pos_limits`, `_reward_dof_vel_limits`, `_reward_torque_limits`, `_reward_tracking_lin_vel`/`_reward_tracking_ang_vel` (exp of squared error / `tracking_sigma`), `_reward_feet_air_time`, `_reward_stumble`, `_reward_stand_still`, `_reward_feet_contact_forces`, and `_reward_target_jt` = `exp(-4 * mean(|dof_pos-target_jt|))`. The paper's Table 1 is a compressed subset/relabeling of this larger legged_gym reward library; mismatch noted.
  - HIT loss: paper states an "L2 feature loss" on predicted future image-feature tokens added to the pose-chunk prediction loss (blending action prediction and forward dynamics prediction), described as regularizing image feature spaces (§6). The code (`HIT/policy.py`) only exposes the `HITPolicy.__call__(qpos, image, actions, is_pad)` signature in the parsed markdown; the loss body itself was not captured in code/md, so the exact weighting of the L2 feature term is "not stated" beyond the paper's qualitative description.
- key trick(s): decoder-only HIT (ACT's encoder-decoder architecture removed) predicting a 50-step pose chunk plus future image-feature tokens for forward-dynamics regularization, to stop the vision policy from overfitting to proprioception and ignoring images (§6); task-agnostic HST trained once on AMASS motion then deployed zero-shot for any shadowing task, rather than one policy per skill (§1).

## Contact / penetration handling
Not addressed as hand-object interpenetration. The only interaction-related reward/penalty found is the generic `_reward_collision` term in code, which penalizes nonzero contact force on a configurable set of "penalized" (non-foot) body links — a whole-body self/environment collision penalty, not an object-interpenetration metric. No two-hand collision term or hand-object penetration measure appears in either paper or parsed code.

## Coordination and embodiment (survey fields)
- two-hand coordination: one policy over both hands. HST outputs joint targets for the whole 19-DoF body plus hand angles are passed straight through from human hand-pose estimation (not RL-controlled); HIT predicts poses for both arms/hands jointly from shared binocular vision + proprioception in a single decoder-only transformer — no separate per-hand policy, no explicit leader/follower role assignment, no relative-frame two-hand observation described.
- torso: mobile humanoid (Unitree H1 base, legs + waist + 33 total DoF), not a fixed torso.
- hand model/DoF each side: Inspire-Robots RH56DFX, 6 DoF per hand (multi-fingered: 1 DoF each for index/middle/ring/little, 2 DoF thumb), identical on both sides.

## Evaluation
- metrics (exact definitions): per-subtask success rate = "number of successful attempts / number of total attempts" for consecutive subtasks within a task (§9); teleop user study reports average task completion time and stand-success rate over 6 participants, 3 recorded trials each after 3 unrecorded practice rounds (§8.1); robustness eval reports minimum pelvis push force causing instability and recovery time (Table 4).
- headline numbers: Table 5 imitation success rates (10 trials/task, real robot): Wear a Shoe and Walk whole-task 60% (HIT) vs 0% for Monocular/ACT/Open-loop, given 40 demos; Warehouse whole-task 90% (HIT and ACT tied); Fold Clothes whole-task 100% (HIT), 40% (Monocular), 100% (ACT); Rearrange Objects whole-task 90% (HIT) vs 70% (Monocular) vs 50% (ACT); Type "AI" whole-task 80% (HIT) vs 40% (Monocular) vs 0% (ACT); Two-Robot Greeting whole-task 90% (HIT and ACT tied). Table 3 teleop comparison: "Ours" whole task 5.20s vs ALOHA 7.15s, Meta Quest 8.87s, Kinesthetic 6.60s, all at 100% stand success except Kinesthetic's 90.5%; only "Ours" completes Rearrange Lower Objects (15.34s). Table 4 robustness: HST withstands 32/44/70/100 N (fwd/back/left/right) vs H1 Default's 24/36/40/40 N, with 1.2s recovery vs 15s.
- baselines beaten: Monocular-vision HIT, ACT (encoder-decoder, from Zhao et al. 2023), and Open-loop trajectory replay (imitation); Kinesthetic Teaching, ALOHA (puppeteering rig), Meta Quest (VR IK) (teleoperation); H1 manufacturer default controller (low-level robustness).
- real robot? Yes throughout — all numbers above are real-robot trials on the customized 33-DoF HumanPlus/H1 platform; 10 trials per imitation task (Table 5), 6 participants × 3 trials for teleop comparison (Table 3).

## Limitations stated by the authors
Fewer DoF than human anatomy (1-DoF ankles restrict agile single-leg motion; 5-DoF arms including wrist limit 6-DoF operational-space control, causing unreachable regions during shadowing); head cameras are fixed/non-active, risking hands/interactions falling out of view; retargeting mapping is fixed and omits human joints absent on the hardware, limiting the diversity of learnable motions; pose estimation fails under large occlusion, restricting operator positioning; long-horizon navigation is out of scope because it needs much larger demonstration counts and accurate real-world velocity tracking (§10).

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "our humanoid can autonomously complete tasks such as wearing a shoe to stand up and walk, unloading objects from warehouse racks, folding a sweatshirt, rearranging objects, typing, and greeting with another robot with 60-100% success rates" using up to 40 demonstrations (§1).
- "Our humanoid features 33 degrees of freedom, including two 6-DoF hands, two 1-DoF wrists, and a 19-DoF body (two 4-DoF arms, two 5-DoF legs, and a 1-DoF waist)" (§3).
- "We use PPO [74] to train our Humanoid Shadowing Transformer in simulation by maximizing discounted expected return" (§5).
