## One-line contribution
DIME teleoperates an Allegro hand from a single RGB camera (MediaPipe fingertip detection, direct 2.5D-to-3D fingertip retargeting, no depth sensing) to collect cheap demonstrations, then compares state/image-based behavior cloning against non-parametric nearest-neighbor imitation (INN/VINN) on the real robot and DAPG/PPO/BCRL RL-finetuning in MuJoCo simulation, finding nearest-neighbor methods dominate parametric BC at the ~30-demonstration data scale (Table I; Sec. IV).

## Setting
- operator interface: vision-based, single RGB camera, no glove/headset/exoskeleton — "we use a single RGB camera and the MediaPipe hand detector to extract 2.5D landmarks of an operator's hand" (Sec. III-B). Communication between hand detector and robot controller is over ROS, "hence compatible with remote teleoperation once the remote machine is registered on our ROS network" (Sec. III-C).
- robot hand controlled: Allegro Hand (4 fingers, no thumb/pinky distinction noted the way holo_dex_2022 handles it — DIME instead repurposes its own pinky-equivalent finger: "Since the Allegro Hand only has four fingers, we use the pinky finger for finer control").
- retargeting formulation (Sec. III-B, "Hand Pose Mapping", quoted): "Rather than inferring and mapping the rotational state for each joint on the human hand to the robot, we map directly from 2.5D space to 3D. Because the model does not output absolute depth estimates, we choose to ignore the depth and treat the fingertips as if they were on a plane above the palm. We then map directly from human hand fingertip locations in 2D to robot fingertip locations in 3D space, keeping the robot fingertips at a fixed height above the palm. We map the index, middle, and ring fingers along the y plane... We define a quadrilateral bound with which the human thumb can be detected and re-mapped to control the xy movement of the robot thumb." Correspondence is purely fingertip-to-fingertip in a fixed-height 2D-to-3D plane mapping (no per-joint correspondence, no cost function/optimization at all — simpler even than holo_dex_2022's joint-copy-plus-thumb-IK scheme, since DIME never estimates 3D hand pose and instead assumes a flat height plane for every fingertip). Calibration: "easy to calibrate and only requires moving the hand to a sequence of reference positions at the extremes of the finger positions... linearly interpolate to produce desired target 3D positions for intermediate locations" (Sec. III-B). Downstream of this mapping, "given desired fingertip locations in 3D space, we compute the required joint angles using a model of the robot and an inverse kinematics solver" (Sec. III-C) — this IK step is on ALL fingers (not just the thumb, unlike holo_dex_2022), converting the mapped 3D fingertip targets into Allegro joint angles. No collision handling and no explicit joint-limit handling of any kind is described for either the fingertip mapping or the IK step.
- end-to-end latency: not reported as a single number. Control-loop rates are given instead: "For every joint, we compute the torque required to compensate for gravity and the control torque from a PD controller to reach the desired joint angle. This control loop is run at 300Hz, while desired positions are streamed at 30Hz." (Sec. III-C). Demonstrations are recorded (image + object state + target fingertip locations) at 5 Hz (Sec. III-D).
- cost of the rig: not stated as a dollar figure — only "a single desktop computer" plus one RGB camera (Sec. III-D) and a ROS-based AR tracking library for object pose are mentioned; no BoM or total system cost is given.
- data rate / demonstrations per hour: not reported as an aggregate rate, but per-demonstration teleoperation time is given per task (Sec. IV-B): "each demonstration for flipping, spinning, and rotating requires 30, 120, and 150 seconds to teleoperate respectively" — i.e. roughly 120, 30, and 24 demos/hour respectively for an expert, before accounting for resets/failures. A first-attempt (untrained-operator) comparison is also given: "we asked four volunteers to collect a demonstration each for the rotating task without any prior training... on average 210 seconds were taken... only 40% longer than a trained expert" (150s).
- task(s) demonstrations were used for: 3 real + simulated dexterous manipulation tasks (Sec. IV-A): Flipping (flip a rectangular object to the palm center within 1 minute, success = lands within 2cm of palm center), Spinning (continuously spin a 3-pronged ROBEL knob, success = 120 degrees rotation in 1 minute; simulation includes knob rotation in the observation, hardware does not), Rotating (continuously rotate a cube in-plane on the palm, success = 90 degrees rotation in 1 minute). Demonstrations feed both (a) real-robot non-parametric imitation (INN/VINN) and parametric BC, and (b) simulated-hand DAPG/PPO/BCRL RL-finetuning (Sec. III-A: "Simulation learning allows for sample-complex policy gradient approaches... real-robot learning allows for sample-efficient non-parametric approaches. We do not run policy gradient-based learning on our real robot due to the sample complexity of such methods along with preventing accidental damage to the robot").

## Method (imitation-learning side, since retargeting/teleop is the survey-relevant contribution)
- 30 real-robot demonstrations collected per task (10 in simulation per task) via the above teleoperation pipeline; transitions with fingertip-position changes under 2cm are excluded from real-robot imitation data "to account for the human operator pausing intermittently while collecting demonstrations" (Sec. III-D).
- State observation (real-robot, state-based): "3D positions of all 4 fingertips and the 3D position of the object with respect to the wrist joint" (15-D, confirmed in Sec. III-F: "15 dimensional observation space containing the 3D fingertip positions for each of the 4 fingers and the 3D position of the object"). Image observation: RGB images of the Allegro Hand, encoded to 2048-D features via a BYOL encoder trained per-task on the demonstration images (Sec. III-F).
- Action space: 3D positions of the 4 desired fingertip locations, relative to the wrist (Sec. III-D) — the same space the fingertip-mapping/IK retargeting itself operates in.
- Non-parametric imitation: INN (state-based k-NN / locally weighted regression) and VINN (image-based, BYOL feature nearest-neighbor) — "the simplest method in this class of algorithms is k-nearest neighbors, which takes the current input, finds the k closest inputs in the training dataset, and averages the outputs" (Sec. III-F).
- RL-in-simulation loss (DAPG, Sec. III-E, quoted): "DAPG incorporates demonstrations into the reinforcement learning procedure... by augmenting the standard policy gradient with a weighted behavior cloning gradient term, gaug = Σ_{(s,a)∈ρπ} [∇θ log πθ(a|s) A^π(s,a)] + Σ_{(s,a)∈ρD} [∇θ log πθ(a|s) w(s,a)] (Eq. 1, recovered by OCR from papers/md/dime_2022.ocr.md; OCR text is noisier than the layout parse, symbols may be imperfect) where ρπ, ρD are the state-action distributions generated by the policy and demonstrations, respectively, and w(s,a) is an exponential weighting function that decays to zero over the course of training." MJRL codebase used for all RL baselines (PPO, BCRL, DAPG); a dense, distance-to-target-linear reward is used only in the simulated MuJoCo environments for RL optimization ("a dense reward function is created for each environment that is linear with the distance between the object and its target pose. This reward function is required for optimization using RL", Sec. IV-A) — the real robot has no such reward, consistent with using non-parametric imitation rather than RL there.

## Evaluation
- exact success criterion (quoted, Sec. IV-A): Flipping — "success when the object flips and lands within 2 cm of the palm's center within 1 minute"; Spinning — "success when 120 degrees of rotation is achieved in 1 minute"; Rotating — "success when 90 degrees of rotation is achieved in 1 minute" (Table I also separately reports a harder 180-degree rotation threshold for the Rotating task).
- number of eval episodes/seeds: "For each algorithm and each task, we run the robot for ten trials" (Sec. IV-E). Simulation RL curves (Fig. 4) report "±1 standard error measured across 10 seeds."
- real robot? Yes for INN/VINN/BC (Table I); RL algorithms (PPO/BCRL/DAPG) run only in simulation, explicitly not on the real robot for safety/sample-complexity reasons (Sec. III-F).
- headline numbers (Table I, real Allegro hand, 10 trials/cell): INN (State-Based) — Flipping 80%, Spinning/Turning 60%, Rotation-90deg 100%, Rotation-180deg 80%. Behavior Cloning (State-Based) — 0% on every task. VINN (Image-Based) — Flipping 90%, Turning 0%, Rotation-90deg 70%, Rotation-180deg 50%. Behavior Cloning (Image-Based) — 0% on every task. "non-parametric nearest neighbors (INN) outperforms parametric behavior cloning approaches across all tasks" (Sec. IV-E); "on the Rotation task, which is quite difficult to solve even by human operators through teleoperation, INN achieves a perfect success rate on 90-degree rotation." VINN fails on Spinning specifically: "unable to learn good representations due to the visual complexity of the scene... inability of BYOL to learn effective representations for cluttered scenes with a limited number of demonstrations" (Sec. IV-E). Simulation (Sec. IV-D, qualitative): BCRL and DAPG produce similar, smoother-than-demonstration policies; PPO alone fails to rotate the cube but unexpectedly matches DAPG on Spinning via "extreme, random movements"; pure BC fails on all simulated tasks too ("Since the number of demonstrations used is relatively small... BC policies are unable to remain in the support of demonstration data and fail").
- baselines beaten: Behavior Cloning (state- and image-based) on the real robot; PPO (pure RL, no demonstrations) in simulation for the Rotating task specifically.

## Limitations stated by the authors (Sec. V, quoted)
- "our demonstration collection pipeline requires the specification of a z-plane. This is because of the inherent depth ambiguity with RGB cameras. We believe this could be resolved by utilizing depth information from stereo or depth cameras."
- "for real-robot experiments, we notice that some tasks such as spinning do not have high success rates. As evidenced by our RL experiments in simulation, we believe that developing new sample-efficient RL finetuning methods can significantly alleviate this challenge."

## Notes for the survey
- Feeds: the cheapest-possible-retargeting end of the survey's retargeting-formulation comparison, alongside holo_dex_2022 (same lead author, same INN/VINN imitation stage, same Allegro hand) — DIME has NO 3D hand-pose estimation at all (fixed-height-plane assumption, no depth), whereas Holo-Dex uses the VR headset's built-in stereo hand tracker; this is the direct mechanistic explanation for Holo-Dex's Table I claim of being "1.8x faster" and able to collect 3/6 tasks Holo-Dex's own paper says single-RGB (DIME-style) tracking "is insufficient to collect even a single demonstration" for — cross-reference both notes when writing the survey's data-rate comparison rather than taking either paper's number in isolation.
- No cost-of-rig dollar figure and no aggregate demos/hour figure in this paper (mark "not stated" in the survey table); the per-task teleoperation-time numbers above (30/120/150s per demo) are the only throughput data available and should be presented as per-demo times, not converted to a headline "demos/hour" without flagging that resets and failures are not included.
- Contact/penetration handling: not addressed — no interpenetration term or discussion anywhere in the paper, consistent with the rest of this survey's teleoperation/retargeting batch.
- At ~30 real-robot demonstrations, parametric behavior cloning gets 0% on every task in both state and image observation spaces (Table I) — a clean, quotable data point for the survey's "how much data does BC need" discussion, directly contrasting with diffusion_policy_2023's/dp3_2024's much higher BC success rates at similar or even smaller (10-40) demonstration counts, worth flagging since it suggests the specific parametric-BC implementation/tuning matters as much as raw demo count.
