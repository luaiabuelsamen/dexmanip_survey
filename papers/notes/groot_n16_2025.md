## One-line contribution
NVIDIA claims GR00T N1.6 — an internal Cosmos-2B VLM backbone, a DiT action head doubled to 32 layers, and several-thousand additional hours of teleoperated pretraining data (bimanual YAM, AgiBot Genie-1, simulated Galaxea R1 Pro, Unitree G1 locomanipulation) — outperforms N1.5 after post-training on real bimanual YAM, AgiBot Genie-1, and Unitree G1 robots, with no numbers given for the improvement.

## Method
- paradigm: VLA (flow-matching DiT action head, per code cross-check below); dual VLM+action-head architecture continued from N1/N1.5.
- backbone: "an internal NVIDIA Cosmos-2B VLM variant... trained on both general vision-language tasks and embodied reasoning tasks like next action prediction"; "flexible resolution... native aspect ratio without padding."
- action head: "2x larger DiT (32 layers vs 16 layers in N1.5)"; "Removes N1.5's post-VLM 4-layer transformer adapter. Instead, we unfreeze the top 4 layers of the VLM during pretraining."; "Predicts state-relative action chunks for most embodiments, rather than absolute joint angles or EEF positions."
- params: checkpoint is named `nvidia/GR00T-N1.6-3B` (Model Card link) — 3B implied by the name only, no parameter count stated in body text.

## Limitations / lessons stated by the authors (Discussion)
Relative actions "prone to error accumulation" with small datasets; pretrained-vs-post-training statistics tradeoff depends on task-distribution similarity; N1.6 "requires more careful tuning to prevent overfitting" despite converging faster; "multi-task language following and out-of-distribution task generalization continue to be challenging for current VLA models... not yet reaching robust generalization."
