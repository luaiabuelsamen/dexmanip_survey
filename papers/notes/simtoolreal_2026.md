# simtoolreal_2026 — SimToolReal: An Object-Centric Policy for Zero-Shot Dexterous Tool Manipulation (Kedia, Lum, Bohg, Liu; arXiv 2026)

sources: papers/md/simtoolreal_2026.md [d8d399c2] ; no code

## One-line contribution
A single goal-conditioned RL policy trained only on procedurally generated primitive "handle+head" objects reaching random 6D goal poses in sim, deployed zero-shot on 24 real tool-use tasks across 6 tool categories by chaining a human-video-derived goal-pose sequence at test time, with no per-object or per-task training or reward engineering (Abstract; Sec. III).

## Setting
- hand(s): 22-DoF Sharpa five-fingered hand ; arm: 7-DoF KUKA iiwa 14 ; single hand, no bimanual (Sec. IV, "Experimental Setup"). Total 29 actuated joints (Appendix C2).
- simulator / physics: IsaacGym, built from the "DexPBT Kuka Allegro Reorientation environment [59]" with substantial modifications (Appendix C1). States that Genesis and MJWarp were tried and rejected because "they do not currently support simulation of parallel environments with different object geometries per scene" (Appendix C1) — a concrete, reproducible reason for the engine choice. Timestep/control rate and env count are not stated in the parse.
- observation: object-centric, minimal at test time — robot proprioception (joint pos/vel ∈ R^29 each, previous joint targets, palm pose, 5 fingertip positions relative to palm ∈ R^15); object/goal state via K=4 object-frame keypoints (object orientation quaternion, keypoints relative to palm ∈ R^12, keypoint errors to goal ∈ R^12, object scale s ∈ R^3 from the grasp bounding box) (Appendix C3). No raw geometry/mass; only current 6D pose + coarse 3D grasp-region bounding box are given to the actor, chosen because they are "reliably available at deployment" (Sec. III-B). LSTM backbone to integrate history and "implicitly infer latent physical and geometric properties that are not directly observed" (Sec. III-B).
- action space: 29-dim, arm (7) as delta position targets (`k_arm = 0.025` scale factor, EMA-filtered, α_arm=0.1) and hand (22) as absolute joint-limit-normalized targets (EMA-filtered, α_hand=0.1) (Appendix C2).
- objects / data: no real training objects — procedurally generated handle+head primitives (cuboid or capsule per part) with handle length ∈[5,30]cm, handle width/diameter ∈[1,4]cm, head length ∈[1,15]cm, head width/diameter ∈[0.5,12]cm; handle density ρ_low ~ U[300,600] kg/m³, head density ρ_high ~ U[300,2000] kg/m³ (Appendix B). Real-world eval: DexToolBench, 12 object instances / 6 categories (hammer, marker, eraser, brush, spatula, screwdriver) / 24 task trajectories, each defined by an RGB-D human demo video (Sec. III-D, Appendix F).

## Method
- paradigm: RL, sim-to-real, zero-shot (no fine-tuning, no distillation stage described) ; algorithm: SAPG, "a variant of PPO" that "maintains a population of policies to promote exploration diversity and updat[es] the leader policy using their collective experience" to avoid PPO's exploration bottleneck in massively parallel sim (Sec. IV-E / III-B "RL Training Details"). Asymmetric actor-critic: critic sees privileged, noise-free ground truth (velocities, instantaneous reward, success count, stateful progress features, undelayed object pose) while the actor only gets the noisy/delayed object-centric observation above (Sec. III-B item c; Appendix C4).
- key trick: "object-centric" reduction — every tool-use task is reframed as tracking a sequence of goal 6D poses {g_k}, so one goal-reaching policy trained on random-pose primitives, not per-task rewards, is claimed to induce grasping, in-hand reorientation, and contact maintenance as emergent sub-skills (Sec. III-A, "Problem Formulation"). At test time the goal sequence itself comes from a perception pipeline (SAM 3D for mesh/grasp-region segmentation + FoundationPose for 6D pose tracking) run on a single human RGB-D video, processed to 3 Hz (Sec. III-C).
- domain randomisation: "observation delays, action-execution latency, noise and delay in object-pose estimates, and perturbations to the grasp-region bounding box," plus "random force and torque perturbations to the object, encouraging the policy to learn strong, stable grasps" (Sec. III-B item b). Exact numeric ranges for these are referenced as being in Appendix D but not present in the parsed text (the parse jumps from goal-sampling strategy in Appendix B to Appendix F DexToolBench details) — SOURCE THIN for the specific DR numbers beyond geometry/density above.
- reward (paper, Appendix A; quote): three primary components, gated by an indicator `I_grasped`: r = r_smooth + (1 − I_grasped)·r_grasp + I_grasped·r_goal (structure stated in prose; the literal combining equation is rendered as a blank image in the parse — SOURCE THIN on the exact formula). **r_smooth**: "we penalize the L1 norm of joint velocities" over the 7-DoF arm and 22-DoF hand (coefficient not given in the parse). **r_grasp** = r_approach + r_lift (decomposition stated in prose, combining formula an image): r_approach "rewards the agent for approaching the object rather than simply maintaining a fixed close distance," via a stateful minimum mean fingertip-to-object distance d̄*_ft that only rewards improvement; r_lift awards a one-time bonus B_lifted when object height z ≥ z_lifted, active only "at the start of the episode before the object has been lifted," after which "r_goal takes over" (I_grasped flips true once z ≥ z_lifted). **r_goal** (dominant post-grasp): dense term minimizing keypoint distance d(o_t,g) to the current goal plus a sparse success bonus B_succ, with only *improvements* in a stateful minimum distance d* rewarded — "Positive reward is only provided for progress towards the goal, preventing the agent from exploiting the reward function by maintaining static proximity" (quoted, Sec. III-B); on success (d(o_t,g) < ε) the agent gets B_succ and a new goal is sampled. Keypoint distance: d(o_t,g) = max_i ‖o_t,i − g_i‖ over D=4 object-frame keypoints (following [4,59]); anisotropic reward keypoint offset scales s^rew=[s_x,s_y,s_z]=[0.14,0.03,0.03] m bias the reward toward pitch/yaw over roll about the tool's long axis (Appendix A) — the one fully numeric reward constant recoverable from this parse.
- reward evaluation/selection: no LLM or evolutionary reward search (unlike Eureka/DrEureka) — a single hand-designed, fixed reward used identically across all procedurally generated objects; Sec. IV-D's correlation between primitive-training reward and zero-shot DexToolBench Task Progress is offered as post-hoc validation that this one fixed reward transfers, not as a selection mechanism between candidates.
- mismatch / gap: the actual weighted-sum equations for r, r_grasp and r_approach are rendered as blank images in the pymupdf4llm parse; term semantics above are reliable but numeric weights beyond the keypoint scales are not recoverable from this parse — reward block is SOURCE THIN on weights.
- interpenetration / contact: not addressed as a measured quantity anywhere in the parse. The only contact-adjacent mechanisms are episode-termination conditions, not a reward or metric: "Table Force Limits: The net force measured by the table sensor exceeds 100 N, preventing aggressive collisions" and an "Object Drop (Hysteresis)" reset if the object returns to the table after being grasped (Appendix C5). No penetration measurement or contact-solver setting is reported.
- key trick(s): the object-centric goal-pose reduction (above) plus the asymmetric critic and SAPG choices (RL block).

## Evaluation
- metric: Task Progress — "percentage of demonstrated goal poses tracked successfully" per trajectory; "a goal pose to be reached if... d(o_t,g) is below our defined success tolerance ε = 2 cm" (Sec. IV, "Experimental Setup"). This is trajectory-following fidelity, not functional task completion — explicitly stated: "We focus on trajectory following of a fixed goal sequence rather than evaluating functional task completion."
- headline numbers: real-world zero-shot eval, 24 task trajectories × 6 categories × 12 object instances, "average Task Progress over 5 trials, totaling 120 real-world rollouts" (Sec. IV-A) — has explicit trial counts, NOT video-only. Per-category qualitative ranking given in prose (eraser highest; screwdriver "most challenging category"; thinner/heavier tools degrade performance) but per-category numeric Task Progress values are only in Fig. 4 (bar chart, not printed as text/table in the parse) — do not quote a specific per-category percentage beyond what's in prose.
- failure analysis (Sec. IV-A, quoted): "pose tracking loss (43.7% of failures), followed by object drops (34.5%), failure to reach the goal pose due to incomplete in-hand rotation (18.2%), and grasp failure (3.6%)."
- baselines beaten: (1) Kinematic Retargeting (IK from estimated human finger positions, "purely kinematic... fails to account for contact interactions") and (2) Fixed Grasp (grasp once, then arm-only motion) — compared on a 2-variation real-world brush-sweep test, 5 rollouts each: SimToolReal 98.0%/82.7% vs. Fixed Grasp 61.0%/10.8% vs. Kinematic Retargeting 8.1%/0% (Fig. 5 caption, quoted numbers) — the paper's "37% task progress" headline improvement (bib `why` field) traces to this comparison. (3) Specialist RL baselines (one policy per category, trained on a single Obj A/Traj A, 10 rollouts per variation per specialist, in SIMULATION only): SimToolReal matches specialists on their own training setup but specialists collapse under trajectory or object variation (Sec. IV-C, Fig. 6) — this specialist comparison is sim-only, not real-world.
- ablations (Sec. IV-E, sim only, 5 seeds): removing SAPG (→ PPO) or removing the Asymmetric Critic each "results in a significant performance drop" on training reward curves (Fig. 8); no real-world ablation numbers.
- real robot? Yes, with explicit trial counts throughout (5 rollouts/trajectory for the main 120-rollout eval; 5 rollouts/variation for the baseline comparison). Not video-only.

## Limitations stated by the authors (Sec. V)
- "does not guarantee functional task completion, especially for high-force interactions" — Task Progress measures trajectory tracking, not task success.
- "Conditioning on object pose goals alone is environment-blind, which can lead to collisions in cluttered scenes."
- "assume tools are rigid... Pose alone can be insufficient to describe the state of non-rigid tools (e.g., scissors)."
- "our high-level goal sequence is fixed and is not replanned dynamically."

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "SimToolReal outperforms prior retargeting and fixed-grasp methods by 37% while matching the performance of specialist RL policies trained on specific target objects and tasks." (Abstract)
- "Positive reward is only provided for progress towards the goal, preventing the agent from exploiting the reward function by maintaining static proximity." (Sec. III-B)
- "many other GPU-accelerated simulators, such as Genesis [10] and MJWarp [87], could not be used because they do not currently support simulation of parallel environments with different object geometries per scene." (Appendix C1)
- "the specialist's performance drops significantly under any variation from the training setup... This indicates that the specialist overfits to the training conditions." (Sec. IV-C)

## Notes for the survey
- New vs. 2022-2023 baselines: prior sim-to-real dexterous work (in-hand reorientation, grasping, spinning) is per-object/per-task with hand-tuned reward and object models (Related Work, Sec. II, citing [14,54,27,78,40] as the narrower-skill precedents). SimToolReal's claim to novelty is entirely in generalizing across objects/tasks via one procedurally-randomized primitive family and one fixed universal reward (random goal-pose reaching), not in a new RL algorithm (SAPG and asymmetric critic are both cited as prior work, [71] and [60]) or a new reward form (progress-only dense + sparse success bonus is a standard shaping pattern, credited to [4,59]).
- "Object-centric" concretely means: the actor's only object information is (6D pose, fixed 3D grasp-region bounding box) — no mesh, mass, or friction — deliberately chosen to be perception-pipeline-recoverable at test time, sidestepping the sim-to-real visual gap rather than closing it.
- Real-robot eval has explicit trial counts (120 rollouts, 5 per trajectory) — usable as a real-robot-with-trial-counts example for the survey's evaluation-rigor table, in contrast to any video-only claims elsewhere in the corpus.
- Feeds: object-centric/goal-conditioned generalist-policy section of the survey; comparison point for DexMachina/ManipTrans-style demo-tracking approaches (this paper explicitly frames itself against "functional retargeting" methods, Sec. II, as requiring per-object/task RL training that SimToolReal avoids); the reward-block comparison table should flag this paper's reward as fully hand-designed (no LLM search, unlike Eureka/DrEureka) but only partially recoverable numerically from this parse.
