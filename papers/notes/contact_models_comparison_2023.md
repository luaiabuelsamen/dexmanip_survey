# contact_models_comparison_2023 — Contact Models in Robotics: a Comparative Analysis (Le Lidec, Jallet, Montaut, Laptev, Schmid, Carpentier; IEEE T-RO 2024, arXiv 2023)

sources: papers/md/contact_models_comparison_2023.md [c7387d83] ; code/md/contact_models_comparison_2023.md [03d4784e]

## One-line contribution
Decouples contact models from their simulator implementations by re-implementing LCP, CCP, RaiSim, and NCP contact formulations and their solvers (PGS, ADMM, Newton, staggered projections, per-contact bisection) in one C++ framework (ContactBench, on Pinocchio + HPP-FCL), then empirically ranks them on physical correctness, self-consistency, and computational efficiency, including an MPC-for-locomotion end task (Abstract).

## Setting
- hand(s): Allegro hand (16-dof), used as one of three benchmark systems (a ball dropped into it) to exercise "non-static" manipulation-type contact scenarios (Sec. IV-C, Fig. 8).
- simulator / physics: not a single simulator — a from-scratch C++ re-implementation (ContactBench, open-sourced) of the algorithmic variants of each contact model, using Pinocchio for rigid-body dynamics/Delassus-matrix Cholesky and HPP-FCL (GJK/EPA) for collision detection (Sec. IV, intro).
- objects / data: illustrative examples (sliding cube, cube dragged by growing tangential force, stacked cubes with 10³:10⁻³ kg mass ratio) plus three robot systems: Solo-12 quadruped (12 dof), Talos humanoid (32 dof), Allegro hand (16 dof) (Fig. 8), and a Solo-12 MPC-locomotion task on flat (µ=0.9) and bumpy/slippery (roughness 10⁻¹ m, µ=0.3) terrain (Sec. IV-D).

## Physics block
- contact model taxonomy (Table II, the paper's central classification): each model is checked against three physical properties — the Signorini condition, Coulomb's law, and the maximum dissipation principle (MDP). **LCP**: Signorini only (Coulomb linearized to a pyramid, biasing friction toward pyramid corners, Fig. 6). **CCP** (cone complementarity problem): Coulomb + MDP, but *relaxes* Signorini — normal force and normal velocity can be simultaneously non-zero during sliding, producing an interaction-at-a-distance artifact of size Δt·µ·‖c_T‖ (Sec. III-B, Anitescu's result). **RaiSim**: Signorini + Coulomb, but *not* MDP — enforcing Signorini during sliding (via the null-normal-velocity hyperplane constraint) is shown algebraically to contradict the MDP optimality condition (Sec. III-C, "which contradicts (12)"). **NCP**: all three (Signorini, Coulomb, MDP) — the paper's reference formulation, Eq. (15), built from de Saxcé's bipotential function, but non-convex and hard to solve directly.
- solver and iterations (Table III, "Characteristics of Numerical Algorithms" — columns: hard contacts / no internal forces / robust / convergence guarantees): LCP-PGS (used by Bullet, ODE, PhysX, DART) — none of the four columns checked; LCP-Staggered-projections — 3/4 checked. CCP-PGS — 2/4; **CCP-MuJoCo** (Newton on the primal QCQP, Alg. 4) — 3/4 (not "no internal forces"); **CCP-ADMM** (Alg. 3, on the dual QCQP) — all 4/4; **CCP-Drake** (Newton, SAP-style) — 3/4; **RaiSim** — 1/4 (Signorini-consistent only, "not robust to conditioning and prone to internal forces," Sec. III-C). **NCP-PGS** — 1/4; **NCP-Staggered-projections** — 3/4. PGS for LCP/CCP/NCP is Algorithm 1/2/adapted-Alg.6, a per-contact Gauss-Seidel sweep; typical PGS convergence is "in the order of hundreds of iterations" for CCP (Sec. III-B, citing [76]); Dual arm / dual-arm-scale systems in the companion Castro paper get ~4-10 mean Newton iterations, whereas PGS "appear[s] to be reasonably fast to reach mild accuracy (ϵ_abs=10⁻⁵) while they eventually saturate before reaching high precision" (Sec. IV-C).
- differentiability: raised as an open problem, not solved here — "the impact of forward simulation artifacts on gradient computation remains unexplored. In particular, some of the relaxations at work, e.g. the artificial compliance added in MuJoCo, result in crucial differences in gradients" (Sec. V, Discussion). No differentiable variant is benchmarked in this paper.
- timestep: default Δt = 1 ms unless stated otherwise (Sec. III-E, end); a dedicated self-consistency experiment (Sec. IV-B) uses a reference trajectory at Δt = 10⁻⁵ s and compares against larger steps; CCP is shown to be the most Δt-sensitive model because it relaxes Signorini (the cube "slides at a height proportional to Δt," Sec. IV-B, Fig. 15).
- friction model: Coulomb's law (‖λ_T‖ ≤ µλ_N, second-order/"ice-cream" cone Eq. 11) with the maximum dissipation principle as the tie-breaking optimality condition (Eq. 12); LCP linearizes the cone to a 4-facet pyramid (`K̃_µ`, Sec. III-A), all others keep the exact second-order cone.
- how penetration is resolved / is depth exposed: for rigid (non-compliant) models, Φ(q)_N ≥ 0 is the Signorini constraint itself (Eq. 6, 8); "one cannot avoid penetration errors ... without using stabilization by reprojection techniques" (Sec. II, "Free motion" discussion) — Baumgarte correction (bias term k_B·max(0,−Φ_N), coefficient k_B ∝ 1/Δt) is the cited stabilization mechanism, or full compliance R (a diagonal regularization matrix acting like a numerical/physical spring). MuJoCo's own R is stated to have "no physical justification ... its only intent is to improve the conditioning of the problem" (Sec. III-B); the paper explicitly states "the compliance added in MuJoCo has no physical purpose and should be considered a numerical trick" (Sec. IV-A, "Effects of compliance"). Signed distance Φ(q)_N is exposed as a first-class quantity throughout (used directly in every algorithm listed).
- GPU or CPU: CPU only. ContactBench is a C++ CPU library; no GPU implementation or GPU/CPU comparison is discussed.
- throughput / hardware: no absolute FPS/steps-per-second table; Fig. 17 reports per-timestep wall-clock runtime (seconds, log scale) for Solo/Allegro-hand/Talos in both cold-start and warm-start modes, comparing ADMM, RaiSim, PGS variants, NCP/StagProj, and Drake — cold-start: second-order methods (Newton-style, e.g. Drake) are *less* efficient than PGS's cheap per-contact iterations; ADMM sits in between; warm-starting "significantly reduc[es]" the gap for ADMM. No numeric FPS values are given in the parsed text — only the qualitative ranking and the log-scale figure.

## Evaluation
- metrics (exact definitions): dimensionless momentum error / NCP criterion ϵ_abs, defined via primal residual ϵ_p = dist_{K_µ}(λ), dual residual ϵ_d = dist_{K*_µ}(c+Γ(c,µ)), and contact complementarity ϵ_c = |⟨λ,c+Γ(c,µ)⟩| (Sec. II, Eq. following (15)), combined as ϵ_abs = max(ϵ_p, ϵ_d, ϵ_c); integral consistency error ∫|q_τ − q̃_τ| dτ / Δt for self-consistency vs. a Δt=10⁻⁵s reference trajectory (Sec. IV-A).
- full comparison conclusions:
  - **LCP relaxation** biases friction direction (pyramid-corner bias, Fig. 6/9), measurably deviating a sliding-cube trajectory off its expected straight line.
  - **CCP relaxation** produces unphysical vertical bouncing on a dragged/sliding cube because it relaxes Signorini (Fig. 10 left); its energy trace shows "a significant gap with the analytical solution" (Fig. 10 right); RaiSim narrows that gap but "dissipates less power than expected, as it does not always enforce the MDP"; only **NCP/PGS** "closely matches the expected analytical behavior."
  - **Underdetermined (hyperstatic) problems**: per-contact algorithms (PGS-family, RaiSim) exhibit spurious "jamming" internal forces at stiction that vanish only when the object starts sliding; global algorithms with proximal regularization (ADMM, and by extension Drake's Newton scheme) avoid injecting these artifacts (Sec. IV-A, Fig. 12).
  - **Ill-conditioning** (10³ kg cube stacked on a 10⁻³ kg cube): per-contact approaches (CCP/PGS, NCP/PGS, RaiSim) fail to converge and violate the NCP criterion; ADMM, staggered projections, and Newton "appear to be robust" (Fig. 13).
  - **Self-consistency w.r.t. Δt**: CCP is the most timestep-sensitive (Sec. IV-B, Fig. 15) because its Signorini relaxation scales with Δt; NCP and RaiSim degrade much less.
  - **Performance/iterations** (Fig. 16): "PGS approaches appear to be reasonably fast to reach mild accuracy ... while they eventually saturate before reaching high precision"; ADMM, Newton, and staggered projections "can find high-accuracy solutions using only a few, but more costly, iterations."
  - **MPC for Solo-12 locomotion**: on flat, barely-slippery ground (µ=0.9) the choice of contact model/solver "hardly affects" tracked base velocity and NCP-criterion violation stays low (Sec. IV-D, Fig. 18). On bumpy, slippery ground (roughness 0.1 m, µ=0.3), RaiSim and CCP "significantly deviate" from the NCP-model locomotion velocity, and NCP/PGS itself occasionally violates its own convergence criterion because PGS fails to converge within the iteration budget — i.e. "Gauss-Seidel-like approaches appear to be sufficient for mild conditions ... but are not robust enough ... when locomotion tasks become more challenging" (Sec. IV-D, Fig. 19).
  - **Overall verdict** (Sec. V, Discussion): "there is no fully satisfactory approach at the moment, as all existing solutions compromise either accuracy, robustness, or efficiency."
- baselines beaten: no single winner is declared; the paper's own re-implementations of MuJoCo's, Drake's, and RaiSim's contact models are directly benchmarked against each other and against LCP/PGS and NCP-family solvers on the criteria above.
- real robot? none — all experiments are in ContactBench simulation.

## Limitations stated by the authors
- "Our implementation uses a backtracking line-search with an Armijo condition instead of the line-search proposed in the original [Drake] paper ... This difference could lead to degraded performances here which should be kept in mind when interpreting the results" (Sec. IV, intro).
- The "no internal forces" property of proximal/global methods (ADMM, staggered) is described as "an empirical observation ... and, to our knowledge, has not yet been proven by the literature" (Sec. III-B).
- "The impact of forward simulation artifacts on gradient computation remains unexplored" for differentiable physics (Sec. V).
- Scalability of the direct/Cholesky-based approaches to very large or highly branched systems is not separately stress-tested beyond the three robots used.

- "there is no fully satisfactory approach at the moment, as all existing solutions compromise either accuracy, robustness, or efficiency." (Sec. V)
- "the compliance added in MuJoCo has no physical purpose and should be considered a numerical trick designed to circumvent the issues due to hyper-staticity or ill-conditioning at the cost of impairing the simulation." (Sec. IV-A)
- "Gauss-Seidel-like approaches appear to be sufficient for mild conditions ... but are not robust enough to ensure convergence of the simulation when the locomotion tasks become more challenging." (Sec. IV-D)

- "Its implementation being closed source, we provide what constitutes, to the best of our knowledge, the first in-depth study and open-source re-implementation of this [RaiSim] contact solver." (Sec. I)

## Notes for the survey
- This is the survey's primary source for a rigorous, apples-to-apples ranking of LCP/CCP/RaiSim/NCP contact models and their PGS/ADMM/Newton/staggered-projection solvers; Table II and Table III (reproduced above as prose) should anchor the physics-engine comparison table across the whole survey.
- Directly supplies the missing quantitative backbone for the `raisim_2018` note (whose own paper PDF fetch failed): RaiSim satisfies Signorini + Coulomb but violates the MDP, and is per-contact (Gauss-Seidel-like), hence not robust to ill-conditioning and prone to jamming internal forces at stiction.
- Complements `castro_sap_contact_2021` (the Drake/SAP paper): this paper re-implements SAP's Newton solver independently (with a different, weaker line search, an explicit caveat) and places it in the broader taxonomy — SAP/Drake gets 3 of 4 marks in Table III, missing only "no internal forces."
- Corroborates `dojo_2022`'s Table II qualitative point (MuJoCo's compliance trades accuracy for conditioning) via an independent, controlled experiment (dragged-cube energy/height drift, Fig. 10, Fig. 15) rather than a single humanoid-drop scenario — the two papers should be cited together for that claim.
- Code md provenance: `code/md/contact_models_comparison_2023.md` is a README + pruned file tree only (the ContactBench C++ solver implementations were not captured in signature form); all algorithmic detail above is from the paper text (Sec. II-III, Algorithms 1-7, Tables II-III).
