# dojo_2022 — Dojo: A Differentiable Physics Engine for Robotics (Howell, Le Cleac'h, Brüdigam, Chen, Sun, Kolter, Schwager, Manchester; arXiv 2022)

sources: papers/md/dojo_2022.md [72992803] ; code/md/dojo_2022.md [be7b5183]

## One-line contribution
A hard-contact, nonlinear-complementarity-problem (NCP) physics engine with an exact nonlinear friction cone, solved by a custom primal-dual interior-point method whose central-path parameter κ is a user-tunable knob trading gradient smoothness for physical accuracy, giving analytic implicit-function-theorem gradients useful for planning/RL/system ID (Abstract).

## Setting
- hand(s): none — this paper's demonstrated systems are Atlas humanoid (drop test), Unitree A1 quadruped, a hopper, half-cheetah, ant, and a box pushed by a UFactory xArm 6; no dexterous hand.
- simulator / physics: Dojo, implemented in Julia with Python bindings (Abstract). Maximal-coordinates state representation, explicitly representing each rigid link's 6-DoF pose plus internal constraint forces (Sec. I). Collision primitives supported: "point, sphere, and capsule collisions with flat surfaces" via a pre-existing (not-in-scope) collision detection module (Sec. V).
- observation / action space: not applicable — a physics-engine paper; policy examples use Gym-like ant/half-cheetah environments with static linear policies (Sec. V-C).
- objects / data: Atlas drop (403 maximal-coordinate states, 31 bodies, 36 actuated DOF, 4 contact points/foot, Fig. 1); box-throw dataset for system identification (Sec. V-D).

## Physics block
- contact model: full nonlinear complementarity problem (NCP) with an exact nonlinear (second-order-cone) friction cone rather than a pyramidal/linearized approximation, "to avoid numerical artifacts like interpenetration of rigid bodies ... and creep" (Sec. I). The cone program's optimality conditions (Sec. IV-A, Eq. 19-20) are cast for a primal-dual interior-point solver with dual variable η and central-path parameter κ.
- solver and iterations: a custom primal-dual interior-point solver (Algorithm 2) based on Mehrotra's predictor-corrector algorithm, extended with non-Euclidean handling of quaternion variables and cone-handling borrowed from CVXOPT (Sec. IV-B). Converges "within 15 iterations for all three robots" tested in the convergence study (Sec. V-A). Tolerances used: r_tol = κ_tol = 10⁻⁵ by default (Algorithm 2); convergence study also sweeps κ_tol/r_tol ∈ {10⁻⁴,10⁻⁶,10⁻⁸}.
- differentiability: yes — gradients through the interior-point solution via implicit differentiation (implicit function theorem) of the KKT/complementarity system (Sec. III-B, IV-C). The central-path parameter κ is explicitly the smoothness knob: "a high value gives a smooth approximation to contact dynamics with smooth gradients ... while a low value gives precise simulation rollouts with hard contact" (Abstract). Gradients for planning/system-ID experiments used κ = 3e-4 (Sec. V-B, V-D).
- timestep: variable by experiment, and explicitly larger than MuJoCo's in matched comparisons: box planning h=0.1 (Dojo) vs. h=0.01 (MuJoCo, which "fails in the scenario with the goal in the air" at that step, Sec. V-B); hopper h=0.05 (Dojo) vs. h=0.01 (MuJoCo); quadruped planning h=0.05; policy-optimization envs (half-cheetah, ant) h=0.05, "MuJoCo requires smaller h=0.01 time steps for stable simulation, whereas Dojo is stable with h=0.05" (Sec. V-C). Atlas drop simulates in real time at 65 Hz (Sec. V-A). Stability was validated from 20-500 Hz on Atlas/A1 drops (Sec. V-A, Fig. 7).
- friction model: nonlinear (exact) Coulomb friction cone via the cone program in Sec. IV-A, contrasted with the pyramidal/linearized cones used elsewhere; a friction-cone comparison test (box sliding after lateral-velocity impact) shows MuJoCo's nonlinear cone has "a minor rotational drift" and PyBullet's nonlinear cone "exerts greater friction force on box that leads to a shorter trajectory," while Dojo "handle[s] the exact nonlinear cone constraint efficiently and reliably ... the result is accurate sliding" (Sec. V-A, Fig. 5).
- how penetration is resolved / is depth exposed: penetration is resolved to machine precision by the hard-contact NCP solve rather than by a compliance/softening term, and depth is directly exposed and measured in Table II — Atlas-drop foot-floor penetration (mm) vs. timestep: MuJoCo −28 mm (Δt=0.01s), −46 mm (Δt=0.001s), and "failure" at Δt=0.1s; Dojo +1e-12 mm (Δt=0.1s), +1e-7 mm (Δt=0.01s), +8e-6 mm (Δt=0.001s) — i.e. Dojo's feet stay infinitesimally above the floor at every tested step size while MuJoCo penetrates tens of millimetres (Table II, Sec. V-A, "Dojo respects floor-feet penetration constraints to machine precision," Fig. 1 caption).
- GPU or CPU: CPU. "All of the experiments were performed on a computer with an Intel Core i9-10885H processor and 32GB of memory" (Sec. V). No GPU support is described; hardware acceleration is listed as future work (Sec. VI-C: "hardware-accelerator support for Dojo would potentially enable faster simulation and optimization").
- throughput / hardware: Table V compares forward-simulation-plus-gradient wall-clock time for 1000 steps at Δt=0.01s, actions random, on the i9-10885H/32GB machine: Humanoid — MuJoCo 1.512±0.045 s, Drake 5.463±0.078 s, Brax 6.975±0.485 s, Dojo 1.750±0.135 s; Unitree A1 — MuJoCo 1.114±0.007 s, Drake 3.870±0.024 s, Brax 11.064±0.521 s, Dojo 5.235±0.071 s; Franka Panda — MuJoCo 0.335±0.001 s, Drake 2.352±0.055 s, Brax 10.954±0.395 s, Dojo 1.159±0.077 s; Skydio X2 — MuJoCo 0.047±0.002 s, Drake 0.571±0.011 s, Brax 7.953±0.484 s, Dojo 0.807±0.003 s. MuJoCo is fastest on every system; Dojo "significantly outperforms ... Brax, and delivers a comparable performance as Drake," attributed to maximal-coordinate dynamics and implicit differentiation avoiding O(n²) finite-difference cost (Sec. V-A).

## Evaluation
- metrics: iLQR planning cost, goal-constraint violation, iteration count (Table III); foot-floor penetration in mm (Table II); reward and simulation-step count for policy optimization (Table VI); sim-to-real position/flip-outcome match for box-pushing (Table IV); parameter recovery error for system ID.
- headline numbers: Table III — box-up task: Dojo cost 14.5/violation 3e-3/106 iters and succeeds, MuJoCo "failure"/violation 1.0; hopper: Dojo cost 8.9 vs MuJoCo 26.7. Table VI — half-cheetah: ARS reward 46±24 (3e4 sim evals), AGS(Dojo) 44±24 (5e3 evals, "5 to 10 times more sample efficient"), AGS(random) 39±27 (8e3 evals); ant: ARS 64±15 (2e5 evals), AGS(Dojo) 54±28 (2e4 evals), AGS(random) 59±24 (4e4 evals). System ID: learned friction coefficient and box-vertex geometry "within 5% of the true geometry and friction coefficient" (Sec. V-D). Sim-to-real box push (Table IV): average final-position sim-to-real gap ≈0.5 cm (1.25%) across 5 trials at 30-50 cm initial distances, and flip-outcome predictions "closely matched" the real robot at all 5 distances.
- baselines beaten: MuJoCo (planning: box/hopper accuracy, not speed), Brax (speed and Reward-per-sample), Drake (comparable speed).
- real robot? Yes — UFactory xArm 6, box-pushing task, 5 initial distances (30/35/40/45/50 cm), PD position control at matched joint-space commands sim vs. real (Sec. V-A "Sim-to-Real gap evaluation," Table IV, Fig. 8).

## Limitations stated by the authors
- "In terms of features, reliability, and wall-clock time, MuJoCo — the product of a decade of excellent software engineering — is impressive" (Sec. VI-B).
- "Dojo's approach of solving an NCP with a primal-dual interior point method requires more computation per time step compared to existing simulators that use a soft-contact model (e.g. MuJoCo and Drake), but allows for accurate simulation with a lower sample rate, making wall-clock comparisons between the two simulators difficult" (Sec. VI-B).
- The interior-point solver solves a nonconvex problem per step and "may have a danger of reaching poor local minima or not converging" (Sec. VI-B), and the same risk applies to using Dojo's gradients for downstream trajectory/policy optimization.
- Future work notes: only sphere/capsule/halfspace collision primitives currently, no convex-mesh or triangle-mesh support yet; no adaptive time-stepping; no hardware acceleration (Sec. VI-C).

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "Dojo respects floor-feet penetration constraints to machine precision. Other simulators struggle to maintain the floor contact constraint, especially at low simulation rates." (Fig. 1 caption)
- "MuJoCo is enforcing soft contact whereas Dojo simulates hard contact." (Sec. V-B)
- "Among all four simulators, MuJoCo exhibited a clear advantage in computation speed." (Sec. V-A)
- "After training, the learned parameters are within 5% of the true geometry and friction coefficient for the box from the dataset." (Sec. V-D)

## Notes for the survey
- Table II is the cleanest paper-reported number in this whole batch for "penetration depth is exposed and measured, in mm, engine vs. engine, at controlled timestep": use it directly as the anchor comparison for MuJoCo's soft-contact penetration vs. a hard-contact NCP engine, but flag that it is a single-scenario (Atlas foot-floor drop) benchmark on the authors' own re-implementation of MuJoCo settings, not a general claim.
- Contrast with `contact_models_comparison_2023`, which reaches a similar qualitative point (compliance in MuJoCo trades physical accuracy for conditioning/robustness) via a different, controlled cube-drag experiment rather than a humanoid drop.
- Code md provenance: `code/md/dojo_2022.md` is a 7,298-character stub (README + minimal file listing) with no Julia solver source captured; all solver/algorithm detail above is from the paper text only.
