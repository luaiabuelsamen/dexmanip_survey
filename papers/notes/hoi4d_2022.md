# hoi4d_2022 — HOI4D: A 4D Egocentric Dataset for Category-Level Human-Object Interaction (Liu et al., CVPR 2022)

sources: papers/md/hoi4d_2022.md [82c5f0bf] ; code/md/hoi4d_2022.md [15478666]

## One-line contribution
2.4M egocentric RGB-D frames over 4,000 sequences of participants interacting with 800 object instances (16 categories, 7 rigid + 9 articulated) across 610 indoor rooms, with frame-wise 4D panoptic segmentation, MANO 3D hand pose, category-level 9D object/part pose, and action-segmentation labels, benchmarked on three tasks (Abstract).

## Setting
- capture setup: head-mounted rig — bicycle helmet + Kinect v2 RGB-D (time-of-flight, long-range) + Intel RealSense D455 RGB-D (structured-light, short-range ~1 m), pre-calibrated and synchronized, worn simultaneously so the two sensors "provide a natural testbed for cross-sensor transfer learning" (Sec. 3.1). No markers worn by camera wearers (Sec. 1). Each video captured at 15 fps for 20 seconds (Sec. 4).
- subjects: the Abstract states "collected by 4 participants," but Sec. 1's body text states "4000 sequences of 9 participants interacting with 800 object instances" — this is a direct numeric contradiction within the same paper (Abstract "4" vs Sec. 1 "9"); flagged rather than resolved, per HOWTO rule on ambiguous numbers.
- objects: 800 object instances, 16 categories (7 rigid, 9 articulated), 50 unique instances per category; every instance has a CAD model reconstructed from multi-view high-resolution RGB scans, sourced mainly from ShapeNet/SAPIEN-Assets category selection (Sec. 4).
- scenes: 610 different indoor rooms (Abstract); scenes are manually split into "simple" (single interacted-with object, low camera-view variety, mean 1.67±0.79 objects present) and "complex" (10-20 objects from the 800-object pool placed in clutter, mean 6.02±2.93 objects present) (App. A.2, Table 7). Object-point-fraction of the scene point cloud: simple 6.3% (3D static scene) / 10.7% (4D dynamic scene); complex 13.9% / 17.0% (App. A.2, Table 8).
- sequences / frames / hours: 4,000 sequences, 2.4M RGB-D frames total (Abstract, Sec. 4); at 15 fps × 20 s/sequence, 4,000 sequences ≈ 22.2 hours of capture (derived from the stated per-video duration and count, not stated directly by the paper).
- tasks: two different counts appear — Sec. 4 body text says "we have defined 54 tasks across all 16 categories," while App. A.3 says "There are 76 tasks in all 16 categories" — again a direct numeric mismatch inside the same paper, flagged rather than reconciled. Every object gets a pick-and-place task plus category-specific functionality tasks (e.g., "placing a mug inside a drawer," which may require first opening the drawer if closed) (Sec. 3.1, Sec. 4).

## Annotation types (hand model, joint hand+object, verbatim metric/loss definitions)
- hand model: MANO (Sec. 3.4). Shape β ∈ R^10 fixed from the real data-capturer's measured hand; pose θ_h = {θ_m, R, t} ∈ R^51 optimized, with θ_m ∈ R^45 (3 DoF × 15 joints) plus global rotation R ∈ SO(3) and translation t ∈ R^3. Annotation pipeline: 4 stages — annotation (2D keypoints, 21-keypoint convention, but annotators only click 11: wrist + 5 fingertips + 5 second-knuckles, with occluded points estimated), initialization (per-frame 3D fit by minimizing a joint/2D/depth/point-cloud/mask loss, warm-started from the previous frame), propagation (linear interpolation of θ_h across unannotated frames), refinement (re-optimization with an added contact loss and temporal-consistency loss over 6-11-frame batches containing 2-3 annotated frames, plus manual correction of detected failure frames) (Sec. 3.4). Keypoints are annotated on only 20% of frames per video.
- joint hand+object annotation: hand pose optimization includes an object-interaction contact term, and object pose optimization (Sec. 3.5) is run against the same RGB-D/2D-segmentation data, but the two are fit as separate (not single joint) optimizations sharing sensor data and 2D motion-segmentation masks — not a single hands+object simultaneous solve as in H2O's Eq. 1. **Two hands are not jointly annotated**: the authors state directly (Sec. 7, "Limitations") that "human manipulation tasks with two hands are not covered" — HOI4D is single-hand-per-sequence only.
- category-level object/part pose: 9D pose (rotation + translation + 3 box scales) for rigid objects from manually placed amodal oriented bounding boxes, annotated every 10 frames; articulated objects treated as independent per-part objects, each with its own bounding box (Sec. 3.5, "Object measurement and annotation"). Propagated to intermediate frames by linear interpolation of translation/rotation/joint angles, then refined by a differentiable-renderer (SoftRas) optimization: "L_2D and L_d are computed by SoftRas, which penalize the silhouette discrepancy between the rendered object mask and the ground truth mask... and the depth residual between the cropped real depth map and the depth map of rendered reconstructed mesh," plus a Chamfer-distance term L_cd "between the reconstructed object point cloud and the collected object point cloud," and a temporal-consistency term L_tc; articulated joint angles are constrained "within the physical limits of each joint" (Sec. 3.5, "Pose optimization").
- hand-pose loss terms (App. A.4) — recovered by OCR from papers/md/hoi4d_2022.ocr.md (OCR text is noisier than the layout parse, symbols may be imperfect). Initialization-stage total loss (Eq. 1): "θ̂h = argmin_θh (λjLj +λ2DL2D +λdLd+λpcLpc+λmLm)". Refinement-stage total loss (Eq. 2): "θ̂h = argmin_θh (λjLj + λdLd + λpcLpc + λmLm + λContactLContact + λtcLtc)". **Joint angle loss** (Eq. 4): "Lj = Σ_{i=1}^{45} max(θi −θm[i], 0) + max(θm[i] −θi, 0)" where θi/θi (OCR renders both bounds identically — likely θ_i_lower/θ_i_upper in the original, ambiguous from OCR) are the lower/upper limits of joint angle parameter θm[i]. **2D joint loss** (Eq. 5): "L2D = Σ_{i=1}^{21} c[i]‖proj(Jθm[i]) −Janno[i]‖²2", terms as previously quoted. **Mask loss** (Eq. 6): "Lm = 1 − ‖Îm ⊗Im‖1 / ‖Îm ⊕Im −Îm ⊗Im‖1" where ⊕/⊗ are pixel-wise sum/product (OCR's operator assignment vs. the prose "pixelwise product/sum" may be swapped — quoted as OCR gives it). **Depth loss** (Eq. 7): "Ld = Σ_{p∈Îm⊗Im} ‖D̂p −Dp‖1", l1 depth loss over pixels where the rendered and ground-truth masks intersect. **Point cloud loss**: "chamfer-distance from the rendered mano hand vertices to the ground truth point cloud which refers to the depth image cropped by 2D hand mask" (prose only, no separate equation number given in OCR either). **Contact loss** L_Contact (Eq. 8, recovered): "LContact = λRLR + (1 −λR)LA" where attraction loss LA "penalize[s] hand vertices near the object's surface but are not in contact," repulsion loss LR "penalizes hand and object interpenetration," and the contact weighting coefficient λR ∈ [0,1] balances them, with λR = 1 when the action-segmentation label indicates no hand-object interaction ("These terms is manually tuned for different contact modes"). This settles what the layout parse could not: LR and LA themselves are still not given as separate closed-form equations anywhere in either parse (only described in prose) — that part of the original caveat still holds.
- no explicit penetration-depth or intersection-volume statistic is reported on the released annotations themselves — contact handling here is purely a fitting-time regularizer (like H2O's L_phy), not a released per-frame metric.

## Method (dataset + 3 benchmark tasks)
- paradigm: dataset + three baselined tasks, no RL/IL method proposed by the dataset paper itself (Sec. 6): (1) category-level object and part pose tracking (baseline: CAPTRA); (2) 4D point-cloud video semantic segmentation; (3) fine-grained video action segmentation (baseline: ASFormer). 7:3 random train/test split over the 4,000 sequences (Sec. 6).
- cross-dataset evaluation (Sec. 5, Table 2), verbatim setup: hand pose vs H2O using Mesh Graphormer backbone, "root-relative MPJPE(mm), lower is better"; object pose vs NOCS on the "bottle" category using CAPTRA, "5°5cm accuracy, higher is better"; action segmentation vs GTEA using ASFormer on 5 shared classes (take, open, pour, close, put), "frame-wise accuracy, higher is better." Numbers: hand pose train\test — HOI4D→HOI4D 22.3, HOI4D→H2O 70.4, HOI4D+H2O→HOI4D 24.3; H2O→HOI4D 48.9, H2O→H2O 19.9, HOI4D+H2O→H2O 15.9. Object pose — HOI4D→HOI4D 55.3, HOI4D→NOCS 34.2, HOI4D+NOCS→HOI4D 57.1; NOCS→HOI4D 50.4, NOCS→NOCS 70.5, HOI4D+NOCS→NOCS 83.7. Action segmentation — HOI4D→HOI4D 52.3, HOI4D→GTEA 7.9, HOI4D+GTEA→HOI4D 48.6; GTEA→HOI4D 14.1, GTEA→GTEA 77.4, HOI4D+GTEA→GTEA 90.4. Interpretation quoted: "we observe an error increase of 2.2× (from 22.3 to 48.9) due to domain gaps. However, when we evaluate the H2O trained model on HOI4D, a more severe error increase of 3.5× happens, suggesting models trained on H2O generalize much worse than on HOI4D... This suggests HOI4D complements H2O better than the opposite." (Sec. 5).

## Evaluation (in-corpus benchmark tasks, Sec. 6; 7:3 random sequence split)
- **6.1 Category-level object/part pose tracking.** Baselines: BundleTrack (generic CAD-free 6D pose tracking) and a point-to-plane ICP (Open3D) baseline, both initialized from a perturbed ground-truth pose, "the same [protocol] as in prior work" (BundleTrack, CAPTRA refs). Metrics, verbatim: "5°5cm: percentage of estimates with orientation error <5° and translation error <5cm. R_err: mean orientation error in degrees. T_err: mean translation error in centimeters." 4 rigid categories (toycar, mug, bottle, bowl) + 1 articulated (laptop: keyboard, display parts). Table 3 (5°5cm / R_err / T_err): ICP — toycar 0.7/88.3/47.6, mug 1.2/67.9/20.1, bottle 2.1/53.9/28.4, bowl 3.2/39.4/23.9; BundleTrack — toycar 9.7/21.0/13.9, mug 12.9/57.5/4.1, bottle 19.3/18.16/7.7, bowl 22.6/19.37/5.6. Table 4 (laptop parts): ICP — keyboard 0.9/47.3/19.8, display 1.5/84.2/41.2; BundleTrack — keyboard 24.2/8.6/6.8, display 12.2/20.5/9.47. Cross-reference: "BundleTrack can achieve an accuracy of 86.5 (5°5cm) on the NOCS dataset... Now it only reaches 19.3 on HOI4D" (bottle category) — attributed to "sensor noise, complex backgrounds, hand occlusions, as well as fast motions."
- **6.2 4D point-cloud video semantic segmentation.** 38 semantic categories total in the indoor scenes, split into 2 groups (objects, background); a curated subset of 376 videos across 14 categories (7 objects + 7 background) is used, 4,096 points/frame, no colour. Metric: mIoU per group and overall. Baselines PSTNet and P4Transformer (outdoor-4D-segmentation SOTA at the time). Table 5 (objects / background / mIoU_all): PSTNet 31.4 / 72.6 / 52.0; P4Transformer 44.6 / 77.7 / 61.2. "existing methods perform significantly better on the background categories than the object categories since the object categories have smaller sizes, more flexible movements, and more severe occlusion problems."
- **6.3 Fine-grained video action segmentation.** I3D features (2048-d/frame, extracted per Sec. 5.1, 15 fps) feed MS-TCN, MS-TCN++, and ASFormer. Metrics: frame-wise accuracy (Acc), segmental edit distance, segmental F1 at IoU overlap thresholds 10/25/50%. Table 6 (Acc / Edit / F1@10 / F1@25 / F1@50): MS-TCN 44.2/74.7/55.6/47.8/31.8; MS-TCN++ 42.2/75.8/54.7/46.5/30.3; ASFormer 46.8/80.3/58.9/51.3/35.0 (best of the three). Cross-dataset contrast: "[ASFormer] can only achieve an accuracy of 46.8 on HOI4D but can obtain 85.6 on 50Salads" — interpreted as "the current network learns more about the order of actions but lacks the ability to perceive the current action itself."
- baselines beaten: n/a in the sense that these are all external SOTA methods being stress-tested by HOI4D, not a HOI4D-native method beating priors; the paper's point in all three tasks is that outdoor/synthetic/instance-level SOTA degrades sharply on HOI4D's real, occluded, egocentric, category-level setting (explicit in each subsection's discussion, quoted above).
- real robot?: none — HOI4D itself is a human-capture dataset with no robot execution.

## Appendix D: HOI4D → dexterous-hand imitation-learning feasibility study (not a robot benchmark, a sim-transfer demo)
App. D defines a "category-level dexterous manipulation" task, **Pick Up**, in a SAPIEN simulator (timestep 0.05, ManiSkill-style environment) with an Adroit Hand (30 DoF) picking up toy-car category objects (App. D.1). Success = toy car brought to a target height and held static for a period; episode time limit 200 steps. Observation: joint angles+velocities of the Adroit Hand, global root position+velocity, and an 8-camera (4 close/90°-apart + 4 far) point cloud (xyz + a hand/car segmentation channel) downsampled to 1,200 points. Action: velocity-controller targets for all 30 Adroit Hand joints, normalized to (-1,1). Reward: 3-stage — (i) palm-to-object distance + palm-orientation alignment, (ii) relative-velocity discouragement + fingertip-to-object-mesh distance, (iii) height-based reward once lifted (App. D.1, reward described only in prose, not as an equation with explicit weights).
Demonstrations: HOI4D human MANO (51-DoF) hand poses are retargeted to the 30-DoF Adroit Hand via a DexMV-inspired task-space-vector optimization (15 vectors: inter-fingertip + wrist-to-fingertip, à la DexPilot) (App. D.2, "Hand joint retargeting"), then combined with CAD-model object poses into state-only demonstrations, then differentiated (cubic-spline fit per joint angle) into state-action demonstrations.
Baselines: SAC (RL) vs GAIL (IL, using SAC internally, "carefully select 12 demonstrations with high quality from different toy cars as our expert trajectories") (App. D.3), both with a PointNet+Transformer backbone (ManiSkill's baseline architecture).
Results (Table 10, 3 random seeds, mean success rate % (std)): RL (SAC) 3.5 (3.2); GAIL 17.4 (7.9). "the IL algorithm can outperform the RL algorithm... success rate of RL and IL algorithms are both low," attributed to using raw point-cloud rather than privileged ground-truth state as observation. This is a feasibility demo on one object category (toy car) with only 12 demonstrations, not a general dexterous-manipulation benchmark result — cite it only as "HOI4D can bootstrap Adroit-hand IL on a single category with 12 demos, 17.4% success," not as a general claim about HOI4D-derived policies.

## Limitations stated by the authors
"The main limitation of HOI4D is that human manipulation tasks with two hands are not covered since the single-hand manipulation tasks remain challenging for current research. Furthermore, the two-handed setting brings more challenges that we hope to study in the future such as cooperation of hands." (Sec. 7). This directly means HOI4D is **not** a two-hands-jointly-annotated dataset — every sequence is single-hand. Also implicit from the method sections: hand-pose optimization "may fail due to ambiguous hand poses or a bad initialization" requiring manual correction (Sec. 3.4); object-pose annotation is only manual every 10 frames with the rest propagated/optimized, "not all annotations are fully reliable" (Sec. 3.5).

## Notes for the survey
- Feeds the datasets section as a large egocentric category-level (not instance-level) HOI corpus, with articulated-object coverage (9 of 16 categories) that most other single-hand egocentric datasets lack; useful as the "category-level, no two-hand claim" contrast to grab_2020/oakink_2022/h2o_dataset_2021.
- Corpus papers citing HOI4D (grep -l "HOI4D" over papers/md, ≤15 cap; 11 hits, all appear to be genuine citations of this dataset — no acronym collisions found as with H2O/OmniH2O): artigrasp_2023, being_h0_2025, arctic_2022, being_h05_2026, egodex_2025, gigahands_2024, groot_n1_2025, hot3d_2024, oakink2_2024, taco_2024, unidex_2026.
- Two internal numeric contradictions must be flagged wherever HOI4D scale is quoted in the survey: (1) participant count is "4" in the Abstract vs "9" in Sec. 1 body text; (2) task count is "54" in Sec. 4 vs "76" in App. A.3. Do not silently pick one; cite both with their locations if the number matters to an argument.
- App. D is directly relevant to this project's own MANO→dexterous-hand retargeting interest (DexPilot-style task-space-vector retargeting from HOI4D's MANO fits to a 30-DoF Adroit Hand, then GAIL beating SAC 17.4% vs 3.5% success on a single toy-car category with 12 demos) — a small, early, single-category precedent for "human video/mocap to dexterous-hand IL," worth citing in the survey's retargeting-pipeline section alongside DexMV (which it explicitly builds on).
