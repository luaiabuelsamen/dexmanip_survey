# dydexhandover_2025 — DyDexHandover: Human-like Dynamic Dexterous Bimanual Handover using RGB-only Perception (Zhou, You, Wang [Xiaomi Robotics], arXiv 2025)

sources: papers/md/dydexhandover_2025.md [sha256 b7985b6d] ; no code

## One-line contribution
Multi-agent RL (MAPPO under CTDE) trains a thrower and a catcher policy on a dual-arm humanoid to perform dynamic bimanual object throw-and-catch handover from monocular RGB only, with the thrower regularized toward a pre-trained human-demonstration policy via KL divergence for human-like motion (Sec III, Sec III-C).

## Setting
- hand(s): dexterous hand at each arm's end, 11 DoF (6 actuated) per the intro ("a 11-DOFs (6 active) dexterous hand"); arm: 7-DoF per arm; the whole system is the upper body of the "M92C" humanoid, "18-DOFs (7 for the arm and 11 for the hand) on each side" (Fig. 2 caption). Bimanual: yes — one arm-hand throws, the other catches.
- simulator / physics: NVIDIA Isaac Sim / "NVIDIA Isaac Lab framework [31]" (Sec IV-A). Physics runs at 120 Hz, both agents controlled at 60 Hz (Sec IV-A: "The physics simulator runs at 120 Hz, with both agents controlled at 60 Hz").
- observation: monocular head-mounted RGB camera only ("a headmounted monocular RGB camera", Sec I; "A monocular RGB camera is mounted on the robot's head", Sec IV-A). Visual features f ∈ R^8 from a pretrained CBAM-based ResNet [29] backbone + 2-layer MLP (Sec III-B1). Thrower observes current f; catcher observes a temporal stack τf = {f_t, ..., f_{t-5}} (6 past steps) "to implicitly capture additional velocity-related features" (Sec III-A2). Local state also includes target position p_target ∈ R^3 and joint positions q^throw, q^catch ∈ R^13 (Sec III-A2). Global (critic-only, CTDE) state additionally has joint velocities q̇^throw, q̇^catch ∈ R^13 and privileged object position p_obj ∈ R^3 and linear velocity v_obj ∈ R^3, obtained from the simulator (Sec III-A2: "We can obtain this additional privileged information from the simulation").
- action space: joint-space, per agent: 7-DoF arm via "desired joint velocities" q̇_d,arm ∈ R^7, and 6 actuated hand DoF via "desired joint positions" q_d,hand ∈ R^6 (Sec III-A1). Decimation: 2 (Table I).
- objects / data: 9 training objects (Cone, Cube, Big Cube, Cuboid A/B/C, Sphere, Big Sphere, Cylinder) and 9 unseen test objects (Dex Cube, Chef Can, Sugar Box, Bottle, Fish Can, Meat Can, Long Cone, Capsule, Long Capsule) (Table III, Fig. 3). Human demonstrations: "a small number of human throwing demonstrations" collected via teleoperation of the right arm in sim, recording (A^throw, q^throw, p_target, p_obj, I) (Sec III-B). Exact demonstration count not stated.

## Method
- paradigm: RL (multi-agent) with a supervised-pretraining stage for a human policy and a vision encoder used as a regularizer; "Human-Regularized MAPPO" (Algorithm 1, Sec III-C).
- algorithm: MAPPO under a CTDE (centralized training, decentralized execution) architecture, modeled as a Dec-POMDP with two agents, thrower and catcher (Sec III, III-A). Two added components on top of vanilla MAPPO: (1) human policy regularization via KL divergence between the thrower's Gaussian policy π ~ N(μ, σ²) and the pretrained human policy; (2) "hybrid advantage estimation" A_hybrid(s_t, s_{t+1}, a_t) = A_GAE(s_t, a_t) + β1·A_I(s_t, s_{t+1}) + β2·A_N, where A_I(s_t, s_{t+1}) = min(V(s_{t+1}) − V(s_t), 0) is an "internal advantage function introduced in [12]" and A_N ~ N(0,1) is injected exploration noise (Sec III-C2, Eq. 14).
- key trick(s): pretraining a human-demonstration policy π* (3-layer MLP, MSE loss, Adam lr 1e-4, 10k epochs) from teleoperated throws, then using KL-divergence regularization against it during RL so the thrower's throwing motion stays human-like and generalizes better to unseen objects (Sec III-B2, III-C1); pretraining the CBAM-ResNet visual encoder separately via MSE regression to object area-ratio δ and mask centroid (x_c, y_c) labels, lr 1e-4 (Sec III-B1).

**A. Embodiment block**
- hand: 11-DoF dexterous hand (6 actuated) per side, on the "M92C-series humanoid robot" (Sec I, Sec IV-A); vendor not stated.
- arm: 7-DoF per arm; floating base not stated; bimanual: yes (upper-body dual-arm humanoid, Fig. 2 caption: "upper body of the M92C humanoid, which features 18-DOFs (7 for the arm and 11 for the hand) on each side").
- simulator + version: NVIDIA Isaac Sim, built on "the NVIDIA Isaac Lab framework [31]" (Sec IV-A); specific version not stated.
- sim timestep / control rate: physics at 120 Hz, agents controlled at 60 Hz (Sec IV-A); decimation 2 (Table I).
- number of parallel envs: not stated.
- GPU / wall-clock: "Intel i9-13900K CPU and NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU" (Sec IV-A). Wall-clock training time not stated; ablation training curves are reported vs. "200k steps" (Fig. 6 caption).

**B. Learning block**
- paradigm: RL (MAPPO) for the throw/catch policies; supervised regression (MSE, Adam) for the human-policy and vision-encoder pretraining (Sec III-B).
- algorithm and implementation: MAPPO (own training loop, Algorithm 1); no repo/library named — "not stated" beyond "MAPPO algorithm" (Sec I contribution 2, Sec III-C).
- teacher/privileged->vision distillation: not a teacher-student distillation in the classic sense; rather the critic (CTDE) sees privileged object position/velocity and joint velocities that the actor never sees (Sec III-A2). Separately, a human-demonstration policy is used only as a KL regularization target for the thrower, not distilled into it.
- observation vector: see Setting above (per-agent local state = p_target, q^throw/q^catch ∈ R^13, and f ∈ R^8 or τf for the catcher); exact concatenation/order beyond this is not stated (equations were not recovered from the parsed PDF markdown).
- action space: as above — 7-dim joint-velocity targets (arm) + 6-dim joint-position targets (hand), per agent (Sec III-A1).
- domain randomisation (Table II, "Randomization Parameters"): Object Mass ~ μ(0.3, 0.5) sampling; Object Color ~ μ(RGB 0–1) sampling; Robot Joint Stiffness ~ μ(0.75, 1.5) scaling; Joint Damping ~ μ(0.75, 1.5) scaling; Restitution ~ μ(−0.04, 0.04) additive; Friction ~ μ(−0.04, 0.04) additive; Observation Noise ~ N(0.0, 0.02) additive; Observation Bias ~ N(0.0, 0.001) additive; Action Noise ~ N(0.0, 0.002) additive; Action Bias ~ N(0.0, 0.0001) additive; Environment Background: p = 0.3, N(0,1) additive.

## Evaluation
- **C. Reward / objective block** (paper-only; no code released for this key). Quoted verbatim from Sec III-A3, "Reward Design": "Given the distances l_target (object to target), l_obj (object to left palm), l_hand (right palm to left palm), a boolean f_contact indicating left-hand contact with the object, the number of robot joints n, and the torque of the joint τ. We design five types of reward terms:" — named "Distance Reward" ("Encourage the robot to move the object toward the target point"), "Catching Reward" ("Encourages the left hand to get closer to the object"), "Contact Reward" ("Encourage the catcher to make contact with the object"), "Action Penalty" ("To encourage smooth and energy-efficient control commands, we apply a penalty based on the instantaneous joint power"), and "Hand Proximity Penalty" ("To prevent the policy from exploiting direct hand-to-hand transfers in the overlapping workspace, we penalize configurations where the two hands are too close"). The individual per-term formulas, previously rendered as images and not recovered as text in papers/md/dydexhandover_2025.md, were recovered by OCR from papers/md/dydexhandover_2025.ocr.md (OCR text is noisier than the layout parse, symbols may be imperfect): "Rdist = exp(−10 · ltarget)" (Eq. 5); "Robj = exp(−10 · lobj)" (Eq. 6); "Rcontact = I(fcontact)" (Eq. 7); "Paction = −Σ_{j=1}^{n} (τj q̇j)^2" (Eq. 8); "Phand = −exp(−10 · lhand)" (Eq. 9). Combined weighted sum, previously recovered as text: "R = 4R_dist + 0.5R_obj + R_contact + 0.0001P_action + P_hand" (Eq. 10, Sec III-A3). This single shared reward R feeds both the thrower and the catcher — the paper does not define separate reward expressions for "the throwing (giver) policy" vs. "the catching (receiver) policy"; it states plainly "Both hands share aligned objectives, forming a fully cooperative relationship" and "each policy... receives feedback through a shared reward" (Sec III, opening paragraph). On top of this shared reward, the thrower's training objective additionally includes the KL-divergence human-policy regularization term (Sec III-C1) — recovered by OCR from papers/md/dydexhandover_2025.ocr.md (OCR text is noisier than the layout parse, symbols may be imperfect): the KL divergence (Eq. 12) "DKL(π∗‖πthrow) = Eathrow∈Athrow[log(π∗(a∗|o∗)/πthrow(athrow|othrow))] = log(σthrow/σ∗) + (σ∗2 + (µ∗−µthrow)2)/(2σthrow2) − 1/2", and the regularized throwing-policy objective (Eq. 13) "J(θthrow) = (1 −λreg)·J(θthrow) −λreg·DKL(π∗‖πthrow)" where "λreg denotes the regularization weight, which balances the degree of constraint imposed by the human policy on the throwing policy."
- **D. Contact / penetration handling**: not addressed as a penetration term. Contact appears only as a positive shaping signal ("Contact Reward... Encourage the catcher to make contact with the object", using boolean f_contact, Sec III-A3) and as a failure-mechanism trigger: "Unexpected contact: Any unintended arm contact with the environment or self-collisions triggers a reset" (Sec III-A4). No interpenetration measurement or penalty term is defined; contact solver settings not stated.
- **E. Evaluation block**: Two metrics defined verbatim (Sec IV-A): "1) Hit Rate. This metric is defined as the proportion of objects that successfully hit the hand palm of catcher." and "2) Success Rate. This metric is defined as the proportion of objects that are successfully caught by the catcher and remain in hand until the end of the episode." Episode termination / failure conditions (Sec III-A4): object falls below z_obj < 0.1 m; left-palm-to-target deviation exceeds 0.4 m; hand-to-hand distance l_hand < 0.1 m (reset, "to prevent direct hand-to-hand object transfer"); unexpected/self contact; object out of camera view. Max episode time: 3 s (Table I). Eval protocol (Sec IV-B): "the trained policy is rolled out for 1,000 episodes, with objects randomly sampled from the corresponding set in each episode" — i.e. N = 1,000 episodes for the training-object set and N = 1,000 episodes for the unseen-object set. Headline numbers (Table III, "Evaluation results on predefined metrics"): training objects (9 objects) — Hit Rate avg. 100%, Success Rate avg. 99% (per-object success rate 98–100%, e.g. Cube 99%, Big Sphere 98%, Sphere/Cone 100%); unseen objects (9 objects) — Hit Rate avg. 92%, Success Rate avg. 75% (per-object range 40% Bottle to 98% Long Cone). All numbers are simulation only — there is no real-robot evaluation anywhere in this paper; the conclusion states real-robot transfer is future work ("Future work will explore incorporating safety mechanisms into the policy learning process and further enhancing policy generalization to enable transfer to real robots", Sec V). Ablation (Table V, training-curve source Fig. 6, "Each method was trained for 200k steps"): Ours (Hit/Success, train/unseen) 100%/99% train, 92%/75% unseen; "w.o. Human-Reg." 100%/97% train, 65%/53% unseen; "w.o. Hybrid Adv." 100%/99% train, 86%/74% unseen; "w.o. Multi-Agent" (i.e., single-agent PPO) 89%/79% train, 78%/51% unseen; "Open-Loop" 40%/0% train, X/X unseen (not evaluated). Baselines (Table IV, "Different baselines on Bimanual Dexterous Dynamic Handover") are quoted from other papers, not re-run: Bi-DexHands [11] (State obs, Abreast Catch, PPO/MAPPO/HAPPO, 1 train/1 test obj, success rate "unknown"); Dynamic Handover [4] (RGB-D, Underarm Catch, MAPPO, 11/14 objs, 95%/37%); DexCatch [12] (Point Cloud, Abreast Catch, PPO, 9/11 objs, 72.95%/77.89%); Bimanual Catch [28] (State, Bimanual Catch, HAPPO, 15/15 objs, "unknown"); DyDexHandover (Ours) (RGB, Abreast Catch, MAPPO, 9/9 objs, 99%/75%).

## Limitations stated by the authors
"Considering the dynamic of the environment and the discrepancies between simulation and the real world, these instabilities may pose safety risks during actual deployment. Future work will explore incorporating safety mechanisms into the policy learning process and further enhancing policy generalization to enable transfer to real robots." (Sec V). No real-robot deployment attempted or reported.

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "Both hands share aligned objectives, forming a fully cooperative relationship." (Sec III)
- "the throwing policy is guided by a human-policy regularization scheme, encouraging fluid and natural motion, and enhancing the policy's generalization capability" (Abstract)
- "DyDexHandover achieves nearly 99% success on training objects and 75% on unseen objects" (Abstract)
- "To our knowledge, it is the first method to realize dual-arm in-air handover using only raw RGB perception." (Abstract)
- "without incorporating human policy regularization, the policy tends to overfit the training objects and generates unnatural actions, which in turn degrades its generalization capability" (Sec IV-C)
