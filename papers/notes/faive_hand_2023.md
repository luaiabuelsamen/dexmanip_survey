# faive_hand_2023 — Getting the Ball Rolling: Learning a Dexterous Policy for a Biomimetic Tendon-Driven Hand with Rolling Contact Joints (Toshimitsu, Forrai et al.; IEEE Humanoids 2023)

sources: papers/md/faive_hand_2023.md [13feb753] ; code/md/faive_hand_2023.md [e46ae11f, github.com/srl-ethz/faive_gym_oss]

## One-line contribution
Introduces the Faive Hand "Proto 0" — a biomimetic, tendon-driven hand whose joints (except the thumb CMC) are rolling-contact joints with no fixed rotation axis — models the joints in IsaacGym via linked virtual hinge pairs, and achieves zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of an RL policy for single-axis in-hand sphere rotation.

## Hand record
- maker / lab: Yasunori Toshimitsu, Benedek Forrai, Barnabas Gavin Cangan, Ulrich Steger, Manuel Knecht, Stefan Weirich, Robert K. Katzschmann — Soft Robotics Lab, ETH Zurich (title page, affiliations); this design lineage was later commercialised by mimic robotics (per bib "why" field, not the paper itself).
- DoF / actuated DoF: "It contains 11 actuatable degrees of freedom, with 3 in the thumb and 2 for each of the other fingers. Each finger contains a coupled joint at the distal end, and thus there are 16 joints in total" (Sec. II-A). Per finger: DIP+PIP are coupled by a tendon and driven together as one joint pair (1 flexor + 1 extensor tendon drive both), MCP is actuated antagonistically by one motor with two tendons — so 2 actuated DoF per non-thumb finger but 3 physical joints (DIP, PIP, MCP) per finger, 16 joints total across 4 fingers + thumb. Thumb CMC is "recreated using 2 hinge joints" (not a rolling-contact joint, unlike the rest, Sec. II-A).
- actuation type: tendon-driven with rolling-contact joints — "composed of two articulating bodies with adjacent curved contact surfaces connected by a pair of crosswise ligament strings... (like the children's toy Jacob's ladder)" (Sec. II-A). Tendons controlled by "16 Dynamixel XC330-T288-T servo motors. 6 of them have two tendons attached antagonistically" (Sec. II-C-a). No internal joint-angle encoders: "the hand currently does not have internal joint angle encoders, which are being developed for a later version" — joint angles are instead estimated from tendon length via geometric modelling plus an extended Kalman filter (EKF) (Sec. I-C, II-C-b).
- cost of BOM: not stated as a figure. The paper only frames cost qualitatively/comparatively: "The Shadow Hand is by far the most humanlike hand, but it comes with a steep price tag of 110k GBP as quoted from their website" (Sec. I-B-b), and states the aim "to eventually provide a low-cost platform" (Sec. II intro) without ever quoting a BOM number for the Faive Hand itself. The bib entry likewise carries no cost figure. Do not attribute a dollar cost to this hand from this source.
- build time: not stated.
- weight: "at a total weight of the hand system of only 1.1 kg" (Sec. II-A).
- fingertip / grip force: "The Faive Hand can grasp a payload of up to 10 kg, demonstrated here with a dumbbell" using "a downwards facing five-fingered power grasp" (Sec. II-A, Fig. 3a) — a whole-hand payload figure, not a per-fingertip force measurement.
- tactile sensing: none on the physical hand. Note: the RL observation table (Table II) lists a simulated "fingertip force" (15-dim, critic-only, privileged) — this is a simulated force sensor used only for training, not a physical tactile sensor on Proto 0 (no tactile hardware is described anywhere in Sec. II).
- control interface and rate: low-level control converts joint-angle commands to Dynamixel servo-motor commands via an analytically derived tendon-length function l = f(q) from CAD geometry (Sec. II-C-a); joint angles are recovered from tendon-length measurements using an EKF (Sec. II-C-b), calibrated at boot against a jig that holds the hand in a known pose (Sec. II-C-b, end). In simulation/training: "The simulation was run at 60 Hz, while the policy ran every three steps, resulting in a 20 Hz policy" (Sec. IV-A). The trained actor MLP is exported to ONNX and run on the real robot (Sec. III end) — no separate real-robot control-loop Hz is stated beyond this 20 Hz policy rate. Code repo `faive_gym_oss` is the IsaacGym training environment, built on IsaacGymEnvs (README); it is explicitly distinguished from the (unparsed, not in this corpus) real-robot control repo `faive_franka_control` referenced for running exported policies on hardware (README, "Export a trained RL policy").
- licence: a `LICENSE` and separate `LICENSE-NVIDIA` file exist in the repo's top level (code/md file tree) but neither's content/type was captured by the parser — not stated which licence(s) apply.
- what the paper demonstrates: zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of a closed-loop RL policy for single-axis (y-axis) in-hand rotation of a sphere, trained in IsaacGym and deployed on the physical Faive Hand, evaluated with and without domain randomization (DR) and in a reversed-rotation-direction condition (Sec. IV). Also reports the whole-hand 10 kg payload grasp (Fig. 3a) as a hardware capability demo, and a failed attempt at cube reorientation on the real robot (Conclusion) as a negative result.

## Setting
- hand(s): Faive Hand "Proto 0" (11 actuatable DoF, 16 joints).
- simulator / physics: IsaacGym, GPU-based, "4096 environments were simulated in parallel on a single NVIDIA A10G GPU," 60 Hz sim / 20 Hz policy (Sec. IV-A). Rolling-contact joints are modelled as two "virtual" hinge joints whose axes run through each rolling cylinder's axis, "constrained to rotate together in the MJCF... file by linking them together with tendon/fixed elements" — the MJCF tendon-constraint info is preserved when loaded into IsaacGym via limit stiffness/damping settings on the tendon properties (Sec. II-B). README confirms: "Prepare a MJCF (URDF) model of your robot... Even models that can be perfectly simulated in MuJoCo might not work in IsaacGym."
- observation: Table II — actor observations: joint pos command (11), joint pos history (55, past 5 steps), previous actions (11); critic-only (privileged) additions: joint pos (11), joint velocity (11), joint torque (11), object pos (3), object quat (4), object linear/angular vel (3+3), fingertip position/quaternion/lin vel/ang vel/force (15/20/15/15/15). Actor uses only proprioceptive joint data — no object pose — "simplifying the sim2real process" given the sphere's rotational symmetry (Sec. III-B).
- action space: relative joint-angle change, clipped to [-1,1], scaled by a max joint speed v_max = 5 rad/s and timestep Δt, then clipped to the robot's [q_min, q_max] range (Sec. III-C, quoted formula referenced but not reproduced verbatim in the parsed text beyond the description).
- objects / data: a spherical toy ("gachapon capsule") ball instrumented with an Arduino Nano 33 BLE IMU+Bluetooth for independent (non-policy) rotation measurement via a Madgwick filter (Sec. IV-B).

## Method
- paradigm: model-free RL (A2C/PPO) with asymmetric actor-critic observations, sim-to-real zero-shot transfer.
- reward or loss (Table I, quoted terms and weights):
  - Object rotation: `min(∓ω_y + 1.2, ±ω_y + 4)`, weight 0.01, "reward the rotation in the y axis (can be reversed)" — max reward when y-axis angular velocity is between ∓3 and ∓1 rad/s, linearly decreasing outside that band (Sec. III-A). Computed from a numerically-differentiated object-orientation signal rather than IsaacGym's native (noisy) angular-velocity measurement, because the native signal let policies "exploit the physics of the simulator" without truly rotating the sphere (Sec. III-A).
  - Torque penalty: `-||τ||_2`, weight -0.02, "prevent joints from applying large torques."
  - Action penalty: `-||a||_2`, weight -0.002, "prevent large actions."
  - Drop penalty: triggered when `||x_obj - x_hand|| > 24 cm`, weight -1.0, "penalize object drops (one-time penalty after which environment is reset)."
  - Algorithm: "advantage actor-critic (A2C) using asymmetric observations... the PPO algorithm [25] with the implementation from the open-source repository rl_games [26]," actor/critic as separate MLPs, 4 hidden layers [512,512,256,128], ELU activations (Sec. III intro, IV-A).
- key trick(s): analytic tendon-length-to-joint-angle geometric model f(q) derived from CAD via symbolic differentiation (sympy) for the muscle Jacobian; EKF-based joint-angle estimation from tendon-length measurements (no joint encoders); scaling real-robot joint-position measurements by 0.5 before feeding the policy, found empirically to compensate for tendon stretch under contact load and better match sim behavior (Sec. IV-B).

## Evaluation
- metrics (exact definitions): training reward and mean y-axis object angular velocity, averaged over 9 training runs per condition with ±σ bands (Sec. IV-A, Fig. 5). Real-robot evaluation: distribution of ball angular velocity ω_y measured independently via the embedded IMU/Bluetooth ball, compared against the reward's "target region" band (Sec. IV-B, Fig. 6).
- headline numbers: policies trained "in about one hour on a single NVIDIA A10G GPU" (Sec. I-B-a). In sim, DR and non-DR policies reach similar mean ω_y, but DR has larger variance; "the non-DR policy fails to rotate the sphere, just rocking it back and forth" on the real robot, while "the DR policy succeeds in consistently rotating the ball, achieving the target rotational velocity for the majority of the measurements" — the reversed-direction DR policy also succeeds on hardware (Sec. IV-B). No single scalar success-rate number is given; the result is reported as a velocity distribution against the target band (Fig. 6).
- baselines beaten: DR vs. no-DR ablation only (no other hand or algorithm is benchmarked head-to-head in this paper).
- real robot? yes — the closed-loop policy is deployed on the physical Faive Hand Proto 0 for sphere rotation (Sec. IV-B); cube reorientation was also attempted on hardware and failed (Conclusion, quoted below), reported as a negative result.

## Limitations stated by the authors
- Attempted x/z-axis ball rotation failed to train a consistent policy in simulation: "We attribute this phenomenon to the lack of joints for abduction and adduction on this hand prototype" (Sec. IV-A).
- No joint encoders: joint angles must be estimated via tendon length + EKF, which is imperfect, especially under contact — the real-robot policy required an empirical 0.5x scaling correction on joint-position observations to compensate (Sec. IV-B).
- Real-seed variance was much larger on hardware than in sim; "Some policies even stopped moving the finger after a few seconds... getting stuck in a hand pose where the policy outputs zero values for actions," requiring cherry-picking the best seed per condition (Sec. IV-B).
- Cube reorientation (quoted, Conclusion/Sec. V): "the policy did work in simulation, but failed on the real robot... We attribute the failure on the real robot to a multitude of factors, such as poor joint angle measurement from the EKFs, especially when there is contact, and a lack of proper system identification to ensure accurate actuation dynamics in the IsaacGym simulator."
