# simpler_2024 — Evaluating Real-World Robot Manipulation Policies in Simulation (Li, Hsu, Gu, Pertsch, Mees, ... Su, Vuong, Xiao; CoRL 2024)

sources: papers/md/simpler_2024.md [6a9481f3] ; code/md/simpler_2024.md [06accaca]

## One-line contribution
SIMPLER: purpose-built SAPIEN environments for the Google Robot (RT-series) and WidowX (BridgeData V2) setups in which policies trained on real data are evaluated; with system-identified controllers and "Visual Matching" (green-screened real backgrounds + baked object/robot textures), simulated success rates rank policies the way ~1500 paired real episodes do (MMRV 0.056 avg, Pearson r 0.924 avg on Google Robot, Table I).

## Setting
- hand(s): none dexterous. Parallel-jaw grippers on the Google Robot (mobile manipulator from RT-1) and the WidowX-250 6DOF (Appendix B). Single arm.
- simulator / physics: SAPIEN (Sec. V); reproduced in Isaac Sim for the ablation (Sec. VI-D, Fig. 10, Table XI). Simulation frequency 500 Hz (Google Robot, Algorithm 1) / 501 Hz (WidowX, Algorithm 2); control frequency 3 Hz Google Robot "following [6]", 5 Hz WidowX (Algorithms 1-2; README: "Google Robot environments use a control frequency of 3hz, and Bridge environments use a control frequency of 5hz. Simulation frequency is ~500hz"). Contact solver: "the Projected Gauss-Seidel solver in PhysX causes mesh penetration behaviors during the process of object grasping. Thus, we enable the Temporal Gauss-Seidel solver in both SAPIEN and Isaac Sim's simulation backends" (Appendix C-B). Throughput: "A single environment renders at 3.5k simulation steps per second on a consumer NVIDIA 4090 GPU at 640 x 512 image resolution. Under a 500 Hz simulation frequency, this amounts to a 7x speedup over real eval" (Sec. V). Code README: Bridge envs ported to ManiSkill3 "can run 10-15x faster than the ManiSkill2 version" (branch maniskill3; not in the fetched code). Physical properties simplified: uniform density from "common material density in GPT-4 or Google search" or mass/volume for the empty coke can; friction "based on their common material properties" (Sec. V).
- observation: RGB only. Google Robot: "customized egocentric camera mounted on the robot head"; WidowX: "Logitech C920 third-view camera" (Appendix C-B). Camera intrinsics recovered from real video with fSpy if unknown (Sec. V).
- action space: 6D end-effector delta pose + gripper from the policy; Google Robot: IK then Ruckig time-optimal joint planning with velocity/acceleration/jerk limits (arm 1.5, 2.0, 50.0; gripper 1.0, 7.0, 50.0), small-action filter |g_a| < 0.01 (Algorithm 1). WidowX: IK to joint position target each control step (Algorithm 2). Joint PD stiffness/damping set by SysID (Sec. IV-A).
- objects / data: assets from Objaverse, 3D scans, One-2-3-45++ single-view reconstruction, or manual modelling (cabinet only); sizes matched in Blender; CoACD convex collision (Sec. V, Appendix C-B). Textures baked with the GeTex pipeline (SAM crop, pose alignment by differentiable rendering with Nvdiffrast, unprojection, optional Zero123++ novel views; Appendix C-B). Policies evaluated: RT-1 (Begin / 15% / Converged), RT-1-X, RT-2-X, Octo-Base, Octo-Small (Sec. VI-A).

## Method
- paradigm: evaluation protocol (real-to-sim); no policy is trained. Two setups (Sec. IV-B, README):
  - "Visual Matching" = "(1) green screening, i.e. segmenting out interactive simulated assets and overlaying them onto real-world backgrounds; and (2) texture matching, which involves projecting real object textures onto simulation assets and tuning robot arm colors using real videos" (Fig. 5 caption). Green screen: I' = M ⊙ I_sim + (1 − M) ⊙ I_real, with I_real the inpainted first frame of a real eval video and M the sim segmentation mask (Sec. IV-B).
  - "Variant Aggregation" = "we create a 'base' version of our simulation environment and then creating 'variants' of this environment along four axes of visual variation: background, lighting, distractors, and table texture. For each axis, we construct 2 variations of the base setup similar to [69], covering backgrounds from different rooms, lighter and darker lighting, fewer and more distractors, and solid color and complex table textures ... We average policy performance in simulation across all variants" (Appendix C-C).
- control gap / SysID (Sec. IV-A): open-loop replay of real demonstration actions in sim; loss from translation and rotation error between the real and simulated end-effector trajectories (the equations for L_sysid were lost by the PDF converter, lines 104-110); trajectories taken from the existing RT-1 and Bridge demo datasets ("do not need to collect any new data"); PD parameters (p, d) optimised by simulated annealing over a normalised search range, "3 rounds of simulated annealing", each initialised from the previous best with a reduced range. Tooling: tools/sysid/ in the repo (README "Code Structure").
- metrics (Sec. III-B): Pearson r ("range [-1, 1], higher is better") and Mean Maximum Rank Violation, "range [0, 1], lower is better" (Fig. 3). Verbal definition (the formula was dropped by the converter at line 92): "The key underlying quantity is the rank violation between two policies πi and πj, which weighs the significance of the simulator incorrectly ranking the items by the corresponding margin in real-world performance. MMRV aggregates the N^2 rank violations by averaging the worst-case rank violation for each policy." Motivation: Pearson "only assesses the linear fit" and "does not reflect the range of values it is computed over"; Spearman "disregard[s] the underlying margins between real values". Kruskal-Wallis per policy between real and sim trial-outcome vectors reported as "Kruskal-#Policy p < 0.05" for absolute-performance shift (Appendix G-B, Table XIV). Code: simpler_env/utils/metrics.py exposes mean_maximum_rank_violation(perf_sim, perf_real), pearson_correlation, print_all_kruskal_results and REAL_PERF (the real success-rate table); tools/calc_metrics.py reproduces the paper numbers (README). Only signatures are in the code md; bodies not captured.
- key trick(s): Octo sim results averaged over 3 seeds (diffusion head); Google Robot sim results averaged over 4 tuned robot arm/gripper colours "to account for changes in arm texture during real robot rollouts" (Sec. VI-A). Visual matching helps "only if applied jointly and to all parts of a scene" (Sec. VI-D, Table III).

## Evaluation
- task / success definitions and trial counts (Appendix B; counts are the real-world protocol, sim multiplies by #variants or #arm colours x #seeds):
  - "pick coke can": grasp the empty coke can and lift; 3 orientations (horizontal, vertical, standing) x 25 grid positions = 75 trials.
  - "move {obj1} near {obj2}": triplet in a triangle; 5 triplets of 8 objects x 2 patterns x 6 role assignments = 60 trials.
  - "(open/close) (top/middle/bottom) drawer": robot at 9 floor grid positions x 3 drawers x 2 = 54 trials.
  - "open top drawer; place apple into top drawer": 3 robot positions x 9 apple positions = 27 trials; instruction switched "once the robot outputs the 'terminate' token or after half of the time limit has elapsed".
  - WidowX: "put the spoon on the towel" (square of edge 15 cm, spoon horizontal/vertical, 2 x 12 = 24 trials); "put carrot on plate" (same); "stack the green block on the yellow block" (3 cm blocks, squares of 10 and 20 cm, 24 trials); "put eggplant into yellow basket" (24 trials). Partial success ("Grasp Spoon" etc.) reported alongside final success (Table V).
  - Success predicates are implemented in the ManiSkill2_real2sim envs (README "Code Structure": mani_skill2_real2sim/envs/), which are not in the fetched code md; env names are e.g. GraspSingleOpenedCokeCanInScene-v0, MoveNearGoogleBakedTexInScene-v1, OpenDrawerCustomInScene-v0, PutSpoonOnTableClothInScene-v0 (README table).
- headline numbers:
  - Table I (Google Robot, 6 checkpoints): MMRV Validation MSE 0.375 avg / VarAgg 0.143 / VisMatch 0.056; Pearson r 0.308 / 0.778 / 0.924. Per task VisMatch: Pick Coke Can MMRV 0.031 r 0.976; Move Near 0.111 / 0.855; Drawer 0.027 / 0.942.
  - Table IV (VisMatch): Pick Coke Can real vs sim RT-1 (Converged) 0.853 vs 0.857, RT-2-X 0.907 vs 0.787, Octo-Base 0.293 vs 0.170, RT-1 (Begin) 0.133 vs 0.027. Open Top Drawer & Place Apple MMRV 0.000, r 0.969. RT-1 (Begin) real drawer eval stopped after 2 trials for safety (footnotes 2-3).
  - Table V / Fig. 7 (WidowX, VisMatch): MMRV 0.000 on all but Put Carrot on Plate success (0.111); per-task r 0.575-1.000; Fig. 7 average MMRV 0.014, r 0.890.
  - Fig. 8 / Table VI (distribution-shift axes background, lighting, distractors, table texture, camera pose; RT-1 w/o and w/ augmentation): Δreal vs Δsim camera pose −0.46 / −0.47 and −0.38 / −0.39; table texture −0.39 / −0.14 and −0.17 / −0.19; MMRV 0.000 / 0.016, r 0.831 / 0.970. Solid vs patterned table: real 4% vs 25% drop, sim 2% vs 24% (Sec. VI-C).
  - Table VIII (novel real shift, arm wrapped in gift paper): Octo-Base sim range [0.000, 0.293] and real 0.293 → 0.000; RT-1-X sim [0.507, 0.653], real 0.760 → 0.520.
  - Table II (SysID ablation, Pick Coke Can): control loss 0.131 / MMRV 0.031 with SIMPLER SysID vs 0.267 / 0.070 and 0.432 / 0.100 for perturbed settings.
  - Table III (visual ablation, drawer tasks, 3 policies): full VisMatch MMRV 0.050, real-sim gap 0.136; every partial combination MMRV 0.087-0.142.
  - Table X: coke can mass 10-80 g x gripper friction 0.25-2.0 gives r 0.957-0.990, MMRV 0.031 throughout; cabinet joint friction 0.0125-0.20 gives MMRV 0.055 (0.105 at 0.15), r 0.903-0.941. Fig. 10 / Table XI: Isaac Sim VarAgg MMRV 0.058, r 0.919 vs SAPIEN 0.082 / 0.923.
  - Table XIII: adding a single-task RT-1 (real 0.680, sim 0.403) keeps MMRV 0.027, r 0.959. Table XIV: Kruskal p < 0.05 for 0-3 of 6 policies per Google Robot task, 0-1 of 3 per WidowX task.
- baselines beaten: validation action MSE as a ranking proxy (Table I, XII: WidowX Pearson r for MSE is negative on all four tasks, −0.342 to −1.000).
- real robot? Yes: all real numbers above are paired real evaluations on the Google Robot and WidowX with the trial counts in Appendix B; "over ∼1500 evaluation episodes" total (Fig. 1 caption).

## Limitations stated by the authors
(Sec. VII) rigid-object tasks only; green-screening "is limited to fixed cameras and does not accurately capture object shadows and other visual details"; pipeline "involves some manual effort in curating assets and assembling scenes", articulated objects "take the most human effort" (Sec. V). Goal is relative ranking, "a simulator will always be an imperfect proxy" (Sec. III-A).

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "the main goal of simulated evaluations is not to obtain a 1:1 reproduction of a policies' real-world behavior, but instead a strong correlation in relative policy performance between simulation and real rollouts." (Sec. III-A)
- "we show that for imitation learning, validation MSE is not a good proxy for a policy's real-world performance, leading to high MMRV and low Pearson r." (Sec. VI-B)
- "we recommend SIMPLER-'Visual Matching' as the default approach since it directly minimizes visual discrepancies between real and simulated environments." (Sec. VI-B)
- "the approaches we introduced in Section IV-B for narrowing the visual gap between simulated and real scene can significantly improve real-and-sim evaluation performance correlation, but only if applied jointly and to all parts of a scene." (Sec. VI-D)
- "for the tasks we tested, which for the most part involve rigid body manipulations, the choice of physics simulator is not critical" (Sec. VI-D)
- "altering these parameters has a moderate (≤ 15%) impact on the success rates of different policies" while MMRV/r stay good (Sec. VI-D, Table X).

## Notes for the survey (which sections this feeds; contradictions with other notes)
- Evaluation section: the argument for simulated evaluation of dexterous policies rests on this paper, but its evidence is gripper-only, rigid objects, 3-5 Hz control, and correlation over 6 (Google) or 3 (WidowX) policies; none of it touches multi-finger contact, which is exactly where "physical property gap" sensitivity (Table X, ≤ 15% absolute shift) would be expected to grow. Say so when extrapolating.
- Metrics to cite: MMRV + Pearson r for sim-vs-real ranking; Kruskal-Wallis for absolute shift. Trial counts per task (24-75) and averaging over seeds/arm colours are a usable template for a dexterous real-to-sim protocol.
- Perturbation axes (background, lighting, distractors, table texture, camera pose; 2 variants each) overlap with colosseum_2024's axes; cross-check when writing the robustness subsection. Camera pose and table texture dominate here (Fig. 8).
- Ties to maniskill3_2024 (SAPIEN backend; Bridge envs ported to ManiSkill3 per README) and isaaclab_2025 (Isaac Sim reproduction, Table XI).
- Source gaps: MMRV formula and SysID loss equations missing from the parsed PDF; success predicates live in the un-fetched ManiSkill2_real2sim repo; metrics.py bodies not captured.
