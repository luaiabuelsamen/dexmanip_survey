# dexvla_2025 — DexVLA: Vision-Language Model with Plug-In Diffusion Expert for General Robot Control (Wen et al., arXiv 2025)

sources: papers/md/dexvla_2025.md [sha256 17c827d9] ; code/md/dexvla_2025.md [commit fc21a822]

## One-line contribution
A billion-parameter diffusion "action expert" (ScaleDP) bolted onto a Qwen2-VL-2B VLA backbone, trained with a 3-stage "embodied curriculum," evaluated across four real-robot platforms — three parallel-jaw/gripper arms and one Franka fitted with an Inspire multi-fingered hand. "Dex" in the name refers to the plug-in diffusion expert and to a "dexterous manipulation" claim spanning embodiments, not to hand hardware alone: most reported numbers (shirt folding, bussing, sorting, bin picking, laundry, packing) are on gripper arms; the multi-fingered hand appears only in one task (drink pouring, §4.2 + generalization Table 4) and in a zero-shot transfer probe (App. A.5) constrained to 1 DoF.

## Setting
- hand(s): four embodiments (Fig. 4, App. D.1): (a) "Franka with gripper" — "A Franka Emika robot with 7 degrees of freedom, equipped with a Robotiq parallel jaw gripper"; (b) "Franka with dexterous hand" — "An Inspired Dexterous Hand mounted on a Franka Emika robot... SE(3) end-effector pose (3D position + 3D orientation) plus 6-dimensional hand joint space" (12-dim total); (c) "Bimanual UR5e" — "Two UR5e robots, each with a Robotiq parallel jaw gripper"; (d) "Bimanual AgileX" — "Two 6-DoF AgileX arms," 14-dim config/action space; end-effector type not named in the text (14-dim = plausibly 2×(6+1) but the paper never uses the word "gripper" for this platform — not stated).
- arm: Franka Emika (7-DoF), UR5e ×2, AgileX 6-DoF ×2. single/bimanual: (a),(b) single-arm; (c),(d) bimanual.
- simulator / physics: real-robot only for the main results; App. A.2 additionally reports LIBERO (a simulated Franka+gripper benchmark) but no simulator/engine details are given in this paper.
- observation: multi-view RGB (ZED 2 ×2 + ZED Mini wrist for both Franka rigs; RealSense L515 top + 2× 435i wrist for UR5e; RealSense 435i ×2 wrist + RealSense 457 top for AgileX, "front camera... was not used") plus proprioceptive joint/qpos state (App. D.1); reasoning tokens generated by the VLM are injected into the diffusion expert via FiLM (§3.1).
- action space: per-embodiment joint/EE targets via multi-head diffusion output; dims stated: Franka+gripper not given a number distinct from (b); Franka+hand = 12-dim (EE pose 6 + hand joints 6); bimanual UR5e = 14-dim; bimanual AgileX = 14-dim (App. D.1). Data collection rates: Franka rigs 15Hz, UR5e and AgileX 10Hz (App. D.1); inference: "runs at 60Hz on a single Nvidia A6000 GPU" (Abstract/Intro, p.1-2).
- objects / data: ~100 hours across 91 tasks (App. D.4, Fig. 13/14); h5 schema example in code/md shows action array shape `(100,10)` (README "h5 data structure") — illustrative episode length, not a stated diffusion action-chunk hyperparameter.

## Method
- paradigm: VLA (Qwen2-VL backbone) + diffusion action expert ("ScaleDP"), trained with a 3-stage curriculum they call "Embodied Curriculum Learning" (§3.2).
- backbone VLM: "We use Qwen2-VL 2B which is powerful and efficient" (§D.3); code README: "We construct the VLM backbone by integrating Qwen2-VL-2B... off-the-shelf Qwen2-VL model... without any post training on VLM itself" (code/md README).
- diffusion expert / action head: "We utilized the Scale Diffusion Policy (ScaleDP [29]), a variant of the Diffusion Policy in Transformer architecture, where the largest version of ScaleDP is up to 1B parameters... We put a multi-head output to enable pre-training on ScaleDP with various robot configurations. Each head is responsible for a single robot configuration" (§3.1, "Building diffusion expert"). Architectural detail (§D.3): "the total number of parameters for this model is 1 billion parameters. We use 32 layers, with the hidden stage of 1280, and a number of heads of 16... we use the image resolution of 320×240, with three camera views. Each image is processed independently to a ResNet-50. We use the strategy as in RT-1 [33] to initialize the FiLM layers." No equation is numbered in the paper; the loss is stated in prose (§3.1, "Training objectives"): "the overall training loss is defined as a weighted combination of the diffusion loss (Ldiff) and the next-token prediction loss (Lntp), which are L = Ldiff + αLntp. For all experiments, we set α = 1." No further functional form (e.g., ε-prediction MSE, noise schedule) is given in the paper text.
- code check: code/md/dexvla_2025.md only carries class/function **signatures**, not bodies, for the diffusion head (`policy_heads/models/transformer_diffusion/modeling_scaledp.py`): `class ScaleDP(PreTrainedModel)` with `def forward(self, actions, hidden_states, states, is_pad)` and `def model_forward(self, x, t, global_cond, states)`; config carries `prediction_horizon`, `num_inference_timesteps`, `num_train_timesteps`, `noise_samples` as constructor args but no assigned values are parsed out ("Config files (0)" in code/md — none were captured). The troubleshooting section names the scheduler as diffusers' `DDIMScheduler` (a fp32-cast patch for `scheduling_ddim.py` is quoted, code/md lines ~229-238) — this is the only concrete denoising-process evidence in the parsed code; it is a bug-fix snippet, not the training loss. **Mismatch/limitation: the paper's diffusion loss and denoising process cannot be verified against code bodies from what was parsed — only the multi-head ScaleDP signature and a DDIM fp32 patch are available.**
- action chunk length / control rate: not stated as an explicit named hyperparameter in either source. Per-embodiment data collection rates are given (15Hz Franka rigs, 10Hz UR5e/AgileX, App. D.1); inference rate "60Hz" is stated once (Abstract) without saying which embodiment or chunk length it corresponds to.
- key trick(s): multi-head diffusion output per embodiment (cf. Octo); FiLM-injected reasoning tokens for implicit sub-step planning instead of an external planner like SayCan (§3.2, "Substep reasoning"; App. B.2 ablation).

- arm/floating base: Franka Emika 7-DoF (a,b); UR5e (c) ×2; AgileX 6-DoF (d) ×2, one AgileX variant is "mobile" (dryer-unloading task uses "the AgileX mobile robot").
- bimanual?: (c) and (d) yes; (a) and (b) single-arm.
- simulator + version: not used for the main (real-robot) results; LIBERO used only in App. A.2, no engine/version stated.
- sim timestep and control rate: not applicable/not stated; real data collection at 15Hz (Franka rigs) / 10Hz (UR5e, AgileX) (App. D.1).
- number of parallel envs: not stated (real robot).
- GPU used and wall-clock training time: inference on "a single Nvidia A6000 GPU" (Abstract). Training epochs-per-hour comparison in Table 6 (App. A.3): "Train only Diffusion Expert: 0.89 epoch/hour" vs "Train Entire VLA: 0.32 epoch/hour" — no GPU count/type stated for this comparison, and no absolute wall-clock total given.

### B. Learning block
- paradigm: imitation learning, diffusion-based action expert conditioned on a VLA backbone; teacher-student distillation not used.
- algorithm and implementation: ScaleDP (Scale Diffusion Policy) transformer diffusion head, multi-head cross-embodiment (§3.1); optimizer AdamW(β1=0.9, β2=0.95) (Table 1).
- teacher-student / distillation: not addressed — no privileged information or teacher policy is described.
- observation vector: image tokens from per-view ResNet-50 (Stage 1) / Qwen2-VL vision encoder (Stages 2-3) plus proprioceptive state fed to the diffusion expert's `states` argument (`ScaleDP.forward(self, actions, hidden_states, states, is_pad)`, code/md modeling_scaledp.py signature); exact state vector composition beyond "SE(3) EE pose + hand joints" (App. D.1, embodiment b) not itemized further in either source.
- action space: per-embodiment joint/EE targets, embodiment-specific dimensionality as above; not stated whether position, velocity, or torque targets.
- domain randomisation: not stated / not addressed in either source.

### C. Reward / objective block
- Paper (§3.1, "Training objectives"): "the overall training loss is defined as a weighted combination of the diffusion loss (Ldiff) and the next-token prediction loss (Lntp), which are L = Ldiff + αLntp. For all experiments, we set α = 1, as we observe that Lntp converges during the early stages of training."
- Code: no loss function body was parsed (only class/def signatures); no config file with the α weight or loss term was captured ("Config files (0)"). No mismatch can be checked beyond the α=1 stated in the paper.
- IL details: horizon/action-chunking value not given in either source (see "action chunk length" above); `prediction_horizon` exists as a constructor argument name in `ScaleDPPolicyConfig`/`UnetDiffusionPolicyConfig` but no numeric value is present in the parsed code/md.

### D. Contact / penetration handling
Not addressed. No contact solver, penetration metric, or physics engine is described anywhere in the paper (real-robot evaluation throughout the main results). The only contact-adjacent mechanism is a vision-based grasp-success heuristic used for *data annotation*, not for training reward or evaluation: "We leverage Grounding-Dino and DINOv2 to annotate object bounding boxes and names, along with the gripper's bounding box. We then calculate the intersection over union between the gripper and object bounding boxes to determine grasp success" (App. D.2).

### E. Evaluation block
- success criterion: task-specific point rubrics scored per trial, out of 2-8 points depending on task (App. C.2, e.g. "Laundry folding... scored out of 4," "Shirt folding... scored out of 3," "Drink pouring... scored out of 2. A point is given for grab the bottle and pour to the cup. Another point is granted for place down the bottle"); scores reported as normalized averages over trials, "Following π0, we use a normalized score averaged over 10 episodes per task and method" (§4.1).
- episodes/trials: 10 trials per task/method for main results (§4.1, §4.2, §4.3, Figs. 6/8/11); Table 4 visual-generalization results use 3 trials per setting ("we report the averaged scores across 3 trials"); App. A.5 zero-shot transfer uses "30 novel objects."
- sim vs real: all headline numbers are real-robot except LIBERO (App. A.2, simulated).
- baselines: OpenVLA (7B, OXE-pretrained, fine-tuned on same data/epochs), Octo (93M, diffusion-policy-based), Diffusion Policy, π0 — all described as "fine-tuned on the same dataset and for the same number of epochs as our Stage 2 training" (§4.1) i.e. re-run, not quoted from their papers.

### F. Reproducibility
Code released (github.com/juruobenruo/DexVLA, commit fc21a822 as parsed); pretrained ScaleDP-H (~1B) and ScaleDP-L (~400M) weight links given (code/md README); Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct weights are the official HF checkpoint, not DexVLA-specific. Training scripts (`train_dexvla_stage2.sh`, `train_dexvla_stage3.sh`) and an evaluation script (`evaluate/smart_eval_agilex.py`) are named but only the file tree/signatures were parsed into code/md, not their bodies, so none of the paper's tables (Table 2/3/5/6/7/8) can be reproduced from what code/md captured — only architecture class names and the DDIM fp32 patch are verifiable from code text.

## Evaluation
- headline numbers without task-specific adaptation (Fig. 6, §4.1, gripper/bimanual-gripper embodiments only): DexVLA shirt folding (Bimanual AgileX) 0.92 vs Diffusion Policy/Octo/OpenVLA "struggled to complete any steps"; bin picking easy (Franka with gripper) DexVLA 0.77 [values 0.72/0.3/0.22/0.2 also shown in the flattened figure text for other bars, exact bar-to-method mapping ambiguous in the extracted markdown]; bussing table easy 0.15 region — figure text is flattened and number-to-bar mapping is ambiguous; treat with caution.
- new-embodiment learning (Fig. 8, §4.2): "DexVLA achieves an average of 0.90 point across two tasks" — packing (Bimanual UR5e) and drink pouring (Franka with dexterous hand); figure text shows 0.95/0.85 as top values with 0.35/0.1 and zeros for baselines, exact bar assignment ambiguous in extraction.
- visual generalization (Table 4, 3 trials/setting): Shirt folding (Bimanual AgileX): novel object 0.78, novel scene 0.78, novel object&scene 0.56. Drink pouring (Dexterous hand): novel object 0.83, novel scene 0.67, novel object&scene 0.67.
- long-horizon direct-prompting (§4.3): laundry folding DexVLA 0.4 vs π0 0.2; "On the table bussing task, DexVLA surpasses π0 by 0.08 points."
- ablations: Table 2 (3-stage necessity) — Stage1-only or Stage2-only: shirt folding 0.0, laundry folding 0.0; Stage1+2: shirt folding 0.92, laundry 0.0; Stage1+2+3: shirt folding 0.92, laundry folding 0.4. Table 3 (diffusion expert size, shirt folding avg score): UNet 93M = 0.17, Diff. Expert 410M = 0.63, Diff. Expert 1B = 0.92. Table 7 (substep reasoning, shirt folding): substep in Stage1 only 0.07, Stage2 only 0, both stages 0.92. Table 8: SayCan 0.58 vs substep reasoning 0.70 on Bussing Table (Hard).
- zero-shot cross-embodiment (App. A.5): gripper-trained Stage-2 model deployed on an Inspire 5-finger hand "constrain[ed]... to a single degree of freedom" (matching the 1-DoF gripper it replaces): "Across these 30 objects, we achieve an average success rate of 60%... slightly below the 67% success rate attained with the original gripper." Authors note "we do not yet control all degrees of freedom needed for fully dexterous manipulation" in this test.
- LIBERO (Table 5, App. A.2, simulated, Franka+gripper benchmark): DexVLA Spatial 97.2, Object 99.1, Goal 95.6, Average 97.3, vs π0 Average 97.1, π0-FAST 93.9, OpenVLA 84.1, DP 79.7.
- baselines beaten: OpenVLA, Octo, Diffusion Policy (all re-run/fine-tuned by the authors, §4.1); π0 (§4.3, direct prompting); π0/π0-FAST/OpenVLA/DP on LIBERO (Table 5).
- real robot? Yes throughout except LIBERO; hand/trial counts as above (10 trials/task main results, 3 trials/setting for Table 4, 30 objects for the zero-shot transfer probe).

**dexterous-hand-evaluated: yes** — but only for one real-robot task family (drink pouring/packing new-embodiment tests, §4.2, plus its Table-4 generalization variant and the App. A.5 zero-shot probe), while the shirt-folding/bussing/sorting/bin-picking/laundry/dryer-unloading headline tasks all run on parallel-jaw grippers. Quoted end-effectors across all four evaluated embodiments: "A Franka Emika robot with 7 degrees of freedom, equipped with a Robotiq parallel jaw gripper" (Franka+gripper); "An Inspired Dexterous Hand mounted on a Franka Emika robot... plus 6-dimensional hand joint space" (Franka+dexterous hand); "Two UR5e robots, each with a Robotiq parallel jaw gripper" (Bimanual UR5e); "Two 6-DoF AgileX arms" with end-effector unnamed (Bimanual AgileX) (all App. D.1).

## Limitations stated by the authors
- "While the model has learned from diverse robot behaviors and progressively developed dexterous skills for complex tasks, it faces limitations in very long-horizon, contact-rich scenarios such as folding crumpled shirts or executing continuous bin-picking" (Intro, p.2).
- Zero-shot gripper→dexterous-hand transfer (App. A.5) is explicitly incomplete: hand constrained to 1 DoF, and "the performance gap arises from three main challenges: (1) the dexterous hand's appearance differs markedly from the gripper... (2) the wrist camera's mounting position shifts significantly... (3) the difference in hand height changes the effective object grasping height. Although we do not yet control all degrees of freedom needed for fully dexterous manipulation, these results demonstrate that DexVLA's visual and camera-view representations transfer effectively across embodiments."
- Table 2 ablation shows the full 3-stage recipe is necessary: Stage-1-only or Stage-2-only alone give 0.0 on both shirt and laundry folding.

## Notes for the survey (which sections this feeds; contradictions with other notes)
Feeds the survey's "VLA action-head design" and "cross-embodiment curricula" sections, and the "does 'dexterous' mean multi-fingered?" caveat list alongside any other paper whose title claims dexterity but evaluates mostly on grippers. DexVLA's own zero-shot experiment (App. A.5) is itself evidence that its architecture has not been shown to control a multi-fingered hand beyond 1 effective DoF in that probe; the one genuine multi-DoF hand result (drink pouring, 6-dim hand joint space, Franka+dexterous hand) is fine-tuned per-embodiment with 100 demonstrations (§C.1) and is not part of the cross-embodiment Stage-1/2 pretraining mixture (App. D.4 lists only Agilex ARX, Agilex PIPER, single UR5e, single Franka — no dexterous-hand data in the 91-task, 100-hour pretraining set). No contact/penetration measurement exists in this paper to cross-check against simulation-based dexterous-hand papers in the survey.
