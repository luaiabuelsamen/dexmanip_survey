## One-line contribution
Bunny-VisionPro uses an Apple Vision Pro headset's hand/wrist tracking to teleoperate a bimanual 24-DoF Ability-hand system through three decoupled real-time modules — fingertip-vector hand retargeting with a novel reduced-dimension loop-joint reformulation (10.2x speedup), a unified IK+singularity+self-collision arm-motion optimization, and a low-cost ERM haptic feedback device driven by the hands' own FSR tactile sensors — beating AnyTeleop-based teleoperation on 9/10 Telekinesis-benchmark tasks and on custom bimanual short/long-horizon tasks, and producing higher-quality demonstrations for downstream IL (ACT/Diffusion Policy/DP3), with a 22% average success-rate gain (Sec. 3-5).

## Setting
- operator interface: VR headset (Apple Vision Pro) hand and wrist tracking, no glove or exoskeleton; a custom-built wearable haptic feedback device (ERM vibration motors on the operator's hands, driven by the robot's fingertip FSR tactile sensors) is layered on top for a subset of experiments (Sec. 3.4, 4.3) — "many vision-based teleoperation systems... neglect haptic feedback... we have developed a cost-effective haptic feedback system using Eccentric Rotating Mass (ERM) actuators." Hand pose is streamed from Vision Pro to the control computer via a third-party library [45]; the system is explicitly modular, "each module to run in a separate computation process, preventing latency accumulation" (Sec. 3.1).
- robot hand(s) controlled: 6-DoF Ability Hand (per hand), bimanual — "two UFactory xArm-7 robotic arms, each equipped with a 6-DoF Ability hand, resulting in a 24-DoF system. Each hand incorporates 30 tactile sensors distributed across five fingertips" (Sec. 4.2). Ability Hand has loop joints (four-bar linkages) explicitly handled by the retargeting formulation (see below).
- retargeting formulation (Sec. 3.2, "Robot Hand Motion Retargeting", quoted): "translates human finger poses into corresponding robot joint positions. This is achieved by minimizing the difference between the fingertip keypoint vectors [46] of the human hand and the robotic hand, formulated as an online optimization problem... The objective function L_hand consists of two terms: (i). The first term minimizes the distance between the scaled human hand keypoint vectors αvi and the robot hand keypoint vectors computed by forward kinematics function FKi(q), where vi represents the i-th fingertip keypoint vector, N is the total number of vectors, ||.||2 denotes Euclidean norm, α is the scaling factor to adjust for size differences between the human and robot hand and q is the joint positions of the robot hand bounded by the lower and upper limit, ql and qu. (ii). The second term enforces temporal smoothness by penalizing large joint position changes ∆q between consecutive frames, with weight β." — same fingertip-keypoint-vector family as dexpilot_2020/anyteleop_2023 (cites the same underlying formulation [46], i.e. dex-retargeting/AnyTeleop's vector optimizer), with explicit joint-limit constraints (ql, qu) and solved online via Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP), not SLSQP as in dexpilot_2020. Novel contribution beyond the base formulation: real-time handling of *loop joints* (passive joints whose position is a function of active joints, common in the Ability Hand's four-bar linkages) — "For a robot with n joints, the positions of n-k passive joints depend on the other k active joint... Instead of adding equality constraints to the optimization problem... which can lead to instability and increased computation time... we reformulate the problem into a reduced dimension where the optimization variable has k dimensions (active joints) instead of n... the gradients of the passive joints are backpropagated to the active joints." Measured effect: "a 10.2x speedup when using this method to solve the four-bar linkage structure... in the Ability Robot Hand" (Table 1: loop joints as constraints 34.98ms vs. loop joints ours 3.43ms vs. no loop joints 2.54ms). Collision handling in the hand-retargeting cost itself: not addressed (no self-collision or hand-object term in L_hand) — collision avoidance is confined to the separate arm-motion objective.
- arm-motion retargeting/control formulation (Sec. 3.3, quoted): a "unified and efficient optimization objective L_arm... that integrates IK, collision avoidance, and singularity management for real-time motion control." IK term: "p_ee and p_wrist represent the robot end effector and human wrist position, respectively, while q_ee and q_wrist are their corresponding quaternions... The weights β_pos and β_rot allow for balancing the errors between position and rotation." Singularity-avoidance term L_sin: "J represents the spatial Jacobian matrix, s0 is the smallest singular value, s_low is a conditional trigger for penalizing singularity, and λ acts as a temperature factor... We opt for the manipulability index [det(JJ^T)] as the objective due to its suitability for gradient-based optimization while using s0 as the condition. Thus, L_sin only takes effect when the robot approaches singularity." Self-collision term L_col, explicitly addressing hand/arm collision (quoted): "For collision detection, the GJK algorithm [52] is often employed but can be time-intensive even with convex meshes. To efficiently compute the self-collision cost L_col, we model each robot link as a collection of m spheres. The function dist(.,.) calculates the Euclidean distance between spheres ei and ej, while 1(.,.) serves as an indicator function to evaluate the necessity of collision checking, with ε provides numerical stability. Collisions are disregarded between spheres within the same link or between spheres of a parent and its direct child." This is the most explicit, quantified collision-handling formulation in this survey's teleoperation batch — joint limits are handled in the hand-retargeting objective (ql, qu) and collision avoidance is handled in the separate arm objective (sphere-approximated self-collision cost L_col), rather than in one combined term.
- end-to-end latency: not reported as a single number, but per-module profiling times are given (Table 1, i7-12700KF CPU): Retargeting (w/o loop joints) 2.54ms; Retargeting (loop joints as constraints, the naive baseline) 34.98ms; Retargeting (loop joints, this paper's method) 3.43ms; Motion Control (IK only) 0.74ms; Motion Control (+Collision) 7.85ms; Motion Control (+Singularity) 10.42ms; Motion Control (+Collision+Singularity) 15.93ms; Haptic Feedback (PWM) 0.04ms. "even with singularity and collision avoidance, our modules run in real-time at >60Hz" (Sec. 4.1).
- cost of the rig: not stated as a total dollar figure; the haptic device is characterized only qualitatively as "cost-effective" (Sec. 3.4) using an ELEGOO UNO (Arduino) board, ERM motors, and FSR sensors already built into the Ability hand — no BoM total given.
- data rate / demonstrations per hour: not reported as an aggregate demos/hour figure, but Table 3 gives per-task collection time in minutes for a fixed demonstration-count target (implied from success-rate denominators): e.g. Clean Pan — AnyTeleop+ 74.6 min to collect 50 successes (out of 78 attempts) vs. Ours 30.4 min for 50 successes (out of 58 attempts); Sweep Floor — AnyTeleop+ 129.4 min for 30 successes (61 attempts) vs. Ours 45.4 min for 30 successes (40 attempts). Aggregate comparison (quoted, Sec. 4.2): "our system outperforms AnyTeleop+... achieving 11% higher success rate with 45% task completion time... reducing episode lengths by 19%... a 43% increase in arm joint position changes [for AnyTeleop+]" — i.e. roughly 2.2x faster wall-clock collection for the same demonstration yield, though not stated as a demos/hour rate directly.
- task(s) demonstrations were used for: (1) direct teleoperation comparison on the 10-task Telekinesis benchmark (Table 2, single-arm) and 6 custom bimanual tasks — 3 short-horizon (Grasping Toy, Cleaning Pan, Uncovering and Pouring) and 3 long-horizon (Wiping Glass, Sweeping Floor, Preparing Coffee) (Sec. 4.2, Table 3); (2) downstream imitation learning with ACT, Diffusion Policy, and DP3 trained on demonstrations from Bunny-VisionPro vs. AnyTeleop+, evaluated for in-distribution success (SR), spatial generalization (SR-SG), and unseen-object generalization (SR-U) on Grasp Toy, Clean Pan, and Uncover & Pour (Sec. 5, Table 4); (3) long-horizon DP3 policies on Wipe Glass/Sweep Floor/Prepare Coffee with and without tactile (FSR force/impulse) input as an additional policy observation modality (Sec. 5.2, Tables 5-6).

## Evaluation
- exact success criterion: binary per-trial task completion (e.g. "Grasp toy and place in box"; "Grasp container, remove lid, and pour into bowl") judged against the task description in Tab. 3 / App. C; no numeric distance/angle threshold is stated in the parsed text (unlike e.g. holo_dex_2022's explicit degree/second thresholds).
- number of eval episodes/seeds: Telekinesis benchmark — 10 trials/task (Table 2, matching the Telekinesis/AnyTeleop protocol). Custom tasks (Table 3) — a fixed target number of successful demonstrations per task (50 for short-horizon, 30 for long-horizon), with attempted trials given in the "Success" column as successes/attempts (e.g. "50/58"). Imitation learning (Table 4) — 10 trials per task/policy/generalization-condition. Haptic-feedback user study (Sec. 4.3) — 5 untrained operators x 5 trials/task x 2 tasks.
- headline numbers (with table): Table 2 (Telekinesis benchmark, 10 trials) — Bunny-VisionPro matches or beats Telekinesis and AnyTeleop on 9/10 tasks, e.g. Two Cup Stacking 9/10 (ours) vs. 3/10 (Telekinesis) vs. 7/10 (AnyTeleop); underperforms only on Scissor Pickup (7/10 vs. AnyTeleop's 8/10) "due to the limited DoF in the Ability hand." Table 3 (custom tasks) — "11% higher success rate with 45% task completion time... reducing episode lengths by 19%... 43% increase in arm joint position changes [for AnyTeleop+]" (quoted). Table 4 (IL, 10 trials) — "Bunny-VisionPro significantly outperforms AnyTeleop+ by an average of 22% success rate across three tasks using three different policies... exceeds by 14% SR-SG and 26% SR-U." Long-horizon IL (Sec. 5.1): "the policy achieves notable success rates - 73% for sub-tasks and 38% for entire tasks - with only 30 demonstrations." Tactile ablation (Table 6): mixed/negative — "utilizing tactile data does not necessarily enhance outcomes... using force data delivers slightly inferior performance compared to impulse data... Impulse, by contrast, is more robust against such [zero-drift] variations."
- baselines beaten: Telekinesis [3] and AnyTeleop [4] (Table 2, numbers taken from original papers); AnyTeleop+ (AnyTeleop's pipeline re-integrated with Vision-Pro-based hand tracking for a fair custom-task comparison, Tables 3-4).
- real robot? Yes, entirely real-robot for both teleoperation and IL evaluation — dual xArm-7 + Ability hand rig, two RealSense L515 cameras (front + top).

## Limitations stated by the authors (Sec. 6, quoted)
- "Vision Pro's hand tracking is inaccurate when fingers are self-occluded, causing jerky control commands. This issue could be mitigated by fusing data from wearable devices with Vision Pro."
- "Subtle changes in haptic feedback are difficult to sense when the robot touches an object lightly. In future work, we will consider using actuators with larger contact areas and better sensitivity, such as piezoelectric actuators."

## Notes for the survey
- Feeds: the retargeting-formulation comparison table as the most explicit collision/joint-limit-handling case in this batch — pair with dexpilot_2020 and anyteleop_2023 (all three use fingertip-keypoint-vector correspondence) but flag that Bunny-VisionPro is the only one of the three to give a fully quantified, named self-collision cost (sphere-approximated GJK-alternative L_col) and a solved efficiency problem (loop joints / passive-joint reformulation, 10.2x measured speedup) rather than leaving collision/redundancy handling unaddressed or delegated wholesale to a downstream motion planner.
- This is also the strongest head-to-head retargeting-quality evidence in the survey for how the *teleoperation system itself* (not just the policy) changes downstream IL outcomes: same three IL architectures (ACT, Diffusion Policy, DP3) trained on demonstrations from two different teleoperation systems (Bunny-VisionPro vs. AnyTeleop+) with the identical robot/task/camera setup, isolating the effect of teleoperation quality on IL success — worth a direct citation in whichever survey section argues that retargeting/teleoperation quality (not just policy architecture) matters for downstream imitation learning.
- Tactile-as-policy-input result is a rare negative/mixed finding worth flagging under the "record misses" principle: adding FSR tactile data to a DP3 policy did not consistently improve success rate, and using raw force (vs. differentiated impulse) was worse due to sensor zero-drift (Sec. 5.2, Table 6) — a useful counterpoint for any survey claim that adding tactile sensing straightforwardly helps IL.
- No dollar cost-of-rig figure and no direct demos/hour statistic — the survey's cost/throughput table should mark "not stated" for the rig cost and derive throughput only from the minutes-per-N-demonstrations numbers in Table 3, with the caveat that "N" (30 or 50) and attempt counts vary by task.
