# sparsh_2024 — Sparsh: Self-supervised touch representations for vision-based tactile sensing (Higuera, Sharma et al.; CoRL 2024, FAIR at Meta)

sources: papers/md/sparsh_2024.md [cec1645a] ; code/md/sparsh_2024.md [fee6a05a, github.com/facebookresearch/sparsh]

## One-line contribution
Sparsh is a family of self-supervised (masked-image, self-distillation, and JEPA) touch-representation encoders pre-trained on 460k+ tactile images spanning three sensor families (DIGIT, GelSight 2017, GelSight Mini), evaluated on TacBench — a new six-task standardized benchmark — where frozen Sparsh features beat task- and sensor-specific end-to-end models by 95.1% on average under limited labeled data.

## Sensor record (note: this is a representation-learning paper over sensors, not a sensor hardware paper — the "sensor record" below describes what Sparsh trains on and evaluates, per HOWTO's instruction to record what TacBench covers and how)
- sensing principle: not a new sensor — Sparsh is trained on and evaluated across three existing vision-based tactile sensor families, explicitly named (Sec. 3, quoted): "we introduce Sparsh, a family of touch representation trained with SSL across multiple sensors such as DIGIT [3], GelSight 2017 (with markers [1]), and GelSight Mini (without markers)." All three share the vision-based principle: a deformable elastomer imaged by an internal camera. GelSight 2017 uses marker-tracked shear estimation ("obtaining a shear field for sensors with markers... is trivial via marker tracking," Sec. 5.2); DIGIT and GelSight Mini are markerless, for which Sparsh instead learns shear/slip information implicitly via background subtraction against a no-contact reference image (Sec. 3).
- output signal and resolution: raw sensor output is per-sensor tactile images (per Fig. 2: DIGIT and GelSight/Touch-and-Go/ObjectFolder datasets are all camera images); for [T1] force estimation, "GelSight Mini images are of higher resolution (HD) compared to DIGIT (320×240)" (Sec. 5.1) — note this DIGIT resolution figure (320×240) differs from digit_2020's own reported 640×480 (Table I of that paper); this note records both as stated in their respective sources without reconciling them, since Sparsh's data-collection setup or a cropped/downsampled operating resolution may differ from the sensor's native output. Frame rates used in data collection: "tactile images from DIGIT at 60fps and GelSight at 25fps" (Sec. 5.1, [T1] force estimation dataset). Model tokenization: for image-based SSL methods, two frames concatenated across the channel dim with a temporal stride of 5 samples, I_t ⊕ I_{t-5} → x ∈ R^{h×w×6}, corresponding to "an inference window of approximately 80 ms" at 60 FPS — "the reaction time that humans need to adjust the grip force when detecting partial slip" (Sec. 3); for V-JEPA, 4-frame clips at [t, t-2, t-4, t-6] ∈ R^{4×h×w×3}, ~100 ms window (Sec. 3). Inference throughput: "models support inference rates of upto 112FPS (measured on an Nvidia RTX3080)" (Sec. 3) — Sparsh itself is "limited by data streaming rates, and not by inference time" at that throughput.
- size / form factor: not applicable to Sparsh itself (a software encoder family); for the sensors it covers, this note defers to their own hardware papers/pages — see digit_2020.md for DIGIT (20×27×18 mm, 20 g). The bib entry's "why" field notes (unverified vendor claim, not from papers/md or code/md): "GelSight Mini itself has no paper: spec page https://www.gelsight.com/gelsightmini/ (camera-based elastomer, ~32x24 mm gel, USB, ~$500 class, 2022)" — flagged here as a bib-author annotation per HOWTO rule 6, not confirmed from a parsed source.
- what simulator model exists: none described in this paper — Sparsh is trained and evaluated entirely on real sensor data (curated Touch-Slide dataset plus existing YCB-Slide, Touch-and-Go, and ObjectFolder datasets, Sec. 3) and real-robot rollouts (Sec. 7.1, bead maze); no tactile simulator (e.g. TACTO, Taxim) is used, extended, or mentioned as future work.

## Setting
- hand(s) / robot(s): for [T1]/[T2] (force/slip) and [T3] (pose): a fixed sensor rig with indenters (hemisphere, sharp, flat shapes) and a probe, not a full hand (Fig. 3a-b). [T4] grasp stability: a parallel-jaw gripper setup adapted from the "Feeling of Success" dataset (Sec. 6.2). [T6] bead maze: a real robot arm with a 7-DoF action space performing a fine-motor manipulation task (Sec. 7.1, Fig. 3c).
- simulator / physics: none; all TacBench data is real-world.
- observation: task-dependent — raw or history-windowed tactile images per sensor, optionally concatenated with proprioception (robot joint angles q) for the bead-maze policy (Sec. 7.1).
- action space: bead maze policy predicts joint-angle changes Δq ∈ R^7 over a temporal horizon via Diffusion Policy with the vision encoder replaced by a frozen pre-trained Sparsh encoder (Sec. 7.1).
- objects / data: SSL pre-training corpus of ~661k tactile images total (~462.7k, i.e. 70%, used for pre-training, the rest held out for probe-based monitoring) curated from a new "Touch-Slide" dataset plus existing YCB-Slide, Touch-and-Go, and ObjectFolder datasets (Sec. 3, Fig. 2) — "70% or 462.7k images are used for SSL pre-training which is an order of magnitude larger than any prior work on touch representations" (Sec. 3, quoted). TacBench per-task labeled datasets: [T1] force estimation, 75k time-aligned samples of 3-axis force + end-effector pose + tactile image (Sec. 5.1); [T2] slip detection, 125k samples with 13% slip instances, 25k reserved for evaluation (Sec. 5.3); [T3] pose estimation, time-synchronized DIGIT-image/object-pose pairs binned to ±5 mm translation / ±2° rotation resolution (Sec. 6.1); [T4] grasp stability, an adapted "Feeling of Success" dataset, 64% successful/36% failed grasps, ~8k training / 1.3k evaluation grasps (Sec. 6.2); [T5] textile recognition, 4467 short video clips (10-25 frames) of a GelSight-2017 (marker) gripper grasping 20 textile classes (Sec. 6.3); [T6] bead maze, 50 real teleoperated demonstrations (~34k tactile-image/joint-angle training pairs), evaluated with 10 randomized novel start locations (Sec. 7.1).

## Method
- paradigm: self-supervised representation learning (five SSL variants: MAE, DINO, DINOv2, I-JEPA, V-JEPA — Fig. 2b-d), evaluated via a frozen-encoder + trainable-decoder ("frozen evaluation") protocol against task/sensor-specific end-to-end (E2E) baselines of identical capacity (Sec. 4, "Evaluation protocol"); one downstream task ([T6]) uses imitation learning (Diffusion Policy) with the pre-trained Sparsh encoder substituted for Diffusion Policy's usual vision encoder.
- reward or loss: [T1] force estimation — L1 loss on normalized forces scaled to [-1,1], Adam optimizer, metric = average RMSE across 3 axes (Sec. 5.1). [T2] slip detection — joint decoders: cross-entropy for slip classification, mean absolute error for normalized force-change (Δ) regression, metric = F1 score due to class imbalance (Sec. 5.3). [T3] pose estimation — regression-by-classification, cross-entropy loss per DOF over a discretized SE(2) grid, Adam optimizer, metric = multiclass accuracy (Sec. 6.1). [T6] bead maze — Diffusion Policy's standard training objective (not restated in the parsed text) with a frozen Sparsh tactile encoder in place of the vision encoder (Sec. 7.1).
- key trick(s): background subtraction against a no-contact reference frame for markerless sensors (DIGIT, GelSight Mini) to expose static-shear information and improve cross-sensor generalization (Sec. 3); dual-frame temporal concatenation (stride-5, ~80 ms window) for image-based SSL methods so slip/pose tasks that need temporal reasoning are representable (Sec. 3); regression-by-classification for continuous pose targets (Sec. 6.1).

## Evaluation
- metrics (exact definitions): given per task above — RMSE (force), F1 score (slip), multiclass accuracy (pose bins), classification accuracy (grasp stability, textile), position error over 3 cm/15-timestep chunks vs. held-out demonstration trajectory plus real-rollout "distance traversed before failure" over 10 novel start locations (bead maze, Sec. 7.1).
- headline numbers: "On average, Sparsh achieves a 95.1% improvement compared to an end-to-end approach when all models have access to only 33%-50% of the labeled dataset per downstream task" (Sec. 8, Discussion, matching the Abstract's headline figure). "Sparsh (DINO) outperforms Sparsh (IJEPA) by 5.6% across the benchmark" on average (Sec. 8). Grasp stability: "Sparsh (IJEPA) or Sparsh (VJEPA) reach ∼80% classification accuracy, surpassing results from [8] that combined tactile and vision modalities" using touch alone (Sec. 6.2). Bead maze: Sparsh (DINO)/(IJEPA) show "a difference of ∼16%" lower trajectory position error than E2E, and outperform E2E by "∼20-53%" in real-rollout distance traversed before failure — but "none of the models succeeds in completing the full maze on real robot rollouts" (Sec. 7.1). Force/slip at very low label budgets: "force error below 0.1N with Sparsh (DINO)" using "as little as 10% or 1% of the labeled data" (Sec. 8).
- baselines beaten: task- and sensor-specific end-to-end (E2E) models of matched encoder/decoder capacity, across all six TacBench tasks, particularly under low-labeled-data budgets (33%-50%, and down to 1-10% for force/slip); Sparsh (IJEPA)/(VJEPA) grasp-stability results also beat the vision+tactile combined baseline from the "Feeling of Success" paper (ref [8]) despite Sparsh using touch alone.
- real robot? yes for [T6] bead maze (real teleoperated demonstrations, real closed-loop rollouts); [T1]-[T5] use real but non-policy sensor/robot data-collection rigs (fixed probes/indenters, a gripper), not closed-loop manipulation policies.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Quoted (Sec. 8, "Limitations"): "Open-source tactile datasets we considered in this study predominantly feature discrete contact interactions. We believe that incorporating data rich in shear interactions can further improve the representations. We do not ablate the length of tactile image history for learning the representations... Our bead maze policies with pre-trained touch representations deployed on the real robot are only able to complete the maze partially before compounding error leads to the bead falling out of the fingers. Further research is needed to understand how to effectively leverage pre-trained touch representations in behavioral cloning for robot manipulation tasks." Also noted in Sec. 7.1: bead-maze real-rollout failure attributed to "lack of force control and system-level confounding variables not captured during training," including "the high precision required to keep the bead in place, the impossibility of error recovery once grip is lost, and trajectory drift due to local decision-making," and that specialist/from-scratch policies can outperform pre-trained ones on this narrow task domain "due to... overfitting" of the narrow task, "a trend similarly observed when studying pre-trained vision models" (Sec. 8, Analysis).

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "SSL pre-training for touch representation outperforms task and sensor-specific end-to-end training by 95.1% on average over TacBench, and Sparsh (DINO) and Sparsh (IJEPA) are the most competitive, indicating the merits of learning in latent space over pixel space for tactile images." (Abstract)
- "we introduce a family of models trained with the latest SSL algorithms for the three most commonly used families of tactile sensors: DIGIT, GelSight 2017, and GelSight Mini." (Sec. 2, Related work)
- "Tasks traditionally challenging for markerless sensors (like DIGIT and GelSight Mini), such as shear force (and field) estimation and slip detection, become solvable with our general touch representations." (Sec. 8, Analysis)
