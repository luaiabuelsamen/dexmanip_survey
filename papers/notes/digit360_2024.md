# digit360_2024 — Digitizing Touch with an Artificial Multimodal Fingertip (Lambeta, Wu, Sengül et al.; arXiv 2024, FAIR at Meta)

sources: papers/md/digit360_2024.md [bdb3afd2] ; code/md/digit360_2024.md [b78b6b91, github.com/facebookresearch/digit360]

## One-line contribution
Digit 360 is a hemispherical, omnidirectional multimodal fingertip sensor (~8.3 million taxels) that combines vision-based structured-light touch imaging with in-fingertip audio, pressure, IMU, temperature and gas sensing plus an on-device AI accelerator for reflex-like local control, evaluated for spatial resolution, force sensitivity, and multi-modal action/material classification.

## Sensor record
- sensing principle: primarily vision-based/optical — "It embodies an elastomer that serves as the touch-sensing interface, with a subcutaneous camera that measures the deformation of the elastomer through structured light" (Sec. 1) — belonging "to the family of vision-based tactile sensors" (Sec. 1). Layered on top of this optical core is a genuinely multimodal sensing stack: "Contact intensity and geometry, static and dynamic forces, surface audio textures and vibrations, heat change, fingertip velocity/acceleration/orientation, and even identification of some airborne chemical compounds" (Sec. 1). Five subsystems drive the design: elastomer interface (a chemically deposited silver thin-film reflective coating on a solid, non-rigid silicone volume, replacing hand-painting/airbrushing/dip-coating for thinner, more consistent coatings, Sec. 2.1), optical system (custom "solid immersion hyper fisheye lens," 1.1 um CMOS pixel size, deliberately allowing chromatic aberration and shallow depth of field for touch-specific optics rather than general-purpose imaging, Sec. 2.2), illumination system (a rigid solid volume with a controlled, non-Lambertian surface-scattering profile chosen to optimize image contrast vs. background-illumination uniformity, Sec. 2.3), multi-modal sensing (in-fingertip audio microphones, MEMS pressure sensors, IMU, heat and gas sensors, Sec. 2.4), and on-device AI processing (an embedded neural-network accelerator, Sec. 2.5).
- output signal and resolution (quoted, Abstract): "high-resolution sensors (≈8.3 million taxels) that respond to omnidirectional touch... resolve spatial features as small as 7 um, sense normal and shear forces with a resolution of 1.01 mN and 1.27 mN, respectively, perceive vibrations up to 10 kHz, sense heat, and even sense odor." Spatial resolution methodology: defined as "the minimum feature size that can be resolved with a modulation transfer function (MTF) ≥ 0.5," simulated at ≥6 um (region 1), ≥8 um (region 2), ≥22 um (region 3), and validated with a two-pronged micro-indenter down to "≥7 um for region 1" (Sec. 3). Force error by region (Fig. 2 caption): normal-force median error 1.01/1.09/1.41 mN and shear-force median error 1.27/1.48/1.64 mN for regions 1/2/3 respectively. Camera frame rate: "Digit 360's fast camera, which operates at 240 Hz" (Sec. 3) — vs. "typical vision-based tactile sensors... operate [at] lower sensing frequencies, such as 60 Hz" (i.e. the original DIGIT's rate, Sec. 3). Audio/vibration sensing extends "upwards to 10 kHz," far above the 240 Hz visual rate (Sec. 2.4). Fig. 5f reports the sensor beats prior sensors "4X for spatial, 6X for normal forces, and 9X for shear forces" on the metrics evaluated (comparison baseline sensor(s) not named in the parsed text beyond the figure).
- size / form factor: described as "an artificial finger-shaped sensor" / "a modular, finger-shaped, multi-modal tactile sensor" with "a similar geometry to a human finger" (Sec. 1, Sec. 2) and a "hemispherical compliant embodiment" (Abstract) — no explicit width/height/depth or weight figure (unlike digit_2020's Table I) appears in the parsed paper text. Code README (quoted): "Digit 360 is composed of a 5 printed circuit board (PCB) stack with a 2 part mechanical housing, a custom hyperfisheye lens, and an artificial fingertip."
- what simulator model exists: code README, quoted in full: "To facilitate reinforcement learning environments, we provide a set of sectorized and convex meshes described in URDF formats found in the [meshes](./meshes/) folder... Both convex and sectorized meshes can be imported into IsaacSim." This is a geometric/collision URDF mesh model for RL environment integration (importable into NVIDIA IsaacSim), not a full optical/contact-image simulator (unlike TACTO or Taxim for the original DIGIT, see digit_2020.md) — no rendering-of-tactile-images capability is described for Digit 360 in either parsed source.
- corpus papers using Digit 360: a targeted grep (`grep -il "digit360\|digit 360\|digit-360"` across all of papers/md and code/md) found zero hits outside digit360_2024.md itself — no corpus paper's own parsed text mentions Digit 360 by name. Note a discrepancy: the Digit 360 code README lists "Related Works using Digit 360": "In-Hand Object Rotation via Rapid Motor Adaptation, Haozhi Qi et al." (hora_2022 in this corpus) and "General In-Hand Object Rotation with Vision and Touch, Haozhi Qi et al." (rotateit_2023 in this corpus) — but both predate Digit 360's Oct 31, 2024 announcement (hora_2022, rotateit_2023), so they cannot have physically used the Digit 360 sensor; this is most plausibly the README crediting intellectually related prior work by an overlapping author (Haozhi Qi is a co-author of digit360_2024 and of hora_2022/rotateit_2023), not a report of the sensor being used in those papers. Do not cite hora_2022/rotateit_2023 as "uses Digit 360" in the survey; if anything, cite them as this sensor's stated research lineage/motivation, per the code README's own framing, and flag the terminology.

## Setting
- hand(s): a "4-finger robotic hand" mounted with a robotic arm as an "action platform" for the multi-modality data-collection scenario (Sec. 3, "Multi-modality based characterization" — making scrambled eggs, tapping/stirring a spatula against a pan).
- simulator / physics: not used for any reported experiment in the paper (all evaluation is on the physical sensor); the URDF mesh assets for IsaacSim (code README) are provided as a released artifact for future RL work, not demonstrated with a training result in either parsed source.
- observation: for the multi-modal classification task — per-finger, per-modality tensors over a 1.33 s / T=10 window: visuotactile T×120×120×3 (finger-independent) or T×120×120×12 (finger-dependent, 4 fingers concatenated), inertial measurement T×3 (or T×12), surface pressure T×4 (or T×12... reported as "10x12" in the text, likely a typo for T×12), surface audio (T×4)×64×1 (or ×4) (Sec. 3, Fig. 6 caption).
- action space: not applicable (sensor characterization + classification paper, no control policy trained end-to-end besides the reflex-arc demo).
- objects / data: ~620k samples across 4 fingers, action-material pairs (tapping, stirring, etc. against materials: coffee, cheese, rubber, soap, liquid coffee, butter — Fig. 3b, Sec. 3); a scrambled-eggs cooking scenario (Fig. 4c) and a raw-vs-hard-boiled egg state-change demo (Fig. 4b); a liquid-level-in-opaque-container tapping task (Fig. 3a); gas/odor object-identification task reaching "91% accuracy" and "61% accuracy... within 6 seconds" for faster classification (Sec. 2, Fig. 3d-e).

## Method
- paradigm: sensor design and characterization (not a manipulation-policy paper); supervised deep learning is used only for (a) force-prediction models (image/taxel input to mN force output) and (b) multi-modal action/material classification.
- reward or loss: not stated for the force-prediction model (only "we train a deep learning model from this dataset," Sec. 3, no loss formula given). Classification: a modality-specific-encoder architecture — temporal modalities (pressure, inertial) via MLP, vision and surface audio via an untrained ResNet-18 encoder, concatenated features fed to two output MLP heads for action and material classification (Sec. 3, Fig. 6a) — no explicit loss function quoted.
- key trick(s): chemical (not hand-painted/airbrushed) silver thin-film deposition for a thinner, more consistent reflective coating (Sec. 2.1); custom solid-immersion hyper-fisheye lens tailored to the hemispherical sensing volume rather than an off-the-shelf lens (Sec. 2.2); rigid solid silicone volume with controlled (non-Lambertian) surface scattering to jointly optimize background-illumination uniformity and image contrast (Sec. 2.3); on-device AI accelerator for local "reflex arc"-style sensor-to-actuator control, cutting event-to-action latency from ~6 ms (external host, on a Digit sensor) to 1.2 ms (Digit 360, on-device) — a ~5x reduction, with the text separately citing "a 2x reduction in latency and jitter" for on-device vs. host processing in general (Sec. 2.5, Sec. 3 end, Fig. 5d).

## Evaluation
- metrics (exact definitions): spatial resolution — minimum resolvable feature size at MTF ≥ 0.5 (line pairs per millimeter), validated via a two-pronged micro-indenter and taxel intensity line-profile inspection (Sec. 3). Force sensitivity — median (and 95th-percentile) prediction error in mN from a deep-learning model trained on micro-indenter-applied normal/shear force vs. sensor-output tuples, evaluated in three regions from fingertip to side (Sec. 3, Fig. 2a-b). Classification accuracy — action and material classification accuracy (%) from the multi-modal DNN, compared across finger-independent vs. finger-dependent input concatenation and across modality-ablation conditions (Sec. 3, Fig. 6, Table 4 [table contents not reproduced in the parsed excerpt]). Latency — mean and standard deviation of event-to-action latency for on-device vs. host-based processing (Sec. 2.5, Fig. 5d).
- headline numbers: spatial resolution 7 um (measured) / 6-22 um (simulated, by region); force error 1.01 mN normal / 1.27 mN shear (region 1, best case); vibration sensing to 10 kHz; camera at 240 Hz vs. "typical" 60 Hz; gas/odor object-state identification "91% accuracy," reaching 61% accuracy within 6 s of approach; on-device latency 1.2 ms vs. 2.5 ms (Digit 360, host) vs. >6 ms (original Digit, host) (Fig. 5d); overall claimed improvement over "existing sensors" of "4X for spatial, 6X for normal forces, and 9X for shear forces" (Fig. 5f).
- baselines beaten: the original DIGIT sensor (digit_2020, cited as "Lambeta et al., 2020" and as "Digit" in the latency comparison, Fig. 5d) on latency and (per Fig. 5e-f, not itemized further in the parsed text) on spatial/force sensitivity; "typical vision-based tactile sensors" operating at 60 Hz vs. Digit 360's 240 Hz camera (Sec. 3).
- real robot? partially — all sensor-characterization results (spatial resolution, force error, latency) are on the physical sensor and a micro-indenter test rig; the multi-modal classification dataset is collected via "a robotic arm and a 4-finger robotic hand" acting as an "action platform" (Sec. 3) manipulating real objects (spatula/pan, eggs, bottles). No sim-to-real or policy-learning result is reported in this paper.

## Limitations stated by the authors
- Even at 240 Hz, "highly dynamic movements may not be fully captured" by Digit 360's camera (Sec. 3) — i.e. the vision channel remains a temporal bottleneck relative to the 10 kHz audio/vibration channel.
- The finger-independent classification model underperforms the finger-dependent model because "intertidal [inertial] and pressure-based modalities perform worse" without the implicit cross-finger/whole-hand grasp encoding (Sec. 3) — a limitation of single-finger-only sensing pointed out via the ablation.
- Discussion section (Sec. 4) is present in the paper's structure but its content was not captured in the parsed excerpt used for this note beyond the section heading; treat any further stated limitations there as "not captured" rather than absent.

## Notes for the survey
- Simulator model for the survey's "what simulator exists" column: Digit 360 ships URDF geometric/collision meshes for IsaacSim RL environments (code README), not a tactile-image renderer — contrast explicitly with the original DIGIT sensor, for which the separate TACTO simulator (tacto_2020) renders synthetic tactile *images*. Do not conflate the two kinds of "simulator model" when filling in the survey's table.
- Licence: a `LICENSE` file exists in the repo (code/md, "This project is licensed under [LICENSE](LICENSE)") but its type/content was not captured by the parser — not stated which licence, same caveat pattern as several of the hardware-hand notes in this batch.
- Access model differs from the other tactile sensors in this batch: per the code README's "Call for Proposals," Digit 360 units are not simply bought or self-manufactured from open files but are "made available at no charge to researchers" selected via a Meta/GelSight Inc. proposal process — worth flagging in the survey as a different openness model from DIGIT's low-cost, self-manufacturable design (digit_2020.md).
- No corpus paper in this survey's parsed set can currently be cited as an actual user of Digit 360 (see grep result above); the sensor's very recent release date (Oct 2024) likely explains this gap — revisit this "used by" list if newer papers are added to the corpus.
