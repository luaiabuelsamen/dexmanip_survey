# bench2dex_2026 — Bench2Dex: Benchmarking Visuo-Tactile Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation Across Dexterous Hands (Yang et al., arXiv 2026)

sources: papers/md/bench2dex_2026.md [f70ca3ec] ; code/md/bench2dex_2026.md [766d542d]

## One-line contribution
An Isaac-Lab benchmark of 26 long-horizon bimanual tasks across 12 bimanual arm-hand embodiments, with a shared surface-aligned ray-casting tactile interface that maps 12 different hand geometries into a common 8-bit image-like tactile representation, ~1.3K teleoperated demonstrations across 8 modalities, executable stage-level metrics, and a 7-perturbation invariance/equivariance robustness protocol, on which GR00T N1.5 leads matched-scene success (48.5%) but the ranking compresses under combined perturbation (Abstract, Sec. 3, 4).

## Setting — hands, vendor, DoF

- What the **code** does show (not the same as hardware DoF — these are the DexPilot-retargeting *active* teleop joint sets, i.e. `target_joint_names` in `teleop/hand_cfgs/*.yml`, after mimic/abduction joints are locked or ignored for retargeting stability): Ability 6 (`thumb_q1, thumb_q2, index_q1, middle_q1, pinky_q1, ring_q1`); Revo2 6 per side (`*_thumb_metacarpal/proximal, *_index/middle/ring/pinky_proximal`); RH56DFX 6 per side (`*_thumb_1/2, *_index/middle/ring/little_1`); RH5DG2 13 per side (thumb yaw/mcp/dip + index/middle pitch/mcp/pip + ring/pinky mcp/pip); Schunk SVH 9 per side (Thumb_Opposition/Flexion, Index_Finger_Proximal/Distal, Finger_Spread, Pinky, Ring_Finger, Middle_Finger_Proximal/Distal). ## Simulator / physics
Built on **Isaac Lab** [ref 37,38] (Sec. 1, 3). No physics engine version, timestep, substep count, or control-loop frequency is stated in the parsed paper text. The code's environment README shows an install pin to `isaacsim[all,extscache]==5.1.0` (code/md README), which fixes the Isaac Sim/PhysX version used but the paper itself does not quote this number, so it is recorded here as a code-only fact, not a paper claim. No `#parallel envs` figure is given (teleoperation is single-environment/human-in-the-loop; evaluation launches "26 × 4 × 4 × 50 = 20,800 evaluation episodes" but not a stated environment-parallelism count) (Sec. 4.1).

## Observation and action
- 8 recorded data modalities (Table S7): RGB images, depth maps, robot states (joint qpos/qvel/qeffort), object states (poses/velocities), tactile signals (unified surface-aligned tactile images), 2D boxes, 3D boxes, occupancy (TSDF/mesh-based).
- 6 calibrated camera viewpoints for offline replay rendering: chest, overhead, stereo left, stereo right, left wrist, right wrist (Sec. 3.2, "Replay pipeline").
- Tactile channel: per tactile site s, an 8-bit image T_{s,t} ∈ {0,...,255}^{H×W} (H=W=240 released resolution) built by casting rays from precomputed surface points along inward normals against task-object meshes; first-hit depth = penetration past the nominal contact surface; a two-pass contact-consistency check (forward hit + reverse ray from just short of the hit point must also intersect) validates each sample; max cast range 15 mm; static scene geometry (tabletop) is excluded from raycast targets (Sec. 3.3, App. C.1). Depth-to-int8 quantization is piecewise: "0.005 mm per integer level for z < 0.5 mm" (fine near contact), "a coarser 0.03 mm per level for larger... values," saturating at 255 for depths ≥ 5.15 mm; followed by Gaussian spatial smoothing with σ=1.5, kernel size max(1, ⌊9/step⌋) rounded to odd (9×9 at step=1) (App. C.1). Both the raw metric ray depth (float32, m) and a binary contact-validity mask are also stored per site (`tactile/distance_along_normal`, `tactile/contact_mask`, HDF5 schema, App. D).
- Action space (policy-specific, App. F): ACT — absolute joint targets, chunk length T_pred=30, T_o=1, ℓ1 reconstruction + KL(weight 10) cVAE loss, hidden dim 512, feed-forward dim 3200, ResNet-18 per-camera encoder over 4 views, AdamW lr 1e-5, batch 32, ≤6000 epochs, 1× RTX 4090. DP — image-conditioned 1D U-Net, widths (256,512,1024), kernel 5, 128-d diffusion-step embedding, T_o=3, T_pred=8, T_exec=6 (receding horizon), DDPM ε-prediction over 100 steps / 100 inference denoise steps, AdamW lr 1e-4, batch 512, 500-step warmup + cosine decay, EMA 0.9999, ≤300 epochs, 8× H100. π0.5 — full fine-tune of `pi05_base`, non-LoRA PaliGemma-2B + Gemma-300M flow-matching expert, state/action dims sized to each embodiment's active DoF, 4 RGB views + proprio + instruction, T_pred=20 (max token length 280), T_exec=20, AdamW cosine 1e-4→1e-6 over 2000 steps, 100-step warmup, batch 256, 32 workers, fsdp_devices=1, EMA disabled. GR00T N1.5 — full-parameter fine-tune (no LoRA), state padded to 64-d, action head extended to 64-d with masked padded channels, 4 RGB views + proprio + instruction, flow-matching head, T_o=1, T_pred=16, 4 denoising steps at inference, T_exec=16, AdamW lr 1e-4, 5% linear warmup + cosine decay, batch 64, bf16, 20,000 steps, 1× H100.

## Method (benchmark + baseline evaluation, not itself an RL/IL method)
- paradigm: benchmark; 4 baselines evaluated (ACT, DP trained from scratch; π0.5, GR00T N1.5 fine-tuned from public checkpoints) (Sec. 3.4, 4.1).
- reward/loss: n/a for the benchmark itself; per-policy losses are as in the action-space block above (ACT ℓ1+KL cVAE; DP/GR00T flow-matching/DDPM denoising objectives; π0.5 flow matching).
- key trick(s): (1) decoupling of lightweight online teleoperation recording (only action + minimal state, to preserve real-time responsiveness) from an offline replay pass that regenerates the expensive RGB-D/tactile/box/occupancy modalities deterministically from the same seed (Sec. 3.2); (2) a tactile "registry" mapping each of the 12 hands to site names, surface assets, resolution, normal convention and site-body attachment, avoiding hand-specific branching in data-collection code (Sec. 3.3); (3) reach-and-stop protocol requiring the terminal predicate to hold for a configured dwell time (default 0.5 s) before counting success, to reject transient/unstable contacts (Sec. 3.5, App. E.1).

## Evaluation
- exact success criterion: "stable success" — terminal predicate must remain true for a continuous dwell time Δ_e (default 0.5 s), formalized as S_e = 1[j_e^stable exists] where j_e^stable = min{j ≤ n_e : h_{e,j} ≥ Δ_e} and h is accumulated dwell time since the predicate last became true (App. E.1, "Reach-and-stop success"). SR = N^-1 Σ S_e.
- secondary/diagnostic metrics (verbatim definitions, App. E): **LSCR** (latched stage completion rate) = mean over episodes of (1/K_e) Σ_k L_{e,k}, where L_{e,k}=1 once stage k's predicate is true AND its dependencies were satisfied by previously-latched or concurrently-reached stages, in an acyclic dependency graph, and once set L_{e,k} stays 1 ("Latching prevents progress from being erased when an early predicate is intentionally reversed by a later action, such as closing door after opening it"). **Completion time**: success-conditional mean time to stable success. **Safety**: hard-violation indicator B_e = D_e ∨ H_e (drop event D_e, tracked-object high-speed event H_e by default; joint-limit only if a task promotes it); safe-success indicator S_e(1-B_e); SafeSR, DropR, HSR ("These events are not mutually exclusive, so their rates need not sum to HVR"); pooled violation-step rate over executed physics steps. "High speed is a severe-motion proxy rather than a contact-force or collision measurement." **Robustness/relative ratio (RR)**: SR_p / SR_None for shifted channel p ∈ {Equi., Inv., Full}, aggregated as the mean over P+ = {Equi., Inv., Full}. **Diagnostics** (App. E.2): ever-instantaneous SR (I_e = max_j C_{e,j}, no dwell requirement), terminal-state SR (F_e = C_{e,n_e}), task efficiency TE = H_q^expert·δt_q / actual time (unbounded, success-conditional, "comparable only under matched simulation timing..."), grasp stability index GSI = weighted combination of lift L, hold-state H (1/0.35/0.15/0.0 for held/lifted-stable/lifted-only/other), motion-damping M=exp[-½(‖v‖/σ_v + ‖ω‖/σ_ω)], quality Q, and anti-slip A=1-min(1,s/s_max), all components in [0,1] ("a proxy based on object lift, stable holding, object motion, hold duration, and slip events" since "reliable hand–object contact geometry and hand forward kinematics are not uniformly available for every supported embodiment"), tool selection accuracy (TSA) and tool switch success rate (TSSR, "unavailable for episodes without a switch attempt").
- perturbation/robustness axes: 7 perturbation types split into two groups (Sec. 1, 3.6, App. G). **Invariance** (correct action unchanged): scene background (60 iTHOR/USD scenes, 50 seen/10 unseen), tabletop texture (11,824 materials, 9,436 seen/2,388 unseen, 80/20 deterministic split), lighting (distant light: intensity [1000,1500], RGB channel [0.4,1.0], pitch [-30°,-5°], yaw [-90°,90°], angular size [0.5°,1.0°]; dome light: intensity [150,350], color between [0.8,0.8,0.6] and [1,1,1], exposure [-0.5,0.5]), distractor objects (1-3 per episode from 68 assets, 52 seen/16 unseen, scale [0.8,1.2]×, placed with 5 cm table-edge margin and a central exclusion zone x∈[-0.25,0.25] m, y∈[-0.20,0.35] m), camera pose (world cameras: ±5 mm translation/look-at, ±3 cm distance, ±1° RPY; wrist cameras: ±5 mm translation, ±1° RPY; stereo pair shares one perturbation sample). **Equivariance** (correct action must change with the perturbation): object pose (explicit objects: ±2 cm xy, ±10° yaw, progressively reduced if colliding; free objects resampled within task zones), table height (±0.05 m around nominal 0.75 m, robot mount height unchanged). Four evaluation channels (Table S10): `None` (matched anchor, all factors replayed exactly), `Equi.` (only equivariance resampled), `Inv.` (only invariance resampled, from the unseen split), `Full` (both resampled independently, no anchor).
- headline numbers (Table 2, Sec. 4.2, 1,300 rollouts per policy per channel = 50 rollouts × 26 settings): matched `None` condition — GR00T 631/1300 (48.5%), ACT 383/1300 (29.5%), π0.5 355/1300 (27.3%), DP 168/1300 (12.9%); GR00T is the strict task-level leader on 23/26 settings. Combined `Full` shift — GR00T 258/1300 (19.8%), π0.5 256/1300 (19.7%), ACT 169/1300 (13.0%), DP 50/1300 (3.8%); by task-level leads under `Full`, π0.5 leads on 12 tasks vs GR00T's 8 (ACT 2, 4 ties). Four-channel mean SR: GR00T 28.3%, π0.5 20.3%, ACT 18.0%, DP 7.1%. All-task mean LSCR: GR00T 47.3%, π0.5 36.1%, ACT 33.5%, DP 23.0% ("higher by 19.0, 15.8, 15.5, and 15.9 percentage points" than mean SR respectively). Full-to-None SR ratio: ACT 44.1%, DP 29.8%, π0.5 72.1% (highest ratio despite not highest absolute), GR00T 40.9%. Isolated-axis asymmetry: ACT/DP score higher under `Equi.` than `Inv.` (15.4% vs 14.0%; 6.3% vs 5.2%); π0.5/GR00T reversed (13.2% vs 21.0%; 18.8% vs 26.0%). Task-level heterogeneity (Sec. 4.4): 12/26 settings have nonzero `Full` success for all 4 policies, 3/26 (Jigsaw Puzzle Assembly, Fridge Fruit Shelf Sorting, Soup Serving) are zero for all 4, 11/26 are mixed; Microwave Bowl Loading has the largest cross-policy `Full` total (29,4,21,33 for ACT/DP/π0.5/GR00T).
- baselines beaten: n/a — this is the benchmark's own baseline comparison (ACT, DP, π0.5, GR00T N1.5 evaluated against each other, not against an external prior).
- real robot?: none; all evaluation is in Isaac Lab simulation.

## Limitations stated by the authors
"the hand and tactile models in Bench2Dex have not been calibrated against matching physical hardware... Bench2Dex focuses on a shared simulation interface... rather than reproducing a specific physical hand–sensor system." (Sec. 5, "Limitation and Outlook"). "the tactile interface in Bench2Dex does not reproduce the output of any specific physical sensor" (Sec. 1). Evaluation caveats (Sec. 4.2-4.4, self-described, not a formal "Limitations" section but stated as caveats): 50 rollouts/cell are evaluation episodes not independent retraining replicates so between-training variance is not estimated; tasks and embodiments are not factorially crossed so task-level contrasts cannot isolate embodiment effects; `Full` is not guaranteed harder than `Equi.`/`Inv.` per-instance (it is lower in 89/104 task-policy comparisons, tied in the rest, but this is not enforced by the sampling design).

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "no existing benchmark supports visuo-tactile data collection across multiple dexterous hands under one setting" (Sec. 1)
- "the most informative interaction is often hidden from external cameras" (Sec. 3.3)
- "A policy that works on one hand and fails on another is not necessarily a weaker policy – it may simply not have been exposed to that hand's shape during training." (Sec. 1)
- "high speed is a severe-motion proxy rather than a contact-force or collision measurement" (App. E.1, "Safety")
- "Reporting the two groups separately, rather than as one aggregate robustness score, lets a drop in success rate be traced to nuisance sensitivity or to genuine task generalization" (Sec. 1)

## Notes for the survey
- This is the more thoroughly-instrumented of the two 2026 dexterous benchmarks for our evaluation section: 12 embodiments vs DexVerse's 6 hands×3 arms, a real tactile modality (DexVerse has none), and an explicit invariance/equivariance perturbation taxonomy that is more principled than DexVerse's plain visual-variation toggles. Use its SR/LSCR/GSI/reach-and-stop-dwell-time definitions as the metric vocabulary reference for our own evaluation protocol.
- SOURCE THIN on hand hardware specs: no paper text gives per-hand DoF or full vendor names (only Sharpa Wave, BrainCo Revo2, Schunk SVH, Shadow Hand are self-identifying); the `target_joint_names`/`joint_limits_override` counts pulled from `code/md` are retargeting-active-joint counts, not hardware DoF, and are flagged as such above — do not cite them as hand DoF elsewhere in the survey without independently checking each hand's own spec sheet (several of which likely already have their own notes in this corpus, e.g. inspire/shadow/allegro entries, but those are out of scope for this note per HOWTO rule 1).
- Physics engine version/timestep and licence type are both SOURCE THIN — the code pins Isaac Sim 5.1.0 in its install instructions but the paper never states a physics timestep, and the repo's LICENSE file content was not parsed.
- The GSI "proxy" metric and its explicit admission that "reliable hand–object contact geometry and hand forward kinematics are not uniformly available for every supported embodiment" is a direct parallel to this project's own render-before-believing stance on contact metrics — worth a cross-reference in the survey's metrics-critique section.
