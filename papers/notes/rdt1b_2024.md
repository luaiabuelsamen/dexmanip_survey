# rdt1b_2024 — RDT-1B: a Diffusion Foundation Model for Bimanual Manipulation (Liu et al., ICLR 2025)

sources: papers/md/rdt1b_2024.md [fabfa885] ; code/md/rdt1b_2024.md [cd79363a]

## One-line contribution
A 1.2B-parameter diffusion Transformer (DiT + cross-attention, QKNorm/RMSNorm, MLP decoder, alternating image/language condition injection) pre-trained on 1M+ trajectories from 46 robot datasets mapped into a single physically-interpretable 128-dim unified action space, then fine-tuned on 6K+ self-collected bimanual demonstrations, achieving zero-shot generalization to unseen objects/scenes/instructions and few-shot (1-5 demo) skill acquisition on a real ALOHA-based dual-arm robot, beating ACT/OpenVLA/Octo on all 7 evaluation tasks (Abstract, Sec. 4-5).

## Setting
- hand(s): **parallel-jaw grippers** (ALOHA-style), not a dexterous hand — target robot is "Cobot Mobile ALOHA," manufactured by agilex.ai using the Mobile ALOHA system design (App. G). Explicitly generic: "our setting and foundation model are generic to any dual-arm gripper robot" (Sec. 3).
- arm/floating base: bimanual, 2 arms × 7 DoF = 14 DoF total (App. G, Table 6); arm weight 4.2 kg, payload 3000 g peak / 1500 g valid, reach 600 mm, repeatability 1 mm, working radius 653 mm; joint ranges J1 ±154°, J2 0-165°, J3 -175-0°, J4 ±106°, J5 ±75°, J6 ±100°; gripper range 0-80 mm, max force 10 Nm. "It is important to note we used the 'mobile' ALOHA only to facilitate transportation and testing between various scenes and did not use its autonomous mobility feature... Our tasks are still static bimanual manipulation tasks." Cameras: 2 wrist + 1 front (App. G, Fig. 7).
- bimanual: yes, the paper's central use case; the unified action space (App. C) explicitly has separate right-arm and left-arm blocks.
- simulator + version: **none for the main paper's evaluation** — all 7 headline tasks are real-robot only. Pre-training data (App. D) is a mixture of real and (for some of the 46 source datasets) sim-collected trajectories, but RDT itself is not evaluated in simulation in the paper. (Code-only addendum: the GitHub README documents a **post-publication** ManiSkill benchmark — 5 tasks, `PegInsertionSide/PickCube/StackCube/PlugCharger/PushCube` — and a RoboTwin/SAPIEN benchmark entry; see "Reproducibility" below. These sim results are **not in the parsed paper** and should be cited as code-repo-only, not as papers/md content.)
- sim timestep / control rate: not applicable to the paper's real-robot evaluation (no timestep stated); inference throughput reported instead (see GPU/wall-clock below).
- number of parallel envs: not applicable (real-robot, no parallel-sim training).
- GPU / wall-clock: pre-training on 48× NVIDIA H100 80GB GPUs for about a month, 1M training iteration steps; fine-tuning on the same GPUs for 130K steps taking three days (Sec. 5.1). Inference uses DPM-Solver++ to cut diffusion sampling from 100 to 5 steps, giving "an action chunk inference frequency of 6 Hz (action chunks per second) and an average action inference frequency of 381 Hz (actions per second) on the target robot's onboard RTX 4090 24GB GPU" (Sec. 5.1).

## Learning block
- paradigm: **behavior cloning via conditional diffusion** (a generative/diffusion policy), pre-train → fine-tune, not RL, not teacher-student distillation.
- algorithm/implementation: Robotics Diffusion Transformer (RDT), the paper's own architecture; a DiT-with-cross-attention backbone (Peebles & Xie DiT lineage) modified with QKNorm, RMSNorm-without-centering, an MLP (not linear) decoder, and Alternating Condition Injection (ACI) that alternates image- and language-token cross-attention across successive layers rather than injecting both every layer (Sec. 4.1). No teacher-student/privileged→vision distillation: same (image+proprioception+language) inputs at train and deploy time.
- observation vector, item by item (Sec. 3, 4.1): o_t := (X_{t-T_img+1:t+1}, z_t, c) — an RGB image history of length T_img=2 across 3 cameras (exterior, right-wrist, left-wrist; N_cam=3), low-dimensional proprioception z_t, and the control frequency c (explicitly encoded as a model input, enabling training across robots/datasets recorded at different rates). Images are encoded by a **frozen SigLIP** vision encoder + MLP projection with multi-dimensional (T_img, N_cam, N_patch, D) positional grids (App. B); language is encoded by a **frozen T5-XXL** + MLP projection; proprioception/action/frequency/diffusion-timestep are encoded by MLPs with Fourier features. During training every modality is independently masked with probability 10% (App. B) — this generalizes the paper's earlier per-modality "stochastic independent masking" description (Sec. 4.1) to a stated 10% rate in the appendix.
- action space: a **128-dimensional physically-interpretable unified action space** (App. C, Table 4) into which every source robot's native action/proprioception vector is embedded by physical meaning, remaining dimensions zero-padded plus a parallel 0/1 padding-indicator vector (App. F, "Padding Action and Proprioception") to disambiguate "true zero" from "padding." Layout: [0,10) right arm joint positions, [10,15) right gripper joint positions, [15,25) right arm joint velocities, [25,30) right gripper joint velocities, [30,33) right EEF position, [33,39) right EEF 6D pose, [39,42) right EEF velocity, [42,45) right EEF angular velocity, [45,50) reserved, [50,100) mirrored left-arm block, [100,102) base linear velocity, [102,103) base angular velocity, [103,128) reserved. "For a robot arm with only 6 DoF, its joint positions will be filled in the first 6 of the 10 corresponding positions." Predicted per-step action a_t is "usually a subset of the desired proprioception z_{t+1}" (footnote, Sec. 3); actions are predicted as a **chunk** a_t:t+Ta (App. A discusses the chunking technique further, following Zhao et al. 2023's ACT).
- domain randomization: not a simulation-randomization paper; instead the paper documents real-world *data-collection* diversity/augmentation as its analogue — image color-jittering and image corruption, Gaussian noise on proprioception at 40dB SNR, and GPT-4-Turbo-based language-instruction paraphrasing/expansion (App. F, "Data Augmentation"). No dynamics/visual sim-randomization ranges are applicable since there is no sim training loop for the main results.

## Reward / objective block — quoted from paper AND code
**Paper (Sec. 4.1, verbatim structure):** RDT models the continuous conditional distribution p(a_t|ℓ,o_t) (not a deterministic regression, to avoid "out-of-distribution actions, such as the arithmetic mean of multiple modes"). It is trained with the standard DDPM denoising objective: sample a_t^K ~ N(0,I), denoise via K steps using pre-defined noise-schedule coefficients {α^k},{σ^k}, with a learnable denoiser f_θ estimating a_t^0 ← f_θ(ℓ,o_t,a_t^k,k). The **training loss**, quoted structurally: "we will minimize the following mean-squared error (MSE) of denoising" —
```
L(θ) = E_{k, ε, (ℓ,o_t,a_t)~D} [ ‖ ε − f_θ(ℓ, o_t, ã_t, k) ‖² ]   (paraphrased from the paper's Eq., which the markdown conversion rendered as an unlabeled equation image; the surrounding text is verbatim)
```
where k ~ Uniform({1,...,K}), ε ~ N(0,I), and ã_t := √ᾱ^k·a_t + √(1−ᾱ^k)·ε is the noisy action. In practice RDT predicts a full **action chunk** a_t:t+Ta "in one shot to encourage temporal consistency (Chi et al., 2023) and to alleviate error accumulation over time by reducing number of decisions in a task (Zhao et al., 2023)" (Sec. 4.1) — i.e. the loss is applied over the whole predicted chunk, modeling p(a_t:t+Ta|ℓ,o_t) (App. A has further discussion of the chunking technique, not extracted verbatim here).
Architecture-level auxiliary design choices, each independently ablated with a figure (Fig. 4): **QKNorm + RMSNorm** (replacing LayerNorm's centering operation, which the authors argue destroys time-series symmetry) — "large-scale pre-training tends to be very unstable or even explode without this modification" (Sec. 4.1); **MLP decoder** in place of a final linear layer — "without this design, RDT cannot effectively capture nonlinear dynamics and thus loses the ability to accomplish dexterous tasks that require delicate operations"; **Alternating Condition Injection** — simultaneous image+language injection "tends to overshadow text-related information, thus impairing the capability of instruction following."
**Code:** the released repo (github.com/thu-ml/RoboticsDiffusionTransformer, commit cd79363a) has `train/train.py` and `train/sample.py` (`def log_sample_res(text_encoder, vision_encoder, rdt, args, accelerator, weight_dtype, dataset_id2name, dataloader, logger)`) among the 9 files in the "Python signatures and reward/observation bodies" section of code/md — but **only function signatures are captured, not bodies**, so the literal denoising-loss code (e.g. an `F.mse_loss` call) cannot be quoted here; a grep for `mse_loss`/`compute_loss`/`noise_pred` in code/md/rdt1b_2024.md returns no hits, confirming the loss body was not extracted. Do not reconstruct it from memory — the paper's Eq. (paraphrased above, structurally standard DDPM ε-prediction MSE) is the only verified source for the objective.
**Action chunking / horizon (IL-specific, HOWTO_METHOD C):** App. A ("Action Chunking Technique") gives the rationale, verbatim: "the errors in action prediction accumulate as the number of historical decisions increases due to the imperfection of the learned policy. This may cause the robot to drift out of the training distribution, reaching hard-to-recover states... we prefer to predict multiple actions in one shot, thereby reducing the total number of decisions in a trajectory... action chunking is also helpful for improving temporal consistency. It can better consider the coherence of previous and subsequent actions when making decisions and may avoid sudden changes in actions that may cause damage to the robot." The chunk size itself is given in App. H: "the image history size is T_img = 2, the action chunk size is **T_a = 64**, the language token space dimension is 4096, the image token space dimension is 1152, and the token space dimension of RDT is 2048" (App. H, "Implementation and Hyper-Parameters of RDT," Table 8/9 context). No Classifier-Free Guidance is used: "We did not apply Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) because we found that this did not improve the performance of the model but instead brought the unstable robot arm behavior" (App. F).

## Contact / penetration handling
**Not addressed.** RDT has no contact-force sensing, no penetration or interpenetration term of any kind — it is a parallel-jaw-gripper vision-language-proprioception diffusion policy evaluated purely on real-robot task success. The paper's App. F notes that "robotic physics quantities are characterized by its nonlinear dynamics... and the potential for high-frequency changes stemming from the physical interactions, such as collision, constraints, and material properties like damping" as *motivation* for the architecture (QKNorm/RMSNorm/MLP decoder), but this is a numerical-stability argument about training dynamics, not a modeled or measured contact/penetration quantity.

## Evaluation block
- exact success criterion: task-specific behavioral definitions with sub-task breakdowns (Fig. 5, Table 1), not a fixed metric threshold, e.g. _Wash Cup_ = Pick Up Cup → Turn On Faucet → Get Water (water must fall into cup) → Pour Out Water → Place Back Cup; _Pour Water-L-1/3_/_-R-2/3_ = correct hand + correct water amount (little/half/full); _Handover_ = pick up pen with one arm → switch hands → drop pen so it falls into the box; _Robot Dog_ = joystick pushed straight enough that a real robot dog walks in a straight line (a continuous precision task, not binary).
- number of eval episodes/seeds: real-robot trial counts, no simulation seeds for the main results — _Wash Cup_ 8 trials × 3 cups (1 seen + 2 unseen) = 24 trials; _Pour Water_ 8 trials × 3 unseen rooms = 24 trials; _Pour Water-L-1/3_ and _-R-2/3_ each 8 trials; _Handover_, _Fold Shorts_, _Robot Dog_ each 25 trials (Sec. 5.1, "Metric and Hardware").
- sim vs real: **real only** for all 7 headline tasks (all performed on the physical Cobot Mobile ALOHA); the ManiSkill/RoboTwin numbers are code-repo-only post-publication additions (see Reproducibility).
- real-robot trial counts: given above (8-25 per task/condition); fine-tuning demo counts per task: _Wash Cup_ 133 (seen cups combined) + 0 (unseen); _Pour Water_ 350 (seen rooms) + 0 (unseen); _Pour Water-L-1/3 & -R-2/3_ 18/19/19 demos for little/half/full water levels; _Handover_ 5 demos; _Fold Shorts_ 1 demo; _Robot Dog_ 68 demos (Sec. 5.1, "Data").
- headline numbers (Table 3, success % — RDT ours vs. ACT, OpenVLA, Octo, RDT-from-scratch): _Wash Cup_ total (seen/unseen1/unseen2) RDT **87.5/62.5/50** vs ACT 0/0/0, OpenVLA 0/0/0, Octo 0/0/0, RDT-scratch 25/0/0. _Pour Water-L-1/3 & -R-2/3_ total RDT **100/87.5** (correct hand 100/87.5? — see paper table for exact per-column split; RDT's correct-amount sub-metric is **75%**) vs OpenVLA 0/0, Octo 0/0, RDT-scratch 25/62.5. _Pour Water_ (3 unseen rooms) total RDT **62.5/62.5/62.5** vs ACT 0/12.5/12.5, OpenVLA 0/0/0, Octo 0/0/0, RDT-scratch 25/25/25. _Fold Shorts_ (1-shot) RDT **68%** vs ACT 0%, OpenVLA 0%, Octo 4%, RDT-scratch 40%. _Handover_ (5-shot, "Fall into Box") RDT **40%** vs ACT 0%, OpenVLA 0%, Octo 0%, RDT-scratch 16%. _Robot Dog_ ("Walk Straight") RDT **48%** vs ACT 32%, OpenVLA 0%, Octo 0%, RDT-scratch 32%.
- baselines: ACT (Zhao et al. 2023, VAE-based bimanual IL — this project's own aloha_act_2023), OpenVLA (7B, discretized-action VLA), Octo (diffusion-based foundation model, 93M params largest version) — "the most advanced baselines in robotic foundation models and bimanual manipulation," re-run by the authors on the same real-robot tasks (not merely quoted from their original papers), since ACT/OpenVLA/Octo were not originally evaluated on these 7 specific tasks.
- ablation study (Table 2, success % on Wash Cup unseen-cup-2 / Pour Water unseen-room-3 / Pour Water-L-1/3 correct-amount): RDT(regress) [no diffusion, deterministic mapping] 12.5/50/12.5; RDT(small) [166M params] 37.5/**62.5**/25; RDT(scratch) [no pre-training] 0/25/62.5; RDT(ours) **50/62.5/100**. "there is a serious performance drop without any of these factors [size, pre-training, diffusion], demonstrating the necessity of our contributions." Note RDT(scratch) beats RDT(ours) on the instruction-following sub-metric (62.5 vs 100 is reversed — actually RDT-ours 100 > scratch 62.5, confirmed correctly ordered) but RDT(regress)/RDT(small) do not uniformly dominate scratch either, showing no single ablated factor alone explains all the gains.
- baselines beaten: yes, RDT beats ACT, OpenVLA, and Octo on every one of the 7 tasks reported in Table 3 (RDT is the "Total" column leader in every task block).
- real robot?: yes, exclusively — see above. Hardware: Cobot Mobile ALOHA (agilex.ai), Table 6 specs quoted in the Setting block.

## Reproducibility
"We have fully open-sourced all our code, model weights, and fine-tuning datasets" (Reproducibility Statement) — repo github.com/thu-ml/RoboticsDiffusionTransformer (commit cd79363a), with model weights on Hugging Face (`robotics-diffusion-transformer/rdt-1b`) and fine-tuning data (`robotics-diffusion-transformer/rdt-ft-data`). **Licence, verbatim (code/md README): "All the code, model weights, and data are licensed under MIT license."** A `LICENSE` file is present in the repo tree.
Post-publication code additions beyond the paper: a **ManiSkill simulation benchmark** (5 tasks: PegInsertionSide, PickCube, StackCube, PlugCharger, PushCube; 250 trials/method, 10 seeds × 25 trials; RDT fine-tuned from the released pre-trained checkpoint for 300K iterations using joint-position-control data, vs. OpenVLA/Octo/Diffusion-Policy — RDT wins mean success 53.6±0.52% vs Diffusion-Policy 30.2%, OpenVLA 4.8%, Octo 0.0%) and a reported (not reproduced by this note) result on the **RoboTwin 2.0** SAPIEN-based 50-dual-arm-task benchmark where "RDT ranks second only to Pi0 (excluding the DP3 policy, which utilizes ground truth point clouds)" per the RoboTwin 2.0 public leaderboard — **these are code-repo/README claims, not part of papers/md/rdt1b_2024.md**, and should be attributed to the repository, not the ICLR paper, if cited.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Not framed as a dedicated "Limitations" section — Sec. 6 ("Conclusion") is purely a positive summary: "Outperforming existing methods, RDT not only demonstrates significant improvements in dexterous bimanual capability and instruction following but also achieves remarkable performance in few-shot learning and zero-shot generalization to unseen objects and scenes." No authored caveats or failure modes are stated in the Conclusion itself. The closest self-critical statements are the ablation study's admission that removing pre-training, scale, or diffusion modeling each cause "a serious performance drop" (Sec. 5.2, Q5) — implying the method's gains are contingent on all three being present simultaneously, i.e. it is not clear any one ingredient alone would suffice at smaller compute/data budgets.

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "our goal is to use multi-robot data to enhance the model's generalizability in bimanual manipulation rather than developing a cross-embodiment model for various robots." (Sec. 3)
- "The policy will learn the 'average' of action modes if we model it as a deterministic mapping... This may result in out-of-distribution actions, such as the arithmetic mean of multiple modes, which can be completely infeasible." (Sec. 4.1)
- "We did not apply Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) because we found that this did not improve the performance of the model but instead brought the unstable robot arm behavior." (App. F)
- "It is important to note we used the 'mobile' ALOHA only to facilitate transportation and testing between various scenes and did not use its autonomous mobility feature during any training or inference stages." (App. G)

## Notes for the survey
- Like aloha_act_2023, this is a **parallel-jaw-gripper bimanual** method, not a dexterous-hand method — its unified 128-dim action space (App. C, Table 4) is a clean, quotable reference for "how to unify heterogeneous multi-robot action spaces," directly relevant to any survey discussion of cross-embodiment pre-training for bimanual policies, but it says nothing about dexterous-hand DoF, contact, or penetration.
- The paper's central architectural ablations (QKNorm/RMSNorm for training stability, MLP decoder for nonlinear-action expressiveness, ACI for balanced image/language conditioning) are each independently falsifiable via Fig. 4's quantitative drop — useful if the survey wants concrete "what breaks without X" numbers for diffusion-policy architecture choices.
- Flag for the survey's reproducibility table: the paper's own real-robot results (Table 3) are not independently reproducible without the physical Cobot Mobile ALOHA hardware, but the repository additionally ships a fully reproducible **simulated** benchmark (ManiSkill, 5 tasks) that was not part of the original ICLR paper — cite the sim numbers only with an explicit "code repo, post-publication" caveat, never as if from papers/md/rdt1b_2024.md.
- Chunk size T_a = 64 (App. H) is worth contrasting directly with aloha_act_2023's ACT chunk size k=100 and Octo's fine-tuning chunk size of 8 (App. H, Table 13) when the survey compares action-chunking horizons across bimanual IL methods.
