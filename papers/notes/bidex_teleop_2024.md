# bidex_teleop_2024 — Bimanual Dexterity for Complex Tasks (Shaw, Li et al., CoRL 2024)

sources: papers/md/bidex_teleop_2024.md [15cea952 / arxiv 2411.13677] ; no code

## One-line contribution
BiDex: a low-cost (~$6k glove system, ~$30-35k with two xArms and two LEAP Hands) bimanual teleoperation rig combining Manus Meta motion-capture gloves for fingertip tracking with GELLO-style teacher arms for wrist tracking, shown to collect faster, higher-completion-rate bimanual dexterous demonstrations than Apple Vision Pro or SteamVR, and to train better behavior-cloning policies as a result (Abstract, §3, §5).

## Setting
- hand(s): LEAP Hand (16 DoF, 4 fingers, multi-fingered) for the main comparisons; LEAP Hand V2 (21 DoF, hybrid rigid/soft, human-sized, with an active articulated palm) for the "extreme dexterity" experiments (§4.2). Both multi-fingered, not parallel-jaw grippers. Arm: two xArm 6 arms (tabletop/mobile) or two full-size arms tracked via GELLO teacher arms (§3.2, Table 6). Bimanual: yes, over 50 DoF total system (Abstract).
- simulator / physics: none — real-hardware teleoperation and real-robot BC only; no simulator anywhere in the paper.
- observation (for the trained BC policy, §14/Fig. 5): RGB image (tokenized via a ViT, weights initialized from the "Soup 1M" model) + current hand joint angles (proprioception, via a linear layer); note current arm joints are deliberately excluded from proprioception ("A key decision that greatly improves policy generalization is to exclude current arm joints from the proprioception... force the model to extract object information from image observations, rather than overfitting to predict actions close to current arm states", §14). For teleoperation data collection itself (Algorithm 1), the observation logged is `o_t = {A_l_t, A_r_t, H_l_t, H_r_t, {I_c}_t}` (left/right arm joints, left/right hand joints, camera images).
- action space: teleop action logged as `a_t = {K_l_t, K_r_t, q_l_t, q_r_t}` — kinematic teacher-arm joint targets for each arm plus IK-derived finger joint targets for each hand (Algorithm 1). For the trained BC policy: absolute joint angles of two arms and two hands; with LEAP Hand this is stated as "16 dimensions for each hand and 6 dimensions for each arm for a total of 44 dimensions" (§5.2).
- objects / data: task-specific demonstration counts — ACT policies trained on "around 50 demonstrations" (§5.2) for LEAP Hand tasks (Handover, Cup Stacking, Bottle Pouring); a separate comparison (Appendix §10) collected "100 demonstrations on the Pringles can handover task using both systems [BiDex and Apple Vision Pro]" and trained policies with varying demonstration counts. Objects: YCB Pringles can, cups, bottles, boxes, chairs, trash, drying rack/bowls/spoons (long-horizon "Clearing the Dishes" task, Fig. 4), plus drill, pot, plate, hanger, chopsticks, basket for LEAP Hand V2 tasks.

## Method
- paradigm: IL / behavior cloning (Action Chunking Transformer, ACT, from Zhao et al. [15]), used only to validate teleoperation data quality — this is a teleoperation-hardware paper, not primarily a policy-learning method paper.
- reward or loss: not an RL paper; no reward function. BC loss is not explicitly written out in the text (ACT's standard reconstruction/CVAE loss is referenced by citation but not re-derived or quoted); training hyperparameters given instead (Table 7): AdamW, lr 3e-4, weight decay 0.05, β=(0.9,0.95), batch size 64, cosine decay, 10000 total steps, 500 warmup steps, image augmentation (GaussianBlur, Normalize, RandomResizedCrop), action chunk length 100, ~1 hour wall-clock on an RTX 4090 (24GB).
- key trick(s): mounting the robot hands "underneath the arms in the same orientation as if it were a gripper" so the human arm and teacher arm are perpendicular and non-colliding during teleoperation (§3.2); face-to-face ("facing each other") tabletop arm configuration (borrowed from ALOHA/Zhao et al.) instead of side-by-side, chosen to avoid operator-teacher-arm collisions, improve workspace visibility, and widen the shared bimanual workspace (§3.3); excluding arm proprioception from the BC policy's input to force reliance on vision (§14).

## Contact / penetration handling
Not addressed as a simulated interpenetration metric (no simulator exists here). The only "collision" discussed is a physical, teleoperation-ergonomics one: the mounting orientation of the robot hands/teacher arms is chosen specifically so "the human arm and teacher arm output are perpendicular to each other and do not collide" (§3.2) — i.e., avoiding operator self-collision with the teleoperation hardware, not hand-object or two-robot-hand interpenetration. No hand-object contact/penetration measurement is reported anywhere in the paper.

## Coordination and embodiment (survey fields)
- two-hand coordination: two independent tracking/control chains, one per side — the teleoperation system runs symmetric per-arm/per-hand pipelines (Algorithm 1 has explicit `_l`/`_r` subscripts for arms, hands, gloves, kinematic models and IK), so a human operator drives both hands simultaneously but each side is captured, retargeted, and actuated independently; no explicit leader/follower role or relative-frame two-hand observation is used at the teleoperation layer. The trained BC policy (ACT) is a single policy over the concatenated 44-dim two-arm/two-hand action space (§5.2), i.e., one policy over both hands at the imitation-learning stage, but the underlying teleoperation data-collection architecture is symmetric dual independent streams, not a leader-follower rig.
- torso: both. Tabletop setup uses two arms fixed opposite each other; a separate mobile setup mounts the two arms plus an articulated PR2-like vertical torso on an AgileX Ranger Mini mobile base for in-the-wild tasks (§3.3, "Mobile Manipulation").
- hand model/DoF each side: identical hands on both sides in every experiment — LEAP Hand (16 DoF) for the main Tables 1-3, or LEAP Hand V2 (21 DoF) for Table 4; both multi-fingered, not grippers.

## Evaluation
- metrics (exact definitions): "Completion Rate" (% of trials completed, tabletop and mobile task tables) and "Time Taken" (seconds) per task, per teleoperation system (Tables 1, 2); imitation-learning success is reported as raw fraction "successes/total trials" (Table 3, 4); user study reports average completion time, success rate, and 1-5 subjective ratings (accuracy, responsiveness, ease of use, confidence) over 10 trials per participant after a 3-minute practice session (§9, Appendix).
- headline numbers: Table 1 (tabletop, LEAP Hand): BiDex completion rate/time — Handover 95% / 6.5s, Cup Stacking 75% / 15.5s, Bottle Pouring 85% / 14.9s — vs Vision Pro (60%/21.6s, 40%/38.8s, 70%/35.5s) and SteamVR (80%/17.5s, 85%/16.5s, 60%/15.5s). Table 2 (mobile, 20 trials/task): BiDex — Chair Pushing 95%/16.4s, Box Carry 95%/29.7s, Clear Trash 75%/74.6s — vs Vision Pro (75%/15.0s, 75%/33.7s, 50%/79.8s). Table 3 (ACT trained on BiDex LEAP Hand data, ~50 demos): Handover 7/10, Cup Stacking 14/20, Bottle Pouring 16/20. Table 4 (ACT on LEAP Hand V2, 20-trial evals): Pouring 15/20, Drill Lift 15/20, Pot Bottle Pouring 15/20, Plate Pickup 13/20 (table formatting in the source is ambiguous about which task maps to which number — the row lists "Drill Lift Pot Bottle Pouring Plate Pickup" as run-together headers with values 15/20, 15/20, 15/20, 13/20; flagged as ambiguous). Appendix §10/Fig. 6: BiDex-trained policies reach higher success rates with fewer demonstrations than Apple-Vision-Pro-trained policies on the same 100-demo Pringles handover comparison, attributed to Vision Pro's end-effector-space commands amplifying small prediction errors into large joint-space errors versus BiDex's direct joint-space commands.
- baselines beaten: Apple Vision Pro teleoperation (finger MANO-like tracking + wrist IK) and SteamVR tracking (lighthouse + IMU pucks, same Manus gloves for hands) — both described in §4.1 and Appendix §12-13.
- real robot? Yes throughout, no sim numbers. Trial counts: Tables 1/3 tabletop tasks and Table 3 IL evaluation use the counts noted above (10-20 per cell); Table 2 mobile tasks are "averaged across 20 trials"; user study (§9) is 10 trials per participant per system.

## Limitations stated by the authors
"Due to the lack of haptic feedback, the human operator has to rely on visual feedback for teleoperation and cannot feel what the robot hand is feeling. Additionally, they cannot exert intricate force control and can only control the kinematics of the robot hand and arm which can make it challenging for fine-grained manipulation tasks." Authors suggest haptic feedback integration as future work (§6).

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "We introduce BiDex, an extremely dexterous, low-cost, low-latency and portable bimanual dexterous teleoperation system which relies on motion capture gloves and teacher arms" (Abstract).
- "A key decision that greatly improves policy generalization is to exclude current arm joints from the proprioception" (§14).
- "policies trained from the Apple Vision Pro perform worse, mainly due to abrupt wrist movements... The Apple Vision Pro commands in end-effector space, and a small prediction error can result in large errors in joint space. While BiDex commands directly in joint space, which results in smoother actions" (§10).
