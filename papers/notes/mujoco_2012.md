# mujoco_2012 — MuJoCo: A physics engine for model-based control (Todorov, Erez, Tassa, IROS 2012)

sources: papers/md/mujoco_2012.md [sha256 31bab104] ; code/md/mujoco_2012.md [commit 2fec9223]

## One-line contribution
A new physics engine ("Multi-Joint dynamics with Contact") that simulates in generalized coordinates with recursive algorithms and replaces LCP contact with new velocity-stepping solvers (implicit-complementarity, convex, diagonal), aimed at the derivative/parallel-evaluation needs of model-based control and trajectory optimization rather than gaming (Abstract, Sec. I).

## Setting
- hand(s): none shipped; a "Hand (32 DOF)" model appears only as one of four synthetic benchmark systems in the timing tests (Sec. IV-A, Table 1), with no vendor named. The code parse (file tree of the current `google-deepmind/mujoco` repo, depth 3) shows no hand asset directories — hand XMLs live in the separate Menagerie repo, not in this parse.
- arm: none named; benchmark models are "Isolated", "Chain", "Hand", "Humanoid" (Table 1) and a 3D humanoid with 18 DOFs for trajectory optimization (Sec. IV-B, Fig. 3C). single/bimanual: n/a.
- simulator / physics: MuJoCo itself — generalized (joint) coordinates, recursive CRB (inertia matrix) and RNE (bias forces) algorithms, sparse L^T D L factorization (Sec. II-A step 2).
- observation / action space / objects: n/a — this is the engine paper, not a task paper.

## Physics
- **Contact model**: soft, convex. Collision detection produces contact Jacobians (`Jc`) via "dedicated pair-wise functions when possible, and otherwise ... a general-purpose convex collider (implemented by libccd)" (Sec. III-B, geom). Geom types: "plane, sphere, capsule, ellipsoid, box, cone, mesh." "Non-convex meshes can be rendered but are not used in collision detection; instead the user should decompose them into convex meshes" (Sec. III-B). Number of contact points per pair is not stated (dedicated pair functions vs. general convex collider; count not given).
- **Solver**: three interchangeable contact solvers, all replacing the standard LCP-with-friction-pyramid approach (Sec. II-B):
  1. *Implicit complementarity solver* (most accurate, based on ref. [11]): reformulates the complementarity problem in a hybrid variable x and solves the resulting nonlinear equation "by converting it to an unconstrained optimization problem," using "a customized non-smooth Newton method" (Sec. II-C). "Each iteration involves factorization of a n_f-by-n_f matrix." Iteration counts are shown only as a figure ("average number of Newton-like iterations as a function of the number of contacts nc and the friction coefficient mu", Fig. 1) — no numeric values survived parsing.
  2. *Convex solver* (based on ref. [12]): minimizes contact-space kinetic energy subject to the friction cone (hard constraint) and a soft non-penetration cost R(f_N) added "because otherwise the inverse dynamics could not be defined for trajectories that happen to have penetration" (Sec. II-D, Eq. 6). Described as "a family of solvers" — interior-point, or projected Newton / conjugate-gradient / Gauss-Seidel; "Presently a cone-projected Gauss-Seidel method is our favorite [9]." The Table 2/3 timing results use "an interior-point method for solving the convex optimization problem (6)"; the authors state projected methods "appear to be faster, although we have not yet done careful testing" (Sec. IV-B).
  3. *Diagonal solver* (fastest, least accurate): a "mass-aware spring-damper" that uses the diagonal of the A matrix to keep contacts critically damped, solved independently per contact (ignores contact-contact interactions) with the same friction-cone projection (Sec. II-E).
- **Differentiable contacts**: not claimed as a feature in this paper. The convex solver is stated to be *invertible* — "given a and gamma... we can recover f and v0" — which underlies MuJoCo's exact inverse dynamics through contacts (Sec. II-D, II-G), but this is inverse dynamics, not gradient/backprop differentiability; the paper does not use the word "differentiable."
- **Timestep / substeps**: no default numeric timestep is given for the engine in general. One example: "With a time-step of 15 msec (at which our simulations are stable)" for a humanoid running-gait synthesis task (Sec. I) — contrasted with ODE needing "time-steps below 1 msec." Two integrators offered: semi-implicit Euler (uses next-step velocity; Eq. in Sec. II-A) and a 4th-order Runge-Kutta "modified to work with next-step velocities rather than accelerations" (Sec. II-A). No substep/decimation concept is described (that is a later, GPU-era addition).
- **Friction model**: Coulomb friction with complementarity conditions (Sec. II-B, Eq. 5): "if there is slip then the friction force must act in the direction opposite to the slip velocity" and "the contact force must lie inside the friction cone" of coefficient mu. The friction cone is kept as an exact cone (not linearized into a pyramid) in the convex and diagonal solvers — the paper explicitly rejects the "replace the friction cone with a pyramid and convert ... into an LCP" approach used by "Lemke's algorithm or ... PATH solver," calling it non-scaling and error-introducing (Sec. II-B).
- **Penetration handling / exposure**: penetration is resolved as a *soft* cost, not a hard constraint: "non-penetration (f_N >= 0) is imposed with a cost R(v)" in the convex solver (Sec. II-D). A regularization term R is added for three stated reasons: A is often singular; without it "the inverse cannot be defined"; and it lets contacts act "from a distance," useful in continuation methods for optimization, by "increasing R with distance" (Sec. II-D). The diagonal solver computes "the desired next-state velocity v* which would result if penetrations decayed like critically-damped springs" (Sec. II-E) — i.e., penetration decay is exponential/spring-like, not instantaneous. Fig. 2 (ball-drop test) is captioned "Note how the contact impulse is smoothed, and yet there is no penetration" — a qualitative, not numeric, claim. Whether penetration depth itself is exposed as a queryable quantity to the user is not stated in the paper.
- **GPU or CPU**: CPU only, explicitly parallel across independent instances rather than within one instance: "the same dynamical system can be evaluated in parallel (in shared memory or distributed architectures) for different states and controls" for finite-difference derivatives (Sec. I). Timing tests run on single- and multi-core Intel desktops (Xeon/i7, up to two 6-core X5860 with hyper-threading = 24 virtual cores; Sec. IV-B).
- **Throughput** (exact tables, all CPU, single machine unless noted):
  - Table 1 (Sec. IV-A): smooth-dynamics evaluations/sec, single thread, averaged over 100 runs on four Intel CPUs (X9650 3 GHz, X5570 2.93 GHz, i7 940 2.93 GHz, X5860 3.33 GHz — machine-per-column not disambiguated): Isolated(34 DOF) SD/FAST 122000 / MuJoCo 151000 / MuJoCo+sparse-factorization 133000; Chain(31) 128000/103000/41000; Hand(32) 89000/101000/76000; Humanoid(29) 130000/123000/75000.
  - Table 2 (Sec.   - Table 3 (Sec. IV-B, "inverse of Table 2"): dynamics evaluations/sec — 1 thread: 42000 (6 contacts) / 63000 (4 contacts) / 200000 (0 contacts); 24 threads: 390000 / 549000 / 1320000. ## Evaluation
- metrics: raw dynamics-evaluations-per-second and CPU-seconds per trajectory-derivative evaluation (Tables 1-3); no task success metric (this is an engine, not a policy, paper).
- headline numbers: see Throughput above (Tables 1-3; Abstract's ~400,000 evals/sec, 12-core, 18-DOF humanoid, 6 contacts).
- baselines beaten: SD/FAST (smooth-dynamics-only comparison, Table 1) — MuJoCo "quite comparable," faster on Isolated and Hand, slower on Chain and Humanoid smooth dynamics; ODE cited narratively (not benchmarked in a table) as needing sub-millisecond timesteps vs. MuJoCo's 15 ms for stable humanoid locomotion (Sec. I).
- real robot? no.

## Limitations stated by the authors
- The implicit-complementarity solver is solving an NP-hard problem: "the algorithm cannot always find the exact solution (which has 0 residual)," though "it finds the exact solution almost all the time" (Sec. II-C).
- Complexity is dominated in practice by cache misses, not floating-point operation counts, on modern CPUs — theoretical O() complexity results are noted as not predictive of real performance (Sec. II-F).
- The convex solver's projected (Newton/CG/Gauss-Seidel) methods are only conjectured faster than the interior-point method actually used for the reported timings — "we have not yet done careful testing" (Sec. IV-B).
- The paper omits a full explanation of the modeling convention as "beyond the scope of this paper" (Sec. III-A).
- "It will soon be made publicly available" (Abstract) — MuJoCo was not yet released at publication.

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "Contact responses are computed via efficient new algorithms we have developed, based on the modern velocity-stepping approach which avoids the difficulties with spring-dampers." (Abstract)
- "Around 400,000 dynamics evaluations per second are possible on a 12-core machine, for a 3D homanoid with 18 dofs and 6 active contacts." (Abstract)
- "non-penetration (f_N >= 0) is imposed with a cost R(v) because otherwise the inverse dynamics could not be defined for trajectories that happen to have penetration." (Sec. II-D)
- "the pyramid approximation introduces errors ... [LCP methods] do not scale well for larger problems ... the problem is generally NP-hard." (Sec. II-B)
- "Note how the contact impulse is smoothed, and yet there is no penetration." (Fig. 2 caption)
- "the only way to assess performance reliably is to run extensive timing tests." (Sec. II-F)

## Notes for the survey (which sections this feeds; contradictions with other notes)
- Physics-engine background section: this is the founding paper for MuJoCo's soft-convex contact family (implicit-complementarity / convex / diagonal), which mujoco_convex_contact_2014 formalizes and names ("soft convex complementarity"); cite this note for the historical solver taxonomy and mujoco_convex_contact_2014 for the finalized/named formulation.
- No GPU, no batching, no differentiability claim here — contrast directly with brax_2021, isaacgym_2021, mujoco_playground_2025 (MJX) which build GPU/differentiable pipelines on top of or alongside this contact family decades later.
- No hand or dexterous-manipulation content at all; the "Hand" in Table 1 is an unnamed 32-DOF benchmark skeleton, not a robot asset — do not cite this paper for hand asset inventories.
- Throughput numbers here are CPU, single/dual-machine, pre-parallel-environment era; not comparable to later GPU-parallel FPS figures (isaacgym_2021, brax_2021, maniskill2_2023) without noting the ~13-year gap and totally different measurement protocol (per-core dynamics evals vs. batched env steps/sec).
