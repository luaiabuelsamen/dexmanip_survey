# ace_teleop_2024 — ACE: A Cross-Platform Visual-Exoskeletons System for Low-Cost Dexterous Teleoperation (Yang, Hu, Fang, Fang, Strand, Held; CoRL 2024)

sources: papers/md/ace_teleop_2024.md [sha256 6a84240f] ; no code

Parse caveat: the hand-retargeting cost equation (Appendix B, quoted below in prose only) is an unrecovered image, exactly as in anyteleop_2023 (ACE explicitly reuses that formulation, citing [14]=AnyTeleop/dex-retargeting). ## One-line contribution
ACE is a low-cost ($600), 3D-printed bimanual exoskeleton with hand-facing wrist-mounted cameras that fuses exoskeleton forward-kinematics (for accurate wrist/end-effector pose) with MediaPipe vision-based finger tracking (for hand pose), then maps both through a scale-adjustable IK-based control scheme (Normal/Mirror/Bimanual/Gripper/Hand modes) to teleoperate five different embodiments (arm+hand, humanoid+hand, quadruped+gripper, arm+gripper), outperforming the direct-joint-matching GELLO baseline on a target-reaching precision task and enabling downstream imitation learning on 6 real multi-step tasks (Sec. 3-4).

## Setting
- operator interface: visual exoskeleton — a 3D-printed, 2-arm (7-link, 6-DoF each) exoskeleton worn on both arms, each arm's wrist carrying a 2-DoF camera mount with "two hand-facing low-cost webcams" for MediaPipe-based finger tracking (Sec. 3.1, 3.2). Not a glove (no per-finger sensors on the hand itself), not a VR headset, not pure vision — a hybrid: exoskeleton joint encoders give wrist/end-effector pose via forward kinematics, RGB cameras mounted on the exoskeleton give hand/finger pose via MediaPipe (21 keypoints in wrist frame). Servos: DYNAMIXEL XL330-M288-T with 12-bit encoders, U2D2 controller. Dual-base design: fixed desktop base (stable, precise, better for long sessions) or mobile base (for humanoid/quadruped tasks needing operator movement). Donning time: "less than 30 seconds" (magnetic modular connectors); wrist-size change "almost instant," upper/lower arm length adjustment "under 2 minutes" (Sec. 3.1).
- robot hand(s)/embodiments controlled: demonstrated on 5 combinations (Sec. 3, Fig. 5) — xArm7 + Ability Hand, H1 humanoid + Inspire Hand, B1 quadruped + Z1 arm, Franka + parallel-jaw gripper, GR-1 humanoid + parallel-jaw gripper. Hand-joint retargeting reuses the AnyTeleop/dex-retargeting fingertip-keypoint-vector formulation (Appendix B, quoted below) — ACE's own contribution is the wrist/end-effector pose pipeline and cross-platform control-mode layer, not a new hand-retargeting cost.
- retargeting formulation: two separate levels. (1) **End-effector (wrist) control mapping** (Sec. 3.2, quoted): treating the 6D robot end-effector pose xe and 6D human wrist pose xh, "we map xh to xe as [equation, unrecovered] where γ is the control scale, ch is the center of the human workspace, and ct is the center of the task workspace." This scale-and-recenter mapping, solved via IK rather than direct joint-matching, is instantiated in three modes: **Normal Mode** — "x_e^normal is the same as in Eq. (1), where γ is the ratio between the robot and human workspace radius"; **Mirror Mode** — "x_e^mirror = -γ_mirror(xh - ch) + ct" (face-to-face operation with a large robot, motion mirrored); **Bimanual Mode** — γ and ct adjusted "so that the left and right hand side workspaces align," specifically so "when the camera mounts are about to collide, the robot hands touch each other" (addressing the exoskeleton's own hardware collision constraint, not a robot-hand collision constraint). (2) **Hand/finger retargeting** (Appendix B, quoted verbatim, identical formulation to anyteleop_2023): "This process is often formulated as an optimization problem [14], where the difference between the keypoint vectors of the human and robot hand is minimized. The optimization can be defined as follows: [equation, unrecovered] where qt represents the joint positions of the robot hand at time step t, vit is the i-th keypoint vector for the human hand computed from the detected finger keypoints, fi(qt) is the i-th forward kinematics function which takes the robot hand joint positions qt as input and computes the i-th keypoint vector for the robot hand, ql and qu are the lower and upper limits of the joint position, and α is a scaling factor to account for hand size difference. An additional penalty term with weight β is included to improve temporal smoothness." Joint limits (ql, qu) are explicitly handled in this hand-retargeting optimization exactly as in AnyTeleop; no hand-self-collision or hand-object collision term is described anywhere in either mapping. For 2-DoF parallel-jaw grippers, retargeting is bypassed entirely by **Gripper Mode**: "The distance between the thumb tip and the index fingertip is linearly mapped to a range of 0 to 1."
- end-to-end latency: not reported as a single number; "hand pose tracking frequency of approximately 27Hz, which can be further improved to 100Hz with a higher-frequency camera" (Sec. 3.2) is the closest throughput figure given.
- cost of the rig: **explicitly quoted and compared** (Table 1, "Comparison of Teleoperation Systems"): ALOHA $20k (parallel-jaw, joint-matching), Mobile-ALOHA $32k (parallel-jaw, joint-matching, mobile base), GELLO $0.6k (parallel-jaw, joint-matching), AnyTeleop ~$0.3k (anthropomorphic, vision-only), DexCap $4k (anthropomorphic, hand mocap+vision, mobile), **ACE $0.6k** (both gripper and anthropomorphic hand support, vision+FK, mobile base) — ACE is positioned as matching GELLO's cost while adding cross-platform and anthropomorphic-hand support that GELLO lacks.
- data rate / demonstrations per hour: not reported as a demos/hour aggregate, but Table 3 gives average per-demonstration collection time (seconds) and yield (successes/attempts) per task: Vacuum Keyboard 45.6s avg, 30/38 successful; Serve Candies 37.6s avg, 45/53; Wipe Whiteboard 33.5s avg, 30/37; Grasp Dolls (xArm) 11.6s avg, 120/123; Put in Tennis (H1) 21.4s avg, 25/29; Grasp Dolls (H1) 13.7s avg, 62/66 — convertible to roughly 60-300 demos/hour depending on task, though the paper itself does not state a per-hour figure.
- task(s) demonstrations were used for: precision/agility comparison against GELLO on a simulated bimanual target-reaching task across small/medium/large workspace and target sizes (Sec. 4.1, Table 2, 6 operators); and 6 real multi-step imitation-learning tasks (Sec. 4.2, Table 3, Fig. 8) — Vacuum Keyboard (grasp, activate, clean, place — xArm7+Ability), Serve Candies (grasp, open, throw cap, pour, throw tube — xArm7+Ability), Wipe Whiteboard (grasp, wipe — xArm7+Ability), Grasp Dolls (grasp, place, generalizing from 9 small+2 large training doll types to 15 small+3 large test types — both xArm7+Ability and H1+Inspire), Put in Tennis (grasp, put in — H1+Inspire). Policies: 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) [52] for xArm platforms (point clouds from 2 camera views + proprioception, action = arm-wrist + hand target poses) and ACT [8] for H1 (single top-mounted RGB camera input).

## Evaluation
- exact success criterion: target-reaching task (Sec. 4.1, App. C) — "success was recorded when both end-effectors reached their respective target area and remained within the target regions for a short duration"; formalized in App. C as an "inside" test comparing end-effector position/radius to target position/radius, with r_target > r_ee always. Imitation-learning tasks: per-substep binary success rate (Table 3, e.g. Vacuum Keyboard broken into Grasp/Activate/Clean/Place sub-steps each scored separately), no single numeric distance/time threshold stated beyond the sub-task semantics themselves.
- number of eval episodes/seeds: target-reaching — 6 operators across 4 workspace/target-size scenarios (small/small, medium/small, medium/medium, large/large), aggregated as average reach time, reach velocity, EE velocity, effective ratio (optimal/actual moving distance), and success rate (Table 2). Imitation learning — "We tested 10 episodes for each task" (Sec. 4.2); data-collection yield reported as successes/attempts per task in Table 3 (e.g. 30/38, 45/53, 120/123).
- real robot? Yes for imitation learning (Table 3, all 6 tasks); the target-reaching precision comparison against GELLO was run "in a simulation" (Sec. 4.1) using an XArm model, not on hardware — explicitly labeled as such.
- headline numbers: forward-kinematics-based end-effector pose has "average forward kinematics error is around 1mm" (Sec. 4.1); a tape-extension teleoperation accuracy test (20cm->40cm) gives "an average error of only 3mm" (cumulative teleoperation + robot control error). Table 2 (simulation, small workspace/small target): ACE reach time 4.69s vs. GELLO 13.6s, effective ratio 61.1% vs. 26.2%, success rate 97.1% vs. 47.6%; ACE's advantage shrinks or reverses at medium/medium (GELLO reach time 3.65s vs. ACE 4.05s, GELLO success 93.8% vs. ACE 87.5%) — "the direct joint-matching method in GELLO provides accurate control [but] is only suitable for a specific workspace and task size (medium size as defined in our experiment)." Table 3 (real robot, 10 IL eval episodes/task, sub-task success rates): Grasp Dolls (xArm) Grasp 0.9/Place 0.8; Vacuum Keyboard Grasp 1/Activate 0.6/Clean 0.6/Place 0.5; Serve Candies final sub-step (Throw Tube) drops to 0.4, reflecting long-horizon compounding error.
- baselines beaten: GELLO [9] (direct joint-matching teleoperation system, simulation target-reaching comparison, Table 2); qualitative comparison table (Table 1) against ALOHA, Mobile-ALOHA, AnyTeleop, DexCap on cost/EE-tracking-method/cross-platform/mobile-base axes (not a task-performance comparison).

## Limitations stated by the authors (Sec. 5, quoted)
- "The camera mount prevents the wearer's hands from coming together and, additionally, increases the burden of rotational movements due to the added moment arm. Although functionally addressed by our scaled controller, certain operations are less intuitive."

## Notes for the survey
- Feeds: the cost-of-rig comparison table directly — this is the only paper in the survey batch that quotes a structured, multi-system dollar-cost comparison (Table 1: ALOHA $20k, Mobile-ALOHA $32k, GELLO $0.6k, AnyTeleop ~$0.3k, DexCap $4k, ACE $0.6k), useful as a primary source for the survey's cost column across several other notes (anyteleop_2023, holo_dex_2022) that do not themselves quote a total dollar figure.
- Feeds the retargeting-formulation table as a second confirmed user of AnyTeleop's fingertip-keypoint-vector hand-retargeting cost (same joint-limit-constrained, temporally-smoothed formulation, same unrecovered-equation caveat) — ACE's own contribution is entirely in the wrist/end-effector control-mapping layer (workspace-rescaling IK, Normal/Mirror/Bimanual modes) which sits upstream of and separate from the hand-retargeting optimization.
- Contact/penetration/collision handling: the only "collision" concept addressed anywhere in this paper is a hardware self-collision of the exoskeleton's own camera mounts (Bimanual Mode's γ/ct adjustment so "the robot hands touch each other" precisely when the camera mounts would collide) — this is an operator-hardware collision constraint, not a hand-object or hand-self collision term in the retargeting optimization; the survey's contact-handling column should record this distinction rather than crediting ACE with hand-object collision handling.
- Precision result (Table 2) is a genuine head-to-head demonstration that direct joint-matching (GELLO) degrades specifically at small/fine workspaces while ACE's IK-rescaled mapping is more size-invariant, but with the caveat that GELLO actually wins on medium/medium (the workspace GELLO's scale was designed for) — a nuanced, not one-sided, result worth preserving in the survey rather than a blanket "ACE beats GELLO."
