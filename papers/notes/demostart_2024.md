# demostart_2024 — DemoStart: Demonstration-led auto-curriculum applied to sim-to-real with multi-fingered robots (Bauza, Chen, Dalibard, Gileadi, Hafner, Martins, Moore, Pevceviciute, et al.; arXiv 2024, Google DeepMind)

sources: papers/md/demostart_2024.md [8b3ec66f] ; no code

## One-line contribution
DemoStart is an auto-curriculum RL method that turns a handful of sub-optimal, action-agnostic demonstrations into a sequence of start-state task parameters of varying difficulty, filters them online for training signal (Zero-Variance Filtering), and trains an MPO agent from sparse binary success rewards alone; the resulting state-based teacher is then distilled (behavior cloning) into a vision+proprioception student that transfers zero-shot to a real 7-DoF arm + 3-finger hand (Abstract; Sec. III).

## Setting — CAVEAT: this is not a human-mocap-tracking paper
This paper is out of family with the other notes in this batch: there is **no human hand motion capture, no human dataset, and no human-to-robot hand retargeting anywhere in the method.** "Demonstrations" are recorded directly on the robot embodiment (in simulation, via a human operator using a 3DConnexion SpaceMouse to teleoperate the simulated arm+hand; for one real-robot baseline, via a magnetic tracker + Shadow Hand Glove teleoperating the real robot directly) — Appendix VII-B.4/B.5. There is no kinematic correspondence problem to solve because the demonstrator drives the robot's own action space (or a simplified one) directly; DemoStart is explicitly designed to not depend on the demonstration's actions at all ("DemoStart does not make use of the demonstrations' experience as part of the replay... it is able to leverage demonstrations... where actions are in the wrong action space," Sec. II/III-A). Human dataset / retargeting fields below are marked not applicable accordingly.

- hand(s): DEX-EE Hand [44], 3-fingered, tendon-driven, over-actuated: "each of which with 4 joints and 5 actuators, with a total of 12 DoFs," near-antagonistic finger arrangement (one finger opposing two others) (Appendix VII-B.1). Arm: Kuka LBR iiwa 14 R820, 7 DoF (Sec. IV, Appendix VII-B.1). Not bimanual — single arm+hand, six such robot cells used in the fleet (Appendix VII-B.1).
- simulator / physics: MuJoCo, a "digital twin" of the real cell, using the Kuka iiwa 14 model from MuJoCo Menagerie (Appendix VII-B.3). Agent/environment run at 20 Hz on the real robot (Appendix VII-B.2); simulation control-rate/timestep and env count are not stated in the parsed text.
- observation: privileged, state-based for the teacher (Table V): arm joint pos/vel (7+7), arm TCP position (3), TCP pose (7), TCP rotation matrix (9); per finger (×3): fingertip position (3), fingertip rotation matrix (9), finger joint positions (4), joint torques (4), joint velocities (4), commanded joint positions (4). Last 3 timesteps are stacked, flattened, linearly projected to 512-d, LayerNorm'd, then an 8-layer/512-hidden MLP shared encoder (Appendix VII-A.1). Student (distilled) policy observes 4 camera RGB images (80×80, two wrist + two basket-corner) plus proprioception: arm joint positions (7), TCP position+orientation as a rotation matrix (12), and finger joint angles (12) (Appendix VII-A.3).
- action space: 18-dimensional — 6D Cartesian velocity for the arm (fed through a QP-based differential IK at 200 Hz then a 1kHz impedance controller), plus 12-dim finger joint targets (Sec. IV). Two action specs tested for the fingers: integrated joint velocity or joint position; "joint position on all tasks apart from cube reorientation where we use joint velocity" (Appendix VII-A.1).
- objects / data: no human motion dataset. Demonstrations per task, all collected by teleoperating the simulated (or, for one baseline, real) robot: Plug lifting 5 demos; Plug insertion 20 demos (12 upright-spawn insertions + 8 "flip to upright" demos added after randomizing plug orientation, reused without new insertion demos); Cube reorientation 2 demos of "unstructured interaction"; Nut-and-bolt threading 60 demos (20 full task + 20 lifts + 20 "nut to top of bolt"); Screwdriver-in-cup 20 demos (Sec. IV-A). Real-robot teleoperation baseline separately collected 2067 successful insertion demos + 2116 lift demos (2753 total incl. failures, ~27 hours over 36 days) via a magnetic 6D tracker (arm) + Shadow Hand Glove + custom IK (hand) (Sec. V-A, Appendix VII-B.5).

## Method
- paradigm: RL from sparse binary rewards, demonstration-shaped auto-curriculum (not IL — demonstration actions are explicitly discarded) ; algorithm: MPO [40] for the teacher, in a distributed actor-learner setup [38] with an experience replay buffer [39] (Sec. III-A). Distillation stage uses behavior cloning (offline, from a saved dataset of teacher rollouts, not DAgger) into a Perceiver-Actor-Critic (PAC) [43] student (Sec. III-B, Appendix VII-A.2).
- auto-curriculum mechanism (Sec. III-A, three mechanisms): (1) each demonstration's every timestep is saved as a full environment-state task parameter (TP) start state, harder near the demo's start and easier near its end; (2) Zero-Variance Filtering (ZVF) — an actor only sends training data from a TP if, over T=4 probe episodes, outcomes are neither all-success nor all-failure (i.e. has "training signal"); (3) bias toward earlier-in-demonstration TPs once the agent masters the easier/later ones, to avoid training on "unnatural states" from imperfect demos (e.g. an unstable grasp). Implementation loop per actor: sample a sequence of K=8 TPs (ψ0 always from T_target, then K TPs from one demo split into K temporal chunks), probe each with T=4 episodes under ZVF until one qualifies, then generate M=50 training episodes on it (Sec. III-A, "Implementing DemoStart").
- reward (paper, Appendix VII-A.1, quoted): "if the agent reaches a goal state where the task has been completed, we terminate the episode and give the agent a reward of 1" — a single binary sparse terminal reward per task, no shaping, no per-step dense component, and no reward tied to matching a reference trajectory at all (there is no reference trajectory — DemoStart trains from goal states/TPs, not frame-by-frame tracking). Per-task success functions (Appendix VII-C, verbatim from listed pseudocode):
  - Plug lifting: `lifted = plug_position[2] > 0.11` and `centered = all(abs(plug_position[:2]) < 0.1)`, held true for 0.5 consecutive seconds (11 cm height threshold, 10 cm horizontal centering tolerance).
  - Plug insertion: `norm(plug_position - SOCKET_POSITION) <= 0.002` — 2 mm position tolerance (implicitly requires correct orientation to reach that state; no explicit angular tolerance given).
  - Nut and bolt: `norm(error[:2]) <= 0.01` (1 cm radial) and `error[2] <= z_threshold`, where z_threshold interpolates between full depth (difficulty=1/hard) and the bolt top (difficulty=0/easy) — a curriculum "difficulty" TP parameter, sampled 50% easy during training (Appendix VII-A.1, VII-C).
  - Screwdriver in cup: `cup_upright = cup_z_axis[2] >= 0.985` ("up to 10 degree error") and `norm(screwdriver_handle_position - cup_position) <= 0.01` (1 cm).
  - Cube reorientation: `rotated_goal[2] > 0.99` ("within 8 degrees") and `norm(cube_position) < 0.05` (5 cm from basket center).
- key trick(s): decoupling the curriculum from the demonstration's action space entirely (states only), so poor-quality/partial/wrong-action-space demos are usable; ZVF requires no extra trained agent or centralized controller unlike PAIRED/PLR; a "difficulty" auxiliary TP parameter for nut-and-bolt to bootstrap grasp-and-place before full threading is learnable; BC-based (not interactive/DAgger) teacher→student distillation, enabling reuse of the same pipeline with human-teleoperated real-world data as an alternative source (Sec. III-B).

## Evaluation
- exact success criterion: task-specific, all binary/terminal as quoted above (metres for position/height/radial thresholds, an axis-alignment cosine threshold rather than radians for the two orientation-sensitive tasks — cup upright and cube face — expressed as "z_axis component ≥ 0.985 / >0.99," annotated in the paper's own comments as "≈10°"/"≈8°" respectively, not stated as raw radians elsewhere).
- failure definition during a rollout: never triggered by any interpenetration, torque, or safety proxy in this paper — failure is simply non-achievement of the success condition within the episode horizon (10 s per episode in the 1000-episode sim evaluations, Sec. V-B; sim episodes "terminate after 10s," teleoperation demos terminate after 50s, Fig. 4). Real-robot evaluation episodes: one minute (lift, cube reorientation) or three minutes (insertion), 100 episodes per method/task, all starting from a non-successful state, success = "at least one successful step" (for lift, plug held ≥1s) (Sec. V-C).
- number of eval episodes/seeds: simulation — 1000 episodes ×10 s per condition (Sec. V-B); real robot — 100 episodes per method per task (Sec. V-C).
- headline numbers: Table I (plug insertion, sim) — Vanilla RL 0%, Vanilla RL+Mechanism1 0%, +Mechanism1+SuccessFilter 0%, +Mechanisms 1&2 97.2%, DemoStart (1,2,3) 99.6%, SAC-X 99.2%, DemoStart+BC distillation 99.0%, SAC-X+BC distillation 20.4%. Table II (sim, all 5 tasks, DemoStart only): Plug Insertion 99.6%, Plug Lift 99.7%, Cube Reorientation 99.9%, Nut and Bolt 99.8%, Screwdriver in cup 98.6%. Table III (real robot, 100 episodes): DemoStart distillation — Plug Lift 97%, Plug Insertion 64%, Cube reorientation 97%; SAC-X distillation — Lift 20%, Insertion 1%; BC from real teleop — Lift 64%, Insertion 2%. Table IV (distillation ablations, plug insertion): dropping photorealistic data sim 97.0%/real 29%; fingertip Cartesian poses instead of joint angles sim 98.6%/real 50%; 1 camera (no wrist) sim 97.0%/real 17%, vs. full 4-camera+photorealistic DemoStart distillation sim 99.0%/real 64%.
- baselines beaten: Vanilla RL (same sparse reward, no curriculum), Vanilla RL + Mechanisms 1/1&2/1+SuccessFilter (ablations of DemoStart's own mechanisms), SAC-X [48] (hand-designed auxiliary dense rewards — matches DemoStart in sim but its distilled/real policies are far worse due to "jerky, non-smooth behaviors"), BC from real teleoperation (100x more demonstrations, worse real-world plug-insertion result: 2% vs. 64%).
- real robot? yes — Kuka iiwa 14 + DEX-EE 3-finger hand, 6 physical cells, 100 trials/task/method for plug lift, plug insertion, cube reorientation (cube reorientation only evaluated for DemoStart, "Not evaluated" for the two baselines in Table III).

## Limitations stated by the authors (Sec. VI)
- Shaping only the initial-state distribution (not the trajectory) gives flexibility but "can result in behaviors that don't follow the demonstrations closely," making it harder to give guarantees about the final solution.
- ZVF's variance signal "can be misleading" under environment stochasticity that produces variance without real training signal.
- More informative rewards, if available, would allow more precise behavior shaping (acknowledged trade-off of using only sparse binary reward).
- Method is compute-intensive because ZVF discards most actor-generated data and rewards are sparse; suggest GPU-based simulation or smarter TP pre-selection as future work.
- No real-robot evaluation of nut-and-bolt or screwdriver-in-cup tasks (only plug lift/insertion/cube reorientation were transferred).

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "if the agent reaches a goal state where the task has been completed, we terminate the episode and give the agent a reward of 1." (Appendix VII-A.1)
- "DemoStart does not make use of the demonstrations' experience as part of the replay. As such, it is able to leverage demonstrations that are of poor quality or that are not suitable for behavior cloning, for example due to a different action space." (Sec. III-A)
- "the 20 demonstrations in simulation are equivalent to half an hour of collection time. In contrast, the 2753 demonstrations collected on the real robot... are equivalent to 27 hours non-stop of data collection." (Sec. V-A)
- "we found that it was beneficial to tune the choice of action specification as a hyperparameter on each task... we use joint position on all tasks apart from cube reorientation where we use joint velocity." (Appendix VII-A.1)
- "For instance, in the Screwdriver in cup task, agents take a median time of 3.5 seconds to solve the task, whereas the median time of the demonstrations is 93.2 seconds." (Sec. V-B)

## Notes for the survey
- Feeds: the survey's "counterpoint" framing — this is the clearest example in the corpus of a dexterous-manipulation RL method with NO human motion tracking, NO retargeting problem, and NO dense/tolerance-shaped tracking reward at all, only sparse terminal success plus a curriculum over start states drawn from action-agnostic teleoperated demos. Useful contrast for the survey's reward-taxonomy table: DemoStart occupies the "sparse binary, curriculum via state resets" cell versus the dense exp(-error·weight) tracking-reward family used by PhysHOI/Omnigrasp/DexTrack-style methods.
- Human dataset / retargeting: not applicable — demonstrations are collected by teleoperating the robot's own action space directly (SpaceMouse in sim, magnetic tracker + Shadow Hand Glove on the real robot), so there is no human-hand-to-robot-hand correspondence or optimization step anywhere in the pipeline.
- Object tracking: not a trajectory-tracking method — position/orientation "tracking" doesn't apply; each task instead has a single terminal goal-state predicate (position within a metric threshold, and for two tasks an axis-alignment/cosine-similarity threshold standing in for an orientation tolerance). No frame-by-frame reference is ever compared against.
- Block D (interpenetration): NOT ADDRESSED. No mention of penetration, collision meshes, or contact-solver settings anywhere in the parsed paper; the only contact-related design note is that the screwdriver-in-cup task "increases requirements for gentle contact interactions (i.e. to avoid tipping the cup)" (Appendix VII-C) — a qualitative task-design goal achieved implicitly via the sparse success reward, not a measured or penalized interpenetration/contact term of any kind.
- SOURCE THIN FOR REWARD BLOCK: no — the reward is unusually well specified for every task (all five reward functions are given as literal pseudocode in Appendix VII-C, with exact metric/cosine thresholds), it is simply sparse-binary by design rather than dense/tolerance-shaped like the mocap-tracking papers in this batch.
