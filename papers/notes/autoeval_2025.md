# autoeval_2025 — AutoEval: Autonomous Evaluation of Generalist Robot Manipulation Policies in the Real World (Zhou, Atreya, Tan, Pertsch, Levine; arXiv 2503.24278, 2025)

sources: papers/md/autoeval_2025.md [73484096] ; code/md/autoeval_2025.md [3ea3ff44]

## One-line contribution
A real-robot evaluation loop (Algorithm 1) in which a fine-tuned VLM scores success, a fine-tuned generalist policy resets the scene, and programmatic safety checks page a human only on failure; on five BridgeData-V2 WidowX tasks it matches human-run evaluation at Pearson 0.942 / MMRV 0.015 (Sec. 5.2, Fig. 7) and cuts human evaluator time by ">99%" (Sec. 5.3).

## Setting
- hand(s): none; parallel-jaw gripper on a "WidowX 250 6-DoF robot arm" (Fig. 2 caption, Sec. 4) ; arm: WidowX 250 (<$3500, Sec. 5.3) ; single-arm.
- simulator / physics: none for the main method (real world). Comparison baseline is SIMPLER [46] in ManiSkill (Sec. 5.1 "Comparisons"); the authors add a drawer scene to SIMPLER with "a 3D model of the drawer, with exact dimensions matching the real-world setup" (App. D). Cloth is not simulated because "at the time of writing the simulator of SIMPLER, Maniskill [56], does not support simulating deformable objects" (Sec. 5.1).
- observation: "third-person Logitech C920 HD RGB-camera to capture the top-down 256 × 256 image of the robot workspace" (Sec. 4); constant lighting from "an aluminum tripod light over each robot station" (Sec. 4). Policy server receives "an image observations and a task instruction" (Sec. 4).
- action space: "end effector delta action with blocking control" (Sec. 4); the policy server "returns a sequence of 7D actions for the WidowX robot to execute" (Sec. 4).
- objects / data: three cells, five tasks (Sec. 4, Fig. 3): drawer ("open the drawer", "close the drawer"), sink ("put the eggplant in the blue sink", "put the eggplant in the yellow basket"), cloth ("fold the cloth from top right to bottom left"). Exact language commands listed in App. B. Scenes are "in the distribution of the tasks contained in BridgeData" but "none of the exact scenes are in the BridgeData dataset" (Sec. 4).

## Method
- paradigm: evaluation protocol (autonomous real-world), not a policy-learning method. Learned components: success classifier, reset policy, reset-success classifier, safety detectors (Sec. 3).
- Algorithm 1 (verbatim, Sec. 2/3): "Input: Task T, policy π to be evaluated, initial state distribution ρ(s), success classifier C_T, reset policy π_T, reset classifier C_ρ(s) ... for each trial do / Start State: Start from initial state s0 ∼ ρ(s) / Policy Rollout: Rollout π for K steps / Success Check: Label success using C_T(s_K) / Reset Scene: Rollout reset policy π_T to return initial state to ρ(s) / Failure: If unable to reset or robot unhealthy, notify human operator to help".
- success classifier (Sec. 3, App. F): "approximately 1000 images, which takes less than 10 minutes to collect by tele-operating the robot"; "fine-tune the base 3B parameter [Paligemma] VLM with quantized LoRA using a learning rate of 2e−5, batch size of 4 for 80 iterations" (App. F). Prompts (App. F): sink "is the eggplant in the sink or in the basket? answer sink or basket or invalid"; drawer "is the drawer open? answer yes or no"; cloth "is the blue cloth folded or unfolded? answer yes or no". For some scenes the classifier is three-way (success / failure / "invalid" = OOD, needs human) and doubles as the safety detector (App. F, App. H step 2). Deployment threshold: "We choose to deploy success classifiers in AutoEval that have an accuracy of > 95%", measured on "a heldout test set of roughly 100 images" and on all frames of a teleoperated trajectory (App. F). Hard-example retraining when below threshold (App. F; code README "Dagger" step, `run_eval.py --save_classifier_data`, `scripts/filter_images.py`).
- reset policy (Sec. 3, App. G): "approximately 100 high-quality demonstrations" (Sec. 3) / "50−100 demonstration trajectories via teleoperation" (App. G), "typically less than two hours"; "finetune the generalist OpenVLA policy with LoRA, with batch size 64 and learning rate 10^−4 for 1000 iterations" with "a standard behavior cloning loss" (App. G). Scripted reset for "Close the Drawer" (App. G); in the public deployment both drawer tasks use scripted resets and the sink tasks a fine-tuned MiniVLA (App. I). Deployment threshold: "we choose to deploy reset policies that have a success rate of > 95%" (App. G). A reset-success classifier re-runs the reset on failure; the on-call human is paged "when ... the reset policy fails for N≈3 times in a row" (App. H step 3).
- safety (Sec. 3, App. A): xyz workspace boundary; per-joint maximum effort limit (WidowX has no native impedance control); joint-effort monitoring with software motor reboot at a safe pose; drawer and cloth taped to the table; plastic wrap over the yellow sink. Slack push notifications to on-call operators (Sec. 4).
- code: `auto_eval/success_detector/{base_detector,human,paligemma}.py` and `run_eval.py` appear in the file tree but their bodies are not in the code md. The reset-policy classes are in `auto_eval/robot/policy.py`: `RecordedPolicy` ("Replay some recorded policy with deterministic actions") and `SequenceRecordedPolicy` (docstring: "e.g. the open drawer scripted policy is the sequence of: - close drawer fully - env.reset() - open drawer fully"); `OpenVLAPolicy(lora_adapter_dir=..., dataset_stats_path=...)` loads the LoRA reset policy (README "Learned Reset Policy"). Scripted resets shipped as `scripted_policies/{close the drawer,open the drawer,out of drawer handle,unfold cloth}.pkl`. Safety wrappers imported from `manipulator_gym.utils.gym_wrappers`: `CheckAndRebootJoints, ClipActionBoxBoundary, InHouseImpedanceControl, LimitMotorMaxEffort` (README "Safety"). Mismatch: README's OpenVLA fine-tune example uses `--batch_size 32 --lora_rank 64 --max_steps 3000` vs App. G's batch 64 / 1000 iterations; README says "about 50" reset demos vs Sec. 3's "approximately 100".
- setup cost: "1-3h of human effort, and less than 5 hours total, including model training" (Sec. 3); App. H says "approximately 3 hours of active human effort and a total of 5 hours".

## Evaluation
- success definitions for human-run oracle (Sec. 5.1): "success is counted when the drawer is completely closed or opened at least 1.5cm, respectively, if the eggplant is fully inside the sink or basket at the end of the episode, and if the cloth is folded to at least a quarter of the way diagonally. We randomize the initial position of the eggplant, drawer, and the cloth at the beginning of each episode." Drawer scene additionally randomises "the end effector's initial pose, the drawer's initial pose, and the lighting conditions in the background" (App. C).
- episode length (App. C): drawer tasks 70 steps max; sink tasks 100 steps; cloth 80 steps.
- trials: "For each evaluation method, we run 50 evaluation rollouts for each policy in each task" (Sec. 5.2); six policies (OpenVLA, Open-π0, Octo, SuSIE-LL, SuSIE, MiniVLA; Sec. 5.1), all publicly released checkpoints.
- agreement metrics (Sec. 5.1 "Metrics", verbatim): "(1) Pearson correlation [62], which measures the linear consistency between two random variables ... (2) MMRV (Mean Maximum Rank Violation) [46], which measures the consistency of policy ranking and, as described in Li et al. [46], can be more robust to noise". Definition, recovered by OCR: "given N policies π1..N and their respective success rates R_A,1..N, R_B,1..N estimated via two evaluation procedures A and B, we compute: RankViolation(i, j) = |R_A,i − R_A,j| · 1[(R_B,i < R_B,j) ≠ (R_A,i < R_A,j)]" and "MMRV(R_A, R_B) = (1/N) Σ_{i=1}^N max_{1≤j≤N} RankViolation(i, j)" (recovered by OCR from papers/md/autoeval_2025.ocr.md; OCR text is noisier than the layout parse, symbols may be imperfect). MMRV is computed "with reference to human-run 'oracle' evaluations".
- headline numbers: AutoEval vs human "average Pearson score of 0.942 and MMRV of 0.015 (plotted as 1 −MMRV in Figure 7 of 0.985)" (Sec. 5.2). Val-MSE "actually negatively correlates with real robot performance" (Sec. 5.2; Table 4, 400 BridgeV2 validation trajectories). SIMPLER "effectiveness is policy dependent" (Sec. 5.2), e.g. Open-π0 put-eggplant-in-sink: 6/50 in SIMPLER (Table 3) vs 47/50 AutoEval (Table 1) vs 47/50 human (Table 2).
- per-task counts (x/50; Table 1 AutoEval | Table 2 human; App. C). Caption order is scrambled in the parse; the assignment below is fixed by matching Table 5's "OpenVLA 40/46/1, Open-π0 29/46/7" to Table 1:
  - OpenVLA: open 40|40, close 46|46, basket 1|1, sink 0|0, cloth 13|12
  - Open-π0: open 29|24, close 46|45, basket 7|7, sink 47|47, cloth 12|3
  - Octo: open 1|0, close 5|0, basket 0|0, sink 0|0, cloth 4|2
  - SuSIE-LL: 0|0 on all five
  - SuSIE: open 1|2, close 18|13, basket 0|0, sink 0|0, cloth 9|10
  - MiniVLA: open 33|32, close 49|49, basket 38|38, sink 0|0, cloth 8|8
  Largest AutoEval-vs-human gaps: Open-π0 cloth (+9), SuSIE close-drawer (+5), Open-π0 open-drawer (+5).
- throughput (Sec. 5.3, Fig. 9): 24 h run on drawer tasks, "60,000 evaluation steps (roughly 850 episodes on the drawer scene), with an average speed of 42 evaluation steps per minute"; "only required a total of three human interventions"; intro rounds this to "500 evaluation episodes per 24-hour period" (Sec. 1). Human-time arithmetic (Sec. 5.3): assuming 30 min daytime / 8 h night response and 1 min per reset, "19h of real autonomous evaluation only costs 3 minutes of human time, compared to ≈16 hours if a human evaluator wanted to run the same number of trials by hand" → ">99%".
- consistency (Sec. 5.3, Fig. 10): Open-π0, open drawer, 9 × 50 = 450 trials in ~11 h; first 7 runs (350 episodes) "within ... the natural variance of robot evaluations (±10%)"; drift after ~8 h attributed to motor overheating; mitigation "pause autonomous evaluations for 20 minutes every 6 hours". Fig. 10 error bars: "95% confidence intervals". Two-month reproducibility (App. J, Table 5, ~200 h operation): per-task Δ between −6% and +2%; reset policy and success classifier accuracies both 96%.
- failure analysis (Sec. 5.4, Fig. 11; 50 episodes, Open-π0, eggplant-in-sink): "for only three out of 50 trials, the autonomous evaluation fails, since the episodes mistakenly get classified as successes and the reset policy fails"; trials with motor failures are re-run and excluded from the report.
- initial-state distribution (App. K, Fig. 13): eggplant centroids over three 50-trial runs "roughly cover the task distribution".
- baselines beaten: SIMPLER and val-MSE as proxies for human evaluation (Fig. 7); no policy is "beaten" — the paper evaluates evaluators.
- real robot? yes, all main results; WidowX 250 gripper; 50 trials per policy per task.

## Limitations stated by the authors
(Sec. 7) (1) up-front human effort to train reset policy and success classifier ("a few hours for a new scene"); (2) "AutoEval currently does not support" controlled robustness axes (camera angle, distractors, lighting, table texture); (3) tabletop only — mobile manipulation raises "robust resets at room scale, success estimation under partial observability, and operational safety"; (4) "AutoEval evaluation currently only supports binary success estimates", no task-progress or qualitative scores. Also (Sec. 5.2) "AutoEval does not perfectly match human-run evaluation results, due to occasional failures in success detection and reset policy." App. L: a human pass over a 50-trial report to drop failed resets and relabel "roughly takes 1−2 minutes".

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "a comprehensive evaluation of the recently introduced OpenVLA model [36] against its baselines required more than 2,500 rollouts across four robot setups and three institutions, and a total of more than 100 hours of human labor" (Sec. 1).
- "AutoEval drastically increases the evaluation throughput, enabling 500 evaluation episodes per 24-hour period." (Sec. 1)
- "We choose to deploy success classifiers in AutoEval that have an accuracy of > 95%." (App. F); "we choose to deploy reset policies that have a success rate of > 95%." (App. G)
- "AutoEval performance evaluation are within the margins of what might be considered the natural variance of robot evaluations (±10%)." (Sec. 5.3)
- "simple validation MSE is a poor evaluation metric for robot policies: it actually negatively correlates with real robot performance" (Sec. 5.2).
- "SIMPLER's effectiveness is policy dependent and it cannot provide a reliable policy evaluation." (Sec. 5.2)
- "most room for improvement is in improving the efficiency by reducing the number of motor failures during evaluation, e.g. by implementing a more compliant robot controller" (Sec. 5.4).

## Notes for the survey (which sections this feeds; contradictions with other notes)
- Evaluation section: the concrete numbers to cite are 50 trials per policy-task, ±10% as the authors' own estimate of run-to-run variance, 95% CIs on Fig. 10, >95% component-accuracy gates, and 3 interventions / 24 h. The paper gives no formula for its CIs and no power argument for 50 trials; it inherits Pearson + MMRV from SIMPLER (simpler_2024 note), so cite the MMRV definition from there, not here (truncated in this parse).
- Sim-vs-real evaluation: Tables 1-3 are a direct per-policy comparison of SIMPLER against real success on the same tasks; the Open-π0 sink case (6/50 sim vs 47/50 real) is the sharpest counterexample to simulated ranking. Contrast with simpler_2024's claim of strong correlation — here it is called "policy dependent".
- Dexterous relevance: parallel gripper only, no hands; the reset-policy recipe (fine-tuned generalist, 50-100 demos) and the "reset-free" forward/reverse task pairing (basket ↔ sink, App. C) are what would transfer. Hardware wear (motor overheating after 8 h, Sec. 5.3) is a stated constraint that a tendon or multi-finger hand would likely tighten; the paper says nothing about hands.
- Robustness axes are explicitly out of scope (Sec. 7) — pair with colosseum_2024 for perturbation protocols.
- Code caveat: success-detector and eval-loop bodies are absent from code/md; parameter mismatches between README and App. G are recorded under Method.
