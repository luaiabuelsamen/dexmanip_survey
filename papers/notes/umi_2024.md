## One-line contribution
UMI is a hand-held, GoPro-instrumented parallel-jaw gripper (fisheye lens + side mirrors + IMU-SLAM + continuous gripper tracking) plus a policy-interface design (relative-trajectory action/observation representation, inference-time latency matching) that lets Diffusion Policy trained purely on hand-held human demonstrations transfer zero-shot to different robot arms, enabling dynamic (tossing), bimanual, and long-horizon (dishwashing) tasks and in-the-wild generalization from 1400 demonstrations across 30 environments (Abstract, Secs. III, V, VI).

## Setting
- hand(s): none — parallel-jaw grippers (custom 3D-printed, soft-fingered) on both the hand-held data-collection tool and the deployed robots. arm: UR5 and Franka FR2 (cross-embodiment transfer demonstrated between them, Sec. V-A "Cross-robot generalization"). single/bimanual: both — single-arm (Cup Arrangement, Dynamic Tossing) and bimanual (Bimanual Cloth Folding, Dish Washing) with "a single centralized policy" generating actions for both arms.
- simulator / physics: none — all real-world; no simulator is used at any stage.
- observation: RGB from a single wrist-mounted GoPro with 155-degree fisheye lens (used undistorted, "raw Fisheye images without undistortion", HD2), plus two physical side mirrors in the camera's peripheral view for "implicit stereo" depth, digitally reflected before being fed to the policy (HD3); proprioception = 6-DoF end-effector pose and continuous gripper width, both represented as relative trajectories (PD2.1/PD2.2); for bimanual, relative inter-gripper pose is added (PD2.3). Image observation horizon (I-To) = 2 for most tasks, 1 for the in-the-wild ViT-L/14 model (Table A1).
- action space: a predicted sequence of relative SE(3) end-effector poses ("desired pose at t relative to the initial EE pose at t0", PD2.1) plus continuous gripper width — not absolute, not delta/frame-to-frame. Environment/action frequency 10 Hz for quasi-static tasks, 20 Hz for dynamic tossing (Table A1).
- objects / data: 4 narrow-domain real tasks — Cup Arrangement (305 demos, 2 demonstrators), Dynamic Tossing (280 demos, YCB objects), Bimanual Cloth Folding (250 demos, 2 demonstrators), Dish Washing (258 demos, 1 demonstrator, 7-step sequential task); plus an in-the-wild Cup Arrangement scale-up — "Within 12 person-hours, 3 demonstrators collected 1400 demonstrations... across 30 diverse physical locations... 15 espresso cups of different colors, shapes... and materials" (Sec. VI).

## Method
- paradigm: IL (behavior cloning via action diffusion) ; algorithm: "In this paper, we use Diffusion Policy [9] for all of our experiments, while other frameworks such as ACT [53] could potentially serve as a drop-in replacement" (Sec. III-B) — UMI's contribution is the data-collection hardware and the policy *interface* (observation/action representation + latency handling), not a new loss.
- Loss: inherited unmodified from diffusion_policy_2023 (DDPM/DDIM noise-prediction training on action chunks) — this paper states no reward/loss equation of its own; App. E confirms only the noise-scheduler hyperparameters actually used: "All tasks uses DDIM scheduler with 50 training diffusion steps and 16 inference steps" (Table A1 caption).
- action representation: relative end-effector trajectory (PD2.1, quoted): "a relative trajectory representation, defined for an action sequence starting at t0 as a sequence of SE(3) transforms denoting the desired pose at t relative to the initial EE pose at t0, allows the system to be more robust against tracking errors during data collection and camera displacements" — explicitly chosen over absolute actions ("difficult to define for in-the-wild data collection", Fig. 6 caption) and delta actions ("accumulates error", Fig. 6 caption). Ablation (Sec. V-A, Cup Arrangement, 20 trials): relative-trajectory (ours) not separately re-quoted as a percentage in this ablation set, but "delta action baseline achieves 16/20 = 80%" and "absolute action baseline performs surprisingly poorly with only 5/20 = 25%" against UMI's own 20/20.
- prediction horizon and chunk size: action horizon Ta=6 for every task in Table A1 (Cup Arrangement, Object Tossing, Cloth Folding, Dish Washing, and in-the-wild Cup); observation horizons I-To (image)=2 and P-To (proprioception)=2 for all tasks except the in-the-wild ViT-L/14 model, which uses I-To=1 "to expedite the training and inference of ViT-L... deviating from the two-step observation window approach employed in other tasks" (App. E.1).
- observation encoder: "we utilize the Vision Transformer (ViT) [11] as the vision encoder due to its substantial capacity in comparison to ResNet... employ the pre-trained CLIP ViT and implement a reduced learning rate, set at 10 times smaller" (App. E.1) — but per-task choice varies (Table A1): Cup Arrangement and Dish Washing use ViT-B/16 (CLIP-pretrained); Object Tossing and Cloth Folding use ResNet-34 (no pretraining); the in-the-wild Cup model upgrades to ViT-L/14 (CLIP) "which involves diverse environments and objects." Ablation: for Dish Washing, "training ResNet-34 from scratch to be insufficient... learned a non-reactive behavior... 0/10 = 0%" without the CLIP ViT.
- inference-time procedure: DDIM with 50 training diffusion steps and 16 inference steps (Table A1 caption) — the closed-loop receding-horizon execution of diffusion_policy_2023 is retained, but UMI adds explicit hardware latency compensation on both ends: **PD1.1 observation latency matching** — "align all observations with respect to the stream with the highest latency (usually the camera)... linearly interpolate gripper and robot proprioception streams" to the camera's capture timestamp; **PD1.2 action latency matching** — "send commands ahead of time to compensate for execution latency," discarding the policy's first few predicted actions ("immediately outdated due to observation latency... policy inference latency... execution latency") and only executing actions timestamped after the compensated actuation time. Ablation (Dynamic Tossing, 120 object-tosses): disabling latency matching drops success from 105/120=87.5% to 69/120=57.5%.
- key trick(s): wrist-mounted-only camera (zero external-camera embodiment gap, "mechanical robustness" since no camera-robot-world calibration is needed); fisheye lens preserved undistorted (rectifying to a narrow FoV baseline drops Cup Arrangement success from 20/20 to 11/20=55%); side mirrors for implicit stereo, but only helpful when digitally reflected (17/20=85% without reflection vs. 20/20=100% with reflection, vs. 18/20=90% with no mirrors at all); IMU-aided ORB-SLAM3-based tracking with two custom modifications — "Map as Initialization" (relocalizing to a pre-built map without a fragile from-scratch initialization) and "Marker-enhanced Initialization" (fiducial markers to disambiguate feature matches in low-texture/outdoor scenes); continuous (not binary) gripper-width control via fiducial-marker tracking, exploiting series-elastic soft fingers for implicit force control (HD5); kinematic-based post-hoc filtering of collected trajectories per target embodiment (HD6).

## Evaluation
- metrics (exact definitions): per-task, manually-judged binary success against a written rubric (App. C.3) — e.g. "Cup Arrangement: ...cup is placed upright on the saucer with its handle within ±15° to the left of the robot"; "Dynamic Tossing: ...objects are tossed into their corresponding bins... [bounces count if they hit the bottom]"; "Bimanual Cloth Folding: ...both sleeves are folded inside... silhouette... resembles a square, sized around a quarter of area of the sweater torso"; "Dish Washing: ...plate is clean and placed vertically back on the rack. Residual spots of ketchup with a size <5mm are deemed clean." Evaluation terminated on Safety Concern, Robot Fault, Timeout, or Task Success (App. C.2); "operator manually judges the success or failure of each episode" (App. C.3) — explicitly acknowledged as subjective.
- headline numbers (with the table/figure they come from): Cup Arrangement 20/20 (Sec. V-A), cross-robot (Franka) transfer of the same checkpoint 18/20=90% (Sec. V-A); Dynamic Tossing 105/120=87.5% object-level success (Sec. V-B); Bimanual Cloth Folding 14/20=70% (Sec. V-C); Dish Washing 14/20=70% (Sec. V-D); in-the-wild Cup Arrangement 43/60=71.7% combined (28/40=70% training cups, 15/20=75% testing cups, Sec. VI). Data-collection throughput (Fig. 11): UMI is "more than 3x faster than [space-mouse] teleoperation, at 48% speed of the human hand" on Cup Arrangement, and "64% speed of the human hand" on Dynamic Tossing, where "teleportation [teleoperation] method failed to produce a single successful demonstration in 15 minutes." SLAM accuracy (Fig. 12, vs. OptiTrack MoCap): per-gripper Absolute Trajectory Error 6.1 mm / 3.5°; inter-gripper Relative Pose Error 10.1 mm / 0.8°.
- baselines beaten (ablations, all same-policy internal comparisons, not external SOTA methods): no-fisheye (rectified) vision, absolute-action space, delta-action space, non-reflected/no side mirrors, no inference-time latency matching, no relative inter-gripper proprioception, ResNet (not CLIP-ViT) vision encoder, no in-the-wild training data.
- real robot? Yes, entirely real-robot — no simulation results anywhere in the paper. Trial counts: 20 episodes per narrow-domain task (Sec. V, standard evaluation protocol), 120 object-tosses for Dynamic Tossing, 60 combined trials for in-the-wild Cup Arrangement (35 cafe-table + 25 water-fountain).

## Limitations stated by the authors (Sec. VIII, quoted)
- "since the kinematics limits of the downstream deployment robots are unknown at the time of data collection, we rely on data filtering to ensure the kinematic feasibility of the resulting policy."
- "our SLAM-based action recovery system inherits visual SLAM's requirement for sufficient texture in the environment."
- "collecting data with UMI grippers is still less efficient than human hand demonstration... in part due to the gripper's weight and bulkiness, and in part due to the reduced degrees of freedom compared to human hands."

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "How can we capture sufficient information for a wide variety of tasks with just a wrist-mounted camera?" (Sec. III-A)
- "we found that a relative trajectory representation... allows the system to be more robust against tracking errors during data collection and camera displacements." (PD2.1)
- "The UMI gripper weighs 780g, with an external dimension of L310mm x W175mm x H210mm and finger stroke of 80mm. The 3D printed gripper has a BoM cost of $73, while the GoPro camera and accessories total $298." (Sec. III-A, "Putting everything together")

- "the final success rate decreased to 69/120 = 57.5%" [without inference-time latency matching, vs. 105/120=87.5% with it] (Sec. V-B)
- "finetuning a large pre-trained model with narrow-domain data is insufficient for producing an in-the-wild deployable policy." (Sec. VI, "Takeaway")

- No hand, no retargeting formulation, no interpenetration handling anywhere — collect the "not applicable" for this key explicitly in the survey's teleop comparison table rather than leaving it blank, since UMI is architecturally adjacent (hand-held demonstration interface) to DexCap/DexUMI/DexWild which do carry a hand and a retargeting step.
