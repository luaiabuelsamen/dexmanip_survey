# lbm_careful_examination_2025 — A Careful Examination of Large Behavior Models for Multitask Dexterous Manipulation (TRI LBM Team, arXiv 2025)

sources: papers/md/lbm_careful_examination_2025.md [c9ba4457] ; no code

## One-line contribution
A blind, randomized, statistically analysed A/B protocol (50 real / 200 sim rollouts per task per policy per condition; Beta-posterior violins; Bonferroni-corrected sequential pairwise tests summarised by Compact Letter Display) applied to diffusion-transformer LBMs vs single-task baselines over ~1,800 real and >47,000 sim rollouts (Sec. I, Sec. V).

## Setting
- hand(s): parallel grippers, not dexterous hands. Two Franka FR3 arms; new platform WSG50-110 gripper per arm with TRI finray-style fingers, old platform Franka Hand (Sec. IV-C1, Fig. S1). Bimanual tabletop. 9 hardware stations plus 2 sim stations (Table S1).
- simulator / physics: `lbm eval` on Drake; "Each simulation is deterministic given a random seed (however, the GPU-based policies do not have the same guarantee)" (Sec. IV-C2). Timestep and contact model not stated. Scenarios: DryingRack 13 tasks, Shelf 12, Breakfast 18, Kitchen 5 (unseen in pretraining).
- observation: EE poses w.r.t. station base and w.r.t. the other EE, gripper width, 6 RGB images (2 FRAMOS D415e scene + 2 FLIR Blackfly per wrist; missing cameras zero-padded), one language instruction; n_obs = 2 (Sec. IV-D1).
- action space: EE poses w.r.t. station base + gripper widths; rotation as 6D (top 2 rows of R); 16-step horizon predicted, 8 executed; policy loop 10 Hz (Sec. IV-B2, IV-B3).
- objects / data: Ramen ≈1695 h. TRI-Ramen 545 h / 532 tasks / 64,262 demos = Real 468 h, 362 tasks, 46,063 demos on 9 stations; Sim 45 h, 41 tasks, 7,348; UMI 32 h, 129 tasks, 10,851. OXE-Ramen ≈1150 h subset of Open-X (Sec. IV-D, Table S7 weights).

## Method
- paradigm: IL. DDIM diffusion policy, Diffusion Transformer (8 blocks, 768 emb) conditioned on CLIP ViT-B/16 image CLS tokens (finetuned), frozen CLIP text encoder + trained projection, proprioception, sinusoidal diffusion-step embedding via adaLN (Sec. IV-B2, Table II).
- loss: DDPM noise-prediction loss on 16 action steps (Sec. IV-B1; the equation is an image in the md and is not reproduced).
- training (Table I): pretrain 48k steps, batch 2560, lr 3e-4 constant; finetune 30k (real) / 10k (sim) steps, batch 320, lr 2e-5; single-task from scratch 100k (real) / 25k (sim), batch 320, lr 2e-5.
- key trick(s): per-feature, per-timestep normalisation to [-1.5, 1.5] by 2nd/98th percentiles, 6D rotation left unnormalised (Sec. IV-D2). Low-motion prefix filtering (gripper moved >5 cm or >15 deg) used for finetuning/single-task in sim only, not for pretraining (Sec. IV-D3). A normalisation bug (some datagrams normalised with another source's parameters) was found after evaluation; real experiments were not re-run; sim re-check shows small nominal effect, corrected model better under shift on 4/16 tasks (Sec. IV-D2, Sec. XIV, Fig. S21).

## Evaluation
The statistical protocol, recorded verbatim where it matters (Sec. IV-A):
- trial counts: "Each real-world task was evaluated with 50 rollouts per task per policy per condition. Simulation tasks were run 200 times per task per policy per condition" (Sec. III). Missing sim data: 39 of 238 task/policy/condition combinations short, 104 rollouts = 0.2 % (Sec. VIII-D, Table S4).
- blinding and bundles: "in all our evaluations, the evaluators did not know which policy was being tested (blind testing), policy ordering was randomized to maximize fairness for each policy, and the initial conditions were consistent across the policies, within human error for hardware". "we split evaluation into test bundles with the size of each bundle equal to the number of policies being compared. Each bundle corresponds to a single initial condition with a randomized policy ordering." Initial conditions are re-created by overlaying the live camera on a target image (Fig. 8; homography-canonicalised across stations, Sec. IX-A). Sim initial conditions are fresh seeds from the training distribution, never reused (Sec. IV-A1).
- metrics (exact): success rate SR = successes / runs. Task completion TC: rubric of binary milestone questions; "assigning a +1 credit to a successfully completed milestone and 0 otherwise, then the credits are summed and divided by the number of milestones" (Sec. IV-A2). Sim uses predicates over state; real uses human-filled rubrics (examples Sec. IX-D: CutAppleInSlices 14 milestones, CleanLitterBox 7).
- rubric QA: "a quality assurance (QA) round on ~27% of nearly 2700 real-world evaluation rollouts ... The QA success rate discrepancy was 2.31% and the overall rubric question discrepancy was 6.25%." Five rollouts corrected (Sec. IV-A3).
- per-policy uncertainty: Bernoulli p with "a uniform prior over the Bernoulli parameter as suggested by [52]" (Kress-Gazit et al. 2024), posterior drawn as a violin. "We use violin plots rather than standard Confidence Intervals (CIs) for two reasons. First, violin plots depict the entire distribution of the parameter p instead of a single interval ... Second, CIs can be confusing or misleading when it comes to policy comparison; one may wrongly conclude that two results are not separated with statistical significance if their corresponding CIs overlap" (Sec. IV-A4a). For TC per task the raw distribution is shown; for aggregates a uniform Dirichlet prior on the mean (Sec. IV-A4b).
- pairwise comparison: "we run k(k-1)/2 pairwise tests where k is the number of policy models to be compared at once. In each test, the confidence level is adjusted for multiplicity via Bonferroni correction unless otherwise noted, so that a global 95% confidence level is maintained ... We use the Compact Letter Display (CLD) algorithm [78] ... Two policies that do not share the same letter are separated with 95% confidence" (Sec. IV-A5).
- sequential test (binary): "we use a sequential hypothesis testing framework as suggested in [75], which sequentially compares paired outcomes of each evaluation trial until either a decision is reached or all the trials are consumed. Specifically, we adopt the test originally proposed in [79], which yields reasonable statistical power in the small sample size regime while maintaining a strict Type-I error control for binary data" ([75] = Snyder et al. 2025 STEP; [79] = Lai 1988).
- TC comparison: "we use the Welch's t-test [80] to compare the means ... the discrete nature of the data violates the underlying assumption of the t-test that the data is normally distributed. Thus, the Type-I error is not controlled. However, we ensure that the sample size is sufficiently large (i.e., about 50 or more)" (Sec. IV-A5).
- stated caveats: Type-I error "not globally controlled across tasks when they are presented in one plot"; task aggregation "yields an exchangeable Bernoulli sequence, approximating an i.i.d. sequence"; fixed per-task trial counts violate i.i.d. but "the effect of this violation is negligible in practice" (Sec. IV-A4a, IV-A5).
- difficulty target: "We design our experiments to make tasks quite difficult—targeting policy success rates around 50%" (Sec. III).
- termination asymmetry: real operators stop only on success, danger, or no progress; sim uses a fixed timeout; this explains real > sim SR on TurnMugRightsideUp (Sec. IX-A, III-A).
- headline numbers: seen tasks (Fig. 2) finetuned LBM statistically same-or-better than single-task in 3/3 real and 15/16 sim tasks; strictly better nominal in 2/3 real, 3/16 sim; under shift 2/5 real, 10/16 sim (Sec. III-A). Unseen (Figs. 4, 6): TC finetuned better in 4/5 real and 4/5 sim tasks in both conditions; SR better in 2/5 real, 3/5 sim nominal, 1/5 sim shift. Finetuned LBM needs "less than 30% of the data" in sim; on real SetBreakfastTable, LBM at 15 % data beats single-task at 100 % (Fig. 5). Discussion restates as "3-5x less data". Pretraining fractions (Fig. 7): at 15 % finetune data all five models separable, monotone in pretraining size. Pretrained-only LBM: real 5/8 indistinguishable, 3/8 worse than single-task (Sec. III-A). Anomaly: TurnCupUpsideDown finetuned SR 0.335, robot never moved in 89/200 rollouts (Sec. III-A).
- baselines beaten: single-task diffusion policy of identical architecture, by the finetuned LBM; not by the non-finetuned LBM.
- real robot? bimanual Franka FR3 + WSG50-110; 8 real tasks (3 seen, 5 unseen long-horizon); 50 trials per task per policy per condition; ~1,800 analysed rollouts.

## Limitations stated by the authors
- "we do not explicitly account for the stochasticity across training when we compare two policy architectures ... Our confidence intervals are computed for the first term, but do not account for the second" (Sec. V-A).
- "we expect that both initial condition and scoring mistakes are non-zero ... our real-world results potentially miss small-magnitude effects due to signal-to-noise limitations."
- Normalisation bug not re-run in real (Sec. IV-D2). Sim and real distribution shifts "are not directly comparable" (Sec. III-A). Small CLIP language encoders limit steerability (Sec. V).

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "Many of the effects we observe were only measurable with larger-than-standard sample sizes and careful statistical testing that is non-standard for empirical robotics. Due to the size of current effects and the magnitude of experimental noise, there is significant risk that many robotics papers are measuring statistical noise due to insufficient statistical power." (Sec. V-A)
- "decisions like data normalization have a large effect on downstream performance, often dominating architectural or algorithmic changes" (Sec. V-A)
- "There is a notable difference between a policy that almost, but not quite, succeeds and one that does nothing." (Sec. III)
- "Although we report absolute success rates, the most important results are the relative success rates of the different methods." (Sec. III)

## Notes for the survey
- Feeds the evaluation-protocol section: trial counts (50 real / 200 sim), Beta-posterior violins instead of CIs, Bonferroni + CLD, Lai-1988 sequential test through STEP [75], Welch for graded scores with admitted lack of Type-I control. The "dexterous" in the title is task complexity; the hardware is parallel grippers.
- beyond_binary_success_2026 re-analyses exactly this data (its Table II) and replaces the Welch step with a valid nonparametric sequential test, reporting ~70 % sim / ~45 % hardware trial savings from the same rubrics. suresim_2025 cites this paper as its example of rigorous evaluation.
- Their image-overlay initial-condition workflow is the real-world analogue of our fixed-seed placement; their termination asymmetry (operator vs fixed timeout) is a concrete reason sim and real success rates cannot be pooled.
