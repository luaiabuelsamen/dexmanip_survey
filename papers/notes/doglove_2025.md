# doglove_2025 — DOGlove: Dexterous Manipulation with a Low-Cost Open-Source Haptic Force Feedback Glove (Zhang, Zhu, Chang, Zhang, Zhao, Song, Ding, Wang, Zhu; RSS 2025)

sources: papers/md/doglove_2025.md [sha256 9017daea] ; no code

Parse caveat: unlike the other retargeting papers in this batch, DOGlove's Sec. V retargeting description contains no cost-function equation at all (not even an unrecovered image) — the paper states in prose that it delegates the IK solve to an existing off-the-shelf library (Mink), so there is no paper-original objective to quote verbatim; this is noted explicitly rather than treated as a parse failure.

## One-line contribution
DOGlove is a sub-$600, 3D-printable, 21-DoF (16 encoders + 5 servos) exoskeleton glove that provides bidirectional cable-driven force feedback plus per-fingertip LRA haptic feedback, retargets fingertip positions (via glove FK + the Mink differential-IK library) to arbitrary robot hands, and shows — via blindfolded user studies and teleoperation tasks — that combined haptic+force feedback measurably improves both blind object-property perception and vision-based contact-rich teleoperation success/speed versus a vision-only (AnyTeleop) baseline, while also producing demonstrations usable for downstream DP3 imitation learning (Sec. III-VI).

## Setting
- operator interface: haptic force-feedback exoskeleton glove — 21-DoF motion capture (16 joint encoders + 5 rotary axes from servos), 5-DoF cable-driven force feedback (one Dynamixel XC/XL330 servo per finger via a bidirectional pulley system) and per-fingertip LRA (linear resonant actuator) tactile/haptic feedback, plus an HTC Vive Tracker mount for wrist localization. Not a vision-based or VR-headset system — DOGlove's MoCap is entirely from onboard rotary encoders (Alps RDC506018A resistive rotary sensors, read via a 24-bit ADC and an STM32 MCU), the opposite design point from anyteleop_2023/holo_dex_2022/dime_2022's camera-based tracking.
- robot hand controlled: LEAP Hand mounted on a Franka arm for all real-robot experiments (Sec. VI); also demonstrated (qualitatively, Fig. 7, no quantitative results) teleoperating Shadow Hand, Inspire Hand, and Allegro Hand in MuJoCo simulation, showing the retargeting pipeline is not LEAP-hand-specific.
- retargeting formulation (Sec. V-A, "Action Retargeting", quoted in full — no cost equation is given, only the pipeline description): "Our approach combines Forward Kinematics (FK) to compute human fingertip positions and Inverse Kinematics (IK) to calculate the corresponding robotic hand positions. When wearing DOGlove, the human operator secures their fingertips inside the finger caps. Since the glove acts as a rigid body, the relative positions of the fingertips with respect to the glove's origin can be accurately calculated. With DOGlove's anthropomorphic kinematic design and precise MoCap capabilities, fingertip positions are effortlessly determined using the glove's built-in FK. To map these positions to a robotic hand, we utilize Mink [44], a differential inverse kinematics library, to generate smooth and feasible motions for the robotic hand." Correspondence is purely fingertip-position (task-space), justified by "Previous studies [38, 11, 34] highlight the significance of fingertips, as they are the primary contact area during object interactions... we apply the 5-DoF haptic force feedback to the human operators' fingertips and adopt a retargeting method focused on fingertip positions." A single scalar scaling factor addresses hand-size mismatch: "A size discrepancy often exists between the human hand and the target robotic hand. To address this, we introduce a scaling factor when calculating IK, allowing adaptation to different robotic hand sizes." No joint-limit or collision-handling detail is stated in the paper's own text — these are implicitly whatever Mink (the third-party IK library) provides by default, not something DOGlove's authors specify or claim credit for. This is the only paper in this batch that offloads the retargeting optimization entirely to an existing general-purpose IK library rather than defining or reusing a fingertip-keypoint-vector cost of its own.
- haptic force retargeting (Sec. V-B, quoted): a 1-D force sensor (3 kg range, 1 g precision) on each LEAP-hand fingertip drives a 3-threshold combination strategy (Table I): "<10g: no haptic, no force; 10-50g: haptic only; 50-100g: haptic+force; >100g: force only (haptic stops)." Force feedback is realized by mapping clamped [0g, 3000g] fingertip force readings linearly to the Kp gain of the glove's Dynamixel servos (current-based position control); haptic feedback uses "waveform ID 56... Pulsing Sharp 1-100%" from the TI DRV2605L/Immersion TouchSense library. Threshold rationale (quoted): "we set the first threshold at 10g to initiate haptic feedback... force feedback is applied only after the force sensor readings exceed 50g... a third threshold is set at 100g, where haptic feedback stops, leaving only force feedback active" because "continuous haptic feedback during teleoperation can create a misleading sensation, interfering with the operator's ability to perceive the subtle properties of the object's surface."
- end-to-end latency: "The MoCap system operates at a maximum frequency of 120Hz, while the haptic force feedback system achieves a maximum frequency of 30Hz. Together with the retargeting algorithm, the system ensures seamless operation at a minimum frequency of 30Hz" (Sec. III-D, "Low latency").
- cost of the rig: "Commercial products such as the SenseGlove Nova [29] and Manus VR [21] cost more than 5,000 USD... DOGlove provides a low-cost solution under 600 USD" (Sec. III-A). Assembly: "The entire glove can be assembled within 6 hours" (Sec. III-B). Weight: "the total weight to 550g" (Sec. VII, Limitations) using 5 commercial servos.
- data rate / demonstrations per hour: not reported as a throughput number anywhere — the paper states fixed demonstration counts per IL task (40 for Press-and-Move-Box, 40 for Pick-and-Place-Teddy-Bear, 3 human-collected for Rotating-and-Placing-the-Carton) with no elapsed collection time, so no demos/hour figure can be derived.
- task(s) demonstrations were used for: (1) a blindfolded user study (5 untrained operators, eyemask+headphones) distinguishing 5 object pairs by haptic/force feedback alone (Sec. VI-A); (2) two teleoperation tasks used to compare feedback conditions against an AnyTeleop MoCap-only baseline — Bottle-Slipping (with and without visual feedback, Sec. VI-B) and Rotating-and-Placing-the-Carton (Sec. VI-C); (3) downstream imitation learning with DP3, trained on DOGlove-collected demonstrations for 3 real tasks — Press and Move Box (40 demos), Pick and Place Teddy Bear (40 demos), and Rotating and Placing the Carton (3 demos) (Sec. VI-D).

## Evaluation
- exact success criterion: User study — binary correct/incorrect object-pair discrimination per trial (Table II, no distance/time threshold). Bottle-Slipping w/o visual: "a 15-second countdown timer is set for each trial. If the bottle successfully slips without falling within the 15 seconds, the trial is denoted as successful." Bottle-Slipping w/ visual: success = "bottle slips without falling," plus a secondary continuous metric, "Slipping Deviation... the difference between the target sliding distance (9cm) and the actual slipping distance." Rotating-and-Placing-the-Carton: "successful if the carton rotates more than 45 degrees and is successfully placed into the bucket," plus "Completion Time." IL tasks: binary success per trial (box moved to target location; bear placed in designated box; carton rotated+placed), no numeric threshold beyond the task description.
- number of eval episodes/seeds: User study — 5 untrained operators x 5 object pairs (Table II, "x/5" format). Rotating-and-Placing-the-Carton quantitative comparison (Table III) — 10 trials per feedback condition (Only Force, Only Haptic, Haptic+Force, No Haptic/Force, AnyTeleop). IL evaluation — 20 trials each for Press-and-Move-Box and Pick-and-Place-Teddy-Bear, 10 trials for Rotating-and-Placing-the-Carton.
- real robot? Yes, entirely real-robot (LEAP Hand + Franka arm) for all quantitative results; no simulation numbers reported (simulation use, Fig. 7, is qualitative retargeting-visualization only, on Shadow/Inspire/Allegro hands).
- headline numbers: User study (Table II) — basic pairs 1-3 solved 5/5 under every feedback condition; challenging Pair 4 (same size/shape, different softness) 4/5 (Only Force) vs. 3/5 (Only Haptic) vs. 3/5 (Haptic+Force); challenging Pair 5 (different size AND softness) 0/5 (Only Force) vs. 3/5 (Only Haptic) vs. 2/5 (Haptic+Force) — "force feedback is more sensitive to softness, while haptic feedback is more sensitive to shape." Rotating-and-Placing-the-Carton (Table III, 10 trials): Only Force 9/10 @ 18.92s; Only Haptic 9/10 @ 21.16s; Haptic+Force 10/10 @ 19.89s; No Haptic/Force 4/10 @ 24.76s; **AnyTeleop 1/10 @ 54.85s** — the largest quantified gap between a fingertip-position/glove-based retargeting method and a vision-based retargeting baseline (AnyTeleop) in this survey batch, on the SAME robot hand and task. Imitation learning (Sec. VI-D, 3D Diffusion Policy): Press and Move Box 17/20 (85%); Pick and Place Teddy Bear 14/20 (70%, "failures primarily due to the teddy bear slipping out of the robotic hand when not grasped firmly"); Rotating and Placing the Carton 9/10 (90%) from only 3 human demonstrations.
- baselines beaten: AnyTeleop [25] ("a widely recognized vision-based hand retargeting method... used as the MoCap baseline") on the Bottle-Slipping and Carton-Rotation tasks — attributed cause (quoted, Sec. VI-B): "Due to differences in retargeting strategies, even a slight change in human finger position can lead to a significant deviation in the LEAP Hand's movements. As a result, AnyTeleop struggles to perform the slipping task effectively."; internal ablations across the 5 feedback conditions (Only Force / Only Haptic / Haptic+Force / No Haptic-Force) isolate the contribution of each feedback channel.

## Limitations stated by the authors (Sec. VII, quoted)
- "the weight of DOGlove is inevitably high, as it utilizes 5 commercial servos, bringing the total weight to 550g. Additionally, in agile teleoperation scenarios, performance is constrained by the servos' maximum speed and torque output."
- "although DOGlove is designed to accommodate most hand sizes, it may be uncomfortable for some users."

## Notes for the survey
- Feeds: the glove-vs-vision retargeting comparison directly — DOGlove's Table III (10 trials, same LEAP-Hand+Franka rig, same task) is the survey's single cleanest head-to-head between a glove/exoskeleton-tracked fingertip-IK retargeter (10/10 with full feedback) and a camera-based fingertip-keypoint-vector retargeter, AnyTeleop (1/10) — useful as primary evidence for a "tracking-source quality matters as much as the retargeting cost formulation" argument, though note the confound that DOGlove ALSO adds haptic/force feedback that AnyTeleop's comparison condition lacks, so the 1/10 vs. 10/10 gap should not be attributed to retargeting formulation alone.
- This is the only key in the batch whose retargeting "objective" is explicitly NOT a paper-original cost function — it delegates to Mink, an existing general IK solver, with no equation given even as an image. Flag this in the survey table as "no retargeting objective stated (uses off-the-shelf IK library)" rather than as a missing quote/parse failure.
- Cost-of-rig ($600, sub-$600 explicitly stated) and assembly time (6 hours) are both directly quoted numbers, useful anchors for the survey's cost/build-effort column alongside ace_teleop_2024's Table 1 ($0.6k-$32k range across systems) — DOGlove sits at the same price point as ACE and GELLO but adds haptic force feedback neither of those systems has.
- Contact/penetration handling: the paper's force/haptic-feedback thresholding (Table I) is itself a proxy for detecting and communicating hand-object CONTACT (not interpenetration) to the human operator, not a simulation-side penetration metric or constraint — worth distinguishing carefully from this survey's simulation-side interpenetration-measurement papers (e.g. toporetarget_2026) when compiling the contact-handling column, since DOGlove's "contact awareness" is a teleoperation UX feature, not a physics/geometry measurement.
