# holo_dex_2022 — Holo-Dex: Teaching Dexterity with Immersive Mixed Reality (Arunachalam, Silwal, Evans, Pinto; ICRA 2023)

sources: papers/md/holo_dex_2022.md [sha256 12826731] ; code/md/holo_dex_2022.md [commit 3a5c2011]

## One-line contribution
Holo-Dex uses a commercial Meta Quest 2 VR headset (its built-in 4-camera hand tracker) to teleoperate an Allegro hand via a simple joint-angle-copy + thumb-fingertip-IK retargeting, then trains vision-only nearest-neighbor policies (VINN/BYOL) from a handful of demonstrations, solving 6 contact-rich dexterous tasks and generalizing zero-shot to novel objects (Abstract; Sec. IV-V).

## Setting
- operator interface: VR headset (Meta Quest 2) — "The headset surrounds the human in a virtual environment at a resolution of 1832x1920 and a refresh rate of 72Hz. The base version of this headset is affordable at $399 and is relatively light at 503g." (Sec. IV-A). Hand pose comes from the headset's built-in 4-monochrome-camera tracker (internally calibrated, no external calibration routine needed, streams at 72Hz) — no glove, no exoskeleton.
- robot hand controlled: Allegro Hand (16-DoF, no pinky-equivalent finger). "Since the Allegro hand does not have a pinky finger, we ignore the teacher's pinky joints" (Sec. IV-C). Robot hand controlled asynchronously via ROS with a PD controller outputting torques at 300 Hz, with a gravity-compensation module for steady-state error (Sec. IV-D).
- retargeting formulation (Sec. IV-C, "Human to Robot Hand Pose Retargeting", quoted in full): "This is done by first computing the individual hand joint angles in the teacher's hand. Given these joint angles, a straightforward method of retargeting is to directly command the robot's joints to the corresponding angle. In practice, this works well for all fingers except the thumb. The thumb presents a unique challenge to our Allegro robot hand since its morphology does not match a human's hand. To address this, we map the spatial coordinates of the teacher's thumb fingertip to the robot's thumb fingertip. The joint angles of the thumb are then computed through an inverse kinematics solver. Since the Allegro hand does not have a pinky finger, we ignore the teacher's pinky joints." Correspondence: direct joint-angle copy for index/middle/ring fingers; fingertip-position IK specifically for the thumb (the only finger with a task-space/keypoint correspondence, not a joint-angle correspondence); pinky is dropped entirely (no correspondence). No collision handling and no joint-limit handling of any kind is described for this retargeting procedure — the paper states only "The overall pose retargeting procedure does not require any calibration or user-specific tuning to collect demonstrations. However, we find that thumb retargeting can be improved by finding user-specific maps from their thumb to the robot's thumb." (Sec. IV-C). This is explicitly the simplest retargeting formulation in this survey batch — no optimization, no cost function, and no explicit collision/joint-limit terms of any kind.
- end-to-end latency: "when the VR headset is on the same local network as the robot hand, we achieve latency under 100 milliseconds" (Sec. IV-D). The retargeting computation itself "is computationally inexpensive and can stream desired robot hand poses at 60 Hz" (Sec. IV-C); the teacher observes the robot in real time at 60 Hz to allow error correction.
- cost of the rig: $399 for the Meta Quest 2 headset (Sec. IV-A) — this is the only hardware cost quoted; the Allegro hand's own cost is not restated in this paper.
- data rate / demonstrations per hour: not reported as a demos/hour aggregate, but Table I gives average per-demonstration collection time (seconds) for HOLO-DEX vs. DIME [28], separately for "Expert" and "New User" operators: Planar Rotation 30s(expert)/125s(new user) vs. DIME 60s/150s; Object Flipping 5s/6s vs. 6s/21s; Can Spinning 10s/68s vs. 15s/76s; Bottle Opening 30s/48s vs. N/A/N/A (DIME could not collect this task at all); Card Sliding 150s/N/A vs. N/A/N/A; PostIt Note Sliding 120s/N/A vs. N/A/N/A. Headline comparison: "HOLO-DEX can collect successful demonstrations 1.8x faster compared to DIME" and, for 3 of 6 tasks requiring precise 3D movement, "single-image [DIME-style] teleoperation is insufficient to collect even a single demonstration" (Sec. V-B). Observation is recorded at a reduced 5Hz from three RGBD cameras during teaching "due to the large data footprint and associated bandwidth required from recording multiple cameras" (Sec. IV-D) — i.e. the teleoperation control loop runs faster (60-72Hz) than the recorded demonstration data (5Hz).
- task(s) demonstrations were used for: six real-robot dexterous manipulation tasks (Sec. V-A): Planar Rotation (rotate an object 90 degrees on the palm within 1 minute, 120 demos collected), Object Flipping (flip object 90 degrees within 1 minute, 30 demos), Can Spinning (spin a can 90 degrees within 30s, 30 demos), Bottle Opening (single-handed cap-turning 360 degrees within 180s, 30 demos), Card Sliding (slide+grasp a card off a desk within 120s, 30 demos), PostIt Note Sliding (same as Card Sliding but a thicker/heavier object, 30 demos). All demonstrations feed a downstream visual imitation-learning stage (VINN/BYOL nearest-neighbor policy, Sec. IV-E) — not a diffusion or transformer policy.

## Evaluation
- exact success criterion (quoted per task, Sec. V-A): Planar Rotation — "success if the robot is able to rotate the object by 90 degrees under a minute"; Object Flipping — "success if the robot is able to flip the object by 90 degrees within a minute"; Can Spinning — "success if the robot is able to spin the can by 90 degrees under 30 seconds"; Bottle Opening — "success when the robot rotates the bottle cap by 360 degrees under 180 seconds"; Card Sliding / PostIt Note Sliding — "success when the robot is able to lift the card off and stably grasp it from the table under 120 seconds."
- number of eval episodes/seeds: Table II reports success counts out of 10 base-object trials per task per algorithm (BC, BC-Rep, VINN), plus a separate generalization evaluation of 5 rollouts x 10 novel objects = 50 trials per in-hand task (Planar Rotation, Object Flipping, Can Spinning) — "VINN (New Objects)" column: Planar Rotation 35/50, Object Flipping 50/50, Can Spinning 45/50 (Sec. V-D, Table II). The teleoperation-speed study (Table I) used a within-subject comparison across 5 untrained operators plus 1 "expert" operator, no seed count reported for policy training.
- real robot? Yes — entirely real-robot, Allegro hand, no simulation used anywhere.
- headline numbers (Table II, 10 trials per base task unless noted): BC 0/10 and BC-Rep 0/10 on every task; VINN 10/10 (Planar Rotation), 9/10 (Object Flipping), 10/10 (Can Spinning), 10/10 (Bottle Opening), 3/10 (Card Sliding), 3/10 (PostIt Note Sliding) — "VINN outperforms both Behavior Cloning algorithms on all tasks... for the two tasks that involve sliding and picking, the performance of VINN is quite low at 30%. We believe this is due to our robot's inability to sense touch" (Sec. V-C). Untrained-operator study (Sec. V-B): 5 untrained users solved 4/6 tasks on their first try, failing only on the sliding tasks; training on the system gave "a nearly 2.6x speedup in demonstration collection."
- baselines beaten: DIME [28] (single-RGB-image teleoperation, compared on collection speed and feasibility, Table I); Behavior Cloning and Behavior Cloning from pretrained representations (BC-Rep) [69] (compared on task success rate, Table II).

## Limitations stated by the authors (Sec. VI, quoted)
- "for the harder manipulation tasks, such as sliding a card our learned policies achieve poor performance. Integrating tactile sensing to HOLO-DEX could remedy this issue."
- "our retargeting procedure only applies to robots that can map to human joints. This limits its applicability to robots with different morphologies (e.g. aerial robots, quadrupeds, etc.)."

## Notes for the survey
- Feeds: the retargeting-formulation comparison table as the simplest baseline case — direct joint-angle mapping (no optimization, no cost function, no collision/joint-limit handling at all) contrasted against dexpilot_2020's and anyteleop_2023's fingertip-vector optimizations and geometric_retargeting_2025's learned neural retargeter. Also feeds the operator-interface / cost-of-rig comparison: $399 total (VR headset only) is the cheapest quoted rig-cost figure in this survey batch so far, against dexpilot_2020 (4-camera studio, no dollar figure but clearly more expensive infrastructure) and anyteleop_2023 (single RGB-D camera, no dollar figure stated).
- Direct sibling of dime_2022 (same lead author, same INN/VINN imitation-learning stage, same Allegro hand) — Table I's DIME-vs-HOLO-DEX comparison in THIS paper is the primary source for how much faster VR-headset tracking is than single-RGB-camera tracking for the SAME downstream pipeline; cross-reference when writing dime_2022's note rather than treating the two as independent data points.
- Contact/penetration handling: not addressed at all — no interpenetration metric or collision term anywhere in the retargeting or control pipeline; the authors attribute their worst results (sliding tasks, 3/10 success) explicitly to a hardware gap (no tactile sensing) rather than a penetration/contact-modeling gap, worth noting precisely because it is a different failure attribution than the sim-side papers in this survey.
- Demonstration-recording frequency (5Hz) is throttled below the teleoperation control-loop frequency (60-72Hz) purely for multi-camera bandwidth/storage reasons — a data-collection-pipeline detail worth flagging if the survey compares "demonstration frequency" across teleop systems, since it is not the same as the system's achievable control rate.
