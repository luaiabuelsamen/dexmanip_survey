# deximit_2026 — DexImit: Learning Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation from Monocular Human Videos (Mu, Yang, Bao et al., arXiv 2026)

sources: papers/md/deximit_2026.md [30ce5455 / arxiv 2602.10105] ; no code

## One-line contribution
DexImit is an automated, RL-free pipeline that turns arbitrary-viewpoint monocular human manipulation videos (real or generated) into physically-plausible bimanual dexterous robot demonstrations via four stages — 4D hand-object reconstruction at near-metric scale, LLM-based subtask decomposition with an "Action-Centric Scheduling" algorithm for bimanual coordination, force-closure grasp synthesis + keyframe motion planning, and comprehensive data augmentation — then trains a 3D Diffusion Policy on the synthesized data for zero-shot real-world deployment (Abstract, §III).

## Setting
- hand(s): real-world deployment uses "XHands" (multi-fingered dexterous hands, exact DoF not stated in the parsed text) mounted on two UR5e arms (§IV-D, "Experimental Setup"). Simulation experiments (Table II) presumably use a bimanual dexterous-hand setup matching the reconstruction pipeline's MANO-based hand model, but the exact simulated hand/DoF is not explicitly named in the parsed text beyond references to "bimanual dexterous hand setting."
- simulator / physics: not named explicitly as a specific engine in the parsed sections (no "Isaac Gym"/"MuJoCo"/"SAPIEN" string found in the read sections); trajectory generation uses grasp synthesis (BODex-style optimization, §III-C) and keyframe-based motion planning rather than a physics-simulated RL rollout, so DexImit's "simulation" role is closer to a kinematic/quasi-static physical-plausibility check than an RL training environment. This is a data-generation-then-IL pipeline, not an RL paper.
- observation: for the trained policy, 3D point clouds — "We use 3D point clouds as the observation" (§III-D) — captured via a Microsoft Azure Kinect depth camera in the real-world setup (§IV-D).
- action space: not explicitly restated as a policy output vector in the parsed text; the underlying data-generation actions are end-effector pose targets derived from grasp synthesis and keyframe motion planning (rigid hand-object transform applied to the end-effector pose, §III-C), later consumed by a 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) whose action-space definition is inherited from Ze et al.'s DP3 rather than restated here.
- objects / data: six simulation task categories evaluated against baselines (Table II: Put Cup, Grapefruit, Fruits, Pour, Pot, Stack Cups), each trained from "a dataset of 100 demonstrations" per task after augmentation (§IV-B). Four real-world meta-tasks (§IV-D): Place Apple (unimanual), Place Potato&Pepper (independent bimanual), Place Pot (bimanual grasping), Pour Water (cooperative bimanual). Four additional long-horizon task-difficulty studies (§IV-C): Make-Beverage (ultra-long-horizon), Cook, Cut-Apple (tool use), Stack-Cup (fine-grained, six cups into a pyramid).

## Method
- paradigm: automated demonstration synthesis (reconstruction + constrained optimization + motion planning, NOT RL, NOT teleoperation) feeding downstream IL (3D Diffusion Policy, DP3) — most similar in spirit to DexMimicGen/HumanoidGen's generation-then-IL structure, but generating from video rather than from either seed human demos (DexMimicGen) or LLM-driven scene/task generation (HumanoidGen).
- algorithm: (1) 4D reconstruction combining a depth/tracking model (best combination found empirically: SpatialTracker v2 + FoundationPose++, "ST2+FPose", Table I) mapped into a shared world frame at near-metric scale; (2) Qwen3-VL-based video understanding for task/subaction annotation, feeding a custom "Action-Centric Scheduling" algorithm (Algorithm 1) that manages a priority queue of tasks across N embodiments over a T-step horizon, dispatching pregrasp/grasp/motion/release subactions per embodiment; (3) force-closure grasp synthesis (following BODex) plus keyframe-based motion planning treating the grasped hand+object as one rigid body; (4) a 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) trained on the augmented synthetic data.
- teacher-student / privileged->vision distillation: not applicable — no RL teacher/student distillation stage exists; the pipeline is generation-then-BC, not RL-then-distillation.
- domain randomization / data augmentation (quoted, §III-D): "Object pose: ...randomize the position and translation of objects"; "Object scale: ...apply scale factors of [0.8, 1.2]" but "retain the original source grasps and motions and only adjust finger articulation to accommodate the augmented scales" because regenerating grasps/motions per scale "leads to inconsistent supervision across demonstrations, which destabilizes training and slows convergence"; "Camera pose: ...randomize both the orientation and position of the camera"; "Observation: ...apply augmentation to the object point clouds by randomly removing 30% of the points and adding noise to the normals of the remaining points (30% perturbation)."

## Reward / objective block (grasp synthesis optimization, quoted per HOWTO_METHOD)
Not an RL reward — DexImit has no learned reward function; grasp quality is instead an optimization objective solved once per grasp event via BODex-style force-closure synthesis (§III-C). Quoted structure: decision variables are the bimanual pose g = {(t_h, R_h, q_h)} (translation, rotation, joint angles per hand h∈{h0,h1}) and contact forces {f_c}; grasp map G_c = [I; (p_c - m)×] O_c built from forward-kinematics contact position/orientation and object center of mass m. "Given target wrenches {w_j} and scaling λ, and weights κ_•, the grasp synthesis minimizes [an objective whose terms are named as]: The terms ψ(d_M(·)), Φ_M, and Φ_hh penalize contact distance, hand-object collision, and hand-hand penetration, respectively." The exact closed-form Eq. (4), recovered as raw LaTeX from the OCR pass (quoted verbatim, including the OCR's own broken spacing/line-wrap artifacts in the LaTeX source): "\begin {aligned} &\min _{\mathbf {g},\,\{\mathbf {f}_{\mathbf {c}}\}}\quad \kappa _{w} \sum _{j=1}^J \Big \| \lambda \mathbf {w}_j - \sum _{{\mathbf {c}}\in \mathcal {C}} \mathbf {G}_{\mathbf {c}}(\mathbf {g}) \mathbf {f}_{\mathbf {c}} \Big \|_2^2 \\ & \quad + \kappa _{\text {con}} \sum _{{\mathbf {c}}\in \mathcal {C}} \psi (d_M(\mathbf {p}_{\mathbf {c}})) \ + \kappa _{\text {coll}} \,\Phi _{M}(\mathbf {g}) + \kappa _{\text {hh}} \,\Phi _{\text {hh}}(\mathbf {g}). \end {aligned}" (Eq. 4) (recovered by OCR from papers/md/deximit_2026.ocr.md; OCR text is noisier than the layout parse, symbols may be imperfect). "Solving Eq. (4) yields a set of physically feasible and collision-free grasp candidates G_oi for object oi." Candidates are then ranked by proximity to the reconstructed human hand pose and evaluated sequentially against a stability criterion until one passes (§III-C).

## Contact / penetration handling
Explicitly and quantitatively addressed at the grasp-synthesis level — this is one of very few papers in this batch with a named penalty term specifically for hand-hand interpenetration: "Φ_hh" penalizes "hand-hand penetration" and "Φ_M" penalizes "hand-object collision," both inside the force-closure grasp-synthesis objective (§III-C, quoted above). This produces "physically feasible and collision-free grasp candidates" as an optimization output, not a continuously-monitored RL penalty during rollout — i.e., collision-freeness is enforced once, at the moment a bimanual grasp pose is synthesized, rather than measured throughout execution. No further contact/penetration metric is reported for the executed motion-planning phase or for the final trained DP3 policy's rollouts.

## Coordination and embodiment (survey fields)
- two-hand coordination: explicit multi-embodiment task/subaction scheduling, not a single relative-frame RL policy — the "Action-Centric Scheduling Algorithm" (Algorithm 1) treats each embodiment (hand) as a separate action queue A_i, and a shared priority queue of Tasks τ = (E_τ, o_τ, S_τ, k_τ) tracks which embodiment set E_τ⊆{1,...,N} is involved in each ordered Subaction s=(a_s,t_s) with a_s∈{pregrasp, grasp, motion, release}; the paper explicitly supports "unimanual actions..., cooperative bimanual actions..., and fully concurrent bimanual actions" (§III-B) without hardcoding a leader/follower role, since role assignment falls out of which embodiment(s) each annotated subaction requires. Downstream, however, one DP3 policy is trained per task on the resulting demonstrations — a single policy over the full bimanual action space, per task.
- torso: not applicable / not described — real-world setup is "two UR5e arms" (fixed-base industrial arms, not a humanoid or mobile platform), each equipped with an XHand (§IV-D).
- hand model/DoF each side: XHands on both arms in the real-world setup (identical both sides, multi-fingered, exact DoF count not given in the parsed text). Simulation hand model/DoF not explicitly restated beyond "bimanual dexterous hand setting" used for the six-task comparison (Table II).

## Evaluation
- metrics (exact definitions): reconstruction success rate over "100 short-horizon tasks" for six depth+pose estimation method combinations (Table I: TA+RANSAC 38%, TA+PCR 11%, VGGT+PCR 32%, DA3+PCR 45%, ST2+PCR 76%, ST2+FPose 82% — the best combination, ST2+FPose, is adopted). "Data usability rate" = proportion of physically plausible, training-ready generated samples, reported qualitatively via a heatmap (Fig. 3) crossing 4 input-data-quality levels (sentence-level generation, in-the-wild video, custom-captured video, manually-corrected video) against 4 task-difficulty levels (unimanual short-horizon; cooperative bimanual short-horizon; independent bimanual short-horizon; cooperative bimanual long-horizon) — exact percentage values are not given as numbers in the parsed text, only as a color-scale figure. Policy success rate (Table II) — trained DP3 evaluated in simulation, exact number of eval trials/episodes per task not stated in the parsed text beyond "we then train a DP3 policy on the resulting dataset and evaluate its performance in simulation" (§IV-B). Real-world evaluation (§IV-D, Fig. 6) is described qualitatively as "consistently high zero-shot success rates" with three named ablations (w/o scale aug, regen grasp, w/o obj pcd noise), but exact real-world trial counts and per-condition percentages are presented only in a figure, not as numbers in the parsed text — flagged as unavailable from the parsed source.
- headline numbers: Table II success rates (baseline comparison, 6 tasks, "-" = failure): Put Cup — RigVid 96, DexMan 94, DexImit 100; Grapefruit — RigVid "-", DexMan 98, DexImit 100; Fruits — RigVid 100 (best baseline), DexMan "-", DexImit 100; Pour — RigVid 50, DexMan "-", DexImit 100; Pot — both baselines "-", DexImit 78; Stack Cups — both baselines "-", DexImit 52. DexImit is the only method to solve Pot and Stack Cups at all, and is tied-or-better on every other task.
- baselines beaten: RigVid (re-implemented and extended from single-arm-gripper to bimanual dexterous-hand setting for fair comparison) and DexMan (re-implemented from the paper since "its official codebase has not been released" — note this is the same DexMan as dexman_2025 in this survey batch, and DexImit's own paper states DexMan "is highly sensitive to trajectory quality; even small per-frame action inconsistencies can lead to training instability," consistent with dexman_2025's own admission of an embodiment-gap failure case on the guitar-lifting task).
- real robot? Yes, for §IV-D — two UR5e arms + XHands, Azure Kinect depth camera, four meta-tasks, zero-shot deployment (no real-world training data), with three augmentation ablations; exact trial counts and numeric success rates for this real-world evaluation are given only in Figure 6, not as numbers in the parsed markdown text, so they cannot be quoted precisely from this source. Runtime: "a single video requiring approximately four minutes of processing" for full data generation (Appendix B.3).

## Data-generation specifics (survey field: demos synthesized / success rate of generation)
Unlike DexMimicGen's fixed N-human-demos -> M-generated-demos ratio, DexImit generates from an arbitrary set of input videos (real, in-the-wild, or model-generated) rather than replaying/perturbing a small seed set of teleoperated demos; the natural "success rate of generation" analogue here is (a) the 4D reconstruction success rate (Table I, up to 82% with the best method combination over 100 short-horizon tasks) and (b) the qualitative data-usability heatmap across input-quality x task-difficulty (Fig. 3, no numeric values recovered from the parsed text). For the policy-training comparison (Table II), "100 demonstrations" were generated per task after augmentation (§IV-B) — this is a fixed target count per task, not a stated conversion ratio from a specific number of source human videos.

## Limitations stated by the authors
"Since DexImit generates data through the sequential execution of multiple modules, errors may propagate along the pipeline, occasionally rendering the resulting data unusable" — a random sample of 100 failure cases was analyzed by which module was primarily responsible (Fig. 7, breakdown not given as numbers in the parsed text). "The current pipeline cannot handle complex in-hand manipulation." Future work: end-to-end data generation to improve efficiency/accuracy; extending to deformable and articulated objects, which "would require more powerful 3D generation models to handle object deformation and articulated kinematics" (§V).

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "The terms ψ(d_M(·)), Φ_M, and Φ_hh penalize contact distance, hand-object collision, and hand-hand penetration, respectively. Solving Eq. (4) yields a set of physically feasible and collision-free grasp candidates" (§III-C).
- "DexMan [22], which relies on reinforcement learning to track video-derived trajectories, is highly sensitive to trajectory quality; even small per-frame action inconsistencies can lead to training instability" (§IV-B).
- "when scale augmentation is applied but grasps and motions are re-generated for each scale, performance degrades drastically, even below the setting without scale augmentation... motions synthesized at different scales are inconsistent, introducing conflicting supervision signals that hinder imitation learning" (§IV-D).
