# objdex_2024 — Object-Centric Dexterous Manipulation from Human Motion Data (Chen, Wang, Yang, Liu; CoRL 2024)

sources: papers/md/objdex_2024.md [17fb38ee] ; no code

## One-line contribution
A hierarchical policy — a Transformer high-level planner behavior-cloned on human wrist motion to output 6-DoF dual-wrist targets conditioned on a desired object SE(3)/joint trajectory, plus a PPO low-level controller that outputs finger joints and a small residual wrist correction — lets a bimanual Shadow Hand + UR10e system track object trajectories from ARCTIC mocap and transfer zero-shot-retrained to a real robot (Abstract; Sec. 4).

## Setting
- hand(s): Shadow Hand ×2, each mounted on a UR10e arm, "arranged in an abreast configuration" (Fig. 3a); bimanual. DoF not stated numerically in the parsed text beyond the observation-space table implying per-arm-hand dof blocks of 0-60/60-120 (30 values each covering position+velocity, i.e. ~15 joint DoF per arm-hand in that slice — not explicitly labeled as "22" or similar; treat as "not stated" precisely).
- simulator / physics: not named in the parsed text (no "Isaac Gym"/"IsaacGym" string found); PPO training with hyperparameters in App. D (not read here — header present at line 496, "D Hyperparameters of the PPO", but body not required for the fields below beyond what's already sourced). Real-world perception runs FoundationPose-based object pose tracking at 15 Hz (Sec. 4.4).
- observation: (Table 5, sim) 527-dim vector: right/left arm-hand DoF position+velocity (0-120), right/left hand end-effector position/rotation/linear vel/angular vel (120-146), object base pose+vel (146-159), articulated object top/bottom part pose+vel (159-185), object DoF position/velocity (185-187), desired object motion trajectory G (187-257), high-level-planner-generated 6-DoF dual wrist action sequence (257-397), right/left hand fingertip pose+linear+angular velocity (397-527). Real-world distilled policy (Table 6) drops velocities and fingertip blocks: DoF position only, end-effector position/rotation, object part position/rotation, desired trajectory, wrist actions (0-287).
- action space: (a_t^W + Δa_t^W, a_t^F) — high-level-planner wrist pose a_t^W (6-DoF ×2 hands) corrected by a learned residual Δa_t^W bounded to ±4 cm translation / ±0.5 rad rotation, plus low-level finger joint actions a_t^F, both output by π_L (Sec. 4.2).
- objects / data: ARCTIC [13], 10 objects × 20 sequences; 9 objects × 16 sequences used for training, remainder held out unseen (Sec. 5, "Data"). High-level planner π_H trained on "the entire ARCTIC dataset:" 339 sequences of human hand mocap, 2.1 million steps (Sec. 4.1).

## Method
- paradigm: hybrid IL (high-level) + RL (low-level) ; algorithm: high-level planner π_H = Transformer, trained with behavior cloning (imitation learning) on human wrist mocap (Sec. 4.1); low-level controller π_L = PPO [70] (Sec. 4.2).
- human dataset retargeting: NOT a finger-level retargeting — the paper's central design choice is to retarget only the wrist (6-DoF SE(3) pose per hand), because "human finger motions are not consistently useful... due to the embodiment gap" (Sec. 1). No IK/optimization correspondence is applied to fingers at all: finger motion is learned purely by RL exploration, conditioned on the planner's wrist trajectory, never imitated from the human hand. The wrist itself needs no correspondence solve since it is represented abstractly as SE(3) pose, common to human and robot arm end-effector. Baselines that DO use classic finger retargeting are explicitly compared and beaten: "Finger Joint Mapping" (match human/robot finger joint angles as BC demonstration, citing DexMV-style retargeting) and "Fingertip Mapping" (match fingertip positions via IK, citing DexCap-style) (Sec. 5, "Baselines"). Ablation "Ours (w. FR)" adds a fingertip-matching reward term and this "does not yield benefits and even leads to lower performance, validating our hypothesis that the embodiment gap makes human finger motion unsuitable for training robot actions" (Sec. 5.2) — an explicit paper-level finding against finger retargeting.
- reward (paper, Sec. 4.2 + App. B.2, quoted): r_t = exp(−(λ1·‖g_t^R − ĝ_t^R‖₂ + λ2·‖g_t^T − ĝ_t^T‖₂ + λ3·‖g_t^J − ĝ_t^J‖₂)) where g^R/g^T/g^J are current object rotation/translation/joint angle and ĝ are the reference values; weights given in App. B.2: λ1=20 (rotation), λ2=1 (translation), λ3=5 (joint angle). Orientation error computed via quaternion dot product between normalized goal and current quaternion "to improve the calculation efficiency" (App. B.2). No separate finger-imitation or contact term is in the main reward (the ablation "Ours (w. FR)" adds one only as a comparison, and it hurts performance).
- key trick(s): decoupling wrist (imitated) from fingers (RL-only) to sidestep the embodiment gap; a small bounded residual wrist correction (±4cm/±0.5rad) layered on the high-level planner's output so the low-level policy can compensate for hand-size mismatch; a Data Augmentation Loop (DAL, Sec. 4.3) that randomizes object size/initial pose/waypoint-interpolated goal trajectories during RL and feeds successful rollouts back to fine-tune the high-level planner (self-training loop between the two levels); teacher-student distillation via DAgger [75] for sim-to-real, dropping velocity/fingertip observations the real system can't measure (Sec. 4.4, App. C.2).

## Evaluation
- metrics: high-level planner alone (Sec. 5.1) — cumulative Translation Error (TE, cm, Euclidean) and Orientation Error (OE, angle difference) over the full wrist trajectory (Table 1). Downstream task success (Sec. 5.2 onward, App. C.4) — Completion Rate: "the percentage of the goal object trajectory completed by the policy. Completion is achieved if and only if the object's pose error is smaller than a threshold (5 centimeters in translation, 2.5 centimeters in object's longest dimension multiplied by rotation angle, and 0.5 radians in joint angle)"; each goal trajectory = 500 action steps. Sim results over 10 seeds, real-world over 20 trials (Sec. 5, standard deviations reported).
- headline numbers: Table 1 (high-level planner, Trained objects/traj) — Ours TE 4.2±0.2 cm, OE 9.6±0.6, vs. MLP TE 5.4±0.2/OE 14.6±0.7 and RNN TE 5.0±0.1/OE 12.4±0.8. Table 3 (single-object policies, per-object completion rate) — Ours beats Finger Joint Mapping / Fingertip Mapping / Vanilla RL "by 50% in completion rate" on average and beats Vanilla RL "by 47.3% on average" (Sec. 5.2); e.g. Box: Ours 100±0.0% vs. Vanilla RL 23.5±3.5%, Fingertip Mapping 14.6±0.3%. Table 4 (generalization) — Ours beats Vanilla RL on Single Obj-Unseen Traj and Multi Obj-Unseen Obj "by more than 28%"; DAL ablation contributes "an average 18% improvement" in Table 4 vs. 7% in Table 3. Table 2 (real-world, 20 trials): Ours reaches up to 100±0.0% (Microwave, Laptop), 82.8±2.1% (Mixer), down to 41.2±13.3% (Ketchup); "more than a 50% completion rate improvement compared to prior methods, which barely achieve any success (<20%)" (Sec. 5.4).
- baselines beaten: Finger Joint Mapping (finger-joint-angle IL, citing DexMV-style), Fingertip Mapping (fingertip-IK IL, citing DexCap-style), Vanilla RL (single PPO policy over arm+hand jointly, no hierarchy). Ablations: Ours w. FR (fingertip-matching reward added — hurts), Ours w.o. DAL (no data augmentation loop — hurts generalization more than in-distribution completion).
- real robot? yes — bimanual Shadow Hand + UR10e, 4 objects shown (coffee maker, laptop, mixer, notebook; Fig. 5), 20 trials per object (Sec. 5.4, App. C.3/C.4), completion-rate metric identical to sim (5cm/2.5cm-scaled-rotation/0.5rad thresholds).

## Limitations stated by the authors (Sec. 6)
- "encounters difficulties in manipulating small-size objects (e.g. scissors, ketchup, etc.)"; suggests incorporating contact-graph-style reward (citing PhysHOI [69]) as future work.
- Does not use ARCTIC's tactile information at all during training.
- Fails when real-world initial pose differs greatly from the goal trajectory's start.
- Hand-caused occlusion degrades real-world pose estimation.
- Joint order must be predefined (no permutation invariance across articulated-object joint orderings); worked around via category-level URDF-consistent joint ordering.
- High-level-planner generalization is within-category only; cross-category generalization (e.g. Box policy driving a Laptop) is forced via a trained category ID, not learned, and is untested at scale because ARCTIC has only 11 objects.

## Notes for the survey
- Feeds: the retargeting-comparison section as the clearest counterexample to finger-level retargeting — this paper's central empirical claim is that retargeting fingers (Finger Joint Mapping, Fingertip Mapping, and even just adding a fingertip-matching reward) actively hurts generalization and completion rate versus retargeting only the wrist and letting RL discover fingers from scratch. Any survey claim that "richer human correspondence is always better" should be qualified against this result.
- Object tracking: BOTH position and orientation, plus articulated joint angle where applicable (g^R, g^T, g^J all in the reward and completion-rate criterion) — this is a genuinely position+orientation(+articulation) tracked method, unlike physhoi_2023's code-level position-only mismatch.
- Failure/termination definition: not an early-termination-during-training criterion in the RL sense (no stated reset-on-deviation rule); rather, "failure" is defined only at evaluation as exceeding the completion-rate tolerance (5cm/2.5cm-scaled-rotation/0.5rad) at any point, same threshold used for sim and real (Sec. 5.2, App. C.4).
- Block D (interpenetration): NOT ADDRESSED anywhere in the parsed paper — no mention of penetration, collision meshes, or contact solver settings; the only contact-adjacent statement is a stated limitation that they did not use ARCTIC's tactile/contact information and suggest (as future work) borrowing PhysHOI's contact-graph reward (Sec. 6). No geometric interpenetration metric is measured or penalized at any point.
- SOURCE THIN FOR REWARD BLOCK: none — the low-level RL reward is fully quoted with weights (App. B.2). The high-level planner's BC loss function itself is never written out explicitly (only "trained... with a behavior cloning algorithm," Sec. 4.1) — flag if the survey wants the exact BC loss form, it is not in this paper.
