# dexverse_2026 — DexVerse: A Modular Benchmark for Multi-Task, Multi-Embodiment Dexterous Manipulation (Yao et al., arXiv 2026)

sources: papers/md/dexverse_2026.md [be0e0b51] ; no code

## One-line contribution
A modular Isaac-Lab benchmark of 100 dexterous-manipulation tasks in 8 categories, spanning 3 arms and 6 dexterous hands with a floating-wrist variant of each hand, plus 3,180 VR-teleoperated demonstrations across state/RGB/depth/point-cloud observations, on which the best of four IL/VLA baselines reaches only 34% mean online success (Abstract, Sec. 3, 4).

## Setting
- hand(s): 6 dexterous hands — Sharpa Wave, WUJI Hand, Shadow Hand, Inspire Hand, Allegro Hand, LEAP Hand (Sec. 1, 3.2). DoF and vendor are not stated for any of the six in the parsed text; the paper only says the hands "cover diverse kinematics, degrees of freedom, joint limits, actuation ranges, and hand morphologies" and defers "detailed embodiment specifications" to "the supplementary material," which is not present in this parse (Sec. 3.2). Each hand also has a floating variant with the wrist directly actuated by prismatic + revolute joints (Sec. 3.2).
- arm(s): 3 robot arms — Franka Research 3, UR10e, xArm 7 (Sec. 1, 3.2). Arm-hand pairing shown only in Fig. 5a (not extracted as text); which of the 3×6 combinations are actually instantiated is not stated in the parsed text.
- single/bimanual: both — most of the 100 tasks are single-arm; a 5-task "Bimanual Coordination" category and a subset of long-horizon tasks (e.g. BimanualLiftTray, BimanualHandover) use two arms (Table 2, App. A).
- simulator / physics: built on "the manager-based environment interface of Isaac Lab" (Sec. 3.1); no physics engine name (e.g. PhysX version), timestep, control rate, or #parallel envs is given in the parsed text beyond "parallel RL env." being a supported feature (Table 1).
- observation: 9 named observation groups — `policy` (previous action), `proprio` (joint positions of every robot joint), `contact` (per-fingertip 3D contact-force vector per configured sensor, in robot base frame, `None` if sensors disabled), `state` (task-specific object/joint poses, derived functional points/axes, long-horizon stage progress; `None` if unpopulated), `privileged` (sim-only: joint velocities, full hand-link body state, object linear/angular velocity — "never enabled by any preset"), `goal` (commanded target; `None` if no separate goal), `rgb`, `depth` (per-camera tensors), `pointcloud` (single-camera or multiview-merged) (App. C). Seven observation-mode presets combine subsets of these with history length 0 or 3 and single/3-view multiview (Table 5, App. C).
- action space: not stated generically; the IL baselines use absolute 28-/56-dimensional joint targets (single-hand / bimanual) predicted as multi-step chunks (App. B).
- objects / data: rigid/articulated assets from PartNet-Mobility, ManiTwin, NVIDIA Isaac Lab/Isaac Sim assets, AutoBio, and public Synthesis assets, plus Meshy-generated meshes when no suitable asset exists; 100 HDR skyboxes from Poly Haven and Isaac Lab/Isaac Sim table materials for visual variation (Sec. 3.4). Demonstrations: 3,180 total — 55 per each of the 56 single-goal tasks (50 with Shadow Hand + 1 with each of the other 5 hands) and 20 per each of the 5 long-horizon tasks (Sec. 4, "Dataset Statistics").

## Task suite (Table 2, App. A "Task List and Visualization")
100 tasks in 8 categories, task = (Ω, S₀, O, A, G) with G a simulator-predicate success condition (Sec. 3): Primitive (9): PickCube, StackCube, RelocateSphere, PickUpStick, RelocateObject, TurnOnSwitch, PushButton, OpenFaucet, GraspTwoItems. Functional (11): OpenCabinet-adjacent grasp/pour tasks — GraspBleach, GraspPan, GraspKettle, GraspCup, RetrieveCup, LiftBucket, FunctionalPourCan, FunctionalPourMug, FunctionalHammerStrike, FunctionalDrillApply, PourWineGlass. Articulation (18): OpenCabinet, LiftLid, OpenDoor, RotateKnob, OpenMicrowave, OpenDrawer, GraspBucket, GraspPot, OpenLaptop, SqueezeScissors, SlideUtilityKnife, LiftBasketHandle, OpenStapler, OpenFlatFolder, OpenPhone, OpenDoubleDoor, UnscrewCap, OpenGlasses. Contact-rich (8): NutThread, InsertPipette, PlugCharger, InsertPen, InsertGear, InsertPeg, PickFromClutter, PickThinObjectFromContainer. Non-prehensile (5): PushSphereUpSlope, PushSmallSphereObstacleSlope, PushT, PivotLargeCuboidAgainstWall, TakeBookOffShelf. Bimanual Coordination (5): BimanualLiftTray, BimanualLiftBasket, BimanualLiftCarton, BimanualLiftDutchOven, BimanualHandover. Multi-goal (39): the `multigraspenvs` composite set — 9 primary articulated tasks {OpenMicrowave, OpenDoor, OpenDrawer, LiftLid, GraspPot, PushButton, RotateKnob, TurnOnSwitch, GraspBucket} × 4 addon tasks {PourCan, PickUpStick, PourMug, RelocateSphere} = 36, plus PourMug as an additional primary paired with the other 3 addons = 3 more, total 39; success requires the addon object lifted ≥ h_addon (one fixed threshold across all 39) AND the primary joint reaching its own τ_primary simultaneously (App. A "Multi-goal composite tasks"). Long-horizon (5): CookFood, MakeCoffee, MicrowaveFood, OvenBake, CleanTable — each a multi-stage ordered/unordered predicate chain (App. A).

Representative success-condition thresholds (App. A, verbatim examples): PickCube — "lifted at least 0.20 m above its resetting height"; NutThread — "centered within about 0.0025 m of the bolt axis and threaded down to within roughly 0.002 m of the target depth, about 1.5 turns down the bolt"; PlugCharger — "inserted into the receptacle with less than 0.0025 m of lateral and vertical misalignment"; BimanualHandover — "the left hand grips firmly the cube with a contact force of at least 1.6 N on at least one link, while the right hand having no active contact with the cube"; OpenDoubleDoor — "both doors reach at least 80% of their full swing range at the same time, and the gap between when each door first stays past that point is no more than 1.0s."

## Method
- paradigm: benchmark + IL/VLA baseline evaluation (not itself an RL/IL method paper).
- baselines: π0.5 (3.3B-param VLA, full fine-tune, Gemma flow-matching action expert, 10-step chunks of absolute 28-/56-dim joint targets, vision+language only — proprioception input disabled, 32 H20 GPUs, 2K steps, AdamW β=(0.9,0.95), wd 1e-10, grad clip 1.0, batch 512, cosine lr 1e-4→1e-6 with 100-step warmup); OpenVLA (`openvla-7b` + OFT recipe, continuous L1-regression 8-step chunk head, LoRA rank 32 on every linear layer, base weights frozen, 8 H20 GPUs, 3K steps, AdamW lr 1e-4 decayed 10× via multi-step schedule, per-GPU batch 8, random-crop aug); Diffusion Policy (state-based 1D U-Net, FiLM conditioning, widths (256,512,1024), 2 res blocks/stage, To=2, Ta=16 stride 1, DDPM 100 steps/20 inference denoise steps, AdamW lr 1e-4 wd 1e-4, batch 256, EMA decay 0.995, ≤300 epochs); DP3 (PointNet encoder on N=512 FPS-downsampled point cloud + proprio, U-Net widened to (512,1024,2048), To=2, Ta=16 executing first 8 before replanning, DDIM 100/10 steps, AdamW lr 1e-4 wd 1e-6 β=(0.95,0.999), batch 128, EMA max-decay 0.9999, 100 epochs) (App. B).
- reward or loss: not applicable — the paper does not train an RL policy; DP/DP3 use the DDPM/DDIM denoising objective and ACT-style baselines are not used here (App. B has no penalty terms).
- key trick(s): config-driven modular environment architecture (Main/Base/Family/Base-Task inheritance chain, Fig. 4) that decouples task logic from embodiment; replay-based demonstration storage (action-state pairs only, observations regenerated on replay) to avoid cross-machine physics divergence (Sec. 4, "Dataset Statistics").

## Evaluation
- metrics: online success rate, mean over 50 rollouts per task (Sec. 4.1, "Results").
- headline numbers (Table 3, 19 tasks, mean success rate): DP3 0.34 (tied for best), π0.5 0.34, DP 0.32, OpenVLA 0.19. Category leaders: DP strongest on Pick-and-Lift (0.51 per-category, text), DP3 leads Functional Tool Use (0.35), π0.5 leads Articulated-object Manipulation (0.35) and Precision Contact (0.29). Zero-success tasks for all four baselines: PushT (0.00 across the board), and InsertPen/SlideUtilityKnife/OpenLaptop "stay at or near zero everywhere" (Sec. 4.1, findings 2-3).
- baselines beaten: n/a — DexVerse is the benchmark itself; the four IL/VLA methods are evaluated against each other and against zero/near-zero performance on tight-tolerance tasks, not against a DexVerse-native prior baseline.
- real robot?: none; all evaluation is in-simulator, closed-loop, same simulator as training (Sec. 4.1).

## Limitations stated by the authors
"The current release focuses on building a broad, reproducible, and multi-task, multi-embodiment benchmark. Future extensions will study real-robot transfer, expand demonstrations across more embodiments and task families, and provide broader standardization for cross-task and cross-embodiment evaluation." (Sec. 5, "Limitation and Future Work"). No stated licence, code release, or asset release plan in the parsed text — the paper repeatedly says data/benchmark are "provided" but no GitHub/licence line appears in this parse, and the bib record's `github` field is null.

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "even the best-performing baselines achieve only 34% mean online success rate, and no single method consistently dominates across task categories" (Abstract / Sec. 1)
- "the priors carried by these backbones come from web images and low-DoF action spaces, which transfer to perception but not to the high-DoF multifinger control manifold of DexVerse" (Sec. 4.1, finding 1)
- "Tight-tolerance tasks collapse for every method: PushT is 0.00 for all four policies, and InsertPen, SlideUtilityKnife, and OpenLaptop stay at or near zero everywhere." (Sec. 4.1, finding 3)

## Notes for the survey
- Feeds the evaluation/benchmark-comparison section as one of the two closest prior benchmarks; unlike bench2dex_2026 it is single-hand-per-episode (bimanual only in 5+long-horizon tasks) and has no tactile modality, but has a broader task taxonomy (100 vs 26) and explicit visual-variation controls (textures/lighting/background/camera) rather than Bench2Dex's structured invariance/equivariance channel split.
- SOURCE THIN on embodiment specifics: hand DoF, per-hand vendor, arm-hand pairing matrix, physics engine/timestep, and licence are all deferred to a supplementary material section not present in papers/md/dexverse_2026.md — do not backfill these from the hand's own spec sheet without re-checking the actual DexVerse supplement.
- Contradicts nothing so far in this corpus but is a useful outside baseline for "large model gap" claims: internet-pretrained VLA (OpenVLA, π0.5) does not clearly beat from-scratch diffusion policies (DP, DP3) on this dexterous suite — worth citing against any claim that scale alone closes the dexterity gap.
