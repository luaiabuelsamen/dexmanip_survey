# egodex_2025 — EgoDex: Learning Dexterous Manipulation from Large-Scale Egocentric Video (Hoque et al., ICLR 2026)

sources: papers/md/egodex_2025.md [7ea269e5] ; code/md/egodex_2025.md [718d9ebd]

## One-line contribution
829 hours / 90M frames / 338,000 episodes of Apple Vision Pro (ARKit) egocentric video across 194 tabletop dexterous-manipulation tasks, with paired 3D upper-body + 25-joint-per-hand pose, confidence values, camera extrinsics and GPT-4-refined language annotation, plus two proposed benchmarks (trajectory prediction, inverse dynamics) evaluated with 14 imitation-learning model variants under a "best-of-K" 3D keypoint-distance metric (Abstract, Sec. 3-5).

## Setting
- capture setup: Apple Vision Pro running visionOS 2, using ARKit ("a production-grade pose tracking software") for markerless bare-hand tracking; egocentric RGB video 1920×1080 @ 30 Hz plus camera intrinsics/extrinsics at 30 Hz; "the camera data records precisely what the collector sees without any pose offsets (unlike, for instance, a head-mounted camera)" (Sec. 3.1). Data recorded in ~10-15 minute "sessions" containing many episodes, with episode boundaries marked by app pause/resume; raw video compressed (uncompressed would be "over 500 TB... about 250× its current size"), decoded at train time with PyTorch `torchcodec` to avoid full-video decode (Sec. 3.1).
- hand model: **not MANO** — ARKit skeletal joint tracking. 25 joints per hand (per bib "why" and App. A.3's full joint enumeration): each finger has Metacarpal, Knuckle, IntermediateBase, IntermediateTip, Tip (5 joints × 5 fingers = 25 per hand), plus the wrist (`leftHand`/`rightHand`) and a 20-joint upper-body skeleton (hip, 7 spine segments, 4 neck segments, shoulder/arm/forearm/hand per side) (App. A.3). Confidence values ∈ [0,1] per joint per frame: "Wrist confidence values... indicate whether each hand is detected as a whole, while finger joint confidence values indicate confidence relative to the wrist" (App. A.3) — i.e., a high-confidence fingertip under a low-confidence wrist is not reliable. No mesh (no MANO V_h vertices) and **no object pose or object mesh annotation at all** — this dataset annotates hands only, not objects.
- two hands + object jointly annotated?: both hands are tracked simultaneously (25 joints × 2 hands + wrist + body), so it is a genuine two-hand egocentric corpus, but there is **no object annotation** (no object pose, no object mesh, no contact/penetration metric) — hand-object "interaction" is only implicit in the RGB video and the natural-language task description, not in any structured object-state channel.
- subjects: not stated in the parsed text (no subject count given anywhere in Sec. 3 or the appendix excerpts read).
- objects / scenes: tabletop-only scene diversity by design ("the scene diversity in EgoDex is limited to tabletop environments... the Cartesian product of scene and behavior is not the focus of our work, which focuses on behavioral diversity," Sec. 3.4); includes FurnitureBench assembly-benchmark tasks and "a significant amount of basic pick-and-place with diverse objects" (Sec. 3.4).
- episodes / frames / hours: 338,000 episodes, 90,000,000 frames, 829 hours total, 2.0 TB on disk (Sec. 3, Abstract). Train/test/extra split (code README, not in the paper body): ~725 h train, 7 h test, 97 h "additional data collected after the 99/1 train/test split was frozen" — i.e. the paper's 1% held-out test set is drawn before this extra data was added. 194 tasks (Abstract; full list in App. A.2, 28 "reset-free" + 14 "reset" tasks enumerated by name in the excerpt headers, remainder not individually listed in this note).

## Task types (Sec. 3.3, verbatim definitions)
- **Reversible**: "pairs of tasks that are the inverse of each other. The distribution of final states for one task is within the distribution of initial states for its inverse. For example, connecting a charger to a device and removing a charger from the device."
- **Reset-free**: "tasks with a final state distribution that falls within its own initial state distribution. For example, throwing a ball in the air and catching it (where gravity acts as the reset)." 28 such tasks listed in App. A.2.
- **Reset**: "tasks in which the environment must be reset to the initial state distribution after each demonstration." 14 such tasks listed in App. A.2.
"Reversible and reset-free tasks enable a higher yield from data collection as they eliminate costly resets, which are not included in the recorded data" (Sec. 3.3).

## Action representation and benchmarks (Sec. 4)
- action a_t: 3D wrist position + 6D wrist orientation (Zhou et al. 2018 parameterization) + 3D position of each of 5 fingertips, per hand → dim = 2 hands × (3 + 6 + 3×5) = **48**. Poses expressed in the current camera frame; each action chunk is a relative trajectory (Sec. 4.1).
- **Benchmark 1 — dexterous trajectory prediction**: predict a_t:t+H from o_0..t (images), s_0..t (skeletal poses), and language l.
- **Benchmark 2 — inverse dynamics**: same but also conditioned on a goal image at t+H, interpretable as a visually goal-conditioned policy — added because "multimodality can be very severe in natural human motion."
- both benchmarks parameterized by horizon H (e.g., H=30 → 1 s at 30 Hz, H=90 → 3 s). Fixed 1% held-out test split for reproducibility, remaining 99% split across train/val as desired (Sec. 4.2).
- **evaluation metric, verbatim**: "best of K" — sample the trained model K times, "compute the distance between the ground truth trajectory and the trajectory closest to it out of the K samples, where 'distance' is calculated as the Euclidean distance between predicted 3D keypoint positions and their ground truth 3D counterparts, averaged over each timestep in the predicted chunk and each of the 12 keypoints (i.e., the wrist and fingertips of each hand)." Interpreted as "average positional error in 3D space between ground truth and prediction in meters" (Sec. 4.3). For deterministic models the value is K-invariant.

## Method / experiments (Sec. 5) — this is a dataset+benchmark paper, not a novel RL/IL method
- baselines: X-IL framework (Jia et al. 2025) policies — 2 Transformer architectures (encoder-decoder "EncDec", decoder-only "Dec") × 3 policy representations (behavior cloning "BC", denoising diffusion "DDPM", flow matching "FM") = 6 core model variants, 14 models trained in total (including horizon/goal/scale ablations). 50,000 gradient steps, batch size 2048, 8× NVIDIA A100 GPUs; default model 200M params, an ablation at 500M params.
- headline numbers (Table 2, 2-second horizon, Avg/Final Distance in m at K=1/5/10): Dec+BC 0.045/0.045/0.045 avg, 0.062/0.062/0.062 final (K-invariant, deterministic); Dec+DDPM 0.053/0.044/0.041 avg, 0.071/0.050/0.044 final; Dec+FM 0.052/0.042/0.040 avg, 0.071/0.049/0.043 final; EncDec+BC 0.044/0.044/0.044 avg (best at K=1), 0.060/0.060/0.060 final (best at K=1); EncDec+DDPM 0.052/0.042/0.039, 0.071/0.048/0.043; EncDec+FM 0.051/0.041/0.038 avg (best at K=5,10), 0.070/0.047/0.041 final (best at K=5,10). Findings: "encoder-decoder ('EncDec') models consistently outperform their decoder-only ('Dec') counterparts by a small margin"; "the encoder-decoder flow matching ('FM') model outperforms the other models for K=5 and K=10 by up to 34%"; "for the K=1 setting, BC outperforms both diffusion and flow-matching by about 15%."
- horizon ablation (Table 3, Dec+BC): H=30(1s)/60(2s)/90(3s) avg distance 0.031/0.045/0.053 m, final distance 0.049/0.062/0.069 m — "reducing the horizon from 2 seconds to 1 second improves average and final distance by 31% and 21% respectively, while increasing the horizon from 2 to 3 seconds worsens average and final distance by 18% and 11% respectively."
- goal-conditioning ablation (Table 4, Dec+BC, H=2s): no goal image 0.045/0.062 m avg/final; with goal image 0.035/0.029 m — "reduces average distance by 22% and final distance by 53%."
- model-size ablation: 500M-param Dec+BC gets avg 0.045 / final 0.062 m, "exactly the same as the default 200 million parameter model" — "medium-size model capacity is sufficient for the current dataset size."
- data-scaling: Fig. 5 shows avg/final distance monotonically improving as training-set fraction increases from 5% to 100% (log-scale x-axis; no table of exact numbers extracted, curve only).
- baselines beaten: n/a — this is an internal ablation study across EgoDex's own baseline model family, not a comparison against external prior work's numbers.
- real robot?: none — all data and evaluation are in the human-video domain; Sec. 6 ("Research Use Cases") and Sec. 2.2 discuss robot transfer only as future/related work (co-training with small robot datasets, pretrain-then-finetune, visual-encoder pretraining, or human-video-derived manipulation priors fine-tuned with RL/IL), citing EgoMimic and Humanoid Policy Human Policy as examples of that pattern, not as EgoDex's own result.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Scene diversity is explicitly limited by design: "the scene diversity in EgoDex is limited to tabletop environments... Scene diversity can be introduced with modern visual data augmentation methods such as image-to-image generative models" (Sec. 3.4) — i.e. the authors treat this as an intentional scope choice with a suggested mitigation, not an oversight. The dataset has zero object-state annotation (no object pose/mesh/contact), which the paper frames as future opportunity rather than limitation: "Researchers can also study which objects are involved in each action and how. For example, one could model the contact points, grasps, and trajectories when using a tool" (Sec. 6, "Perception") — confirming object/contact modelling is explicitly *not* part of the released annotation.

## Notes for the survey
- Feeds the datasets section as the largest-scale (829 h / 90M frames) two-hand egocentric corpus in this batch, but it is markedly different in kind from GRAB/OakInk/H2O/HOI4D: **no object annotation whatsoever** (no pose, no mesh, no contact/penetration metric), ARKit skeletal joints instead of MANO, and its own benchmark tasks are pure hand-trajectory-prediction / inverse-dynamics regression, not grasp-quality or physical-plausibility evaluation. Position it in the survey as a scale/diversity source for hand-motion pretraining, not as a source of contact-metric ground truth.
- Genuinely two-hands-tracked (both hands' full 25-joint skeletons recorded simultaneously per frame), unlike hoi4d_2022 (single-hand only) and oakink_2022 (single-hand, handover pairs two different subjects' single hands) — but "two hands tracked" here means two independent hand skeletons in one egocentric stream, not a bimanual-task or two-hands-on-one-object structured label.
- Corpus papers citing EgoDex (grep -l "EgoDex" over papers/md and code/md, ≤15 cap): being_h0_2025 (paper + code), being_h05_2026, h_rdt_2025, bai_unified_manip_survey_2025, hot3d_2024, dexycb_2021, egoscale_2026, wm_dex_human_videos_2025, metis_2025.
