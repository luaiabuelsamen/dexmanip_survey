## One-line contribution
Two models — Gemini Robotics-ER 1.5 (an embodied-reasoning VLM used as a "thinking" orchestrator) and Gemini Robotics 1.5 (a multi-embodiment "Thinking VLA" action model with a new Motion Transfer training recipe) — combined into an agentic system, trained and evaluated across three real robot platforms (ALOHA, Bi-arm Franka, Apptronik Apollo humanoid) with zero-shot cross-embodiment skill transfer.

## Method
- paradigm: multi-embodiment VLA pretraining ("advances the frontier of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) pre-training", §1) plus a "Thinking VLA" interleaving natural-language reasoning traces with action, combined with a separate embodied-reasoning VLM (GR-ER 1.5) in an agentic orchestrator/action-model split.
- backbone VLM(s): both models "inherit Gemini's multimodal world knowledge" and are "built on the latest generation of Gemini (Comanici et al., 2025)" (§1, §2.1). No separate names beyond "Gemini Robotics-ER 1.5" and "Gemini Robotics 1.5" are given. Parameter counts: **not stated** anywhere (checked Table 2 model card and §2.1 — Google does not disclose them). The report does say the VLA is smaller than the ER model: "the Thinking VLA is a smaller model that is primarily optimized for action output" (§5, line 349).
- action head / output formulation, quoted verbatim (Table 2, "Output(s)"): "Gemini Robotics-ER 1.5 generates text (e.g., numerical coordinates) in response to the input. Gemini Robotics 1.5 generates continuous numerical values that represent robot actions, and additionally text when thinking mode is enabled." No diffusion, flow-matching, or discrete-token terminology is used anywhere in the paper to describe the GR 1.5 action head; the report only says the action model "translates instructions issued by the orchestrator into low-level robot actions" (§2.1, "Action model") and, in the walkthrough example, characterizes lower-level output as "an inner monologue of primitive motions such as 'move the gripper to the left' or 'close the gripper'" (§2.1, lines 52-58).
- action chunk length / control rate: not stated for GR 1.5. The only Hz number in the paper (5Hz, §4.2 line 298) is for real-time ER success-detection evaluation, not VLA control.
- Motion Transfer (MT): "a new model architecture and training recipe for the VLA. These enable the model to learn from different robots and data sources, to form a unified understanding of motions and the effects of physical interactions, enabling skills to transfer across very different robot embodiments" (§2.1, "Motion Transfer"). No architectural or loss detail beyond this description is given.
- reward or loss: **not stated**. No loss function, reward term, or training objective formula appears anywhere in the report (checked §2, §3). This is presented as a supervised action-prediction training report; nothing to quote.
- key trick(s): (1) Motion Transfer training recipe for cross-embodiment alignment; (2) Embodied Thinking — natural-language reasoning traces interleaved with actions, applied to both the VLM (high-level planning) and VLA (short-horizon step decomposition); (3) agentic orchestrator/action-model split, with GR-ER 1.5 as orchestrator and GR 1.5 as the callable "specialized tool" for low-level control (§2.1); (4) MuJoCo-based sim evaluation scaling shown to be rank-consistent with real-robot evaluation (Fig. 21, Appendix B.1).

## Evaluation
- metrics (exact definitions): task-specific "progress score" rubrics, e.g. for the Apollo "pick up X, place in white bag" tasks: "1.00: if the robot picked the correct object and placed it in the bag; 0.50: if the robot picked the wrong object but correctly placed it in the bag; 0.50: if the robot picked the correct object but didn't place it in the bag; 0.00: if the robot did anything else." (Table 10, §B.2.3.1). Also a separate binary "success rate" is reported for every progress-score figure (Appendix B.5). Evaluation methodology: "we perform A/B/n testing on real robots. This means that we test all models involved in a particular comparison in an interleaved manner on the same robot work cell" (§2.3, line 68).
- headline numbers (Fig. 3, progress score, and Fig. 35, success rate, both broken out by robot):
  - ALOHA: GR 1.5 vs. Gemini Robotics vs. GRoD across In-Distribution / Instruction / Action / Visual / Task Generalization — GR 1.5 progress scores 0.83 / 0.81 / 0.73 / 0.76 / 0.67 (Fig. 3 top panel); GR 1.5 success rates 0.72 / 0.71 / 0.63 / 0.67 / 0.62 (Fig. 35 top panel).
  - Bi-arm Franka: GR 1.5 progress scores 0.74 / 0.77 / 0.73 / 0.70 / 0.50 vs. GRoD 0.36 / 0.34 / 0.09 / 0.22 / 0.12 (Fig. 3 middle panel).
  - Apollo Humanoid: GR 1.5 progress scores 0.74 / 0.73 / 0.66 / 0.62 / 0.63 vs. GRoD 0.32 / 0.24 / 0.20 / 0.07 / 0.16 (Fig. 3 bottom panel); GR 1.5 success rates 0.64 / 0.64 / 0.56 / 0.51 / 0.40 (Fig. 35 bottom panel).
  - Motion Transfer ablation (Fig. 4/36): on all three robots, "GR 1.5 (full, with MT)" > "multi-embodiment, no MT" > "single-embodiment, no MT" across every generalization axis, e.g. Apollo Task Generalization progress score 0.51 (full MT) vs. 0.35/0.09 for the ablations (Fig. 4 bottom panel).
  - Thinking ablation, multi-step benchmark (Fig. 6/37): progress score with Thinking on/off — ALOHA 0.67/0.51, Bi-arm Franka 0.60/0.55, Humanoid 0.55/0.26; success rate with/without — ALOHA 0.44/0.38, Bi-arm Franka 0.42/0.40, Humanoid 0.26/0.09 (Fig. 37).
  - Long-horizon agentic benchmark, 8 tasks on ALOHA + Bi-arm Franka (§5, Fig. 17): "the Thinking VLA achieves moderate performance with a progress score up to 44 percent... our GR 1.5 agent frequently achieves scores near 80%" (line 349). Per-task progress scores for the full agent (GR-ER 1.5 + GR 1.5) range 0.58-0.88 across the 8 tasks (Fig. 17). Failure-mode breakdown (Table 1): Agent w/ Gemini 2.5 Flash orchestrator — Planning 25.5%, Success detection 6%, Action 13%, Total 44.5%; Agent w/ GR-ER 1.5 orchestrator — Planning 9%, Success detection 4%, Action 9%, Total 22%.
  - Cross-embodiment zero-shot transfer benchmarks (Bi-arm Franka -> ALOHA, 10 tasks, Table 13; ALOHA/Humanoid -> Bi-arm Franka, 11 tasks, Table 14; ALOHA -> Humanoid, 7 tasks, Table 15) give only task-level progress-score rubrics; the aggregated numeric transfer results are reported in Fig. 5 (qualitative panel) and referenced quantitatively only via the Fig. 5 "success rate in addition to progress score" statement (line 168) without a restated numeric table in the main text.
  - No exact trial-count-per-task or episode-count-per-condition is given anywhere (unlike the predecessor paper's "20 trials per task"); only "Over 90% of the evaluation episodes ... were conducted in simulation" (§2.3, line 74).
- baselines beaten: Gemini Robotics (prior generation, ALOHA only) and Gemini Robotics On-Device ("GRoD", all three platforms); ablations against GR 1.5 trained single-embodiment or multi-embodiment without the MT recipe.
- real robot? Yes, all headline numbers are real-robot A/B/n results (with >90% of raw episodes for iteration done in a rank-consistent MuJoCo sim, Fig. 21). Trial counts: not stated per condition.
- GR-ER 1.5 (embodied-reasoning VLM, separate from the VLA, not itself controlling a robot): evaluated on "a collection of 15 widely-used academic benchmarks ... such as text-based image understanding (e.g., BLINK ..., CV-Bench ..., and ERQA ...) and spatial reasoning (e.g. RoboSpatial ..., PointArena ..., Where2Place ..., and RefSpatial ...)" plus a generalist mix (MMMU, GPQA, Aider Polyglot) (§4.1, line 224). Complex-pointing accuracy (Fig. 10): GR-ER 1.5 Average Pointing 80.0%, ahead of Gemini 2.5 Pro (76.0%) and GPT-5 (67.8%) as flattened from the figure. These are perception/reasoning benchmarks, not manipulation success rates, and are not tied to any specific robot embodiment.

## Limitations stated by the authors
No separate "Limitations" section exists (Section 7 is "Discussion", not a limitations section). Limitations are stated inline: MT is less effective for the largest embodiment gap — "the effect of MT is less pronounced here, suggesting that the technique's alignment capabilities may be less effective when the embodiment gap is substantially larger, such as between the highly dissimilar humanoid and other robot forms" (§3.2, line 163); humanoid data scarcity — "For humanoid robots, where data is scarce, the addition of external embodiment data provides the greatest performance boost" (§3.2, line 163, implying baseline humanoid data is limited); the Thinking VLA alone is insufficient for complex long-horizon tasks and is "a smaller model that is primarily optimized for action output" with "limited" world understanding and task planning compared to the ER model (§5, line 349); "simply pairing an off-the-shelf VLM like Gemini 2.5 Flash with an advanced VLA model fails to achieve reliable end-to-end success" (§5, line 382); models "often require long inference time making real-time usage challenging" for success detection (§4.2, line 298).
