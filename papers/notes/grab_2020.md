# grab_2020 — GRAB: A Dataset of Whole-Body Human Grasping of Objects (Taheri et al., ECCV 2020)

sources: papers/md/grab_2020.md [f571520f] ; code/md/grab_2020.md [284cba75]

## One-line contribution
Marker-based MoCap (54 Vicon cameras, 120 fps) of 10 subjects × 51 3D-printed ContactDB objects × 4 intents → 1,334 sequences / 1,622,459 frames of SMPL-X body+face+hand meshes and rigid object meshes, with per-vertex binary contact computed by mesh intersection plus a 4.5 mm proximity tolerance; "use" grasps show 3.25 ± 0.68 mm average penetration (Abstract, Table 1, Sec. 3.3, Sup. S.5).

## Setting
- hand(s): human. SMPL-X (10,475 vertices, 55 joints; hands θ_h ∈ R^60, i.e. 30-DoF PCA pose space per hand) fit with an adapted MoSh++; the release also ships MANO parameters per hand (Sec. 3.2; code README "lhand & rhand"). No robot.
- sensor setup (Sec. 3.1, Fig. 2): Vicon, 54 "Vantage 16" cameras, 16 MP, 120 fps. 99 markers per subject: 49 body (4.5 mm radius spheres on a suit, ≈9.5 mm off the skin), 14 face and 36 finger markers (1.5 mm radius hemispheres glued to skin, ≈0 mm offset, dorsal side only, no glove). Objects: 1.5 mm hemispherical markers glued on, at least 8 per object placed so ≥3 are always visible. MoCap cleaned by four technicians in Shōgun-Post. No RGB captured (Sec. 5, S.4).
- simulator / physics: none; "we track the human and object separately and on a per-frame basis" (Sec. 3.2, S.8).
- objects / data: 51 objects, CAD meshes from ContactDB [6], printed on a Stratasys Fortus 360mc; rigid, known mesh V_o; 6-DoF pose per frame from ≥3 markers (Sec. 3.1–3.2). Table 1: sequences use 579 / pass 414 / lift 274 / off-hand 67 = 1,334; frames 605,796 / 335,733 / 603,381 / 77,549 = 1,622,459. 10 subjects, 5 male / 5 female, all right-handed (Sec. 3.4, 3.5).
- protocol (Sec. 3.4, S.6): intents "use", "pass" (borrowed from ContactDB), "lift", "off-hand pass" (bimanual hand-to-hand); randomised initial object pose; table height randomised 75–120 cm; subject starts and ends in T-pose; "lift" includes re-grasping and random re-placement.
- release contents (S.2; code README "Contents of each sequence"): per-sequence npz with gender, sbj_id, obj_name, motion_intent, framerate 120, n_frames, body{params, vtemp}, lhand/rhand{params, vtemp}, object{params, object_mesh}, table{params, table_mesh}, and contact: "For each motion frame, each object vertex is associated with a number that ranges from 0 to 55. 0 means there is no contact while any other number shows the specific body/hand part that the vertex has contact with" (mapping in `tools/utils.py#L166`). Also object meshes with marker locations, subject shape templates, raw Vicon files.

## Method
- paradigm: dataset + generative model (GrabNet).
- contact metric, verbatim (Sec. 3.3): "we relax this and define contact when the distance, d, is below a tolerance, threshold d ≤ ε_contact. This helps address: (1) measurement and fitting errors, (2) limited mesh resolution, (3) the fact that human soft tissue deforms when grasping an object, while the SMPL-X model cannot model this."
  - over-shooting: "We use a fast method [32,50] to detect intersections, cluster them in connected 'intersection rings', R_b ⊊ V_b and R_o ⊊ V_o, and label them with the intersecting body part ... (1) When a body part gives only one intersection, we annotate as contact points on the object, V_o^C ⊂ V_o, all vertices enclosed by the ring R_o. We then annotate as contact points on the body, V_b^C ⊂ V_b, all vertices that lie close to V_o^C with a distance d_{o→b} ≤ ε_contact. (2) In case of multiple intersections, i, we take into account only the ring R_b^i corresponding to the largest intersection subset, M_b^i." S.7 adds that for thin objects (a cup) rings on the inner surface are discarded and only outer-surface rings kept.
  - under-shooting: "we compute the distance from each object vertex, V_o, to each non-intersecting body vertex, V_b. We then annotate as contact vertices, V_o^C and V_b^C, the ones with d_{o→b} ≤ ε_contact. We empirically find that ε_contact = 4.5 mm works well for our purposes."
  - labels are binary per vertex; part labels by nearest SMPL-X/MANO bone (S.7). "Heatmaps" = per-vertex contact integrated over frames (Sec. 3.5).
  - contact frames used for analysis: object moved ≥5 mm vertically from its initial position and ≥50 body vertices in contact → 952,514 frames; thresholds have "little influence" (Sec. 3.5, S.11).
- penetration metric (S.5, Fig. S.2): "For each frame we store the max (red), mean (green) and median (blue) vertex penetration." For "use" sequences: "'Use' grasps have 3.25 ± 0.68 mm average penetration, which effectively corresponds to the missing soft-tissue deformation ... 67% of 'use' grasps have ≤ 3.5 mm penetration, 86% ≤ 4.0 mm, 96% ≤ 4.5 mm and 99.9% has ≤ 5.8 mm." The text does not say which of max/mean/median the 3.25 mm summarises, nor the distance function behind "vertex penetration"; the md gives no formula.
- fitting accuracy (S.8, Table S.1, synthetic test with 1 mm Gaussian marker noise and up to 5 dropped markers): adapted MoSh++ Stage-I marker placement 3.09 ± 0.55 mm (median 2.80), Stage-II vertex-to-vertex 4.86 ± 1.83 mm (median 4.48), vs vanilla 4.76 ± 1.03 / 5.59 ± 1.86 mm. Body shape comes from a 3D scan, not estimated.
- GrabNet (Sec. 4): CoarseNet cVAE, object encoded as BPS ∈ R^4096, latent Z ∈ R^16, decodes MANO pose + wrist; losses "KL divergence, weight regularizer, a data term on MANO mesh edges (L1), as well as a penetration and a contact loss" with contact-point weights learned from GRAB; RefineNet takes hand-vertex-to-object distances weighted by learned contact likelihood, 3 iterations, trained on perturbed CoarseNet samples. Loss formulas are not written out in the md.
- code: the md captures only `tools/cfg_parser.py` signatures (Config, makepath); the contact computation is not in the captured code, and the release ships contact labels rather than the code that produced them. No paper/code mismatch is checkable.

## Evaluation
- metrics: contact likelihood per body part / finger; penetration in mm; GrabNet vertex-to-vertex error, AMT Likert 1–5, distance to closest GT hand vertices (cm) and closest GT contact map (%).
- headline numbers: "use" contact frames: right hand 92 %, left 39 %, both 31 %, head 8 %; right-hand per-finger thumb→pinky 100/96/92/79/39 %, palm 24 % (Sec. 3.5). CoarseNet v2v 12.1 / 14.1 / 18.4 mm train/val/test; RefineNet 3.7 / 4.1 / 4.4 mm. Table 2 (6 test objects, 20 grasps each): AMT generated 4.12 ± 1.04 vs ground truth 4.38 ± 0.77; frying pan worst (3.19 vs 4.49); vertices 2.45 cm, contact 4.18 % averages.
- marker bias check: markers lie in "hot" contact areas (Fig. 5 right); k-means (k = 20) shows 3–6 clusters per object with fingers on markers (S.10).
- baselines beaten: ContactDB's contact prediction [6] qualitatively (Fig. 9); no quantitative baseline.
- real robot? none.

## Limitations stated by the authors
- "By focusing on accurate MoCap, we do not have synced image data." (Sec. 5)
- "there is no model of the human body with articulated fingers and face that captures such soft-tissue deformation with contact" (S.5), hence the 4.5 mm tolerance and the residual penetration.
- Contact is estimated from proximity, never measured (Sec. 3.3). Human and object tracked independently per frame (S.8).

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "Since contact cannot be directly observed, we estimate it using 3D proximity between the 3D human and object meshes." (Sec. 3.3)
- "'Use' grasps have 3.25 ± 0.68 mm average penetration, which effectively corresponds to the missing soft-tissue deformation." (S.5)
- "we consider contact frames to be those in which the object's position deviates in the vertical direction by at least 5 mm from its initial position and in which at least 50 body vertices are in contact with the object" (Sec. 3.5)
- "GraspIt optimizes for hand-crafted grasp metrics that do not necessarily reflect the distribution of human grasps" (Sec. 2)

## Notes for the survey
- Feeds the metrics section as the origin of the penetration-depth + contact pair. The 3.25 mm figure is the floor of a marker-fit human mesh against a rigid printed object (fit error itself ≈4.9 mm v2v on synthetic data); it is a capture artefact, not a physics result, and its "mean/median/max" ambiguity should be stated whenever it is cited.
- Our DexTrack audit measures penetration between rigid simulated bodies; the regimes differ and the numbers should not be compared directly. GRAB's ring-based over-shoot handling (ignore inner-surface intersections on thin objects) is the relevant precedent for our thin-object cases.
- Cross-check: dexycb_2021 Table 1 lists GRAB at 1,624K frames / 1,335 sequences vs GRAB's own 1,622,459 / 1,334.
