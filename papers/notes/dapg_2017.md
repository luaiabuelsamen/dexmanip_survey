# dapg_2017 — Learning Complex Dexterous Manipulation with Deep Reinforcement Learning and Demonstrations (Rajeswaran et al., RSS 2018)

sources: papers/md/dapg_2017.md [sha256 555e6470] ; code/md/dapg_2017.md [commit 9a1de23d]

## One-line contribution
DAPG combines behavior-cloning pretraining with an on-policy Natural Policy Gradient (NPG)
fine-tune carrying an auxiliary, exponentially-decayed demonstration-gradient term, cutting
RL-from-scratch training time by up to 30x on four ADROIT-hand tasks under a sparse
task-completion reward (Sec. IV-C, Table I, Sec. VI).

## Setting
- hand(s): ADROIT, 24-DoF anthropomorphic hand. First/middle/ring fingers 4 DoF each,
  little/thumb 5 DoF each, wrist 2 DoF; each DoF position-actuated with a joint-angle sensor
  (Sec. III-B1, Fig. 6). Vendor not stated (only design refs [26],[24],[27] cited). Used only
  as "a simulated analogue" of the hand — no real hardware.
- arm: not stated (pen task only: "the base of the hand is fixed", Fig. 3). single/bimanual:
  single hand, all four tasks.
- simulator / physics: MuJoCo (Sec. III-B2); version not stated. Dry friction in joints; "all
  hand-object contacts have planar friction"; "Object-fingertip contacts support torsion and
  rolling friction" (Sec. III-B2). Tactile sensing exists in-sim but unused ("for simplicity").
  #envs: not stated; Table I implies "200 trajectories of length 2 seconds" per iteration.
  GPU / wall-clock training time: not stated (Table I reports robot-hours of experience, a
  sample-count proxy, not wall-clock compute).
- observation: "hand (joint angles), object (position and orientation), and target (position
  and orientation) details" (Sec. III, line 101). No per-dimension breakdown given; code/md
  has no observation-building code.
- action space: "desired position of hand joints" — joint position targets (Sec. III;
  Sec. III-B1: "actuated using position control").
- objects / data: 4 tasks, 25 successful demonstrations per task, "with task randomization as
  outlined in captions of Figure 2, 3, 5, and 4" (Sec. III-C): `relocate-v0` — move a blue
  ball to a green target (Fig. 2); `pen-v0` — reposition a pen in-hand to a target
  orientation, hand base fixed (Fig. 3); `door-v0` — undo a latch and swing a door open
  (Fig. 4); `hammer-v0` — pick up a hammer and drive a nail into a board (Fig. 5).

## Method
- paradigm: RL + IL hybrid (BC pretrain, then RL fine-tune with an augmented gradient) —
  authors' name: "Demo Augmented Policy Gradient" (Sec. IV title). Grasp/pose generation is
  demo-driven via human VR teleoperation (not analytic, not a generative model): "an updated
  version of the Mujoco HAPTIX system... CyberGlove III system for recording the fingers, HTC
  vive tracker for tracking the base of the hand and HTC vive headset for stereoscopic
  visualization" (Sec. III-C). Exception: pen-v0's "demonstrations" are not human — "we used a
  computational expert trained using RL on a well shaped reward for many iterations. This
  expert serves to give demonstrations" (Sec. III-A2) — so 3 of 4 tasks use human-teleoperated
  demos, pen substitutes an RL-trained expert's rollouts.
- algorithm: Natural Policy Gradient (NPG); "Our implementation of NPG ... is based on
  Rajeswaran et al. [40]" (Sec. IV-B) — not a named public repo in the paper text (mjrl
  implements it per code/md's README, but that implementation is absent from this corpus). BC
  solves "the following maximum-likelihood problem" over demonstration state-action pairs
  (Sec. IV-C1, eq. 5). Eq. 5 recovered by OCR from papers/md/dapg_2017.ocr.md (OCR text is
  noisier than the layout parse, symbols may be imperfect): "maximize_θ Σ_{(s,a)∈ρ_D} ln
  π_θ(a|s). (5)" — i.e. the BC objective maximizes total log-likelihood of demonstrated
  actions under the policy, summed over demonstration state-action pairs. No teacher-
  student/privileged-to-vision distillation (state-based observations throughout; not
  addressed).
- reward or loss (paper AND code, quoted verbatim): the paper gives no per-task reward
  equations or weights in the extracted text — it explicitly defers them: "Further details
  about the tasks, including detailed shaped reward functions, physics parameters etc. are
  available in the project website" (Sec. III-A, external to this corpus). Stated reward form:
  "R : S x A -> R is the reward function which measures task progress. In the ideal case, this
  function is simply an indicator function for task completion (i.e. a sparse task completion
  reward)... various forms of shaped rewards that incorporate human priors ... might be
  required" (Sec. IV-A). The DAPG augmented-gradient term ("we add an additional term to the
  gradient", Sec. IV-C2, eq. 6) recovered by OCR from papers/md/dapg_2017.ocr.md (OCR text is
  noisier than the layout parse, symbols may be imperfect): "g_aug = Σ_{(s,a)∈ρ_π} ∇_θ ln
  π_θ(a|s) A^π(s,a) + Σ_{(s,a)∈ρ_D} ∇_θ ln π_θ(a|s) w(s,a). (6)" with the heuristic weighting
  scheme given right after it: "w(s,a) = λ0 λ1^k max_{(s′,a′)∈ρ_π} A^π(s′,a′) ∀(s,a)∈ρ_D",
  where λ0, λ1 are hyperparameters and k is the iteration counter — matching the previously
  recovered numeric content: "For all the experiments, lambda0 = 0.1 and lambda1 = 0.95 was
  used," weight decaying "via lambda1^k" over iteration counter k (Sec. IV-C2). BC loss form
  (eq. 5) is quoted above.
  CODE SIDE: code/md/dapg_2017.md (3730 chars) is only the `hand_dapg` landing repository —
  README, bibliography, file tree, and one function signature (`def main(env_name, policy,
  mode)` from `dapg/utils/visualize_policy.py`). It contains no reward function, task config,
  or observation code; the task/reward implementations live in the separate `mj_envs`/`mjrl`
  repos referenced by the README but absent from this corpus. **Explicit gap: this block
  cannot be cross-checked against code at all** — code/md is confirmed thin (a landing page,
  not the algorithm/task repo), and the paper defers to an external website. Only
  lambda0/lambda1 are verifiable numbers, and only from the paper.
- key trick(s): BC-initialized policy so exploration starts task-relevant (Sec. IV-C1);
  demonstration noise injection — "uniform random [-0.1, 0.1] radians ... added to the
  actuators per timestep" during demo use, "to combat distribution drift" (Sec. III-C);
  asymptotic decay of the demo-gradient weight over training (Sec. IV-C2).
- domain randomization: object/target position "randomized over the entire workspace" (Fig. 2,
  relocation); pen target orientation "randomized to cover all configurations" (Fig. 3); door
  position "randomized" (Fig. 4); nail position "randomized" (Fig. 5); robustness study varies
  object mass/size per Fig. 9 (approx. mass 0.2-1.4, size 0.02-0.05; exact bounds/units not
  stated). No numeric randomization ranges given for the base tasks themselves.
- contact / penetration handling: not addressed — no mention of interpenetration, contact
  penalty, or a penetration metric anywhere in the extracted text. Contact solver detail
  given: "the stable contact dynamics of MuJoCo... dry friction in the joints, all
  hand-object contacts have planar friction. Object-fingertip contacts support torsion and
  rolling friction" (Sec. III-B2).

## Evaluation
- metrics: success rate = "percentage of successful trajectories the trained policies can
  generate, using a sample size of 100 trajectories" (Sec. V-A). Table I's N = "number of RL
  iterations needed to achieve 90% success rate"; Hours = robot hours; "Each iteration is 200
  trajectories of length 2 seconds each" (Table I caption).
- exact success criterion, verbatim per task: Relocation — "Task is considered successful
  when the object is within epsilon-ball of the target" (Fig. 2 caption; no numeric epsilon
  given). Pen — "Task is considered successful when the orientations match within tolerance"
  (Fig. 3 caption; no numeric tolerance given). Door — "Task is considered complete when the
  door touches the door stopper at the other end" (Fig. 4 caption). Hammer — "Task is
  successful when the entire length of the nail is inside the board" (Fig. 5 caption). Table
  I's aggregate N additionally requires ≥90% success over the 100-trajectory eval sample.
- headline numbers (Table I): Relocation — DAPG N=52 (5.77h) vs RL-shaped N=880 (98h) vs
  RL-sparse infinity; Hammer — DAPG N=55 (6.1h) vs RL-shaped N=448 (50h) vs RL-sparse infinity;
  Door — DAPG N=42 (4.67h) vs RL-shaped N=146 (16.2h) vs RL-sparse infinity; Pen — DAPG N=30
  (3.33h) vs RL-shaped N=864 (96h) vs RL-sparse N=2900 (322h). "DAPG is able to train policies
  almost 30 times faster" on relocation vs. from-scratch (Sec. V-B); "up to 30x more sample
  efficient" overall (Sec. VI). Fig. 7: sparse-reward random exploration "never experience[s]
  success (except in the in-hand task)" for NPG or DDPG. Fig. 9: DAPG more robust than
  shaped-reward RL to held-out object mass/size, and only DAPG succeeds on a randomized
  mass/size ensemble.
- baselines beaten: NPG-scratch (shaped, sparse), DDPG-scratch (shaped, sparse), DDPGfD [53]
  ("DAPG convincingly outperforms DDPGfD", Fig. 10, Sec. V-B) — all appear author-run.
- real robot? Not conducted: "success remains to be demonstrated on hardware" (Sec. I, Sec.
  VI). All numbers above are simulation-only; grasp/task quality is therefore checked only
  geometrically/kinematically per the per-task criteria above, never with a physical
  force-closure or contact-force check. ADROIT is explicitly "a simulated analogue of a highly
  dexterous manipulator" (Sec. III-B1).

## Limitations stated by the authors
- Real hardware not attempted (Sec. I, Sec. VI).
- Pure RL, even shaped, needs "significant manual effort in reward shaping," is too
  sample-inefficient for hardware, and yields policies that "tend to over-fit to the specific
  objects they were trained to manipulate" (Sec. V-A, Fig. 8, Fig. 9).
- Human demos not collected for the pen task: "lack of tactile feedback in VR" (Sec. III-A2).
- BC alone insufficient: "cloned policies themselves were usually not successful" (Sec.
  IV-C1); BC "can often pick up the hammer but seldom swing it close to the nail" (Sec. IV-C2).
- Future work: real hardware, "novelty based exploration methods," "raw visual inputs and tactile sensing" (Sec. VI).

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "we propose to augment the policy search process with a small number of human
  demonstrations collected in virtual reality (VR)" (Sec. I).
- "DAPG can be up to 30x more sample efficient than RL from scratch with shaped rewards.
  DAPG is able to train policies for the tasks we considered in under 5 hours" (Sec. VI).
- "For all the experiments, lambda0 = 0.1 and lambda1 = 0.95 was used" (Sec. IV-C2).
- "We gathered 25 successful demonstrations for all our tasks" (Sec. III-C).
