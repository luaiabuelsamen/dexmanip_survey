# ruka_v2_2026 — Ruka-v2: Tendon Driven Open-Source Dexterous Hand with Wrist and Abduction for Robot Learning (Liu, Hu et al.; arXiv 2026)

sources: papers/md/ruka_v2_2026.md [d71d6f90] ; code/md/ruka_v2_2026.md [fe4f6080, github.com/ruka-hand/RUKA]

## One-line contribution
Adds a decoupled 2-DoF parallel wrist and per-finger MCP abduction/adduction to Ruka (v1), plus attachable magnetic joint encoders and optional e-flesh tactile fingertips, and shows a 51.3% reduction in teleoperated task completion time and a 21.2% increase in success rate over Ruka in a 10-participant user study.

## Hand record
- maker / lab: Xinqi (Lucas) Liu, Ruoxi Hu, Alejandro Ojeda Olarte, Zhuoran Chen, Kenny Ma, Charles Cheng Ji, Lerrel Pinto, Raunaq Bhirangi, Irmak Guzey — New York University and NYU Shanghai (title page).
- DoF / actuated DoF: Table 1 (quoted row): "Tot. / Act. DOF ... Ruka-v2 (Ours): 20 / 16"; "Wrist DOF: 2" (vs. 0 for Ruka v1, ORCA, Wuji, Sharpa Wave, Unitree Dex5). Fig. 2 caption: "Ruka-v2 features 18 degrees of freedom across the fingers and thumb, with joints at the DIP, PIP, MCP, and Adduction axes on each finger, and IP, MCP, and CMC joints on the thumb. An additional 2 DOF wrist provides flexion/extension and radial/ulnar deviation" (18 finger/thumb + 2 wrist = 20 total, consistent with Table 1). Middle-finger abduction is fixed/non-actuated ("The middle finger remains fixed to provide a stable geometric reference," Sec. 2.3; Table 2 marks Middle adduction "Fixed" / "✗" pass). Optional DIP/PIP rigid coupling add-on described in Sec. 2.4 (fixed-length coupling strings) as an alternative to Ruka v1's uncoupled, passively-compliant single-motor PIP/DIP drive.
- actuation type: tendon-driven, actuators "placed proximally in the forearm to reduce distal inertia and simplify maintenance" (Sec. 2); wrist uses a "decoupled parallel wrist mechanism" where "flexion/extension and radial/ulnar deviation are actuated independently" via linkages meeting at "a passive spherical ball joint" defining the common rotation center, with tendons routed through a through-hole in that joint (Sec. 2.2). Abduction/adduction: "single-tendon actuation with spring return" — one tendon drives adduction, an extension spring restores neutral/abduction (Sec. 2.3, Fig. 4).
- cost of BOM (quote verbatim): Table 1, "Cost (USD) ... Ruka-v2 (Ours): ∼$1.5K" (vs. Ruka v1 ∼$1.3K, Allegro V4 ∼$16K, ORCA ∼$3.5K, Wuji Hand ∼$5.5K, Sharpa Wave ∼$50K‡, Unitree Dex5 ∼$25K; table note: "Costs are approx. USD list or material prices; – = not publicly documented"). Sec. 2.1 "Accessibility": "The total material cost of Ruka-v2 is under $1,500, making it accessible to a wide range of research labs."
- build time: not stated as a single number (unlike Ruka v1's "approximately 7 hours"); Sec. 2.1 states only qualitatively that "the hand's open-source design enables quick, in-house repairs, resulting in significantly less downtime compared to commercial robot hands" and that "the only specialized equipment required is a 3D printer."
- weight: not stated.
- fingertip / grip force: not reported as a force metric; instead the paper reports static payload (Table 4, Sec. 4.2): non-thumb DIP-PIP 1200 g held 15 s, non-thumb MCP 780 g/15 s, non-thumb adduction 150 g/15 s, thumb (all 3 joints curled) 835 g/20 s, wrist supination/pronation 1215 g/20 s, wrist radial-side-up/ulnar-side-up 835 g/20 s each ("the largest weight that could be held stably for the specified hold time without visible failure," Sec. 4.2).
- tactile sensing: Table 1, "Tactile: ✓(opt.)" — optional. Sec. 2.1/Fig. 1D: "We equipped Ruka-v2 with e-flesh fingertips [1] ... This enables optional equipment of tactile sensors on the hand as well" — e-flesh is a magnetic touch-sensing fingertip form factor (cited to Pattabiraman et al., "eflesh," ref [1]), added here chiefly for compliance; full tactile integration is left as future work (Discussion, quoted below).
- control interface and rate: code (same repo/commit as ruka_2025) drives Dynamixel motors over USB-serial; no new rate is stated for direct motor control in the v2 paper. The joint-to-motor mapping is now a closed-form "linear mapping between joint angles θ ∈ [θ_min, θ_max] and motor positions p ∈ [p_min, p_max]" with a per-joint scaling factor c (Sec. 3.1), replacing v1's learned LSTM+MLP controller; retargeting uses "the retargeting module from AnyTeleop [27]" (vector-based Dex Retargeting, optimizing link-vector alignment rather than fingertip IK, Sec. 3.1). Teleoperation uses "the Open Teach framework [33]" with an Oculus VR headset, mounted on a 7-DOF Franka arm (Sec. 3.2); no teleoperation Hz is stated in the parsed text (unlike Ruka v1's stated 25/40 Hz). Attachable magnetic encoders (AS5600, 12-bit resolution) are read via "an ESP32 QTPy microcontroller ... over an I2C multiplexer board" for calibration/data collection only (Sec. 2.5), not part of the control loop.
- licence: not stated in the parsed paper text beyond "All 3D print files, assembly instructions, controller software, and videos are available at ruka-hand-v2.github.io" (Abstract) and Table 1 marking Ruka-v2 "Open-source: ✓" ("hardware & software fully public," table legend). No licence file/type is captured in code/md (see Notes below — code/md for this key is the same ruka-hand/RUKA repo snapshot as ruka_2025, commit fe4f6080, whose LICENSE file content was not parsed).
- what the paper demonstrates: (1) thermal endurance, 5-hour continuous run, Table 3: fingers peak 30.75°C/steady 30.35°C/ΔT 0.81°C, thumb peak 47.33°C/steady 46.97°C/ΔT 1.70°C, wrist peak 46.00°C/steady 43.80°C/ΔT 7.25°C, "No thermal limiting was observed during the 5-hour run" (Sec. 4.1); (2) static payload across finger/thumb/wrist configurations, Table 4 (above); (3) controller accuracy via the magnetic encoders: "total average error of 8.26° and total average percentage error of 10.68%" across 4 index-finger and 3 thumb joints, 20 random angles per joint (Table 5, Sec. 4.3); (4) DIP-PIP coupling redesign reduces trial-to-trial hysteresis vs. the Ruka v1 uncoupled design (Sec. 4.4, qualitative/figure-based, no numeric metric given); (5) teleoperation across 10 single-arm tasks + 3 bimanual tasks on dual 7-DOF Franka arms via Open Teach (Sec. 3.2, Figs. 8-9); (6) autonomous policy learning with BAKU (ResNet-18 vision + MLP proprioception + transformer trunk + action-chunking head) on 3 tasks — bread pick-and-place, music-box opening, pen picking — ~100 teleop demos/task with injected Gaussian noise for recovery-behavior augmentation, evaluated at 10 random positions × 5 rollouts, scores (mean±std out of 10): bread pick-and-place 5.6±2.9, music box opening 5.4±3.1, pen picking 5.8±3.8 (Fig. 10, Sec. 3.3); (7) user study, 10 participants, 3 single-arm tasks (Bread Pick-and-Place, Pen Grasping, Book Opening), 3 trials/task/participant, 120 s cutoff: "51.3% reduction in mean completion time and a 21.2% increase in overall success rates compared to RUKA" (Sec. 4.5).

## Setting
- hand(s): Ruka-v2 (20 DoF/16 actuated incl. 2-DoF wrist), compared in Table 1 against Allegro V4, Ruka v1, ORCA, Wuji Hand, Sharpa Wave, Unitree Dex5.
- simulator / physics: not stated in the parsed paper text (all reported evaluation is on real hardware). Code/md "Simulation Files" section (shared with ruka_2025, same repo) states MuJoCo `xml` files exist under `assets/`, but the v2 wrist/abduction hardware is not confirmed to be reflected there (see Notes).
- observation: policy-learning observation space is "a 23-dimensional proprioception state (7-DOF Franka arm state and the 16-DOF Ruka-v2 joint positions) and an RGB video stream from a fixed camera" (Sec. 3.3).
- action space: BAKU policy predicts "a sequence of future actions" via an action-chunking head (Sec. 3.3); joint-space controller for teleoperation converts retargeted joint angles to motor positions via the linear mapping (Sec. 3.1).
- objects / data: ~100 teleoperated demonstrations per task collected via OpenTeach for the 3 BAKU policy-learning tasks (Sec. 3.3); no dataset release details beyond the project website stated in the parsed text.

## Method
- paradigm: hardware design (wrist + abduction mechanism) with a closed-form/calibrated controller (not learned, unlike Ruka v1), plus a downstream imitation-learning demonstration (BAKU) and a human-subjects teleoperation comparison.
- reward or loss: not applicable/not stated — BAKU is described only architecturally (Sec. 3.3); no loss function is quoted in the parsed text.
- key trick(s): decoupled parallel 2-DoF wrist with a passive spherical ball joint fixing a common rotation center so tendon length is largely invariant to wrist pose (Sec. 2.2); single-tendon-plus-spring abduction/adduction actuation to avoid doubling actuator count (Sec. 2.3); attachable, detachable magnetic (AS5600) joint encoders as a lower-cost substitute for the MANUS glove used in Ruka v1, for calibration and controller-accuracy measurement (Sec. 2.5, Discussion); linear joint-angle-to-motor-position mapping with a per-joint compensation scale factor c, replacing v1's learned LSTM+MLP inverse model (Sec. 3.1).

## Evaluation
- metrics (exact definitions): thermal — peak, steady-state, and ΔT motor temperature over a 5-hour continuous actuation sequence (Sec. 4.1). Payload — "largest weight that could be held stably for the specified hold time without visible failure" (Sec. 4.2). Controller accuracy — absolute error and range-normalized percentage error between commanded θ_expected and magnetic-encoder-measured θ_actual, sampled at 20 random angles per joint across 4 index-finger + 3 thumb joints, each held 1.5 s (Sec. 4.3). User study — completion time (s, 120 s cutoff = failure) and success rate over 3 trials × 3 tasks × 10 participants (Sec. 4.5). Policy learning — mean and std of successes out of 5 rollouts at each of 10 random start positions per task, scaled to a 10-point scale (Sec. 3.3).
- headline numbers: 5-hour thermal run with no thermal shutdown (Table 3); Table 4 payloads (up to 1215 g at the wrist); overall controller error 8.26°/10.68% (Table 5); teleoperation improvement over Ruka v1: 51.3% faster, 21.2% more successful (Sec. 4.5); policy scores 5.4-5.8/10 across the 3 BAKU tasks (Fig. 10).
- baselines beaten: RUKA v1 (direct within-lab comparison, user study only); Table 1 lists cost/DOF/wrist/tactile/durability specs for Allegro V4, ORCA, Wuji Hand, Sharpa Wave, Unitree Dex5 as a comparison table, not a matched experimental benchmark (those numbers are the compared hands' own reported specs, per table footnotes, not re-measured by this paper).
- real robot? yes throughout — no sim-only results reported for Ruka-v2 in the parsed text.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Quoted (Discussion, Sec. 5): "**Assumption of linear joint-to-motor mapping** ... While this yields sufficiently accurate control (Section 4.3), the linearity assumption has not been formally verified. Ruka introduced a data-driven approach to model this relationship; a similar approach could be applied here using the magnetic sensors introduced in this work, replacing the need for expensive motion capture gloves while maintaining accurate control." And: "**Out-of-the-box tactile integration** While we introduce e-flesh fingertips that can be converted into tactile sensors, adding touch sensing to a multi-fingered hand may introduce noise due to magnetic interference between sensors. Future work should characterize the extent of this interference and work toward seamless out-of-the-box tactile integration."
