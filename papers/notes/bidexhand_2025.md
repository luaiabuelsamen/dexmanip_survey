# bidexhand_2025 — BiDexHand: Design and Evaluation of an Open-Source 16-DoF Biomimetic Dexterous Hand (Weng; ICRA 2025 Dexterity Workshop, extended abstract)

sources: papers/md/bidexhand_2025.md [89f39715] ; code/md/bidexhand_2025.md [6cd2c754, github.com/wengmister/BiDexHand]

## One-line contribution
A single-author, cable-driven (N-configuration tendon routing) 16-actuated-DoF hand using anti-parallelogram four-bar linkages to passively couple DIP-to-PIP (and thumb IP-to-MCP) motion, evaluated with the full 33/33 GRASP Taxonomy and a 9/11 Kapandji score, released under an MIT-licensed ROS2 codebase.

## Hand record
- maker / lab: Zhengyang Kris Weng, Center for Robotics and Biosystems, Northwestern University, Evanston, IL (title page; single author).
- DoF / actuated DoF: "features 16 independently actuated degrees of freedom and 5 mechanically coupled joints through novel phalange designs" (Abstract); "16 independently actuated DoF and 5 mechanically coupled joints, resulting in 21 joints in total" (Sec. I). Code README (quoted): "It utilizes a cable-and-pulley system, with 15 servos arranged in N configuration to drive its 15 joints with tendon, and a 4-bar linkage driven 16th joint" — i.e. 15 of the 16 actuated joints are direct N-configuration cable-driven, and the 16th (thumb CMC flexion/extension) is driven through a "compact parallelogram four-bar linkage" (Sec. II-B) rather than a direct tendon. Each of the 4 fingers: 3 actuated DoF (MCP abduction/adduction, MCP flexion/extension, PIP flexion/extension) + 1 passively coupled DoF (DIP, driven off PIP via an anti-parallelogram four-bar linkage, Sec. II-A) = 4 joints/finger, 3 actuated. Thumb: 4 actuated DoF (MCP flex/ext, MCP abd/add, CMC abd/add, CMC flex/ext) with IP passively coupled to MCP flexion via a similar anti-parallelogram linkage (Sec. II-B) — so the "5 mechanically coupled joints" = 4 finger DIPs + 1 thumb IP.
- actuation type: cable (tendon)-driven, N-configuration routing — "one actuator controls a single degree of freedom... Endless tendon loops are used to create an antagonistic pull-pull configuration" (Sec. II-C). Servos are mounted proximally ("in the proximal servo sleeve region, effectively relocating joint inertia away from the fingers"), with a spring-loaded tensioning mechanism in the servo sleeve to remove slack post-routing (Sec. II-C). Code README, V4: "Now using unified FeeTech servos."
- cost of BOM: not stated in the parsed paper text — no dollar figure for the hand appears anywhere in papers/md/bidexhand_2025.md. The code repo README points to a `BOM.md` file ("See BOM.md for more details") but that file's content was not fetched/captured in code/md/bidexhand_2025.md, so no cost figure is available from either parsed source. Do not attribute a cost to this hand; the bib entry's own "specs" field likewise carries no cost number, only force/lift/licence claims.
- build time: not stated.
- weight: not stated.
- fingertip / grip force: "Fingertip force was measured using a calibrated sensor across five independent trials per finger, resulting in an average peak output of 2.14 N" (Sec. IV); Fig. 7 caption additionally states "the robotic finger exerts a fingertip load of up to 2.2 N" (a peak figure distinct from the 2.14 N trial average — note both numbers as they appear, without reconciling them). Lift test: "a successful grasp and lift of a 10 lb (approximately 4.54 kg) dumbbell" in "a hooking gesture" (Sec. IV, Fig. 7b).
- tactile sensing: not mentioned anywhere in the parsed paper or code text — no tactile hardware, sensor, or future-work mention of touch sensing appears in either source.
- control interface and rate: "Position control was implemented on the robotic hand, with the control software developed based on the ROS2 framework. Joint angle commands are transmitted via serial communication to an ESP32 microcontroller onboard, which drives the servo motors" (Sec. II-D). Three control modes: "joint-level control, task-based control, and motion shadowing" — the last maps RGB-camera- or VR-headset-derived hand landmarks to servo joint angles (Sec. II-D); "Although the current implementation relies on open-loop position control, development of a closed-loop solution is underway" (Sec. II-D, also repeated in Conclusion). Code (README quickstart): ROS2 launch commands `hand_motion_shadowing shadowing.launch.xml` and `hand_servo_control multi_servo_control.launch.xml`, connecting over a serial USB port (e.g. `/dev/ttyACM0`); Franka arm integration via `combined_fer_moveit_config`. No explicit Hz figure is given for the hand's own servo-control loop; two ROS2 config files (`fer_real_controllers.yaml`, `ros2_controllers.yaml`) set `update_rate: 1000 # Hz`, but these govern the Franka arm's ros2_control loop, not confirmed to be the BiDexHand servo update rate — do not conflate the two.
- licence: code README, quoted in full: "# License / MIT". This is the code/software licence. CAD licence is not independently confirmed from the parsed sources — the bib entry's "specs" field claims "OnShape CAD released 2025 (CC BY-NC-SA)" but this is the bib author's own annotation, not a statement found in papers/md or code/md; per HOWTO, do not present it as verified. The repo also hosts CAD as STEP/STL files directly (`cad_asset/_stp/bidexhand v4.STEP`, `cad_asset/_stl/*.STL`) and on OnShape (README link), under the same top-level `LICENSE` file as the code unless OnShape states otherwise (not checked).
- what the paper demonstrates: (1) GRASP Taxonomy (Feix et al., 33-class) — "the BiDexHand successfully completed all 33 grasps" (Sec. III-A, Fig. 5, "33 out of 33 grasp poses"); (2) Kapandji thumb-opposition test — "the BiDexHand was able to reach 9 out of 11 reference positions" (Sec. III-B), with the 2 missed poses (#0, #8) attributed to the thumb CMC's two-decoupled-serial-joint design limiting its range at the extremes of opposition; (3) fingertip force measurement, 2.14 N average peak over 5 trials/finger (Sec. IV); (4) whole-hand lift of a 10 lb dumbbell in a hooking grasp (Sec. IV); no learning (RL/IL) results are reported — this is a hardware design and mechanical/dexterity evaluation paper only.

## Setting
- hand(s): BiDexHand V4 (16 actuated DoF, 21 joints total); no other hand is benchmarked head-to-head (comparisons in Sec. I are qualitative, citing prior hands [1]-[4] by DoF/cost trade-off, not a table).
- simulator / physics: none described in the paper. Code side: a URDF is referenced and used by the ROS2/MoveIt stack — `joint_limits.yaml` comment (quoted): "joint_limits.yaml allows the dynamics properties specified in the URDF to be overwritten or augmented as needed" — confirming a URDF exists and is consumed by `combined_fer_moveit_config` (MoveIt2 planning, RViz visualization via the `hand_rviz` package), though no specific `.urdf`/`.xacro` filename appears in the parsed (depth-3, assets-pruned) file tree. No physics engine, contact model, or RL/IL training environment is mentioned anywhere in either source — this hand has no dynamics simulator counterpart in the parsed corpus, only a kinematic/visualization URDF for planning.
- observation: motion-shadowing mode computes hand joint angles from RGB-camera or VR-headset hand-landmark detection (Sec. II-D; code packages `hand_vision` — `hand_angle_node.py`, `yolo_node.py`, `wrist_tracker_node.py` — and `hand_motion_shadowing`).
- action space: joint angle position commands, open-loop (Sec. II-D).
- objects / data: GRASP Taxonomy objects per grasp type (Fig. 5, not itemized in the parsed text beyond the taxonomy reference); a dumbbell (10 lb) for the lift test; no dataset or trajectory data used (no learning component).

## Method
- paradigm: hardware design and mechanical/dexterity evaluation; no RL, IL, or learned control is reported. Control is classical position control (open-loop) plus geometric human-landmark-to-joint retargeting for motion shadowing.
- reward or loss: not applicable — no learning-based control in this paper.
- key trick(s): anti-parallelogram crossed four-bar linkage passively coupling DIP to PIP (and thumb IP to MCP) motion to approximate the human DIP-vs-PIP trajectory (DvPT) sigmoid relationship while reducing actuator count by one joint per finger/thumb (Sec. II-A/B, with the closed-form passive DIP angle θ_D derived from linkage lengths l1, l2 and the actuated PIP angle θ_P); N-configuration cable routing (one actuator per DoF, minimizing cable/pulley count) with a spring-loaded tensioning mechanism for pretensioning (Sec. II-C).

## Evaluation
- metrics (exact definitions): GRASP Taxonomy — 33 discrete grasp types (Feix et al. taxonomy), pass/fail per type based on "securely holding appropriate objects to meet the criteria for each pose" (Sec. III-A). Kapandji score — standard clinical thumb-opposition test, reaching 11 reference positions on the fingers (Sec. III-B). Fingertip force — calibrated sensor, 5 trials/finger, average peak reported (Sec. IV).
- headline numbers: 33/33 grasps; 9/11 Kapandji; 2.14 N average peak fingertip force (also reported elsewhere in the same section as "up to 2.2 N," Fig. 7a caption); 10 lb (4.54 kg) lift.
- baselines beaten: none — no comparative hand-vs-hand benchmark table is presented (unlike leap_hand_2023's or ruka_2025's direct comparisons); Sec. I only cites other hands qualitatively for DoF/cost trade-offs.
- real robot? yes, all evaluation (grasp taxonomy, Kapandji, force, lift) is on the physical hand. No trial counts are given for the grasp taxonomy or Kapandji tests beyond "successfully completed"/"was able to reach" (pass/fail per pose, not repeated-trial statistics); force test explicitly used 5 trials/finger.

## Limitations stated by the authors
- Kapandji poses #0 and #8 unreachable: "limited by the thumb's carpometacarpal (CMC) design, which uses two decoupled serial joints rather than a single composite joint. This constraint reduced the thumb's ability to fully reach the extremes of opposition range" (Sec. III-B).
- Control is open-loop only: "Although the current implementation relies on open-loop position control, development of a closed-loop solution is underway" (Sec. II-D); reiterated in Conclusion as future work ("extending the current open-loop controller to a closed-loop system using vision-based feedback").
- Force results caveat (Sec. IV, quoted): "Although the system was not specifically designed for high-force applications, these results highlight the mechanical robustness of the tendon-driven design and its potential for lifting heavier objects under whole-hand grasps" — an implicit acknowledgment that force performance is incidental, not a design target.
- Conclusion also lists "further optimizing tendon routing to reduce backlash and improve precision" and "exploring learning-based strategies to enhance grasp planning" as future work, i.e. no learning-based grasp planning exists yet.

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "Performance validation demonstrated success across all 33 grasp types in the GRASP Taxonomy, 9 of 11 positions in the Kapandji thumb opposition test, a measured fingertip force of 2.14 N, and the capability to lift a 10 lb weight." (Abstract)
- "the full design files, control software, and documentation for the proposed biomimetic hand are made freely available in an open-source repository." (Sec. V)
- "Joint angle commands are transmitted via serial communication to an ESP32 microcontroller onboard, which drives the servo motors in the tendon-drive mechanism." (Sec. II-D)
