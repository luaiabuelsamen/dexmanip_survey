# oakink_2022 — OakInk: A Large-scale Knowledge Repository for Understanding Hand-Object Interaction (Yang et al., CVPR 2022)

sources: papers/md/oakink_2022.md [596a5ba7] ; code/md/oakink_2022.md [73dc7c85]

## One-line contribution
A two-part knowledge base — an object-centric affordance graph (*Oak*, 1,800 objects / 32 categories) and a human-centric interaction recording (*Ink*, 230K image frames, 12 subjects, 100 real objects) — plus *Tink*, a DeepSDF-based shape-interpolation + contact-mapping + SDF-penalized pose-refinement pipeline that transfers the 1K recorded real-object interactions onto 1,700 other *Oak* objects to synthesize 50K total hand-object interactions (*OakInk*-Shape), benchmarked on hand-mesh recovery, hand-object pose estimation, and grasp-pose generation, with two new generation tasks (intent-conditioned and handover) (Abstract, Sec. 3, 4).

## Capture setup
Two knowledge bases, built separately then linked:
- **Oak base** (object side, Sec. 3.1): 1,800 household objects, "designed for single-hand manipulations," sourced from (1) self-collected online-vendor purchases, (2) ShapeNet, (3) YCB, (4) ContactDB. Organized into a 2-level taxonomy: top level = **maniptool** (has a handle + end-effector, e.g. mug/knife/pincer/drill) vs **functool** (self-contained function, e.g. camera/headphone); sub-level = 32 WordNet categories. "Affordance" is represented per-object as a set of part-level ⟨verb(+prep), something⟩ attribute phrases (e.g. knife blade → ⟨cut, something⟩, handle → ⟨handled(by), something⟩), authored by 10 volunteers reaching consensus on a shared candidate-phrase pool; 30 final attribute phrases across all 32 categories.
- **Ink base** (human side, Sec. 3.2): hardware = 4 RealSense D435 RGB-D cameras (MulCam, image capture) + 8 OptiTrack Prime 13W IR motion-capture cameras (MoCap, object 6-DoF tracking via surface reflective markers), synchronized and cross-calibrated, rigidly mounted around a 1.5×1.2×1 m³ cuboidal interaction volume. 12 subjects, each assigned a subset of the object database; for each object up to 5 intents are recorded — *use* (exercise the object's affordance), *hold* (steady grasp), *lift-up* (right an overturned object), *hand-out* (give, paired with another subject's *receive* — this is a human-to-human handover, i.e. one hand per subject, not a bimanual/two-hand-same-arm interaction). Each action recorded 5 s, idle frames manually discarded, object re-randomized between actions by a director.

## Subjects, objects, sequences, frames, hours
"we collected 230K image frames of 12 subjects performing up to 5 intent-oriented interactions with total 100 real-world objects of 32 categories, and transferred the interactions to the rest of 1,700 virtual counterpart objects. The total number of distinct hand-object interactions is 50,000." (Sec. 3.4). Comparison Table 1 gives OakInk-Image: 230K frames, 12 subjects, 100 objects, 4 views, 5 intents, 1K "intact" (interaction instances), 848×480 resolution; OakInk-Shape: 12 subjects, 1,700 objects, 5 intents, 49K interactions (Table 1's OakInk-Shape row states 49K, one less than the "50,000" total stated in prose — a minor internal rounding/off-by-a-thousand discrepancy, flagged rather than resolved). "OakInk-Core" = the 1 (or 2, for handover) manually-selected steady representative interacting pose(s) per recorded image sequence, used as the *source* poses for *Tink* transfer (Sec. 3.3). No hours figure is stated; not derivable cleanly since frame rate is not given in the parsed text.

## Annotation types (hand model, joint two-hand+object, verbatim contact/penetration metrics)
- hand model: MANO (Sec. 3.2.3) — "MANO represents an articulated and deformable hand by pose θ ∈ R^(16×3) and shape β ∈ R^10 parameter." Hand pose denoted P_h ∈ R^(21×3) (21 standard hand keypoints per [Simon et al.]), mesh vertices V_h ∈ R^(778×3) via a differentiable MANO layer M(·). θ and β solved per frame by minimizing a multi-view 2D-keypoint re-projection error (core term shown in the main paper; "geometrical consistency, temporal smoothing, and silhouette constraint" costs deferred to the appendix and not extracted in this pass) using manually-labeled 2D hand keypoints from all 4 MulCam views via an online crowd-sourcing platform.
- object pose: 6-DoF tracked via surface-attached reflective markers in the MoCap system, transformed into the MulCam camera frame by system calibration (Sec. 3.2.3, "Object Pose").
- two hands / joint hand+object annotation: **not** two-hands-simultaneously-on-one-object. Oak-base objects are explicitly "designed for single-hand manipulations" (Sec. 3.1), and the only two-person interaction is the *hand-out*/*receive* handover pair — each subject uses one hand, and the two hands are annotated as separate single-hand sequences that are later paired, not as a joint bimanual solve. Hand and object pose are jointly available per frame (both tracked in the same calibrated system) but the fitting is not a single simultaneous two-hand+object optimization.
- **contact/penetration definitions, verbatim**: *Contactness* (Sec. 3.2.3, "Contact and Stress Pattern") — "we adopt the 17 hand parts segmentation and part-level anchor location in Yang et al. Based on the efficient contact heuristic in GRAB, we automatically assign a part label to those vertices on the object's surface if an anchor is close to them (within the threshold of 25 mm)... we can imitate the stress pattern by adding a ring-shape spreading and decreasing value in the contact region, as we call it contactness... the contactness takes the maximum value 1 at the point closest to a certain anchor, centrally decreasing as the distance increases, and finally becomes 0 when the distance is greater than 25 mm." Used later as an optimization target in *Tink*'s pose-refinement contact-consistency cost (Sec. 3.3.3); the equation itself, previously an unextracted image, has since been recovered by OCR from papers/md/oakink_2022.ocr.md (OCR text is noisier than the layout parse, symbols may be imperfect; the OCR flattens the sum/fraction layout onto separate lines): Eq. 2, "Econsis = (1/Σγij) Σ_{Ai,V^(i)_h,j} γij ‖Ai − V^(i)_h,j‖_2^2" over the 17 hand-region anchors A_i and their corresponding contact-region object vertices V^(i)_h,j with contactness weight γij. The same OCR pass also recovers Eq. 3, the anatomical cost: "Eanat = Σ_{j∈all} [aj·nt_j + max(φj−π/2, 0)] + Σ_{j∉MCP} aj·ns_j" (aj, φj the axial/angular components of joint j's rotation, nt_j/ns_j the pre-defined twist/splay directions, "MCP" the five metacarpal joints).
- *Tink*'s **interpenetration cost** (Sec. 3.3.3, Eq. 4), previously prose-only with the exact formula not text-extracted, now recovered by OCR from papers/md/oakink_2022.ocr.md (OCR text is noisier than the layout parse, symbols may be imperfect): "Eintp = Σ_{Vh,j} ‖min(SDFO(Vh,j), 0)‖" — "where the SDFO(·) calculates the signed distance value of a 3D hand vertex V_h,j to an object's SDF: O provided at shape interpolation." The total optimization problem (Eq. 5, also recovered): "Vh, Ph ← argmin_{θ,β,Ph0} [Econsis + Eanat + Eintp]", where Vh, Ph = M(θ,β) + Ph0. Optimization runs 1,000 iterations per source-target pair in PyTorch with Adam; results are filtered by a 5-volunteer perceptual-plausibility consensus vote (Sec. 3.3.3, "Perceptual Evaluation").
- **released quality metrics, verbatim names and numbers** (Sec. 3.4, "Geometry Dataset Qualities," Table 3; units cm/cm³): OakInk-Core / OakInk-Shape / FPHAB / GRAB (GrabNet split) / HO3D — Penetration Depth (cm, ↓): 0.18 / 0.11 / 1.95 / 2.53 / 1.16. Solid Intersection Volume (cm³, ↓): 1.03 / 0.62 / 22.87 / 7.61 / 2.08. Simulation Displacement Mean (cm, ↓): 0.98 / 0.94 / 6.60 / 2.04 / 1.91. Simulation Displacement Std (cm, ↓): 1.74 / 1.62 / 5.34 / 3.17 / 2.88. Table caption note: "evaluation on FPHAB and HO3D are only conducted on frames of hand grasping objects (minimal distance ≤ 5 mm)." These four terms (penetration depth, solid intersection volume, simulation displacement mean/std) recur as the standard *OakInk*-family physical-plausibility metrics used again in the benchmark tasks (Table 6).

## Method (Tink transfer pipeline)
- paradigm: dataset + a knowledge-transfer *method* (Tink) that is itself the core technical contribution, not an RL/IL policy.
- Tink = 3 sequential modules (Sec. 3.3): (1) **Implicit shape interpolation** — represent source/target object shapes as SDFs, train a single DeepSDF model per object category over all source+target SDFs, linearly interpolate the two objects' latent shape vectors at N_itpl equally-spaced quantiles (N_itpl = 10, chosen empirically as an efficiency/accuracy trade-off), decode each landmark SDF and reconstruct its mesh via Marching Cubes, giving a path of N_itpl artificial intermediate objects. (2) **Explicit contact mapping** — propagate the source object's per-vertex contact labels along the interpolation path landmark-by-landmark via ICP nearest-vertex correspondence, on the grounds that "contact regions are more invariant against shape deformation" than pose. (3) **Iterative pose refinement** — optimize hand pose θ, shape β, and wrist position P_h0 to (a) attract the 17 hand-part anchors toward their contact-consistent target-object regions (contact-consistency cost, weighted by the contactness γ_ij), (b) an anatomical cost constraining joint rotation axes/angles (adapted from Yang et al.) to avoid anatomically abnormal poses, and (c) the SDF-based interpenetration cost above; 1,000 Adam iterations per source-target pair, PyTorch.
- key trick(s): using contact regions (not raw pose) as the shape-invariant transfer signal, since "compared to pose, contact regions are more invariant against shape deformation"; SDF-based interpenetration penalty reusing the same DeepSDF representation built for shape interpolation.

## Evaluation (3 existing + 2 novel benchmark tasks, Sec. 4)
- **4.1 Hand Mesh Recovery (HMR)**: predict P_h ∈ R^(21×3) and V_h ∈ R^(778×3) from a single image. Split SP0 (default): one view per sequence held out as test, remaining 3 views split 70/5/25 → train/val/test. Metrics: MPJPE, AUC over [0,50 mm], MPVPE (wrist-relative). Table 4 (mm, AUC in parens): I2L-MeshNet 12.10 (0.784) MPJPE / 12.29 MPVPE; HandTailor 11.20 (0.884) / 11.75 MPVPE (HandTailor better on both).
- **4.2 Hand-Object Pose Estimation (HOPE)**: joint P_h and object pose (R_o ∈ SO(3), t_o ∈ R^3, represented as 8 oriented 3D bounding-box corners) from a single image, same SP0 split. Metrics: MPJPE, MPCPE (mean per-corner position error), wrist-relative. Table 5 (mm): Hasson et al. MPJPE 27.26 / MPCPE(all) 56.09 (per-category MPCPE: knife 68.40, lotion 60.70, mug 37.26, camera 68.13); Tekin et al. MPJPE 23.52 / MPCPE(all) 52.16 (knife 57.29, lotion 57.11, mug 35.44, camera 56.87) — Tekin et al. better on both aggregate metrics.
- **4.3 Grasp Pose Generation (GraspGen)**: conditional-VAE (cVAE) hand-pose generation conditioned on object shape (BPS ∈ R^4096); trained/evaluated on OakInk-Shape with an 80/10/10 object-level train/val/test split. Benchmarked method: GrabNet. Four evaluation terms, verbatim: "1) penetration depth, 2) solid intersection volume following [57], and 3) simulation displacement following [23]... 4) a perceptual survey on the network predictions at Amazon Mechanical Turk... rate the generated hand poses with scores ranging from 1 (strongly unsatisfied) to 5 (strongly satisfied)." Table 6 GrabNet column: Penet. Depth 0.67 cm, Solid Intsec. Vol. 6.60 cm³, Sim. Disp. Mean 1.21 cm, Sim. Disp. Std 2.05 cm, Percep. score 3.66/5.
- **4.4 Two novel generation tasks**: (A) **IntGen** — GrabNet + intent word-embedding conditioning, per-category results (mug/trigger-sprayer/camera/lotion-bottle) in Table 6: Penet. Depth 0.45/0.71/1.54/1.57 cm; Solid Intsec. Vol. 4.22/9.99/14.32/18.04 cm³; Sim. Disp. Mean 0.86/0.69/2.88/2.02 cm; Std 1.51/0.81/4.53/2.99 cm; Percep. score 3.86/3.93/3.94/3.98. (B) **HoverGen** — GrabNet + giver's-hand conditioning to generate a receiver's hand for handover: Penet. Depth 0.62 cm, Solid Intsec. Vol. 6.99 cm³, Sim. Disp. Mean 1.30 cm, Std 2.03 cm, Percep. score 4.03 (highest perceptual score of all rows in Table 6, though not lowest on the physical metrics). Qualitative claim: "The generated receiver hand matches our expectation: the receiver's hand should avoid colliding with or hindering the retraction path of the giver's hand" (Sec. 4.4B).
- cross-dataset validation (Sec. 3.4, "Image Dataset Cross Validation," Table 2): image-based 3D pose estimator trained on HO3D / OakInk-Image / their mixture, tested on DexYCB, MPJPE(mm): HO3D→DexYCB 55.38; OakInk-Image→DexYCB 44.81; mixture→DexYCB 39.70 (best) — "OakInk-Image complements HO3D dataset and improves the network model."
- baselines beaten: n/a in the RL/robot-policy sense — all comparisons above are cross-method/cross-dataset accuracy comparisons for CV tasks (HMR, HOPE, GraspGen), not policy baselines.
- real robot?: none. The authors explicitly flag this as future work (Discussion, quoted below).

## Limitations stated by the authors
"Current OakInk does not record dynamic hand interactions with the movable parts of the articulated object (e.g. scissors), and does not consider transferring the interaction knowledge from human hands to the multi-finger robot arms. We will address the limitations in future works." (Sec. 5, "Limitation"). Personal-data handling is discussed at length in App. G ("Discussion on Personally Identifiable Data"): 12 subjects recruited via a third-party crowd-sourcing company under IRB-equivalent approval, paid hourly, consenting to portrait/data usage for "academic and permitted commercial usages"; the released data is desensitized "by blurring the subjects' faces (if any), tattoos, rings, or any other accessories that may reveal the subjects' identity."

## Notes for the survey
- Feeds the datasets section, and directly supplies the survey's standard physical-plausibility metric vocabulary: penetration depth (cm), solid intersection volume (cm³), and simulation displacement (mean/std, cm) — cite these definitions (Sec. 3.4, Table 3 caption) as one of the corpus's clearest verbatim statements of what "physically plausible grasp" means numerically, directly comparable across FPHAB/GRAB/HO3D/OakInk itself.
