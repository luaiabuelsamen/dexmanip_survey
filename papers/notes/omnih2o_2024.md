# omnih2o_2024 — OmniH2O: Universal and Dexterous Human-to-Humanoid Whole-Body Teleoperation and Learning (He, Luo, He, Xiao, Zhang, Zhang, Kitani, Liu, Shi; CoRL 2024)

sources: papers/md/omnih2o_2024.md [bedce4c9] ; code/md/omnih2o_2024.md [750f1fa0]

## One-line contribution
A whole-body Unitree H1 motion-tracking policy (RL teacher with privileged state, DAgger-distilled student on 25-step proprioceptive history and a 3-point head/hands goal) that serves as a universal interface for VR, RGB, language and GPT-4o control; dexterous hands are driven open-loop from VR hand pose; a 40-minute teleop dataset (OmniH2O-6) and diffusion-policy autonomy on four tasks (abstract; Sec. 3, 4.3).

## Setting
- hand(s): Inspire hands on a Unitree H1 with Damiao DM-J4310-2EC motors (App. A). Hand DoF not stated. Finger and wrist commands "do[] not require inference" and are "directly mapped from the Vision Pro data to the corresponding joints" (App. A); Sec. 3: "directly compute joint targets based on inverse kinematics for an off-the-shelf low-level hand controller". bimanual only in the sense that both arms are in the whole-body policy and both hands are teleoperated.
- simulator / physics: not named in the paper. Code is built on legged_gym and rsl_rl (code README); Isaac Gym flags such as `GymDofDriveModeFlags` appear in legged_gym/legged_gym/cfg/asset/asset_base.yaml. 4096 envs (README train commands). No objects in simulation: the task is motion imitation only.
- observation: teacher s^p-privileged = [p_t, θ_t, q̇_t, ω_t, a_{t−1}] (all rigid-body pos/rot/vel), goal = reference pose plus one-frame differences (Sec. 3); state dim 913 (Table 1). Student: 25-step history of 19 DoF positions, 19 DoF velocities, root angular velocity (3), root gravity (3), previous actions (19), plus 27-dim 3-point motion goal; 90 dims per step, 1665 total (Table 6; Sec. 3). "no global linear velocity v_t information is included" (Sec. 3).
- action space: 19 joint-position targets to a PD controller; policy 50 Hz, PD 200 Hz (Fig. 3; App. A). Real system latency 20 ms (App. A).
- objects / data: AMASS retargeted to H1 as in H2O, plus "stable" standing and squatting variants of each clip (Sec. 3, Fig. 2, App. H); evaluation set Q̂ has 14k sequences (Sec. 4.1). OmniH2O-6: six teleoperated tasks at 30 Hz with head RGBD, head/hand goals, joint targets; ~5 min for Catch-Release, Squat, Rock-Paper-Scissors, ~10 min for Hammer-Catch and Basket-Pick-Place, 40 min total (Sec. 4.3); frame counts 13234 / 8535 / 12759 / 9380 / 11118 / 18436 (App. J).

## Method
- paradigm: RL teacher + supervised (DAgger) student ; IL (diffusion policy) on top for autonomy. PPO hyperparameters (Table 18): batch 64, γ 0.99, lr 0.001, clip 0.2, entropy 0.005, max grad norm 0.2, MLP [512,256,128], init noise std 1.0 (RL) / 0.001 (DAgger), 5 epochs. Distillation loss "L = ‖a_t^privileged − a_t‖₂²" (Sec. 3).
- coordination of the two hands/arms: one whole-body policy over all 19 DoF (legs, torso, both arms); the goal is only the three VR points (head, left hand, right hand) relative to the current body (Sec. 3: s^g-real = (p̃ − p, ṽ − v, p̃) for those points). The fingers are outside the policy and outside the reward; there is no hand-hand relative feature. LfD policies output "ϕ frames of motion goals" including "dexterous hand commands" from a head image, and π_OmniH2O tracks them (Sec. 4.3).
- reward (paper, App. E, Table 15; weight in brackets): Penalty — torque limits 1(τ ∉ [τmin, τmax]) [−2]; DoF position limits [−125]; DoF velocity limits [−50]; termination [−250]. Regularisation — DoF acceleration ‖d̈‖² [−0.000011]; DoF velocity ‖ḋ‖² [−0.004]; lower-body action rate [−3]; upper-body action rate [−0.625]; torque ‖τ‖ [−0.0001]; feet air time T_air − 0.25 [1000]; "Max feet height for each step max{h_max − 0.25, 0}" [1000]; feet contact force ‖F‖² [−0.75]; stumble 1(F_xy > 5 F_z) [−0.00125]; slippage ‖v_feet‖²·1(F ≥ 1) [−37.5]; feet orientation [−62.5]; in the air 1(both feet F < 1) [−200]; orientation ‖g_z^root‖ [−200]. Task — DoF position exp(−0.25‖d̂ − d‖) [32]; DoF velocity exp(−0.25‖·‖²) [16]; body position exp(−0.5‖p − p̂‖²) [30]; body position VR points exp(−0.5‖·‖²) [50]; body rotation exp(−0.1‖θ ⊖ θ̂‖) [20]; body velocity exp(−10‖·‖) [8]; body angular velocity exp(−0.01‖·‖) [8]. Curriculum: negative terms scaled by s_current, init 0.5, ×0.9999 when mean episode length < 40, ×1.0001 when > 120, capped at 1 (App. E).
- reward (code, legged_gym/legged_gym/cfg/rewards/rewards_teleop_omnih2o_teacher.yaml `scales`): torques −0.0001, torque_limits −2, dof_acc −0.000011, dof_vel −0.004, lower_action_rate −3.0, upper_action_rate −0.625, dof_pos_limits −100·1.25, dof_vel_limits −50, termination −200·1.25, feet_contact_forces −0.75, stumble −1000·1.25, feet_air_time_teleop 1000, slippage −30·1.25, feet_ori −50·1.25, in_the_air −200, orientation −200, feet_height 0, feet_max_height_for_this_air −2500 with `desired_feet_max_height_for_this_air: 0.25`; task: teleop_selected_joint_position 32, teleop_selected_joint_vel 16, teleop_body_position_extend 30, teleop_body_position_vr_3keypoints 50, teleop_body_rotation 20, teleop_body_vel 8, teleop_body_ang_vel 8. Sigmas (same file): "tracking reward : exp(-error^2/sigma)", teleop_joint_pos_sigma 0.5, teleop_joint_vel_sigma 10, teleop_body_pos_upperbody_sigma 0.03, teleop_body_pos_vr_3keypoints_sigma 0.03, teleop_body_pos_lowerbody_sigma 0.5, teleop_body_rot_sigma 0.1, teleop_body_vel_sigma 10; per-joint weights `teleop_joint_pos_selection` (arms/torso/hip pitch 2.0, other leg joints 0.5); soft limits 0.85 of URDF; `penalty_scale_range: [0.25, 1.0]`, level thresholds 50 / 120; README commands pass `rewards.penalty_curriculum=True rewards.penalty_scale=0.5`. Reward function bodies (`_reward_teleop_body_position_vr_3keypoints`, `_reward_feet_max_height_for_this_air`, etc.) are listed as signatures only in the code md (legged_gym/.../h1 env, lines around `_prepare_reward_function`).
- mismatches paper vs code: stumble −0.00125 (paper) vs −1250 (code); max-feet-height +1000 with max{·,0} (paper) vs −2500 (code, a penalty against a 0.25 m target); the paper writes exp(−c‖·‖) with c ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 0.1, 10, 0.01} where the code uses exp(−err²/σ) with σ ∈ {0.5, 0.03, 0.1, 10}; curriculum level-down threshold 40 (paper) vs 50 (code). Weights otherwise agree.
- interpenetration / contact: none. No objects in the simulation; `_reward_collision` exists in the base env and `collision: -1.` in rewards_base, but the teacher yaml does not set it. Foot contact is handled by the feet-force, slippage, stumble and in-the-air terms above. Object contact happens only on the real robot through the open-loop hands.
- key trick(s): data augmented with standing/squatting variants (App. H: without it the policy "is hard to stand still"); history instead of linear velocity (Sec. 4.1.2); DR (Table 16): friction U(0.2,1.1), CoM offset ±0.1 m, link mass U(0.7,1.3)×, P/D gains U(0.75,1.25)×, torque RFI 0.1×limit, control delay U(20,60) ms, reference offset, push every 5 s at 1 m/s, terrains flat/rough/low obstacles.

## Evaluation
- metrics (Sec. 4.1): Succ — "unsuccessful if the average deviation from reference is farther than 0.5m at any point in time"; E_g-mpjpe and E_mpjpe (mm); E_acc (mm/frame²), E_vel (mm/frame). LfD: MSE loss and success over 10 runs per task (Table 3, Table 17).
- headline numbers: Table 1 (sim, 14k clips): Privileged teacher Succ 94.77 %, E_g-mpjpe 126.51, E_mpjpe 70.68; H2O 87.52 %, 148.13, 81.06; OmniH2O 94.10 %, 141.11, 77.82, E_acc 3.70, E_vel 6.54. Without DAgger with history: 47.11 %. 22-point goal 94.72 % / 127.71 vs 3-point 94.10 % / 141.11 (Table 1c). Table 2 (real, 20 standing motions): OmniH2O E_g-mpjpe 47.94, E_mpjpe 41.87, E_acc 1.84, E_vel 2.20 vs H2O 87.33 / 53.32 / 6.03 / 5.87; VIO-velocity variant fell and is N/A. Table 3 (LfD, 4 tasks, 10 runs each): DP-DDPM 8/10 average, DP-DDIM 7.75/10, BC 1/10; 25 % data 4/10, 50 % 6.5/10, 100 % 8/10; Se-O-Se-A 8.75/10. Table 17 per task at 100 % data: Catch-Release 6/10, Squat 10/10, Hammer-Catch 6/10, Rock-Paper-Scissors 10/10.
- baselines beaten: H2O [3] (sim and real); MLP over GRU/LSTM (Table 1b, 2b); no-DAgger RL (Table 1a).
- real robot? yes — H1 + Inspire hands, Vision Pro / VR, ZED VIO on the waist, 50 Hz policy, two Orin NX or one RTX 4090 laptop (App. A). Tracking on 20 standing sequences (Table 2); LfD 10 runs per task (Table 17); teleop demos and robustness tests (Fig. 5, App. I) are qualitative.

## Limitations stated by the authors (Sec. 5)
- Requires robot root odometry; "Results from VIO can be noisy or even discontinuous".
- No safety guarantees "for extreme disturbances or out-of-distribution motion goals (e.g., large discontinuity in motion goals)".
- Stairs not supported from sparse upper-body goals.
- LfD could use more sensors ("LiDAR, wrist cameras, tactile sensors").

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "The distribution of the motion imitation dataset needs to be biased toward standing and squatting to help the policy learn to stabilize the lower body during manipulation." (Sec. 1)
- "these rewards result in the humanoid stomping to keep balanced instead of standing still. To encourage standing still and taking large steps during locomotion, we propose a key reward function max feet height for each step" (Sec. 3)
- "As control for the robot's fingers and wrists does not require inference, it is directly mapped from the Vision Pro data to the corresponding joints on the robot." (App. A)
- "Without DAgger the policy struggles to learn a coherent policy when provided with a long history." (Sec. 4.1.1)
- "predicting a sequence of actions is crucial, as it enables the robot to effectively learn and replicate the trajectory." (Sec. 4.3)

## Notes for the survey
- Feeds: humanoid teleoperation as a bimanual data source; teacher-student sim-to-real; reward curricula. Not a dexterous-hand contact paper: the hands are open-loop, the simulation has no objects, and no manipulation metric is defined; all "dexterous" evidence is qualitative video plus the LfD success counts.
- Compare humanplus_2024 (same H1 platform, RGB pose input, different hand handling) and hato_visuotactile_2024 (hands inside the learned policy, tactile input).
