# taco_2024 — TACO: Benchmarking Generalizable Bimanual Tool-ACtion-Object Understanding (Liu et al., CVPR 2024)

sources: papers/md/taco_2024.md [2cd9fef9] ; code/md/taco_2024.md [b385ac1e] (TACO-Instructions repo: loaders and data lists only, no annotation pipeline)

## One-line contribution
2.5K real bimanual tool-use sequences (5.2M frames, 12 allocentric 4096x3000 RGB + 1 egocentric RGB-D) of 14 subjects over 196 scanned objects in 131 <tool, action, target> triplets, with MANO hands fitted markerlessly from multi-view keypoints under contact/penetration losses and objects tracked by 4 mocap markers; benchmarks for compositional action recognition, motion forecasting and cooperative grasp synthesis with four generalisation splits.

## Capture setup (Sec. 3.1, App. 8)
- cameras: "12 synchronized industrial FLIR cameras" at 4096x3000; egocentric "helmet equipped with a Realsense L515 camera" at 1920x1080 RGB-D, itself tracked by mocap; "The frequency of our cameras and mocap system is 30 Hz."
- mocap: "NOKOV optical motion capture suite with 6 infrared Mars4H cameras" tracking "four markers on the object surface"; hands are markerless ("We obtain hand motion from multi-view RGB videos").
- gloves: none.
- object models: "industrial EinScan 3D scanner ... up to 100K triangular faces" (Sec. 3.1); README: "206 High-resolution object models (10K~100K faces per object mesh)".
- marker removal: markers inpainted with LAMA after masking rendered spheres of 2x marker radius (Sec. 3.2); U-Net marker-segmentation mIoU drops 63.8 % -> 11.1 % after removal (Sec. 4).

## Scale
- subjects: 14 participants, all right-handed ("all manipulation behaviors are performed by right-handed individuals", App. 10).
- objects: "20 object categories, 196 fine-grained object 3D models" (Sec. 3.3); 17 tool categories, 9 target categories (App. 10); README ships 206 models.
- triplets: 131 <tool category, action, target category> (Sec. 3.3); README v1: 151 triplets over 2317 sequences; pre-release: 244 sequences, 137 triplets.
- sequences / frames: "2.5K motion sequences and 5.2M video frames" (Sec. 1); README v1 lists 2317 sequences (2212 with egocentric, 2120 with marker-removed video). Hours not stated; derived: 5.2M / 13 views / 30 Hz = ~3.7 h.
- actions: 15; "each tool and target object being utilized to perform up to 7 different kinds of actions" (Sec. 3.3). Tool speed "16.6 cm/s and 71.3 deg/s on average" (App. 10, Table 7).
- splits (Sec. 5.1): train : S1 (no generalisation) : S2 (novel tool geometry) : S3 (novel triplet) : S4 (novel tool category) = 4 : 1 : 1 : 1.5 : 2.5; README `v1_overall_data_train_test_split.txt`.

## Annotations (Sec. 3.2, App. 9)
- hands: MANO, Theta_h = {theta in R^48, beta in R^10, t in R^3}; beta per participant precomputed from a two-hands-only capture and fixed (App. 9.3). 2D keypoints via YOLOv3 hand detection + MMPose per view, RANSAC-filtered triangulation to 3D, then two-stage fit: L_2D + L_3D + L_angle + L_tc, then object-aware refinement adding L_p and L_a.
- objects: rigid 6-DoF from mocap markers with marker-to-surface correspondence optimised "to encourage contact between markers and object surfaces while avoiding penetration" (Sec. 3.2).
- contact: not a stored label; contact is enforced in the fit via L_a (Sec. 3.2, App. 9.3). Segmentation masks: rendered silhouettes refined by SAM, tracked by Track-Anything.
- two hands and both objects jointly captured: yes, "two dynamic hands interacting with two objects" (Sec. 1), all in one synchronised volume.
- action labels per sequence (Table 1).

## Interpenetration / contact terms and metrics (verbatim)
- Fitting losses (App. 9.3; equations dropped by the converter, text kept, both now recovered by OCR from papers/md/taco_2024.ocr.md; OCR text is noisier than the layout parse, symbols may be imperfect — the OCR drops the Σ glyph, leaving the bare bound `778 i=1` where a sum to 778 mesh vertices was typeset): "Attraction loss L_a ... encourages the hands near the object to make sufficient contact with it ... V_h[i] is the i-th vertex on hand mesh, V_o[i*] is the vertex on the object closest to V_h[i], and around3D(.) calculates the distance between two points, outputting 1 if the distance is less than 0.01 meter and 0 otherwise. This loss term is computed twice: once between the right hand and the tool, and once between the left hand and the object." Eq. 11: `La = 778 i=1 around3D(Vh[i], Vo [i∗] , 0.01) ∥Vh[i] −Vo [i∗]∥2 ,` (11). "Penetration loss L_p ... Similar to [18], we define penetration loss term as" Eq. 12: `Lp = 778 i=1 max −no (Vo [i∗])T (Vh[i] −Vo [i∗]) , 0 ,` (12) "where the n_o(.) operator computes the normal for a vertex" — i.e. a normal-projected hand-vertex-inside-object penalty, computed hand-tool and hand-target only (not hand-hand, not tool-target).
- Sec. 4: "the attraction loss L_a promotes contact between the hands and the objects but may exacerbate penetration. The penetration loss L_p can mitigate penetration but does not address cases where the hands and the objects are not in contact."
- Grasp-synthesis metrics (App. 12): "the contact ratio (Con. R) indicates the proportion of results that are in contact with the tool, while the interpenetration volume (Pen. V) denotes the average volume that is occupied by both the generated hand and the tool and is computed by voxelizing hand-object meshes to 1mm cubes and counting the intersecting ones. The collision ratio (Col. R) examines conflicts between generated hands and the environment, computing the probability of results penetrating the target object and the left hand." FID over a 64-d PointNet interaction feature trained to decode the action label.
- No penetration statistic is reported for the dataset's own ground truth; quality is argued via cross-dataset hand-pose training (Table 2) and Fig. 6 qualitatively.

## Limitations stated by the authors (Sec. 6)
- "TACO currently does not cover articulated objects."; "it lacks scene diversities"; "our solution to marker removal is an application of generative models, hence the original object appearances cannot be recovered perfectly."
- Grasp-synthesis failure cases show "a large room for improving physical feasibility and naturalness on complex and slender geometries" (Sec. 5.4).

## Quotable claims (verbatim, with section)
- "the first large-scale real-world 4D bimanual hand-object-interaction dataset covering diverse tool-action-object compositions and object geometries" (Sec. 1).
- "Compared to target objects, tools always dominate in interaction and have a significantly fast motion speed (16.6 cm/s and 71.3 deg/s on average)" (App. 10).
- "the right hand commonly controls the tool and thus consistently moves faster than the left hand" (App. 10).

## Notes for the survey
- Feeds: two-hand HOI dataset table; source of BiDexHD's 141 auto-constructed tasks and one of DexTrack's two evaluation sets; the Pen. V / Col. R definitions (1 mm voxel intersection, hand-vs-environment collision probability) are the corpus's only voxel-based interpenetration metric for two-hand-two-object scenes.
- Hand poses are markerless fits, so ground-truth contact is a product of L_a/L_p weighting, not a measurement; any downstream penetration statistic on TACO references inherits that.
- Used or cited by (word-boundary grep over papers/md and code/md): bidexhd_2024, dextrack_2025, gigahands_2024, maniptrans_2025, oakink2_2024, zhao_dexhand_survey_2026, being_h0_2025 (unverified match). rdt1b_2024 and bai_unified_manip_survey_2025 also match "TACO" but refer to a different TACO (Rosete-Beas et al. 2022; a reference-list entry) — false positives.
