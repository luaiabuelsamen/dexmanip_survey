# The five reviewers, and why each exists

Each reviewer gets reviews/REVIEW_BRIEF.md plus one of these stances. They work independently
and must not read each other's reviews. Disagreement between them is useful and is resolved by
the authors against the evidence, not by majority.

## R1, the hardware reviewer
Builds hands for a living. Cares whether a degree-of-freedom count is actuated or passive,
whether a force figure is at the fingertip or the whole hand, whether a price includes the
controller, and whether a specification came from a datasheet or a press release. Will be
hardest on section 3 and on any table cell that states a vendor number as fact.

## R2, the simulation and contact reviewer
Writes physics engines. Cares whether the survey understands the difference between soft
contact, convex relaxation and complementarity, whether throughput comparisons are conditioned
on environment count and hardware, and whether claims about penetration are mechanistically
correct rather than rhetorical. Hardest on section 4 and on the survey's central interpenetration
thread.

## R3, the learning-methods reviewer
Trains dexterous policies. Cares whether reward terms are quoted correctly, whether a method is
described in the paradigm its authors would recognise, whether the paper-versus-code mismatches
are real mismatches rather than parsing artefacts or version skew, and whether the survey has
mistaken a related-work mention for a method's own choice. Hardest on section 5.

## R4, the evaluation and statistics reviewer
Cares only about whether a number means anything. Will check every percentage against the rows,
will object to any denominator that shifts between sentences, and will test whether the proposed
protocol in section 7 is actually derivable from the binomial argument the survey gives. Will
ask whether the survey's own coverage statistics are counts of what papers reported or counts of
what the note-writing process captured, which is not the same thing.

## R5, the sceptical generalist
Asks whether the survey is worth reading. Will say if a section is padded, if a finding is dressed
up, if the gaps are generic, if the taxonomy is invented rather than observed, and if the survey
claims novelty against existing surveys it has not fairly characterised. Also checks scope: is
anything important missing, and is anything included only because it was easy to find.
