\section{The hardware and engine tables}
\label{app:tables}

Sections~III and~IV argue over individual cells of three tables: which column is emptiest, which
cell is blank and why, which figure rests on a press release. Those three are set here rather than
in the body, because a reader consults them rather than reading them through. Every other
tabulation in this survey is in the repository, and Appendix~\ref{app:method} says where.

Nothing in these tables is a measurement by this survey. Every specification is a manufacturer
claim, a figure in a paper, or a figure in a press article. Table~\ref{tab:hands-available} and
Table~\ref{tab:hands-announced} split the 33 hand rows by whether the hand can be bought or built,
and Table~\ref{tab:simulators} carries the 15 engines. The controlled vocabularies in all three
compress a sentence the corpus stores in full: \texttt{corpus/rows/} holds the actuation, tactile,
force-kind, contact-model and solver text at its own length, with the source's own punctuation and
wording, and \texttt{papers/notes/} holds the quotation each was read from.

Each caption states how many of its own cells no source filled. Two conventions behind those counts
are worth stating once. A blank price is a hand with no public list price rather than a free hand;
and a hand with no entry in the manifest was recorded from a page that could not be stored, so its
row should be read as a claim with no retrievable source.
